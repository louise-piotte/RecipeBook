# WP02 Section 04 B2 pili-pulp-flour boundary hold (R186)

Integrated the review as a hold. Keep pili-pulp flour outside active ingredient references, aliases,
legacy identities, and the research ledger until a current Canadian standalone flour product and
product-matched Québec French wording establish the catalog boundary.

The evidence supports a real research food flour made from *Canarium ovatum* fruit pulp, separate from
the edible kernel and the existing `pili nut flour / farine de noix de pili` identity. The Philippine
Department of Agriculture describes the fruit's pulp as edible after cooking, while the University of
the Philippines Los Baños thesis names and characterizes pili-pulp flour. These sources establish the
food-material distinction, not a current Canadian retail product or Québec French product name. The
current Québec `noix de pili` wording in the reviewed market evidence names the edible kernel; do not
promote the literal translation `farine de pulpe de pili` into an active display without separate
Québec terminology evidence.

Keep whole pili fruit, cooked pulp, pulp powder, kernel flour, oils, and unqualified `pili flour` or
`pili powder` separate. The 2009 cookery report covers food trials with pili-pulp flour and recommends
home and small-scale use. The 2023 and 2025 studies report different sample- and process-specific
bulk densities, and one reports flour-preparation yield. These values do not support an active density,
household measure, package conversion, recipe yield, or general substitution. The bundled 114 recipes
and 1,123 ingredient rows have no matching demand.
