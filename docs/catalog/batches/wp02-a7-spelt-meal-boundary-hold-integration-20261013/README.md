# WP02 A7 Spelt Meal Boundary Hold

Integrated 2026-10-13 as a focused source refresh under the open [A7 checklist](../../../RecipeBook-global-ingredient-catalog-plan.txt#L1444). The prior residual grain-meals review already held spelt meal; this update adds current Canadian product evidence and rechecks Québec French naming.

## Decision

Keep spelt meal unresolved and unallocated. A Canadian grain processor lists Meal and Flour separately among its spelt applications, and two Canadian cracker retailers use the exact phrase `Spelt Meal`. These sources do not define the meal's milling specification, grind, or Québec French display. Keep the phrase distinct from active spelt flour, malted spelt flour, spelt flakes, puffed spelt, and einkorn. The recipe bundle has no spelt demand. Add no identity, alias, form, measure, recipe link, conversion, yield, or substitution.

## Evidence and checks

- [InfraReady's spelt page](https://www.infrareadyproducts.com/products/spelt) lists `Meal` and `Flour` as separate applications and identifies a Saskatchewan processor. The application list does not specify a standalone product or grind.
- [Metro's Breton cracker listing](https://www.metro.ca/en/online-grocery/aisles/snacks/salty-snacks/crackers/original-multigrain-and-garden-vegetable-flavoured-crackers-variety-pack/p/055653672906) and [Voilà's Breton multigrain cracker listing](https://voila.ca/products/dare-breton-crackers-multigrain-225-g/215865EA) both use `Spelt Meal` in a composite ingredient list. They do not establish the meal's grain fraction or processing; Metro marks its product out of stock and notes that ingredient information may change.
- OQLF's [épeautre entry](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/8354126/epeautre) supports the French base term for spelt. Its two [gruau entries](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/8363388/gruau) and [technical grits entry](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/8430011/gruau) cover porridge and malt-grit meanings, not a dry spelt-meal display. A Swiss product listing uses `gruau d'épeautre complet`, but does not establish that wording for Québec.
- A German mill's [whole-spelt meal specification](https://www.heimatsmuehle.com/en/commercial-customers/products/spelt-meal-fine-medium-coarse) shows one supplier's meal/flour boundary and fine, medium, and coarse grades. It does not define the Canadian products; its nutrition per 100 g is not a household-volume measure or density.
- The proposal audit counted 1,367 active references, 100 forms, 64 legacy candidates, 1,699 research-ledger records, 114 manifest-listed recipes, and 1,123 ingredient rows. It found no exact spelt-meal owner and no recipe containing spelt or épeautre. The prior residual review already records the candidate as held, so no duplicate legacy or ledger record was added.
- The checklist, roadmap, and progress log now record the source refresh. No catalog or numeric data changed. No tests were run because this was a documentation-only change.

The full source register, exact ownership, picker projection, corpus scan, measurement limits, and next-action criteria are in the [round-42 proposal](../../proposals/wp02-a7-spelt-meal-boundary-review-20261013-round42.md).
