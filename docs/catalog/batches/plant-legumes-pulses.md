## Identity boundary used in this packet

Québec terminology and Canadian crop classification do not use *legume* and *pulse* as interchangeable scopes. The OQLF defines food `légumineuse` broadly enough to include edible pods/seeds, and distinguishes immature fresh forms (for example, petits pois, haricots verts, and fèves de soya vertes) from mature dried seeds. It states that English *pulse* refers only to an edible seed [S1]. Statistics Canada's crop class likewise covers dry pulse crops and explicitly excludes soybeans and fresh green vegetables [S2].

For the existing internal category `LEGUME_AND_PULSE`, retain the broad food-family use needed by the catalog, but do not let the category become an alias or conversion relationship. Keep fresh peas/pods, mature dried seeds, soybeans, and peanut identities/form states distinct wherever a recipe distinguishes them. Statistics Canada excludes soybeans from its pulse-crop class, and peanuts are already in the seed's `NUT_SEED_AND_DRIED_FRUIT` category; the OQLF still treats soy and peanut as Fabaceae/legumes [S1, S2].

## Measurement, substitution, and safety limits

- This batch supports names and identity/form distinctions only. It does not create a density, cup weight, count mapping, dry-to-cooked yield, can/drained yield, or substitution ratio.
- Health Canada CNF serving pages contain food-specific portion data, but entries differ by source food state (for example, dry/raw, boiled, canned drained solids, solids and liquid, and salted/unsalted). Those values are not generic densities and need separate WP07/WP12 review before any measurement use [S16].
- No conversion between dried, soaked, boiled, canned, split, or puréed pulses is inferred. The chickpea dry-to-cooked quantity remains a source-backed range in the WP08 packet and cannot become a single factor here.
- Aquafaba's identity/name is supported, and WP08 has a separate source-supported egg-replacement finding, but the current runtime does not represent count-to-volume replacement or pulse-source/process variation. Keep that finding blocked from automatic use until integration reviews the representation [S24, S26].
- No allergy, food-safety, canning/preservation, or dietary suitability claim is made. Taxonomic or terminology evidence does not establish those claims.

## Rejected or unresolved candidates

- **Aliases for form state:** `canned chickpeas`, `dried chickpeas`, `pois chiches en conserve`, and `pois chiches secs` are not synonyms for the base identity. Preserve the state in explicit forms.
- **Broad “bean/fève” mapping:** OQLF notes Quebec uses `fève` colloquially for common beans but also identifies fava/gourgane and soya under related French “fève” usage. Do not make unqualified `fève` or `bean` a global alias [S1, S6, S14, S22].
- **Fresh versus dry peas:** `petits pois` means shelled green peas; dried split peas are mature dry seeds with split cotyledons. Do not alias `petits pois jaunes` to dried yellow peas or map dry peas to the fresh-pea reference [S1, S2, S10, S17].
- **Mung versus urad:** OQLF documents that `haricot mungo` is used for both *Vigna radiata* and *Vigna mungo*. The active mung-bean row is explicitly scoped to *V. radiata* and has source-backed aliases; keep `black gram` and `urd` out of it. The exact fr-CA display/alias for the separate urad identity remains open [S11, S27].
- **White bean classes:** `haricot blanc` covers multiple white bean varieties; OQLF's example “white kidney bean” is not evidence that navy, Great Northern, cannellini, and white kidney are the same culinary identity [S26, S2].
- **Black chickpea alias:** CFIA's Desi description permits seed coats from tan to black, but that does not support mapping every “black chickpea” or “black gram” string to generic chickpeas. Keep exact Desi/black product context [S4, S11].
- **Flour/meal conflation:** Do not merge chickpea flour with chickpeas. `besan`, `gram flour`, roasted gram flour, pea flour, lentil flour, and bean flours remain separate product/process questions; a broad legume crop taxonomy cannot identify milling, dehulling, roasting, or particle-size preparation [S18, S19].
- **Aquafaba as the bean itself or all can liquid:** aquafaba is a liquid identity; canned beans with liquid are solids plus medium. Neither should resolve to drained beans. Do not infer identical performance across chickpea, other pulse, homemade cooking, soaking, and canned liquid [S16, S24].
- **Universal forms:** No global cooking, canned, puréed, or fermented form is proposed across all legume species. Product form and cooking state must be explicit.
