# WP02 Section A — Japanese strong wheat flour naming hold (2026-10-12)

## Decision

Keep Japanese strong wheat flour, 強力粉 (*kyōrikiko*), outside the active catalog until a Québec
French class display or bilingual package label resolves its name and mapping. No active
reference, aliases, ledger candidate, recipe link, measures, unit mappings, forms, conversions,
yields, or substitutions were added.

Keep the Japanese class distinct from medium flour 中力粉, semi-strong flour 準強力粉, weak flour
薄力粉, and the existing Canadian high-gluten-flour and bread-flour identities. Japanese product use
for bread, pizza, and gyoza wrappers does not establish Canadian flour-grade equivalence.

## Evidence and boundary

- Japan's [Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries](https://www.maff.go.jp/j/heya/sodan/1106/02.html)
  describes four practical flour classes by gluten strength and use: 強力粉 for bread and gyoza
  wrappers, 準強力粉 for Chinese noodles, 中力粉 for Japanese noodles, and 薄力粉 for cakes and
  thickening.
- The [Japan Flour Milling Promotion Foundation](https://www.seifun.or.jp/pages/79/) says Japan has
  no public flour definitions or standards; milling companies use these practical classes based on
  gluten strength and use. This supports a Japanese national class boundary but does not create a
  Canadian or Québec crosswalk.
- OQLF's [farine de blé de force entry](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/8357376/farine-de-ble-de-force)
  defines a strong-wheat flour rich in gluten and used mainly for bread. That preferred Québec phrase
  is already owned by the active high-gluten-flour identity; it does not identify 強力粉 specifically.
- OQLF's [farine tout usage entry](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/25796945/farine-tout-usage)
  describes the Canadian class and notes that flour classification and naming differ by country.
- Federal [TERMIUM Plus](https://www.btb.termiumplus.gc.ca/tpv2alpha/alpha-eng.html?i=1&index=frt&lang=eng&srchtxt=FARINE+FORTE)
  records generic `strong flour / farine forte`; it is not Japanese-qualified and is not added as an
  alias.
- A Canadian-facing [Konnichiwa Mart French product page](https://konnichiwa.ca/fr/products/a415-152),
  whose seller is Nisshin Seifun Welna, calls the SKU with JAN 4902110341614 *Farine à pain Nissin
  Verna*. The [manufacturer page for the same JAN](https://www.nisshin-seifun-welna.com/index/products/4902110341614.html)
  calls it *パン専用小麦粉* (bread-only wheat flour), not 強力粉. This French retail title supports one
  product name, not a Québec French display for the entire class; *farine à pain* already belongs to
  the active bread-flour identity.
- NIPPN's [強力小麦粉 product](https://www.nippn.co.jp/products/powder/flour/detail/1214765_2250.html)
  gives one 1 kg package, product protein value, and bread/pizza/gyoza uses. Those product data do
  not define class-wide measures or composition thresholds. WAFUU Canada's [English listing](https://wafuu.com/en-ca/products/haruyokoi-100-japanese-wheat-strong-flour-for-bread-2kg-baking-master)
  provides no French class name. A French eBay marketplace title is seller-authored and the seller
  is in California; its `farine à pain` wording does not establish a Québec term and already belongs
  to the bread-flour reference.

## Search and recipe findings

The exact-ownership audit found no active, form, legacy, or ledger owner for the Japanese class name
or candidate ID. Existing owners remain explicit: `farine de blé de force` belongs to high-gluten
flour, and `farine à pain` / `bread flour` belong to bread flour. The current search normalizer strips
the Japanese script query to an empty key; romanization can be indexed later if a Québec/Canadian
display is established.

The full manifest-listed corpus has 114 recipes and 1,123 ingredient rows. It has no Japanese strong
flour or candidate-term matches. The two bread-flour recipe lines remain linked to the existing
bread-flour identity.

No unit density, package yield, class-wide measure, conversion, or substitution is supported. The
documentation-only hold makes no database JSON change, so no app, unit, instrumentation, emulator,
or deployment tests were run.
