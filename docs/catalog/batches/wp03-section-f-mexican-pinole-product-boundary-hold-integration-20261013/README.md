# WP03 Section F Mexican pinole product-boundary hold — 2026-10-13

## Decision

Keep Mexican `pinole` unallocated until a Canadian or Québec-facing package/specification identifies
the intended product boundary. Mexican sources describe toasted, ground maize prepared plain or with
sugar, cinnamon, or other additions; current commercial examples also show both a single-ingredient
blue-corn product and a sweetened/spiced red-corn product. Québec French sources use the unchanged
name in a regional report and a prepared-cookie menu, but do not identify a dry package label.

Keep the candidate separate from generic roasted corn flour, cornmeal, masa harina, `pinolillo`,
`piznate`, and prepared drinks such as atole. The recipe bundle has no matching demand. Add no active
reference, alias, ledger candidate, form, measure, density, mapping, recipe link, conversion, yield,
or substitution. The Mexican retailer's tablespoon statements remain source-specific and inactive.

## Evidence

- Mexico's [Secretaría de Agricultura](https://www.gob.mx/agricultura/articulos/maiz-la-raiz-de-las-bebidas-tradicionales-en-mexico?idiom=es)
  associates pinole/piznate with Nayarit and Sinaloa and describes toasted ground maize prepared with
  water and additions such as cinnamon or piloncillo. The [Chihuahua state account](https://chihuahua.gob.mx/contenidos/muestran-tecnica-de-elaboracion-del-pinole-artesanal-durante-el-festival-virtual-indigena)
  describes toasted maize ground to fine flour and sweetened with piloncillo or sugar.
- [Pinole Blue's original product](https://pinoleblue.com/products/original-pinole) lists organic
  roasted ground blue corn as its only ingredient. [Moles Virrey's product](https://molesvirrey.com/tienda/producto/pinole/)
  describes toasted red maize with anise, cinnamon, and sugar. These support different product
  formulations, not one universal formula.
- The Québec French [CISO annual report](https://ciso.qc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/Rapport-annuel-2020-2021-FINAL.pdf)
  uses `pinole` for a Mexican cooperative product, and a [Montréal bakery menu](https://www.ubereats.com/ca-fr/store/carlota-boulangerie-mexicaine-new/AtJj-0udU2uIoW5i9wN-9w?pl=JTdCJTIyYWRkcmVzcyUyMiUzQSUyMjE4NjElMjBSdWUlMjBTYWludGUtQ2F0aGVyaW5lJTIwT3Vlc3QlMjIlMkMlMjJyZWZlcmVuY2UlMjIlM0ElMjJDaElKR1RuYmQyd2F5VXdSQk9mYV9OTmNITkUlMjIlMkMlMjJyZWZlcmVuY2VUeXBlJTIyJTNBJTIyZ29vZ2xlX3BsYWNlcyUyMiUyQyUyMmxhdGl0dWRlJTIyJTNBNDUuNDkzMDk2JTJDJTIybG9uZ2l0dWRlJTIyJTNBLTczLjU4MDMzNTQ5OTk5OTk5JTdE)
  uses `Polvorón Pinole` for a cookie. Neither is a Québec terminology ruling or a dry-package label.
- [Mercado a Casa's Mexican listing](https://mercadoacasa.mx/products/pinole) describes toasted maize
  that may include sugar/cinnamon and gives a tablespoon estimate. That estimate is not adopted as a
  reusable measure or density.
