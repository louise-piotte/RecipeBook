# WP02 A7 R1063 — sorghum fine-shorts boundary hold

Reviewed 2026-10-19 as generic milling-fraction research. The proposal recommends keeping the
candidate out of active catalog and ledger data.

## Disposition

No sorghum-shorts identity is added. A primary 1996 cake-study abstract uses the phrase “fine short”
and reports a study-specific wheat-flour cake result up to 20%; the article page did not expose its
full methods during review. A separate 1988 laboratory milling study defines process-specific
shorts streams and identifies its shorts II as coarse middlings and germ within mill-feed. These
sources do not prove the cake-study sample is the same milling stream or establish a transferable
human-food class. FAO offal discussion does not define sorghum shorts, and the Canada Grain
Commission glossary term `remoulages (farine de passage)` is explicitly about wheat. No Québec
French culinary name is supported.

Keep `ingredient-ref-grain-sorghum`, `ingredient-ref-sorghum-bran`, sorghum flour, grits, semolina,
starch, separated sorghum germ, and `candidate-sorghum-meal` unchanged. Do not attach a milling
fraction as a form under whole grain, transfer the R867 bran sources, or use the cake percentage as
a general substitution. No aliases, measures, density, mappings, forms, recipe links, yields, or
active substitutions are proposed.

The assigned work stayed within generic ingredient diversity. No brand, product-line, flavour,
SKU, retailer-variant, or commercial roster was researched. Commercial-product counts do not add
ingredient diversity; existing branded entries and product-specific evidence remain preserved.

## Evidence

- [1996 primary cake-study abstract](https://journals.ekb.eg/article_429102_0.html), “Addition of Sorghum Fractions to Confectionery Foods.” The fine-short percentage is limited to its tested sample and cake formula; the full methods were not directly accessible.
- [1988 primary milling paper](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/156482658801000103), “Milling High-tannin and Low-tannin Varieties of Sorghum,” pp. 1–3. Its shorts labels and mill-feed definitions arise from specific laboratory processing of three varieties.
- [FAO, *Sorghum and millets in human nutrition*, processing chapter](https://www.fao.org/4/T0818e/T0818E09.HTM), used only for the general human-food/offal boundary.
- [OQLF, “sorgho”](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/26506486/sorgho), supports the crop name, not a milled-fraction name.
- [Canada Grain Commission archived glossary](https://epe.lac-bac.gc.ca/100/201/301/qualite_ble_ont/html/2014/glossary-lexique/ggtabc-abcltg-2-eng.asp%40Term%3DS?nodisclaimer=1), “shorts (flour mill streams),” is wheat-scoped and does not support a sorghum French alias.
