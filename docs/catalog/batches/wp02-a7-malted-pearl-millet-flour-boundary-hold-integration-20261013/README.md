# WP02 A7 malted pearl-millet flour boundary hold — 2026-10-13

## Decision

Keep malted pearl-millet flour unresolved and unallocated. Peer-reviewed studies establish
experimental flour made from malted or germinated pearl millet, but the reviewed Canadian/Québec
search found no product page identifying it as malted or giving it a source-attested French display.
Keep it distinct from malted finger-millet flour, sprouted pearl-millet flour, plain pearl-millet
flour. The ambiguous “activated pearl millet flour” listing does not establish a malted product.

The bundled recipe corpus has no matching demand. Add no active reference, alias, ledger candidate,
form, measure, density, mapping, recipe link, conversion, yield, or substitution. Do not transfer the
plain pearl-millet flour study's tapped-cylinder density observation to a malted product.

## Evidence

- Obadina et al., [Changes in nutritional and physico-chemical properties of pearl millet flour as a
  result of malting](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5686025/): experimental Ex-Borno flour
  malted under study-specific conditions and passed through a 500 μm sieve.
- Owheruo et al., [Physicochemical properties of malted finger millet and pearl
  millet](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6392857/): treats the two millet species separately
  and studies flour from germinated samples.
- [OQLF's pearl-millet entry](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/26506450/millet-perle)
  names the grain; the [Codex standard for pearl millet flour](https://www.fao.org/4/a1392e/a1392e00.pdf)
  covers plain flour. Neither establishes a malted-flour product name in French.
- [S&V Millets' “Activated Pearl Millet Flour”
  listing](https://svmillets.com/product/pearl-millet-flour/) describes flour made from 100% pearl
  millet but does not define “activated” or equate it with malting.
