# WP03 pearled-hominy and pozole-corn boundary review

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-07; terminology recheck 2026-10-12
- **Status:** both candidates reviewed and retained unresolved; no catalog identity or alias added.
- **Scope:** distinguish dry-milled pearled hominy and regional pozole/posole corn from the active
  preserved hominy-corn and ground hominy-grits identities.

## Decisions

| Candidate | Product evidence | Québec French evidence | Disposition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pearled hominy | TERMIUM distinguishes pearled hominy from lye-treated hominy. USDA describes pearl hominy as a dry-milling product, which may be sold as hominy or processed into flaking grits. | *Hominy perlé* appears in a CFIA feed-byproduct description. OQLF's *semoules du maïs* describes hominy broadly and does not establish a human-food label for the pearled product. A 2000 Recettes du Québec article uses *maïs perlé* for a broad maize type without defining the dry-milled product. | Keep the candidate separate from lye-treated `hominy corn / maïs lessivé`, hominy grits, cornmeal, and masa harina. Do not add a reference until a human-food source supports a Québec French product name. |
| Pozole / posole corn | Mexican government and research sources document specialty whole-kernel maize and precooked, nixtamalized, bloomed canned products. A regional New Mexico source describes lime-treated, dried posole corn as distinct from hominy used as a substitute. | No authoritative English–Québec French food-term pair was found. A Québec retailer sells the canned product under a hominy title and describes it as *Maïs Pozolero Blanco*. | Keep the candidate unallocated. Its broad name spans dry regional corn and canned products that overlap the active hominy identity. Narrow a future row to an evidenced product state and display before integration. Do not alias it to hominy corn, hominy grits, cornmeal, or masa harina. |

The distinction is meaningful, but the current evidence does not support a single catalog row for either
candidate. Pearled hominy has a supported dry-milling identity but lacks a human-food Québec French
name. Pozolero corn has stronger bilingual marketplace clues but its canned Québec product is also
sold as hominy; sources also use the name across multiple maize varieties and preparation states.

## Recipe and collision audit

All 114 bundled recipe files and the current 364-reference seed were checked. No recipe text, exact
display key, or alias matched either candidate. The closest active identities are `hominy corn / maïs
lessivé`, `hominy grits / gruau de maïs`, `cornmeal / semoule de maïs`, and `masa harina / farine de
maïs nixtamalisée`. No recipe was relinked; the seed remains unchanged for these candidates.

## Sources

- **WP03-S126 — TERMIUM Plus, [hominy](https://www.btb.termiumplus.gc.ca/tpv2alpha/alpha-eng.html?fchrcrdnm=23&i=&index=enb&lang=eng&sg_kp_wet=1113184&srchtxt=CAUSTIC):** distinguishes lye-treated hominy, pearled hominy, and ground hominy.
- **WP03-S151 — USDA Economic Research Service, [Feed Situation and Outlook, August 1982](https://esmis.nal.usda.gov/sites/default/release-files/dv13zt20x/2j62s871n/m613n2962/Feedsit-08-18-1982.pdf):** its dry-milling section distinguishes pearl hominy from hominy grits, meal, and flour, and says pearl hominy may be sold as hominy or further processed into flaking grits.
- **WP03-S139 — CFIA, [Tableau canadien des ingrédients des aliments du bétail](https://inspection.canada.ca/fr/sante-animaux/aliments-du-betail/documents-incorporer-renvoi/tableau-canadien-ingredients-aliments-du-betail):** the feed-byproduct definition for *farine hominy* mentions *hominy perlé*; this is not human-food naming evidence.
- **WP03-S153 — OQLF, [semoules du maïs (hominy)](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/17036518/semoules-du-mais):** describes hominy broadly as hulled and degermed maize broken or ground to different sizes; it does not name pearled hominy as a human-food product.
- **WP03-PH-20261012-S01 — Recettes du Québec, [Le maïs](https://www.recettes.qc.ca/chroniques/quoi-manger/article/le-mais-29):** a 2000 article says human-food maize includes *maïs perlé* among broad maize types, but supplies no product definition or dry-milling context. It does not support *maïs perlé* as the Québec French name for pearled hominy.
- **WP03-S125 — OQLF, [maïs lessivé](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/17059882/mais-lessive):** supports the existing preserved processed-corn identity and helps keep it separate from dry milling.
- **WP03-S154 — Gobierno del Estado de Puebla, SNDIF [procurement specification](https://licitaciones.puebla.gob.mx/images/BasesEstatales2022/Mayo/GESAL-105-152-2022-CALIENTES_Y_FRIOS_CASAS_DE_ASITENCIA.pdf):** describes *maíz pozolero precocido en lata* as mainly Cacahuacintle, nixtamalized, deheaded, blanched, bloomed, and canned in liquid.
- **WP03-S155 — INIFAP, [Yield and other agronomic traits of pozolero corn varieties](https://cienciasagricolas.inifap.gob.mx/index.php/agricolas/en/article/view/3225):** documents Ancho, Cacahuazintle, and Elotes Occidentales materials used or evaluated as pozolero corn; the name is not one cultivar.
- **WP03-S156 — Visit Albuquerque, [New Mexico Holiday Recipes: Posole](https://assets.simpleviewinc.com/simpleview/image/upload/v1/clients/albuquerque/NMHolidayRecipes_ABQ_Posole_c3c3269d-a512-4c57-b32e-ec017af0f274.pdf):** describes a regional lime-treated, dried corn product and treats hominy as a substitute, not a universal equivalence.
- **WP03-S157 — Rancho Latin Market, [Maïs blanc Hominy à la mexicaine](https://rancholatinmarket.ca/fr/products/goya-white-hominy-corn-mexican-29-oz-maiz-pozolero-blanco-grande-hominy-822g-by-goya):** its French listing calls the same canned item hominy in the title and *Maïs Pozolero Blanco* in the description.
