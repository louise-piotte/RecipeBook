# WP02 Section C R346 — treacle picker-boundary hold

Integrated on 2026-10-16 as a documentation-only boundary decision. Unqualified `treacle`, UK `light treacle`, and `black treacle` remain out of the global catalog. No active reference, alias, form, ledger record, recipe link, measure, conversion, yield, or substitution was added or changed.

The hold preserves the existing identities and their picker behavior:

- Golden syrup keeps its existing `mélasse claire` and `mélasse légère` French aliases. OQLF lists `light treacle` as UK usage under golden syrup, so the unqualified global alias would lose that market scope.
- Blackstrap molasses keeps its OQLF-backed `blackstrap`, `black strap`, and `third molasses` English aliases. OQLF also lists `black treacle` as UK usage, while its note explains that `mélasse noire` has conflicting grade usage; the catalog cannot encode that regional qualifier.
- Fancy molasses, pomegranate molasses, and the unresolved legacy generic-molasses candidate remain separate. The word `molasses` alone does not collapse these identities.
- A separate black-treacle row would also appear for the shorter query `treacle` under current phrase matching. The corpus scan found no treacle or molasses lines to resolve against a recipe.

The reviewed sources support the limits of the decision. The Canadian standard has separate entries for golden/refiners' syrup and refiners'/blackstrap/cooking molasses, but no treacle-named entry in Volume 15. OQLF marks both `light treacle` and `black treacle` as UK terms in their respective entries. Lyle's presents Golden Syrup and Black Treacle as separately named products, and a Canadian retailer lists the imported 454 g tin with the product-specific bilingual ingredient wording `cane molasses / mélasse de canne`. A Canadian Forces French gingerbread handout uses `mélasse noire` and `sirop de mélasse noire` descriptively for black treacle; it does not resolve the OQLF terminology conflict.

No density, volume-to-mass conversion, unit mapping, package yield, form, or substitution is supported for treacle. Package sizes and label data remain specific to their cited products. Reopen the identity decision if a recipe explicitly requires a named treacle product or if search can preserve market-qualified terms.
