# WP02 Section A — Japanese medium-strength wheat-flour naming hold (2026-10-12)

## Decision

Keep Japanese medium-strength wheat flour, 中力粉 (*chūrikiko*), outside the active catalog. Japanese
sources distinguish this class and describe its use for udon, but the review found no authoritative
Québec French term or Canadian bilingual product label. The earlier draft `farine de blé japonaise
de force moyenne (chūrikiko)` is transparent wording, not an attested display. Do not substitute
`farine tout usage`, `farine de blé de force`, or `farine de blé tendre`; the cited Québec definitions
refer to different flour classes and provide no crosswalk.

No active reference, alias, form, measurement, recipe link, conversion, yield, or substitution was
added. No candidate record was created because the missing part is a usable Québec display and the
review has no direct French product evidence to record as an established identity.

## Evidence and limits

- Japan's Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries distinguishes `中力粉` as a medium-gluten
  class used for udon and other Japanese noodles: [flour types](https://www.maff.go.jp/j/heya/sodan/1106/02.html).
- OQLF describes Canadian `farine tout usage` as a hard/soft wheat blend around 12% protein and notes
  that flour classification varies by country: [farine tout usage](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/25796945/farine-tout-usage).
- OQLF's `farine de blé de force` is a high-gluten flour used mainly for bread:
  [farine de blé de force](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/8357376/farine-de-ble-de-force).
- OQLF's `farine de blé tendre` is a softer, lower-gluten flour used mainly for pastries:
  [farine de blé tendre](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/8357375/farine-de-ble-tendre).
- Nippn's product page supports `中力小麦粉` for one Japanese product, but provides neither a French
  label nor Canadian market evidence: [Nippn medium flour product](https://www.nippn.co.jp/products/powder/flour/detail/1194445_2250.html).

The evidence review is bounded: failure to find a Canadian French display does not prove that none
exists. Reopen when a source names the class in Québec French or a Canadian bilingual product source
identifies this Japanese flour class in French. Product-specific wording should not automatically
become a generic alias.

## Ownership and demand review

The proposal review found no exact owner in active references, forms, legacy import identities, or the
research ledger. All 114 manifest-listed recipe files and 1,123 ingredient rows were scanned for the
Japanese class, romanizations, proposed French wording, and ingredient ID; there were no matching
rows or recipe links. Search normalization turns kanji-only queries into an empty query, so kanji
aliases would not provide a safe picker path.
