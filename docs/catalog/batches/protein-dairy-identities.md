## Boundary: identity only

This packet does not research or endorse ingredient weights, volume measures, density, edible portion, package yields, cooking yields, drained yields, or substitution ratios. It does not infer that similar cooking behavior means two ingredients are interchangeable. Animal species, cut, bone/skin status, preservation, dairy fat/form, egg part/state, seafood species, and alternative-protein base are retained only where they change the food a recipe requests. Measurement and yield evidence must be reviewed separately against WP07/WP12; substitution evidence belongs to WP08.

## Accepted terminology evidence versus unresolved decisions

“Accepted” here means that the cited source directly supports the stated term or identity boundary for this review. The selected integrated rows and corrections are listed in the integration update; remaining packet candidates are not approved by that update.

### Source-supported identity observations

- CFIA's Meat Cuts Manual is the controlling Canadian reference for standardized retail cut nomenclature for meat species it covers; its bilingual pork and beef materials support species + cut naming and useful cut-family boundaries [S2, S3].
- CFIA and OQLF support explicit species qualification where the unqualified term could hide a materially different identity: milk source, fish species, lamb vs mutton, and offal species [S1, S4, S5, S7, S20].
- OQLF provides Québec French preferred terms and distinctions for fish, eggs, dairy products, plant-based beverages, and protein alternatives [S8, S12–S17, S21–S36].
- Canadian standardized names distinguish whole milk, partly skimmed/skim milk, milk powders/concentrates, and egg physical products. These are identity/form boundaries only and provide no amount, conversion, density, or yield [S18–S20, S27].

### Unresolved before any seed integration

- Exact identity and label convention for each fish species/subspecies and seafood item must be checked against its CFIA Fish List record, including the paired fr-CA name. Candidate examples above are not a substitute for that lookup.
- Whether the app should represent species-level distinctions for broad recipe words such as salmon, tuna, shrimp, or white fish needs a product decision; no generic class should silently select a species.
- Meat-cut display terms outside the CFIA manual's standardized names, specialty game, offal, and regional North American butcher terms need a bilingual reviewer.
- “Coffee cream,” “half-and-half,” “heavy cream,” “whipping cream,” cooking cream and exact milk-fat classes need current Canadian retail/standard evidence before alias activation.
- Alternative product names and formulas vary by brand. A generic “plant-based meat,” “plant butter,” “plant cream,” “plant cheese,” “plant yogurt,” or egg-replacer identity needs a defined composition/use boundary or an exact named product.
- Candidate category values for new items are intentionally not assigned where the fixed enum boundary is unclear; integration owner decides category and ID.
- Current aliases were not compared exhaustively to every recipe line. Before removal/renaming, integration should confirm recipe text and selected references preserve user intent.

## Rejected or inapplicable evidence

- No recipes, recipe weights, package sizes, nutrition serving weights, raw/cooked yield tables, or meat edible-portion percentages are used to infer identity, measure conversions, or substitutions.
- Unreviewed recipe-site translations and literal machine translations are not accepted as Québec French display-name evidence when CFIA/OQLF/DFO terms are available.
- Similar culinary use is not accepted as evidence for a synonym: e.g. chicken breast and turkey breast; tofu and tempeh; silken and pressed tofu; whole milk and 2% milk; fresh and low-moisture mozzarella; salmon and a particular salmon species.
- A brand name alone is not an ingredient identity or proof that all products in a category share a formulation. Brands may be kept as narrow search aliases only after product-label and collision review.
- CFIA labelling standards are used as a Canadian identity and specificity boundary; they do not by themselves prove what a particular recipe author intended.
- Historical catalog alias presence does not count as source support. The current seed is the audit input, not evidence of equivalence.
