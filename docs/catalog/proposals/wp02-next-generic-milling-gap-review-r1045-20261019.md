## Scope and candidate

The open WP02 Section A7 parent continues review of grain meals and milling products ([plan, line 2290](../../RecipeBook-global-ingredient-catalog-plan.txt#L2290)). R1045 reviews the distinct but unresolved **fine triticale bran** fraction reported in one laboratory milling study. The question is whether this is a generic culinary identity separate from the already active **triticale bran / son de triticale** row.

The evidence supports two different things: one milling study separated and analyzed material called coarse bran and fine bran, while independent studies used triticale bran generally in human foods. It does not show that the fine fraction has a transferable consumer milling boundary or is itself a generally recognized recipe ingredient. Keep the fine-bran candidate out of active catalog and ledger data.

This is a generic ingredient boundary review. No brand, product line, flavour, SKU, retailer variant, or commercial roster was researched.

## Identity boundary and evidence

Jiang et al. (2024) milled triticale grain with a Bühler laboratory mill and analyzed five fractions, including coarse bran and fine bran. This supports the existence of those two study-defined fractions under that milling setup. The same paper discusses the possible use of triticale milling fractions in food innovation, but its fraction analysis does not demonstrate a general consumer-grade “fine triticale bran” product or a recipe that uses the fine fraction as such. This paper is already recorded on the active triticale-bran ledger row as source **WP02-A7-TRITICALE-BRAN-R872-20261019-S01**. Preserve that source and its original locator/claim.

Other primary food studies support use of triticale bran generally: Agil and Hosseinian (2012) use triticale bran in a fermented dairy product study, and Kaszuba et al. (2021) use triticale bran in bread research. Their descriptions do not identify the bran as the fine fraction from Jiang et al.’s milling system. They support the existing triticale-bran identity, not transfer of its use to a fine-grade subidentity. Those sources remain on that existing row as **WP02-A7-TRITICALE-BRAN-R872-20261019-S02** and **WP02-A7-TRITICALE-BRAN-R872-20261019-S03**.

Keep the following boundaries:

- **Triticale bran** remains the already accepted generic fraction separated from triticale grain during milling.
- **Fine triticale bran** is only a fraction label from the 2024 research mill in this evidence set. Do not create a separate generic identity from this single setup.
- Do not equate fine triticale bran with **triticale bran flour**. The cited study names fine bran, not a standardized flour product.
- Keep triticale bran distinct from triticale grain, flour, flakes, germ, sprouted grain, bran-containing blends, feed fractions, and wheat or rye brans.
- Do not transfer research particle characteristics, fraction composition, or performance data to the generic triticale-bran row.

## Bilingual naming boundary

The active identity is **triticale bran / son de triticale**. OQLF supports the base cereal term **triticale** and defines **son** as a cereal milling fraction, but the reviewed records do not establish a preferred Québec compound for a *fine* triticale fraction. The existing provisional compound on the active generic bran row remains owned there; it is not evidence for a fine-grade name.

**Son fin de triticale** could serve only as a transparent descriptive translation in this proposal. It is not supported as an OQLF-preferred term or a current Québec human-food label. Do not add it as an active display or alias. The CFIA grain-label guidance supports explicitly naming the source grain in an English bran common name; it does not define a fine-bran grade or the French compound.

## Numeric and substitution limits

The current evidence supports no general particle-size cutoff, fraction recovery, household measure, cup weight, density, or unit mapping for fine triticale bran. Any fraction definitions, milling settings, compositions, or measurements in the Jiang et al. study stay with its sampled grain and laboratory mill; do not convert them into a retail specification or generic kitchen quantity.

Kaszuba et al. (2021) tested a 10% bran-enriched triticale bread formula; Agil and Hosseinian (2012) tested triticale bran in fermented dairy at a study-specific inclusion level up to 4%. Neither source identifies its bran as Jiang et al.’s fine fraction or establishes a general recipe maximum, replacement ratio, or cross-ingredient substitution. The 2024 fine-bran characterization is not a recipe test. No household measure, cup weight, density, or reusable conversion is supported. Add no measurement observation or substitution rule. Do not change the existing triticale-bran ledger research states through this proposal.

## Minimal proposal

**Hold; make no shared data change.** Retain the single generic **ingredient-ref-triticale-bran** reference and its existing source and evidence ownership. Do not add a fine-bran reference, alias, form, legacy candidate, ledger record, source ID, measurement observation, density, mapping, recipe link, conversion, package yield, or substitution.

Reopen only if independent evidence establishes fine triticale bran as a transferable food-facing ingredient grade, distinguishes it from the general bran fraction by a reproducible consumer-relevant milling boundary, and supports a non-conflicting Québec French name. Keep the A7 parent open.
