## Bounded gap and decision

The live Section P checklist leaves preserved fish states open at [the preserved-seafood heading](../../RecipeBook-global-ingredient-catalog-plan.txt#L4207) and keeps [fish powder, fish balls, surimi, and other named seafood work open](../../RecipeBook-global-ingredient-catalog-plan.txt#L4237). Its existing preservation review keeps universal dried-fish identities and forms inactive without a product or recipe request. Nearby completed decisions cover dried fish maw, dried squid, and dried octopus; this review does not reopen those decisions, generic fish powder, Bonga dried fish powder, generic fish balls, fried fish-ball products, surimi shreds, or crab-flavoured pollock flakes.

This packet reviews only **hwangtae**, the named Korean winter-dried pollack product. Official VisitKorea material distinguishes raw `myeongtae`, dried `bugeo`, frozen `dongtae`, winter-dried yellow `hwangtae`, dried young pollack `nogari`, half-dried `kodari`, and darkened `meoktae`. Korea.net describes hwangtae production as hanging pollack to dry through alternating freezes and thaws. These sources support hwangtae as a distinct process/product name, but do not give a taxonomic species in the wording cited here. [S01–S02]

Current Canadian retail listings reinforce that product presentation matters: H Mart Mall Toronto lists a 70 g item as `CRD) Dried Pollack` with Korean product text `초립동) 황태포 1마리 70G`; The K Mart lists a 454 g `dried pollack` product named `Choripdongi Hwangtaechae`. The pages were listed but showed sold out when accessed. The two listings do not resolve one shared presentation: the Korean name on the Toronto listing identifies `hwangtae-po`, while the other uses `hwangtaechae`. The K Mart page also says “Processing Russian pollock domestically” and, separately, “Main ingredients: 100% cod.” Treat this as conflicting retailer-page text, not a verified species declaration. [S03–S04]

Do not assign the product to Alaska pollock, Atlantic pollock, cod, or a generic pollock identity. OQLF’s `goberge` entry identifies *Pollachius virens* and lists English `pollock` and `pollack`; its separate `goberge de l’Alaska` entry identifies *Theragra chalcogramma*. The available hwangtae sources and the internally conflicting Canadian product listing do not establish which species belongs in this catalog row. The existing Québec term `goberge` therefore must not be attached as a French synonym. [S05–S06]

No OQLF or Canadian/Québec French source reviewed gives a hwangtae-specific French name. If a recipe later establishes this exact product, `hwangtae (poisson séché par cycles de gel et de dégel)` could be evaluated as an explanatory French display, but this is a provisional gloss, not a verified Québec synonym. Do not substitute `goberge séchée`, `goberge de l’Alaska`, or `morue séchée` without a species-identified package and matching recipe context.

Keep hwangtae distinct from generic dried pollack/pollock, `bugeo`, `dongtae`, `nogari`, `kodari`, `meoktae`, cod tongues, dried fish maw, dried squid, dried octopus, Bonga dried fish powder, salted cod, surimi, fish balls, and crab-flavoured pollock flakes. Do not treat the cooked dish `hwangtaegui` (grilled hwangtae) as an alias for the dry ingredient. [S01–S04]
