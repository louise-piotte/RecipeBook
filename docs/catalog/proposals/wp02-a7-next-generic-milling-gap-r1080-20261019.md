## Scope and candidate boundary

The cross-category scope filter applies to every ingredient type. This review considered only a generic, crop-specific milling gap; it did not research brands, product lines, flavours, SKUs, retailers, or commercial rosters. Commercial-product counts do not add ingredient diversity. Preserve existing branded rows and their product-scoped evidence for separate transferability review.

The candidate is distinct in concept from:

- **`ingredient-ref-wheat-aleurone-flour` — wheat aleurone flour.** R1048 is specifically wheat and has its own human-food evidence. Do not transfer its identity evidence, trial claims, servings, or source ownership to rye.
- **`ingredient-ref-rye-bran` — rye bran**, a broader outer-grain milling fraction.
- **`ingredient-ref-rye-bran-flour` — rye bran flour**, a flour made from rye bran rather than a separately isolated aleurone fraction.
- **`ingredient-ref-rye-germ` — rye germ**, **`ingredient-ref-rye-flour` — rye flour**, and **`ingredient-ref-rye-meal` — rye meal**. These remain separate existing identities.

The cited studies distinguish an aleurone fraction from other rye fractions and name rye aleurone flour, so this is a plausible generic coverage gap rather than a proposed branded expansion. However, experimental bread use does not by itself establish a general culinary ingredient identity suitable for activation. The 1999 paper describes breads made for a nutritional study of fermentation in monogastrics; the 2009 paper explicitly describes an experiment with pigs. Neither source establishes ordinary human cooking use, consumer availability, a transferable milling specification, or a general-purpose flour grade. Keep every study's material description, preparation, formula, and findings with that study.

## Québec French naming

`Farine d’aleurone de seigle` is only a transparent descriptive candidate phrase, not an endorsed display or alias. The Office québécois de la langue française (OQLF) GDT entries support the French base terms **aleurone** and **seigle** in their respective terminology scopes; neither attests this compound as a Québec culinary ingredient name. Do not add a French display or alias until the food identity itself is supported and the compound receives terminology review.
