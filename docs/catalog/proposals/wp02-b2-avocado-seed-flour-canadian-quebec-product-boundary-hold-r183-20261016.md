# WP02 B2 R183 — avocado-seed flour Canadian/Québec product-boundary hold

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-16.
- **Status:** Proposal only. This is the sole file changed; no shared catalog, ledger, plan, roadmap, batch record, generated file, or code was edited.
- **Plan fit:** A distinct seed-derived alternative flour under the open WP02 Section 04 B2 flour checklist. The candidate is flour made from seeds of *Persea americana*. It is not flour from avocado pulp, spray-dried avocado fruit powder, extracted avocado-seed starch, or avocado oil.
- **Proposal-local source-ID prefix:** `WP02-B2-AVOCADO-SEED-FLOUR-R183-20261016-S`. No ledger or archive owner was found. The R137 avocado/kluwih seed-starch paper is reused only for the flour-versus-isolated-starch distinction under its existing source ID.

## Recommendation

**Hold avocado-seed flour and all aliases outside the active references and research ledger pending a current Canadian culinary seed-flour product and a product-matched Québec French name.** Primary studies establish that researchers prepare seed flour and test food applications, including a 2025 bread study using a 5% replacement level in its specific formulation and a 2026 study comparing different seed pretreatments and drying methods. They do not establish a standardized consumer flour or a Canadian retail product. The current Canadian avocado-powder listings located do not bridge that gap: AB Distribution presents an unspecified avocado powder as a smoothie-mix product, and TheWholesalerCo calls its spray-dried product a fruit powder without identifying seed material. An eBay Canada storefront listing titled `Avocado Seed Powder` is posted by a UK seller under herbal remedies and gives dosage metadata; it does not establish a Canadian culinary flour product or Québec French name. [S01–S05, S07]

OQLF supports *avocat* as the Québec French name of the fruit *Persea americana* and describes it as having a large, hard seed. It does not attest a French food-product name for flour made from that seed. Keep candidate phrases such as `avocado seed flour`, `avocado seed powder`, `farine de graines d’avocat`, and `farine de noyau d’avocat` research-only. Do not shorten these to `avocado flour`, `avocado powder`, or `farine d’avocat`: those phrases do not distinguish seed flour from pulp-derived flour/powder or existing avocado products. [S06]

Reopen if a current Canadian food label or supplier specification confirms that a product is culinary flour from avocado seed, identifies its process sufficiently to distinguish it from powder, extract, or starch, and supports French wording for the same product in Québec. Until then, add no identity, alias, ledger record, form, measure, density, conversion, recipe link, yield, or substitution.

## Numeric, process, form, and substitution boundaries

- **Study-specific substitution:** The 2025 paper replaces 5% of wheat flour with one of two prepared avocado-seed flour fractions in its particular bread formula. Keep 5% as that study’s treatment level only; it is not a generic wheat-flour ratio for bread, cookies, noodles, or other foods. No recipe-line link or active contextual/global substitution is supported. [S01]
- **Process and composition:** The 2026 pretreatment/drying paper reports differences among sliced-seed samples treated with ascorbic acid, blanching, roasting, or no treatment, then dried by fluidized-bed, hot-air, solar, or freeze-drying methods. Its reported in-vitro digestibility, amylose, and resistant-starch values are tied to those research samples and methods. Do not convert them to commercial specifications, nutrient promises, cooking yields, or flour-grade forms. [S03]
- **Seed flour versus starch:** The 2022 study measures flour and starch isolated from avocado seeds as separate materials and states that further application work is needed. Extraction yields and starch/flour composition are not density, conversion, or household measure data. Keep the R137 starch evidence separate from this candidate. [S02; R137-S02]
- **Forms:** Add none. Do not create raw, dried, blanched, roasted, extruded, defatted, or powdered forms without a marketed product boundary and evidence that these are stable consumer forms. Do not fold whole avocado powder or a beverage mix into seed flour. [S01–S05]
- **Measures and conversions:** No current Canadian seed-flour label provides a seed-flour package measure or serving. AB Distribution’s 2.2 lb item is an unspecified powder beverage mix; TheWholesalerCo’s 225 g–25 kg size selector belongs to an unspecified, sold-out fruit powder listing. Neither supports mass-volume conversion, density, a generic serving, or package yield for seed flour. [S04–S05]
- **Substitutions and cooking yield:** Add none beyond recording the study-specific 5% bread treatment as bounded research context if needed in a future ledger decision. No reusable replacement ratio, cooking yield, soaking conversion, or recipe relationship is established. The current corpus has no matching seed-flour demand.

## Reopen criteria

1. Locate a current Canadian or Québec package, culinary retailer, or supplier specification identifying flour made from *Persea americana* seed, not unspecified avocado powder, pulp powder, supplement/remedy powder, or extracted starch.
2. Resolve whether the consumer product is whole-ground seed flour, defatted flour, an extruded fraction, or another defined preparation. Do not use the 2025/2026 study variants as commercial form names without matching product evidence.
3. Obtain French wording from the product itself or an authoritative Québec terminology source. Keep `farine d’avocat` and `poudre d’avocat` inactive if the plant part remains ambiguous.
4. Repeat active-reference, forms, legacy, ledger, source URL/ID, archive, picker, and bundled-recipe audits before any catalog promotion. Keep the unresolved avocado fruit legacy candidate and active avocado oil separate.
5. Research measures, conversions, yields, and substitutions against the verified product. Keep the bread paper’s 5% treatment and all digestion/functional values as study-specific findings only.

## Root integration decision

**Hold confirmed.** Current Canadian avocado-powder listings do not identify avocado seed as the source material, and no product-matched Québec French seed-flour name was established. The whole-fruit legacy candidate, active avocado oil, and extracted seed starch remain separate. No active reference or research-ledger record was added; the plan records the hold.
