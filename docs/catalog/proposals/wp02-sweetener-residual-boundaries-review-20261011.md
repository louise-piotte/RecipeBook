### Allulose status: what is and is not established

Health Canada's live **List of Permitted Sweeteners** is marked as published 2024-12-18 and superseding 2024-07-11. Searches of the current page for both `allulose` and `psicose` returned no match. CFIA's labeling guidance states that only sweeteners on Health Canada's list are authorized for use as food-additive sweeteners in Canada. This supports the narrow finding that allulose is **not named on the reviewed permitted-sweetener list**; it does not by itself settle its classification or every possible use as a food ingredient [S01–S02].

The current Health Canada pages for completed novel-food safety assessments and non-novel determinations also returned no match for `allulose` or `psicose`. These are negative page-search findings, not determinations. Health Canada's general pre-market guidance says novel foods require assessment before sale, but the materials located here do not identify food-grade allulose itself as novel or non-novel. The previous B2B supplier evidence and these list searches therefore do not close the legal-status question [S03–S05].

The French word itself is no longer an open naming blocker: `allulose` appears in a French ingredient statement from Québec retailer La Moisson, and the OQLF's French biochemistry entry for `cétose` lists allulose among examples. Neither source establishes an official Québec food-label name or Canadian food-use permission [S06–S07]. Keep `allulose / allulose` as the proposed bilingual name, but leave `candidate-allulose` unresolved for catalog activation until a current Health Canada determination or written classification addresses the exact ingredient specification/process and intended use. Do not add `D-psicose`, `D-allulose`, `rare sugar`, or `sucre rare` as aliases in this pass.

### Identity, form, measure, and use boundaries

- The proposed crude-stevia name is limited to the crude **aqueous** leaf-extract class described by Health Canada. A dry extract or a product with an unknown purification level should remain source-qualified until its exact product class is known. No physical form is added.
- Accepting a crude-stevia identity is not a retail-food safety or market-authorization claim: Health Canada describes crude leaf extracts as food ingredients available for personal culinary use, while stating that it could not give a definitive safety opinion for retail foods containing them [S08].
- Monk-fruit juice concentrate is the lower-mogroside food-ingredient class described by CFIA. Its acceptability as an ingredient is not a sweetener authorization or a conversion to monk-fruit extract. No concentration grade, density, or yield is inferred.
- Commercial product labels may change. The cited manufacturer pages establish only the named products' current ingredient distinctions; they do not establish generic blend categories, recipe demand, or transferable conversion behavior.
- This review activates no recipe links, forms, measurement observations, unit mappings, density values, conversions, yields, or substitutions. Product-specific claims documented by the prior review remain scoped there and are not promoted into runtime behavior here.

## Remaining evidence gaps

1. **Allulose:** obtain a current, product-specific Health Canada Food Directorate classification or published determination identifying whether the proposed food-grade material and intended food use fall under the permitted-sweetener/additive rules, a standardized sweetening-agent category, or novel-food review. Include the actual production process/specification and intended use [S01–S03]. Until then, retain the candidate as held even though the bilingual spelling is resolved.
2. **Crude stevia:** no remaining naming gap for a descriptive catalog display. If the wording is ever used as a regulated label common name, obtain CFIA confirmation because the source-backed phrase is descriptive and CFIA's published synonym table addresses purified glycosides, not a standardized crude-extract common name.
3. **Commercial blends:** no exact recipe line currently names a blend. For a future exact-product row, inspect the contemporary package or manufacturer ingredient statement for the same market and form named in that recipe. No generic blend row is justified.
