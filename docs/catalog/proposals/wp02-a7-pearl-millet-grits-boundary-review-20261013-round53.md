- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-13.
- **Status:** Proposal only. Keep the candidate unallocated. This file changes no shared catalog, form, legacy, ledger, recipe, plan, roadmap, batch, or generated file.
- **Proposal-local source IDs:** `WP02-A7-PEARL-MILLET-GRITS-20261013-R53-S01` through `S09`. They are unique to this review and are not ledger records.
- **Scope:** The Canadian-market listing for **S&V Pearl Millet Grits**, including its Québec-facing seller wording. This review does not reopen the active pearl-millet grain or flour identities, generic `millet meal`, millet products such as Sankhal or arraw, or the separate WP03 sorghum-grits identity.

## Recommendation

Keep **`pearl millet grits` unallocated**. Do not add an active reference, alias, French display, ingredient form, or ledger candidate from the evidence reviewed here.

A Canadian marketplace currently lists a two-pound S&V product as `Pearl Millet Grits`, and S&V's own Canadian business pages list millet grits as one of its product families [S01–S03]. This makes the qualified phrase a real product-identity candidate for the A7 review of other grain meals. However, the current S&V pages reviewed here did not provide a pearl-millet-grits product specification, package label, or defined milling process. The Canadian product listings do not say whether their grits are broken whole kernels, coarsely ground endosperm, a screened size fraction, or a different preparation. Technical sources show that millet grits are processed separately from flour and can have materially different particle sizes, but those general sources do not identify the listed product [S08–S09].

The French evidence is not stable enough for a Québec display. The Ottawa seller's French page titles the product `semoule de millet perlé`, calls it `gruau de millet perlé` elsewhere, and also contains references to corn grits in its millet-product instructions [S05]. OQLF defines `semoule` in the cited entry for soft- and durum-wheat milling; that entry does not validate `semoule de millet perlé` [S07]. OQLF's general technical `gruau` entry translates the term as `grits`, but it does not attest the compound `gruau de millet perlé` [S06]. Do not choose between the seller's inconsistent phrases or generate a French name by analogy.

## Product, language, and recipe-demand audit

S&V's official site identifies S & V MILLETS INC. in Mississauga and says the business delivers millet products in Canada. Its website groups `millet grits` with grain, flour, and flake products but the reviewed product pages did not specify a pearl-millet-grits SKU or show its ingredients and process [S01–S02]. Its Canadian retailer list includes Spice Divine in Ottawa, while the Canadian Instacart page separately carries the exact two-pound product title. These sources establish a Canadian sales-channel signal, not current local shelf inventory or a manufacturer-verified specification [S03–S04].

The manufacturer and marketplace evidence is insufficient to decide whether the product is a whole-kernel fraction or a particular grind. ICAR's 2026 millet-processing description treats grit making and flour milling as separate equipment functions and gives a general 1–4 mm range for millet grits. It does not state that this range applies to the S&V product or pearl millet specifically [S08]. Yadav et al. studied pearl-millet grits at two particle sizes, 0.420 mm and 1.410 mm, and found different porridge rheology. This supports treating size as a product-defining variable, not assuming that `grits` denotes one uniform texture [S09]. Neither source ties a specific size, grain fraction, or milling process to the Canadian retail listing.

The seller's French title and copy are not sufficient terminology evidence. In addition to using both `semoule` and `gruau` for the product, the instructions refer to corn semolina/grits in a millet listing. OQLF's technical `gruau` entry associates `gruau` with the English `grits`, but it is a general milling term rather than a species-qualified millet-product recommendation [S06]. OQLF's `semoule` definition names soft- and durum-wheat milling products and does not extend the term to pearl millet [S07]. Do not turn either seller phrase into an alias for the existing grain, flour, or Sankhal references.

I scanned every manifest-listed recipe file and ingredient row: **114 recipes and 1,123 ingredient rows**. A scan of complete serialized recipe files and all ingredient-row strings found **zero** matches for `pearl millet grits`, `pearl millet grit`, `millet grits`, `pearl millet meal`, `millet meal`, `semoule de millet perlé`, `gruau de millet perlé`, `bajra grits`, `bajri grits`, or any `pearl millet` / `millet` wording. There is no bundled recipe demand, matching recipe link, or substitution use case for this candidate.
