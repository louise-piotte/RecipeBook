# WP04 Section P — herring roe product-state boundary review (R416)

- **Review and source-access date:** 2026-10-17.
- **Candidate:** herring roe / œufs de hareng, including the regional market wording rave de hareng and the distinct salted-roe and roe-on-kelp products.
- **Recommendation:** Hold herring roe unallocated as an active reference, alias, form, legacy mapping, or ledger identity. Terminology sources establish the species-qualified phrase and distinguish roe sacs from spawn-on-kelp, but current product sources do not converge on one preparation state. The Québec government confirms herring eggs are marketed, but does not name a state; a current Canadian producer labels its herring-roe product specifically as salted roe (kazunoko); and a current Maritime fishmonger lists rave products without a product specification. The bundle has no matching recipe demand.
- **Scope:** This review covers the herring-specific female-roe boundary and its potential overlap with salted kazunoko. It does not revisit integrated salmon roe, bottarga, milt, trout roe, the R410 Nenu Dieune hold, or the R413 sujiko hold. Keep the earlier R42 kazunoko hold and the roe-on-kelp boundary unchanged.
- **Proposal-only status:** This file is the only proposed change. No shared catalog JSON, research ledger, plan, roadmap, generated index, archive, recipe, or application code was edited. No software tests, build, emulator, or deployment was run.
- **Proposal-local source IDs:** New IDs use **WP04-SECTIONP-HERRING-ROE-R416-20261017-**; audit label **WP04-SECTIONP-HERRING-ROE-R416-20261017-R01**. Reused URLs and source IDs are identified in the source register.

## Why this is the next distinct roe gap

The [Section P roe checklist](../../RecipeBook-global-ingredient-catalog-plan.txt#L7406) remains open after integrated salmon roe, bottarga, milt, and trout roe decisions. Focused reviews already held the Montréal Nenu Dieune listing as an unallocated product with uncertain species and package statements ([R410](wp04-section-p-next-seafood-gap-review-r410-20261017.md)), held sujiko because sources use the name for both raw and salted salmon roe sacs without a current Québec French label ([R413](wp04-section-p-next-seafood-gap-review-r413-20261017.md)), and held plain kazunoko / salted herring roe skeins pending recipe context ([R42](wp04-section-p-kazunoko-salted-herring-roe-product-boundary-review-20261013-round42.md)). This review addresses the remaining species-qualified herring-roe wording and asks whether it identifies a product boundary separate from the held salted-skein product.

The terminology itself is supported. TERMIUM Plus lists English herring roe with French rogue de hareng, rave de hareng, and œufs de hareng, and defines it as unlaid eggs or an egg sac removed from the female fish. Its adjacent record treats roe-on-kelp separately: fertilized eggs attached to kelp, harvested and salted. The current Government of Québec uses œufs de hareng for a marketed product and describes herring eggs as a sought-after export product. Those sources establish a useful phrase and a real commercial food product, but not one consistent state for the phrase.

Current product evidence leaves the state open. North Delta Seafoods identifies Pacific herring as Clupea pallasii and lists its Herring Roe product form as “Salted Roe (Kazunoko).” This is a current, producer-specific product description that overlaps the still-held kazunoko term. The Poissonnerie du Creek page in Pokemouche, New Brunswick lists rave de hareng, rave de hareng broké, and pot listings including “Pot rave hareng 250ml”; it provides no ingredients, preservation method, species, or net roe quantity. The Québec government page uses œufs de hareng without specifying preparation. Keep these sources within their stated scope; do not generalize one supplier’s salted product into the definition of all herring roe.

OQLF’s GDT prefers generic rogue for the commercializable mature female gonad and lists herring among the species. It says rave is regional and was not retained in Québec fish-product marketing, where a term for each specific species is normalized to avoid consumer-label confusion. TERMIUM attests the compound forms but does not make them a Québec product label standard. The Government of Québec’s current œufs de hareng wording supports a plain-language Québec French expression, but it does not settle whether a reference would represent fresh roe, salted skeins, or another finished product.

The uncertainty matters in this catalog. A hypothetical identity-only herring roe row with no aliases would capture both a general anatomical phrase and supplier product names that may refer specifically to salted kazunoko. It would also expand broad picker queries without any bundled recipe selecting the intended product. Keep the term under review until a recipe or a current Québec package label identifies the required state.

## Measurements and substitutions

No matching herring-roe recipe or amount appears in the bundled corpus. The Québec government page confirms commercial importance but gives no measure or preparation. The Creek product list names “Pot rave hareng 250ml” and “Pot de raves d’hareng broké,” without saying whether 250 ml is container capacity, net product volume, or a standard serving. It gives no mass or ingredient declaration. North Delta’s page labels Herring Roe as salted kazunoko; its displayed 50–150 g size range is not identified as roe weight and is not used as one.

An external MAFF recipe for Mame Kazunoko uses 100–150 g salted herring roe with 300 g soybeans. That amount belongs to the specific Fukushima dish and does not establish a standard portion, recipe-book amount, density, conversion, or yield for an unresolved herring-roe identity. No density, unit mapping, package yield, recipe link, conversion, or other reusable measurement is supported. [S05]

The bundled global and contextual substitution-rule files contain no herring-roe rule. Official MAFF recipes provide only scoped culinary examples: one carp-sashimi dish names herring roe, as needed, as an alternative when carp roe is unavailable; a regional nousaba page says dried hoshizame shark was used in place of kazunoko for New Year’s osechi when herring roe was scarce. These examples do not define a general herring-roe substitute or a quantity ratio. Do not create an active global or contextual substitution. Preserve the exact dish and product context if a future bundled recipe selects one. [S04, S06]

## Recommendation and reopen conditions

**Keep herring roe unallocated and the Section P roe checklist open.** The sources support the terms herring roe and œufs de hareng and establish a distinction between unlaid roe sacs and spawn-on-kelp. They do not resolve whether a new broad herring-roe identity would overlap the producer-defined salted kazunoko product, nor does a current Québec package clarify the intended product state. The bundle contains no matching demand, and a hypothetical row would add one result to both broad roe and herring/hareng picker queries.

Reopen when a bundled recipe names herring roe with enough context to distinguish unprepared or fresh roe from salted kazunoko or roe-on-kelp, or when a current Québec product label supplies that distinction. At that point, assess whether the selected term is a separate identity or a product state attached to a more general roe reference. Keep R410’s Nenu Dieune hold, R413’s sujiko hold, R42’s kazunoko hold, and all integrated neighboring identities unchanged.
