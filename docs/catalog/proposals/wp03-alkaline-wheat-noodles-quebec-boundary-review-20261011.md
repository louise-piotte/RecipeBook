# WP03 Section G alkaline wheat noodle boundary review

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-11
- **Recommendation:** retain the open hold for the broad `alkaline wheat noodles` candidate. Canadian technical sources support a narrow yellow-alkaline noodle type and a Québec French scientific term, but the current evidence does not establish a stable consumer identity boundary from ramen or egg noodles.
- **Scope:** audit the live catalog, ledger, legacy candidates, recipe corpus, prior Section G work, Canadian product naming, manufacturer wording, and Québec French terminology. Proposal only; no shared seed, ledger, plan, roadmap, README, code, or tests were changed.

## Evidence and the naming boundary

The Canadian Grain Commission (CGC) uses **yellow alkaline noodles** and **nouilles jaunes alcalines** for a technical wheat-noodle type. Its discussion describes a fresh dough that is sheeted and cut and gives a representative recipe of flour, water, alkaline salts, and sodium chloride. The CGC’s experimental recipe uses kansui; it distinguishes these research noodles from white salted noodles. This supports a narrow technical type, not a universal retail formulation or a general equivalence between every product sold as “alkaline noodles” and yellow alkaline noodles. [S01–S02]

A Canadian retailer currently lists **Frozen Authentic HK Alkaline Noodles (Thin)**, a Hong Kong restaurant pack. The listing says it is frozen and made with Canadian/US flour, but it is sold out at review time, identifies no manufacturer by name, and exposes no ingredient panel. The page’s English title is a retailer listing, not a verified bilingual package label. It supports Canadian retail use of the English phrase for one Hong Kong product only. [S05]

Québec French evidence is narrower than the retail evidence. The CGC French report uses `nouilles jaunes alcalines` and, in another heading, the word order `nouilles alcalines jaunes`. OQLF has no dedicated alkaline-noodle entry surfaced in the targeted search. Its 2014 `nouilles ramen` entry instead defines ramen as quick-cooking fine egg noodles made from wheat flour and gives `nouilles ramen` and `ramen` as preferred French terms. OQLF’s broader `nouilles de blé` entry groups Asian noodles by ingredients and describes Japanese somen/udon by calibre; it does not establish alkalinity as a separate consumer-facing class. [S01–S04]

The central collision is direct: the manufacturer Sun Noodle describes basic ramen noodles as wheat flour, water, salt, and kansui; the Canadian Plant Based Workshop also says kansui is what makes its ramen noodles distinctive. Thus an “alkaline” property can occur inside the existing ramen product identity. These sources do not show that all ramen products are alkaline, or that all alkaline wheat noodles are ramen. They do show that alkalinity alone cannot define a distinct, non-ramen row. [S06–S07]

If a future evidence packet resolves the boundaries, the most defensible provisional label is:

| Field | Provisional wording only |

| English | `yellow alkaline noodles` |
| fr-CA | `nouilles jaunes alcalines` |
| Aliases | None supported for activation |
| Identity scope | Finished wheat-flour noodles explicitly sold or defined as yellow alkaline noodles; a recipe-only mention of kansui or “alkaline” does not qualify a product on its own. |

This is not a recommendation to add that row now. The CGC word order varies, the local retailer uses the broader `HK Alkaline Noodles (Thin)` without “yellow,” and no Québec retail label or manufacturer declaration currently confirms the intended French display and product scope. Do not activate bare `alkaline noodles`, `kansui`, `lye-water noodles`, `Chinese noodles`, `wheat noodles`, `egg noodles`, or `ramen` as aliases. `Kansui` names the alkaline ingredient/process input; it is not evidence that a noodle should be relabeled or reclassified.

## Collision and state limits

- **Ramen:** keep the active ramen identity. A product named ramen remains ramen even when its maker lists kansui. Do not merge ramen into an alkaline-noodle row or treat the two names as aliases.
- **Egg noodles:** OQLF’s ramen entry includes eggs, while the Canadian manufacturer source describes a basic ramen formula without listing egg. This review does not settle the broader egg-noodle definition or the ingredients in the Hong Kong retail item. Do not use “alkaline” as a proxy for egg content.
- **Generic pasta:** keep `pasta / pâtes alimentaires` separate. Flour source, dough processing, optional egg, and market naming can differ; the sources here do not justify a pasta alias or a generic noodle-to-pasta equivalence.
- **Other Asian noodles:** keep rice noodles, mung-bean threads, sweet-potato-starch noodles, udon, somen, and soba separate. A shared wheat base or a broad “Asian noodles” description does not establish identity.
- **States:** official technical sources describe fresh yellow alkaline noodles; the Canadian HK listing is frozen. These are product-specific observations, not a complete fresh/frozen/dried state model. Add no forms or state conversion.
- **Numbers and substitution:** no kitchen quantities, cooking conversion, yield, density, or substitution rule is supported by this identity review.

## Evidence still needed to close the hold

1. A current manufacturer specification or ingredient panel for the intended product family that clarifies whether the target is the narrow yellow-alkaline type or broader Hong Kong/Chinese alkaline noodles, and whether egg is optional or part of the defined product.
2. A Québec-facing product label or independent retailer evidence using a stable French name for that same product; the CGC report is authoritative technical terminology, not consumer-label evidence.
3. A source-backed rule for products that are both marketed as ramen and made with kansui. The catalog needs to know whether the candidate tracks a market-named noodle family or an overlapping preparation/composition attribute before adding aliases or linking recipe lines.

Until these points are resolved, keep the plan item open and make no active reference, aliases, forms, measurements, recipe links, or substitutions.

## Disposition

Retain the hold on `alkaline wheat noodles`. The narrow technical term `yellow alkaline noodles / nouilles jaunes alcalines` is plausible for a future review, but current product-label and boundary evidence does not establish a separate Québec-facing catalog identity alongside the active ramen row. Keep this Section G item open pending the evidence listed above.
