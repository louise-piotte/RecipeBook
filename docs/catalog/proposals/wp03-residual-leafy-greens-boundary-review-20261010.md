## Source-specific measure and substitution observations

These observations are retained with their source scope and are not proposed as general catalog values:

- The University of Hawaiʻi CTAHR food-composition table identifies `Taro, leaves, raw` as *Colocasia esculenta* and records 1 cup of 1-inch pieces as 28 g; it also records three leaves sized 16 × 11.5 inches as 50 g and 35% refuse (stems, midribs, imperfect parts). This is a sample-specific historical Hawaiian measure with explicit cut, size, and refuse conditions. **Do not create a general cup-to-gram mapping, density, or leaf-count measure from it.**
- The Philippine DOST-PCAARRD iVeg recipes list 1 cup of *Moringa oleifera* leaves in two local mixed-vegetable stews. The Davao del Sur law-uy page lists 1 cup of Malabar spinach leaves with alternatives of 1 cup Tinangkong sweet-potato tops or 1 cup moringa leaves, then directs adding the greens at the end and using residual heat. This directly supports a source-specific 1-cup recipe observation and a local alternative choice. **Hold a runtime substitution rule:** the evidence is a named regional dish and one preparation context, with no matching bundled recipe demand. If a later law-uy recipe needs it, review an explicitly contextual rule limited to the leafy-green role in that stew and its finishing method; do not generalize to spinach or other dishes.
- Harvest Fresh's French-language recipe uses one package of sweet-potato leaves for its chili bake, without package mass. **Record only that recipe-local package quantity; do not infer a package conversion, standard yield, or reusable kitchen measure.**
- Washington State University Extension describes marketed sweetpotato greens bunches with 15–20 stems and says sweetpotato greens may be marketed as a spinach replacement. It gives no bunch mass or recipe ratio. **Do not create a stem count conversion or a spinach substitution rule.** The replacement wording is market context, not a recipe-scoped equivalence.
- No directly supported default density, generic leaf-volume conversion, global substitution, or recipe-line substitution link was found for amaranth greens or fenugreek leaves.

## Naming and taxonomy limits

**Amaranth.** OMAFRA's French crop profile lists *Amaranthus* species and describes culinary leaf use across several regions. UF/IFAS Extension likewise describes cultivated species collectively as amaranths and notes that common names vary by type. Use a qualified generic leaf identity rather than assigning one species. `Callaloo` appears in sources but is unsafe as a global alias because it is regionally variable and also names a dish. The existing OQLF amarante evidence supports French base spelling, not merging the leaf with active amaranth grain or flour. Reuse existing ledger source `WP03-S23` (OQLF, `amarante`); do not reuse the unrelated/duplicated `WP03-S14` identifier.

**Sweet potato.** UF/IFAS and WSU Extension identify edible leaves and young shoots of *Ipomoea batatas*. The French display uses the leaf-qualified compound built from Québec's accepted root term `patate douce`, and a Canadian product/recipe source uses `feuilles de patate douce`. The root and leafy products are separate plant parts and separate catalog identities. The market phrase “spinach replacement” does not establish a culinary substitution ratio or preparation match. Keep water spinach separately scoped to *I. aquatica*.

**Taro.** OQLF's existing `taro` entry identifies *Colocasia esculenta* and accepted French base terms, but the entry does not say that the edible product is leaves. CTAHR separately lists raw *C. esculenta* taro leaves and calls them luau leaves. Its Hawaiʻi Nutrition Center explains that Pacific “taro” can also refer to other genera and that taro leaves need careful preparation and cooking. Therefore accept only the *C. esculenta* leaf commodity under a qualified name. The statement about preparation is a source limitation; no raw-form recommendation, safety instruction, or cooking rule is added here. Reuse OQLF ledger source `WP03-J-ROOTS-AROMATICS-20261009-S08` for the base French term and taxon.

**Fenugreek.** OQLF and the India Spices Board already support the plant name and distinguish seed spice from leaf/shoot food use; Kew's accepted *Trigonella foenum-graecum* record independently supports the botanical name and vegetable use. A Canadian retailer labels the produce item `Feuilles de fenugrec`; product sources identify `methi leaves` and dried `kasuri methi`. Accept leaf-qualified search terms only. Reuse existing ledger sources `WP06-SA-20261010-S11` (OQLF) and `WP06-SA-20261010-S12` (India Spices Board); do not create duplicate IDs or reinterpret the active seed row.

**Moringa.** OQLF supplies Québec French `moringa` and *Moringa oleifera*, but notes that leaves, flowers, bark, pods, roots, and seed oil are all consumed. The ingredient row must name the leaf. Hawaiʻi Nutrition Center and Philippine DOST-PCAARRD sources explicitly pair leaf-qualified malunggay/balunggay names with moringa leaves or *M. oleifera*. A separate UC Davis postharvest source discusses edible fresh leaves and dried leaf powder; this supports the part boundary, not equivalence between leaf and powder. Keep the French display part-qualified.
