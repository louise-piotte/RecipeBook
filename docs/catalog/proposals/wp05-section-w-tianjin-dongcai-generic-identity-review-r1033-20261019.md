## Recommendation

Propose identity-only **Tianjin dongcai**, the regionally named preserved Chinese-cabbage condiment associated with Tianjin. Keep the place qualifier in both display names: `Tianjin dongcai` in English and the provisional fr-CA construction `dongcai de Tianjin (chou chinois fermenté)` in French. No standardized Québec French name for this preserve was found. The parenthetical describes the source-supported vegetable and preparation; it is not a standardized preserve name or a search alias.

Add only the English aliases `Tianjin preserved vegetables` and `Tianjin pickled vegetable`, which are source-attested English descriptions. Do not add unqualified `dongcai`, `winter vegetable`, or broad `preserved cabbage` aliases: the shared term *dongcai* is used for regional preserves with different vegetable substrates, and the generic descriptions would blur the named regional identity.

The reviewed sources support a distinct prepared-food identity and seasoning use. They do not establish one universal recipe or production specification. Propose no forms, numeric behavior, recipe links, reusable measures, conversions, yields, or substitutions.

## Identity boundary and bilingual naming

The Tianjin Library describes Tianjin winter vegetable as Chinese cabbage prepared by pickling and fermentation and gives examples of its local seasoning use in wonton broth, noodles, braises, and stir-fries. This supports the generic Tianjin preserve identity, its cabbage substrate, and the English alias family; the page’s history, nutrition, health, sensory, and process assertions are not catalog defaults. [S01]

Jiang et al. distinguish traditional Dongcai by region and identify Tianjin Dongcai as Chinese-cabbage based, while Sichuan Dongcai is mustard based. Their article groups Tianjin and Guangzhou Dongcai under Chinese cabbage but keeps them regionally distinct. The city qualifier is therefore material: do not use the bare name `dongcai` to merge the different regional foods. The article reports one study’s microbial and composition findings and has a 2024 correction; none of those findings is transferred to generic ingredient behavior. [S02]

China Daily uses the English wording “Tianjin pickled vegetable” and “dong cai,” supporting the narrow English alias `Tianjin pickled vegetable`. Its description of Chinese cabbage, red-skinned leeks, and sea salt is one account of a formulation, not a universal ingredient list. [S03] The Woks of Life ingredient glossary labels the food “Tianjin Preserved Vegetables (冬菜, Dōngcài)” and describes its use in soups, stir-fries, steamed foods, and stews; this supports the English alias `Tianjin preserved vegetables`, not a reusable formula or measure. [S04]

For fr-CA, use the borrowed regional name `dongcai de Tianjin` so the identity remains recognizable. OQLF lists `chou de Chine`, `chou chinois`, and `chou vert de Chine` as terms for the raw Chinese-cabbage group; it does not name the preserve. The proposed descriptor `chou chinois fermenté` is a transparent, provisional description, not an OQLF-sanctioned product name. Keep the descriptor in the display only and add no French aliases. [reused WP03K-S11]

The identity is a regional preserved-food preparation. It is not raw Chinese cabbage, generic pickled mustard greens, generic pickled cabbage, Dongbei suancai, Bashang suancai, zha cai, Yibin ya cai, or an umbrella for every food called *dongcai*.

## Numeric, form, and substitution evidence limits

No reviewed source gives a transferable household measure, mass per unit, density, unit mapping, conversion, finished-food yield, or catalog-ready form. Use in soup, noodles, stew, or stir-fry is qualitative culinary-use evidence, not a serving measure. No bundled recipe supplies direct Tianjin dongcai demand.

Do not encode the Tianjin Library page’s process sequence or “months” of fermentation as a universal method or time. The library account mentions garlic, while China Daily describes a source-specific formulation with red-skinned leeks and salt. Jiang et al. discuss a particular distinction involving an approximately 20% garlic addition; that percentage is study/cited-subtype context only, not a transferable proportion or defining formula. The conflicting accounts reinforce an identity-only boundary. No source supports a standard salt percentage, fermentation temperature, cut size, draining state, or safety process for generic catalog use.

No source recommends replacing finished Tianjin dongcai with another ingredient for a defined dish role and amount. Do not infer a substitution from cabbage or preserve-family wording. In particular, do not activate Napa cabbage, mustard greens, Dongbei suancai, Bashang suancai, pickled mustard greens, zha cai, Yibin ya cai, pickled radish, or fermented bamboo shoots as replacements.

If later integrated, record `identityState: verified`, `measurementState: researched_no_supported_value`, and `substitutionResearchState: blocked_pending_evidence`. Add no active global or contextual substitution rules.
