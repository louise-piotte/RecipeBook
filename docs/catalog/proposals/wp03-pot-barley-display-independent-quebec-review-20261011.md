# Independent Québec review: pot-barley French display

## Conclusion

**Provisional.** `Orge en pot` is attested as a narrow Canadian French product heading for pot barley and is a sensible way to distinguish this row from the active `orge mondé` hulled-barley row. The wording is not established as a preferred Québec term: OQLF and TERMIUM do not endorse it, and current Québec retail pages I checked use `orge mondé` for English-labeled pot barley. Keep `orge en pot` as a provisional product display with no French alias; do not add `orge mondé(e)` or `orge entière` to the pot-barley row.

This evidence supports an internal Québec French review packet, but does not prove that a Québec French linguist or bilingual product reviewer has signed off. If the checklist means a human Québec-language approval, leave that sign-off open until such a reviewer accepts or revises the display. This review does not edit the active row or shared checklist.

## Current catalog context

The 2026-10-11 bundled reference currently displays `pot barley / orge en pot`, with no French aliases, and the prior batch has already separated its processing identity from hulled and pearl barley. This packet reviews only the French wording. The central issue is that Québec’s authoritative term `orge mondé` is also used on several Canadian product pages for products whose English name is `pot barley`.

The process distinction is useful context for interpreting the names. Federal food-purchasing descriptions distinguish pot/Scotch barley from hulled barley: pot barley retains more bran than pearl barley, while hulled barley has only the inedible exterior hull removed. A Québec federal French version maps these descriptions to `orge mondé` and `orge entière`, respectively, but OQLF defines `orge mondé` as hulled barley. Product pages also label English pot barley `orge mondé`. These sources confirm a terminology collision; they do not make the two English product classes interchangeable.

## Source access and review limits

The OQLF page, Loblaws search results, OnlineOrganics page, and Farinart specification record opened directly. TERMIUM was visible in the search result with its modification date; its direct open returned a cache-miss error. Metro's current English and French indexed results exposed the same product number and differing names, but direct opens returned 403. Healthy Planet, FoodServiceDirect, and Arva current search results exposed their product titles; direct opens for those pages returned cache-miss errors during this review.

No source reviewed here is a Québec bilingual reviewer’s approval of `orge en pot`. No authoritative OQLF or TERMIUM entry for that exact phrase was found. These limits justify retaining the phrase as provisional rather than presenting it as a settled preferred Québec term.
