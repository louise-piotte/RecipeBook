## Recommendation

Resolve the existing ledger-only candidate as an identity-only raw produce reference for East African highland cooking bananas used as food under the name matooke. The Uganda Gazette No. 79 (29 November 2024) publishes US 997:2023, “Cooking banana (Matooke),” whose scope describes raw *Musa* spp. (AAA-EAH) product supplied to the consumer. This product boundary is sufficiently distinct from the steamed or mashed prepared food also called matooke. Keep that prepared dish outside the ingredient identity. UNBS’s Information Resource Center now lists a newer April 2026 schedule and June 2026 catalogue, but the attachments could not be inspected; this proposal does not treat the accessible June 2025 schedule as the latest edition.

## Regional product evidence and naming boundary

The Uganda Gazette, Vol. CXVII No. 79, dated 29 November 2024, publishes US 997:2023, “Cooking banana (Matooke) — Specification (2nd Edition),” and gives its scope as raw consumer product from *Musa* spp. (AAA-EAH). It separately lists US 998:2023, “Plantain (Gonja),” and gives its scope as *Musa* spp. (AAA-B). The June 2025 UNBS schedule repeats those entries and raw-product scope in summary form. Together these accessible editions establish an official Ugandan boundary between Matooke and Gonja plantain; they do not make every cooking banana or plantain a Matooke banana. The 2025 schedule is edition-specific and a summary, not the complete standard text. UNBS’s Information Resource Center search-indexed page now lists a Schedule of Compulsory Uganda Standards as of 30 April 2026 and a Uganda Standards Catalogue dated 30 June 2026. The page returned 403 during this review, and the newer attachments could not be inspected, so no claim is made here about whether or how those later documents treat US 997 or US 998. [S01, S06, S07]

Akankwasa et al.’s regional study reports that “Matooke” can refer both to East African highland cooking bananas and to the steamed-mashed food prepared from them. Its survey of 123 farmers, 14 focus groups, and 40 traders in western Uganda examines preferences for the bananas and their cooked-food quality. Read together with the raw-consumer-product definition in US 997:2023, this supports a catalog identity for the raw banana group and an explicit exclusion of the finished dish. The paper is regional, not a pan-African naming standard. [S15]

A French-language InfoMusa field report describes Matooke as cooking bananas and discusses them as a staple crop in Uganda; it distinguishes them from beer bananas, dessert bananas, and roasting types. This is useful regional French evidence, but it is historical and does not establish a current Québec term. [S04]

Alimentation Transbec currently lists “*Carton* Banane Ouganda Matooke” in its French produce collection and identifies a physical store in Québec City. The retailer evidence supports local availability and the exact product wording only; it does not authenticate cultivar or show a package weight. The display keeps the core product name “banane matooke.” It omits “Ouganda” as an alias because that is a retailer title descriptor, not a demonstrated general search name. [S02, S03]

The OQLF GDT entry for banane supports the generic Québec French fruit term and describes cooking bananas and sweet bananas as broad groups. It does not provide a Matooke-specific entry. TERMIUM Plus’s “BANANE VERTE” record lists plantain as the preferred English term and cooking banana as a variant; its French terms include banane plantain, banane verte, and banane à cuire. These general records are not Matooke-specific. To preserve the product boundary, do not add banane plantain, banane verte, banane à cuire, generic banana/banane, or plantain as aliases. Reuse the already-owned OQLF source ID rather than registering a duplicate URL. [S27, S05]
