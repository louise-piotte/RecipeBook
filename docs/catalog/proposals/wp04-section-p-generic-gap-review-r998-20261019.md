## Identity and boundary

The cut has a distinct, source-supported location behind the gills along the fish clavicle, with meat, bones, and pectoral-fin tissue. Government of Canada terminology gives the French noun **collier** and examples that include halibut and sablefish collars. A Canadian French culinary source uses the generic ingredient wording **colliers de poisson** and lists salmon, lingcod, and sablefish as examples in the same recipe. The combined evidence supports a generic culinary part identity and the bilingual display proposed here; it does not establish a uniform cut geometry or availability across every species. [R998-S01–S03]

Keep the generic collar separate from the whole fish head, fish cheeks, loose fish bones, post-filleting frames, variable trimmings, skin, prepared fish stock, fillets, steaks, and every species identity. These adjacent parts may touch or be removed during the same butchery step, but their names are not synonyms. Do not add any of them as aliases or infer a fixed universal set of included tissues beyond the boundaries supported by the cited sources.

Preserve the R526 hold on salmon kama / salmon collar unchanged. This proposal does not add salmon collar, salmon kama, salmon neck, or cou de saumon as aliases, a species-specific form, or a salmon identity. The generic cross-species reference records the part only; it does not select a salmon species or promote a restaurant preparation to a raw ingredient specification. Existing species-specific holds and evidence remain intact.
