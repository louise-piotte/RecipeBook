- **Review date:** 2026-10-18.
- **Status:** Proposal-only; no shared catalog, ledger, checklist, roadmap, workflow, integration, recipe, or generated file was changed.
- **Checklist scope:** The open Section P finfish parent for salmon, cod, pollock, halibut, sole, flounder, turbot, sea bass, sea bream, snapper, grouper, tilapia, catfish, carp, mackerel, sardine, anchovy, tuna, bonito, eel, and regional fish ([plan](../../RecipeBook-global-ingredient-catalog-plan.txt#L6804)).
- **Candidate:** Longfin hake, currently accepted as *Phycis chesteri*; historical synonym *Urophycis chesteri*.
- **Recommendation:** **Hold** the candidate outside active references, forms, and the research ledger. Federal and scientific sources establish the taxon and the names `longfin hake / merluche à longues nageoires`, but the reviewed food-market evidence does not establish a current Canadian/Québec consumer identity. Fisheries and Oceans Canada describes it as non-commercial, and OQLF’s normalized Québec fish-product entry for `merluche` identifies only two species in trade, neither this candidate under its current taxon. No bundled recipe asks for it.
- **Data delta:** None. Do not allocate an active reference, alias, legacy candidate, source record, form, measure, recipe link, conversion, yield, or substitution.

## Naming, food scope, and recommendation

The name pair is supported in DFO species-identification material, and ITIS resolves the current taxon despite the older *Urophycis chesteri* synonym. Those facts justify a biological-species record; they do not by themselves justify a consumer ingredient reference. The strongest Québec food-language source is OQLF’s `merluche` entry, which limits its normalized commercial term to two *Urophycis* species in trade. That does not establish a product label for *Phycis chesteri*, particularly given the historical generic-placement synonym. DFO’s field guide independently describes the species as non-commercial.

No current Québec or Canadian consumer package naming this species was found in the targeted searches. That is a bounded search result, not proof that the fish is never sold or eaten. The U.S. FDA entry is not transferred to Canadian labeling. The Québec angling page is not treated as evidence of food use. On the reviewed record, a consumer picker row would overstate the market status of a regional survey species. Keep it unallocated pending a current Canadian/Québec product label, an authoritative Québec commercial-name treatment that includes this taxon, or direct bundled-recipe demand.

If future evidence clears that gate, `PROTEIN` would be the plausible category and `longfin hake / merluche à longues nageoires` the candidate bilingual wording to reassess. That is not an active data proposal here. Do not add `hake`, `merluche`, `merlu`, `whiting`, `Urophycis chesteri`, or *Phycis chesteri* as aliases; the scientific names are evidence keys, and the broad common terms would need a new scope review. No form or numeric behavior follows from the biological size descriptions or survey data.
