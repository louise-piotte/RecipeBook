## Why this is a distinct identity gap

Koshihikari is named as a rice variety by Japan’s Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries (MAFF). The University of California Agriculture and Natural Resources (UCANR) rice-variety material lists Koshihikari under **Short Grains**. Current Canadian and Québec-facing product sources also market rice under Koshihikari wording, including a bilingual Canadian distributor listing and Montréal retail listings. Together, these support a named, variety-qualified food identity alongside the broad short-grain class and the existing rice varieties.

The catalog already contains generic rice, short-grain rice, medium-grain rice, Calrose rice, and a label-defined sushi-rice identity. Those rows do not own the exact Koshihikari name. The proposed row makes the variety searchable without claiming that all short-grain rice is Koshihikari, that Koshihikari is synonymous with sushi rice, or that every Koshihikari product has one origin, milling state, cultivar purity, or cooking behavior.

## Product and Québec naming evidence

The Canadian distributor Ozawa Canada lists **Tamaki Gold Koshihikari Rice** and directly supplies the French title **Riz Koshihikari Gold de Tamaki**. Its product description calls the item Japanese short-grain rice and says it is grown from Koshihikari in California’s Sacramento Valley. This is current Canadian distributor evidence for both the variety-qualified product and French product wording. The cultivation-origin statement is specific to that product and is not an origin property of Koshihikari generally.

Montréal retailer Miyamoto lists **Riz Tamaki Gold Koshihikari 15 lbs** and **NNS Koshihikari #1 2kg**. Those listings corroborate Québec-facing use of the name with more than one seller/label. The two product pages showed “Solde Épuisé” at review time, so they establish current listings and local terminology, not in-stock status.

The proposed French display is the product-neutral phrase **riz Koshihikari**, shortened from directly attested Québec/Canadian product titles. No OQLF entry for this cultivar-qualified compound was located in the reviewed sources. Record this as local market naming evidence, not an OQLF-preferred term; keep independent Québec French review open.
