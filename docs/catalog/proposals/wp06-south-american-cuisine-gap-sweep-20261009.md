- **Scope:** Andean, Brazilian, Southern Cone, Amazonian, and other source-backed South American cuisines
- **Ownership boundary:** This packet proposes identities, aliases, distinctions, and follow-up research. It does not edit the canonical seed, research ledger, recipes, generated Kotlin, tests, roadmap, or plan.

## Scope and evidence standard

This is a bounded, source-led pass, not a claim that any country or cuisine is covered. The source set was chosen to expose source-native ingredient names across everyday food, regional specialties, breads and pastries, desserts, condiments, preservation, festive food, and culinary beverages where the available sources make those uses visible. The sweep did not use an item quota or treat a prominent dish as representative of a whole region.

Candidate dispositions distinguish existing identity, proposed alias, distinct identity, compound or product family, unresolved, and out-of-scope as an ingredient reference. Dish names are retained as evidence even when they are not ingredient rows. Existing references and forms remain authoritative for this comparison; legacy import candidates are not active catalog identities.

Names are kept in the source language wherever a credible English or Québec French term was not found. A borrowed source name in the fr-CA column is marked provisional; this is not a claim that it is established Québec usage. Measurement and substitution evidence are separate: this packet activates no density, yield, conversion, or substitution.

## Recipe scan: method and result

All 114 paths in manifest.recipeFiles were parsed as UTF-8 JSON. The scan covered every entry in ingredients[] (1,123 lines) and examined originalText, ingredientName, ingredientRefId, ingredientFormId, and bilingual preparation fields. A packet-specific term list was built from the source-native candidates below, supported English and French terms, and current neighboring product labels. Text keys used Unicode NFKD normalization, case-folding, combining-mark removal, punctuation-to-space conversion, and whitespace collapse. Phrase matches were checked at word boundaries, then manually reviewed to distinguish an exact regional name from a shared word inside another product name.

One line matched a South America-relevant identity: Quinoa Bites, line 1, “2 cups cooked quinoa,” linked to ingredient-ref-quinoa and ingredient-form-quinoa-cooked. It is ordinary quinoa use in a non-regional recipe, so it is an identity/form benchmark hit, not evidence that a South American recipe is represented. No line matched the other source-native regional candidates. Generic potato and corn text also occurred in ordinary recipes; those lines already point to the potato, sweet potato, corn kernel, corn-on-the-cob, or corn-syrup identities and were not counted as regional candidate hits. No recipe relink is proposed.

## Normalized-key collision review

The collision pass used the same normalization as the recipe scan, with exact normalized-key equality and no fuzzy, stemming, translation, or semantic matching. It indexed active reference English/French display names and aliases; form labels and match terms; and legacy candidate names and aliases. Candidate source spellings were compared against those indexes before any proposed identity or alias was assigned.

Exact matches to live identities or aliases include:

- quinoa → active ingredient-ref-quinoa; existing dry and cooked forms are separate.
- potato and pomme de terre → active ingredient-ref-potato. Potato flour and potato starch are separately active identities; russet potato is a scoped alias of potato.
- black beans → active ingredient-ref-black-beans, with a canned/drained form.
- heart of palm → the English alias of active ingredient-ref-hearts-of-palm.
- panela and rapadura → active identities already supported in the ledger.
- The exact key corn matches only the inactive legacy candidate ingredient-ref-corn. It is not an active generic corn reference. The active neighbors are more specific rows such as corn kernels, corn on the cob, hominy corn, hominy grits, cornmeal, and corn syrups; none is silently promoted to generic corn.

No new South American source-native name in the disposition tables below has an accidental exact-key collision with an active identity or form. The live forms include quinoa, dry/cooked and canned/drained black beans; they do not include the proposed regional products. In particular, current edible cassava flour and tapioca starch are not exact matches for a fresh manioc root, Brazilian cassava products, or the tacacá wording goma de tapioca. Accented and unaccented spellings that differ only by accents normalize to the same search key and are treated as spelling variants only where the cited source supports that relationship.

## Measurement, substitution, and preservation boundaries

No density, unit mapping, weight/volume relation, package yield, cooking yield, rehydration ratio, or global substitute is proposed. Recipe-source quantities are not copied into the catalog and remain attached to their exact source contexts. The source table includes product or process definitions only where those definitions matter to identity.

The tacacá source’s recipe wording that presents polvilho azedo/goma de tapioca as an alternative is not generalized into a substitute rule or alias. Existing baking evidence already distinguishes polvilho doce, polvilho azedo, tapioca starch, and edible cassava flour. Guyana’s cassareep account describes a traditional preserving role, but it is not tested preservation guidance. Bolivia’s potato preservation descriptions and Chile’s dried-bean discussion similarly do not authorize a process or safety claim.
