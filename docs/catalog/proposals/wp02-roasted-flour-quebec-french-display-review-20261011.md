# WP02 — Québec French Review of Active Roasted-Flour Displays

- **Date:** 2026-10-11
- **Status:** Proposal only; no shared catalog, ledger, plan, roadmap, code, test, or recipe files changed.
- **Scope:** Independent terminology and grammar review of the active roasted barley, quinoa, wheat, corn, red-rice, and urid flour references, plus related active entries named in `baking-flours-starches.md`.
- **Inputs reviewed:** `docs/RecipeBook-global-ingredient-catalog-plan.txt`; `docs/catalog/batches/baking-flours-starches.md`; the current bundled ingredient-reference JSON and its 114 manifest-listed recipes; and the prior roasted-quinoa, Hattai-ko/mugikogashi, roasted-urid, and roasted-red-rice proposal packets.

## Recommendation

Keep the six current English product identities and their present aliases. Keep the active French displays as provisional catalog labels for now, with two targeted follow-ups before treating their wording as process-specific: the current quinoa display and red-rice display both use feminine **torréfiée**, which means the flour itself is roasted; the manufacturer evidence does not settle that process sequence for either product. The quinoa manufacturer’s French heading instead repeatedly uses masculine **torréfié**, which can describe the quinoa before milling. Do not normalize this difference by grammar alone.

The current French displays for roasted wheat, corn, and urid flour are grammatically and source-grounded when read as flour that is roasted. Les Moulins de Soulanges explicitly describes roasting its wheat flour and calls its corn product flour **torréfiée**; Niru says its finely milled urid flour is then slow-roasted. The barley display is grammatical but its public page does not specify whether grain or flour is heated. No reviewed source establishes that the distinct manufacturers’ roasted products share an identical processing method.

## Grammar and Québec usage

The OQLF says that an adjective or past participle after a noun plus a *de* complement can agree with either noun, depending on the meaning: `farine de quinoa torréfiée` attaches the feminine adjective to *farine*; `farine de quinoa torréfié` attaches it to masculine *quinoa*. The OQLF also confirms that *farine* is feminine. Accordingly, feminine agreement is required when the intended claim is “the flour is roasted,” but changing a manufacturer’s masculine ending to feminine can change which material the name says was roasted. Agreement alone does not prove a process.

Les Moulins de Soulanges is a Québec source for the French product wording, but its page headings are inconsistent in agreement. Its English and French pages establish the manufacturer’s product names; its product descriptions, not the heading’s ending by itself, are the evidence used below for the treatment stage. OQLF base terms establish ordinary ingredient names such as *farine d’orge*, *farine de maïs*, and *farine de riz*; they do not establish a standardized Québec term for any of the six roasted-flour compounds.

## Source limits and proposed follow-up

- Manufacturer product headings attest the manufacturers’ names, not a common technical standard for *roasted*, *toasted*, or *torréfié* flour.
- The Québec mill’s English/French product pages are primary product evidence, not an independent terminology ruling. Its wheat description specifies roasting the flour; its corn text calls the flour torréfiée while describing thermal treatment of maize starch; its barley and quinoa descriptions do not clearly state treatment order.
- Niru’s red-rice title attests a current named product, while the description identifies finely milled red rice. It does not document the roast step or its order. Niru’s urid page does state milling before slow roasting, but no French display or Latin species.
- OQLF base terms and agreement guidance support the French constructions and grammar only; they do not certify the complete roasted-product names.
- The CFIA *haricot urd* wording occurs in an organic grain-code table and is limited to the bean name/species correspondence.
- The active `toasted barley flour` alias remains specific to the reviewed barley identity. It does not establish a universal toasted/roasted synonym or process equivalence for other grains.

If a later integration revisits these displays, ask Les Moulins de Soulanges whether its quinoa flour is made from roasted quinoa or roasted after milling, and ask Niru the same question for red-rice flour. Until then, retain the present French strings as provisional user-facing labels; add no aliases, product/process equivalences, forms, numeric data, substitutions, or recipe links on the strength of this review alone.
