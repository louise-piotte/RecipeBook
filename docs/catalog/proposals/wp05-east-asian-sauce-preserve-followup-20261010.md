# WP05 East Asian sauce and preserve follow-up — 2026-10-10

- **Packet type:** proposal only; not integrated.
- **Scope:** douchi; white/red fermented bean curd; sesame paste; chili crisp and chili oil; XO sauce; Sa Cha/sha cha sauce; char siu sauce.
- **Prepared and source-access date:** 2026-10-10.
- **Recipe scope:** all 114 recipes in the current bundled-library manifest.
- **Source IDs:** `WP05-EASP-FU-20261010-S01` through `WP05-EASP-FU-20261010-S13`. The prefix was checked against the current research ledger and proposal/batch files before use; no collision was found.

## Recommendation

Accept narrow, separately named identities for **chili oil**, the documented **Chiu Chow-style chili crisp oil**, **XO sauce**, **Sa Cha sauce**, and **char siu sauce**, using the Canadian manufacturer’s English and French labels below. Accepting these names does not make the manufacturer’s ingredient lists universal formulas. Do not add forms, measures, conversions, or substitutions.

Hold **douchi**, **white/red fermented bean curd**, and generic **sesame paste**. The product boundaries are real, but the current evidence does not settle a safe Québec French standalone name and identity granularity for douchi or the bean-curd variants; sesame paste remains too broad to map to the current tahini row. Do not link recipe lines: no bundled ingredient line matches any reviewed candidate.

## Existing state and prior-review audit

The active seed currently has `ingredient-ref-black-bean-sauce` (`black bean sauce` / `sauce de soja noir`), `ingredient-ref-doubanjiang` (`doubanjiang` in both languages), and `ingredient-ref-tahini` (`tahini` / `beurre de sésame`). It also has separate sesame-seed, toasted-sesame-oil, chili-powder, red-chili-flake, and BBQ-sauce entries. There is no exact active identity for the proposed chili oils/crisp, XO, Sa Cha, or char siu names. The forms file has no matching form; no exact candidate identity was found in the legacy-import file.

I reviewed the earlier `wp05-east-asian-condiments.md` and `wp05-east-asian-condiments-followup.md`; they already call for keeping douchi separate from black-bean sauce, fermented bean curd separate from douchi, sesame paste separate from tahini, chili crisp separate from chili oil, Sa Cha separate from satay, and the spelling `sha cha` unresolved. This packet resolves only the candidates for which current Canadian bilingual product pages now support a narrow display name. The Chinese-condiment boundary review already integrated black-bean sauce and doubanjiang; neither is proposed again. The WP04 dried-shrimp/scallop review documents a Fine Seafood XO product, but no XO reference is active. This packet uses a current general XO product label and does not alias the Fine Seafood product to a universal formulation.

The separate WP05 BBQ-alias review has already integrated English alias `barbecue sauce` on `ingredient-ref-bbq-sauce`. This packet does not add that alias again, modify mayonnaise, or connect char siu sauce to the BBQ row.

## Candidate dispositions

| Candidate | Disposition | Narrow proposal and limits |
|---|---|---|
| **Douchi** | **HOLD** | Chinese regulatory material distinguishes 豆豉 from 腐乳; a Canadian trademark goods list glosses `Douchi` as fermented black soybeans [S01–S02]. A Canadian French manufacturer ingredient list uses `haricots noirs fermentés` inside a prepared chili-black-bean sauce [S03], but that is not a standalone douchi label and can be confused with common black beans. Hold both a new row and French alias pending a Québec/Canadian standalone product label or stronger local terminology source. Never alias douchi to prepared `black bean sauce` or to `doubanjiang`. |
| **White/red fermented bean curd** | **HOLD** | The regulator lists fermented bean curd and douchi as distinct fermented soybean products [S01]. Lee Kum Kee Canada’s French Chu Hou paste label lists `tofu blanc fermenté` as one constituent of a prepared paste [S04]; it is not a standalone product label. No comparable current Canadian French source for a red product was found. Hold parent identity and white/red styles until the standalone products and terminology are sourced. Do not equate either with ordinary tofu or red-bean paste. |
| **Sesame paste** | **HOLD** | Keep generic `sesame paste` / `pâte de sésame` distinct from `ingredient-ref-tahini`, sesame seeds, sesame oil, and prepared sesame sauce. OQLF’s `beurre de sésame` entry supports tahini wording, while Usito locates its definition in Middle Eastern cuisine and describes roasted ground seed with oil [S06–S07]. Lee Kum Kee Canada’s French sesame sauce is a prepared product containing sesame paste, soybean oil, and gums [S05]. These sources do not identify one stable East Asian standalone paste formula. No alias to tahini and no new generic identity in this batch. This preserves the accepted WP03 sesame/tahini boundary. |
| **Chili oil** | **ACCEPT** | Proposed bilingual display: `chili oil` / `huile de piment rouge`, the paired Canadian manufacturer names [S08]. This supports a distinct infused-oil identity. The page’s soybean-oil/red-chili ingredient list describes that product only; do not treat it as the formula for every chili oil. Keep it separate from chili crisp, peppercorn chili oil, chili-garlic sauce, and chili flakes. |
| **Chili crisp / crisp oil** | **ACCEPT, product-style scope only** | Proposed bilingual display: `Chiu Chow Style Chili Crisp Oil` / `huile au piment croustillante de type Chiu Chow` [S09]. The Canadian manufacturer describes a chili-and-garlic product held in oil with crisp texture and provides the paired French label. Do not create a universal formula for chili crisp, alias this row to generic chili oil, or add `Chiu Chow`/`Chaozhou` spellings beyond the displayed source wording. |
| **XO sauce** | **ACCEPT** | Proposed bilingual display: `XO sauce` / `sauce de XO` [S10]. The Canadian product is a seafood-based condiment with dried shrimp, dried scallops, chili, and other ingredients. Keep the identity at the named compound level; no universal XO recipe or alias for the separately named Fine Seafood XO product is proposed. |
| **Sa Cha / sha cha sauce** | **ACCEPT `Sa Cha`; HOLD `sha cha` alias** | Proposed bilingual display: `Sa Cha sauce` / `sauce sa cha` [S11]. The Canadian bilingual label describes a regional Chinese sauce with flatfish, shrimp paste, sesame paste, and spices. Do not add `sha cha sauce` as a spelling alias without a direct crosswalk. Reject any Sa Cha-to-satay mapping: the Canadian French satay product is explicitly Southeast Asian and contains peanuts and coconut [S12]. |
| **Char siu sauce** | **ACCEPT, distinct compound** | Proposed bilingual display: `char siu sauce` / `sauce char siu` [S13]. The current Canadian product label describes a prepared Chinese barbecue marinade; its ingredients include sweeteners, fermented soybean paste, soy sauce, garlic, and spices. Keep `sauce barbecue chinoise` out of the stored name and aliases: the manufacturer uses it as a parenthetical descriptor, but `barbecue sauce` is already an alias of the separate North American BBQ row. Do not connect char siu to that row. The prior WP05 BBQ review’s OQLF boundary source [S03](wp05-barbecue-sauce-alias-boundary-review-20261010.md) distinguishes Chinese and North American barbecue sauces. |

For every accepted identity, add only the paired display terms stated above. Do not add brand names, regional spelling variants, generic product descriptions, forms, densities, unit mappings, package yields, recipe links, or substitution rules. Product pack sizes and use suggestions are not mass-volume evidence.
