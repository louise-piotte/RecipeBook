## Boundaries and excluded candidates

- The explicitly integrated identities—mango pickle, lime pickle, labneh, za'atar, amchur powder, and sumac—are omitted. Tahini and sweet mango chutney are also already integrated. The existing MENA follow-up still owns Mixed Pickles; the Toum/preserved-lemon follow-up owns those two candidates. None is re-proposed here.
- Do not treat harissa, zhug, shatta, and ajika as aliases or replacements for one another because each has source-supported regional product identity. Likewise, do not conflate biber salçası with these chili condiments.
- *Amba* is not an alias for mango pickle, mango chutney, or *amba achar*. The latter appears as an Indian pickle product name, which this review did not crosswalk to the Levantine-style amba sauce.
- *Narsharab* stays a separate unresolved candidate. ShahNar describes a pomegranate-juice sauce for fish/meat/kebabs; AZGRANATA describes its `Narsh` product as natural pomegranate juice without sugar/preservatives. These product descriptions are insufficient to make either term an alias for pomegranate molasses.
- Do not generalize one maker's recipe to chermoula, amba, shatta, muhammara, or pomegranate molasses as a universal formula. Product-specific add-ins and recipe variations are not aliases or density evidence.
- Ajvar is not included in this MENA/Caucasus batch; route it to a separate regional review. Other pending candidates such as ginger-garlic paste, tamarind paste, chutneys, kasundi, kewra water, and rose-water products remain with the earlier broad proposal or future review rather than being repeated here.

## Source-to-claim map

All pages below were accessed on **2026-10-08**. Product descriptions establish those products, not universal recipes. Government, standards, and language-office sources are preferred where available; local makers are used for product naming and recipe examples.

1. **Harissa identity and EN/fr-CA terminology:** [OQLF, GDT “harissa”](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/8357796/harissa). OQLF gives the French and English term, classifies it under food/spice/seasoning, defines the North African hot-pepper/oil/spice purée, and notes a powdered presentation.
2. **Chermoula identity and boundary:** [WIPO, *Scoping study on the gastronomic tourism sector in Morocco*](https://www.wipo.int/ip-development/en/docs/Scoping_Study_en_Morocco.pdf) associates chermoula with Moroccan fish; [Le Creuset Canada, French chermoula recipe](https://www.lecreuset.ca/fr_CA/poulet-en-brique-a-la-chermoula-et-tomates-cerises-fondantes/LCRCA-0399.html) confirms French spelling and one herb/spice/oil recipe example; [Mina, tomato chermoula simmer sauce](https://mina.co/products/tomato-chermoula-simmer-sauce) distinguishes a tomato simmer-sauce product from the broader chermoula name.
3. **Amba identity and recipe variability:** [Galil Brands, Bomba Amba](https://galilbrands.com/bomba-amba) describes an amba sauce made with pickled unripe mango and spices; [National Mango Board, “Amba Pickled Mango”](https://www.mango.org/recipes/amba-pickled-mango/) gives one recipe example, not a universal formula. [Amba Foods, Amba Aachar](https://www.ambafoods.co.in/amba-aachar) is used only to document a separate Indian pickle product label, not an alias.
4. **Zhug identity and spelling:** [Blank Slate Kitchen, “Zhug”](https://www.blankslatekitchen.com/products/zhug/) uses zhug for its Yemeni hot sauce and lists zhug/zhoug/zhough/schug/schugg/skhug/zhugg as spelling variants; its additional Arabic-name crosswalk is not activated. [Cuisinart Canada, French zhug recipe](https://www.cuisinart.ca/fr/recipes/saucesanddressings/zhug-recipe.html) supports the borrowed French display `zhug`.
5. **Shatta identity and formula variation:** [Mawasem/New Farm Palestine, “Chili Sauce”](https://mawasem.ps/products/chili-sauce/) says its Palestinian chili sauce is also known as shatta and describes its own pepper/garlic/salt formula; [Canaan Palestine, Shutta](https://canaanpalestine.com/products/shutta) provides a second producer product example with a different listed formula. Neither makes generic chili sauce a synonym.
6. **Biber salçası identity, variants, and package-size ambiguity:** [Tat, hot pepper paste](https://www.tat.com.tr/aci-biber-salcasi) labels a Turkish hot pepper paste and lists sweet and hot pepper paste product categories; [Burcu, sweet pepper paste](https://www.burcu.com.tr/en/product/burcu-sweet-pepper-paste/) documents a separate sweet product example; [Tukaş product catalogue](https://www.tukas.com.tr/dosya/ffc674bd81..pdf) lists *Biber Salçası* and *Acı Biber Salçası* separately, with net-weight and container-size notation such as 350 g (370 cc). That pair is not treated as measured net volume or a density value. The records support the product family and variant boundary, not a universal formula.
7. **Muhammara identity, fr-CA spelling, and variation:** [Health Canada, Muhammara dip](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/food-guide/eating-support/kitchen/recipes/muhammara-dip-red-bell-pepper-walnut.html) provides a Canadian public recipe; [Beytoot, Toronto Muhammara](https://beytoot.ca/products/muhammara) and [Yaza Foods, Muhammara](https://yazafoods.com/products/muhammara/) show named commercial products with differing formulations; [Les Filles Fattoush, French “Mouhamara” recipe](https://lesfillesfattoush.com/blogs/recettes/recette-mouhamara) supports the fr-CA spelling alias. [Harvard T.H. Chan, Turkish muhammara](https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/turkish-muhammara/) supplies the single-recipe pomegranate-molasses/honey note only.
8. **Rose syrup identity and fr-CA name:** [Monin Canada, “Sirop de rose (Rose)”](https://monin.ca/fr/products/rose-syrup) uses the French term and identifies its product as a syrup for drinks/cocktails. This supports the product name and broad use, not general density or formula.
9. **Pomegranate molasses name and narsharab boundary:** [Les Filles Fattoush English product page](https://lesfillesfattoush.com/en/products/melasse-grenade) and [French product page](https://lesfillesfattoush.com/products/melasse-grenade) use the proposed English and French names and list a 200 mL Québec product; [Soha Nuts, Litani pomegranate molasses](https://sohanuts.com/fr/products/litani-pomegranate-molasses) documents a Lebanon-made product described as reduced pure pomegranate juice. For the separate name boundary, [ShahNar](https://www.shah-nar.az/en/) describes narsharab sauce from pomegranate juice, while [AZGRANATA, Narsh](https://azgranata.az/product/narsh/) describes a pomegranate-juice-only product. These pages do not establish equivalence among the names.
10. **Date syrup name, product boundary, measurement, and substitution:** [OQLF, GDT “sirop de dattes”](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/8433496/sirop-de-dattes) gives the French term, English `date honey`, date-pulp definition, concentration variation, 37–40 °Bé range, and rejection of French *miel de dattes*; Baumé concentration is not density. [Fineberry Foods, date products](https://www.fineberry.ca/dates-products-range.html) labels date syrup/sirop de dattes and distinguishes it from date paste and date powder. [Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, simple date syrup recipe](https://www.eatright.org/recipes/desserts/simple-date-syrup-recipe) states possible honey/maple uses and gives one homemade-recipe serving weight (1 tbsp / 15 g), not a commercial conversion.
11. **Tkemali identity and product boundary:** [Sakpatenti, Georgian GI specification for Tkemali](https://www.sakpatenti.org.ge/media/publication_pdf/BL112022_eng.pdf) defines Tkemali as Georgian cherry-plum sauce and documents its color/fruit/herb-spice variation; [Aroma Georgia, Tkemali sauce](https://aroma.ge/eng/products/sauces/tkemali) documents a manufacturer product; [CSauvé, French “Tkemali sauce aux prunes géorgienne”](https://csauve.ca/gastronomie/recette.php?id=1116) supports the fr-CA descriptive label. The existing generic `plum sauce` is not treated as an alias.
12. **Ajika identity, spelling, and recipe variants:** [UK Government, Georgian GI specification for Ajika](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6437e4e2db4ba9000c448e40/1_Ajika_%E1%83%90%E1%83%AF%E1%83%98%E1%83%99%E1%83%90_Other_products_of_Annex_I_of_the_Treaty__spices_etc._.pdf) records Ajika as a Georgian condiment and specifies variable regional recipes; [Georgia Travel, “Ajika”](https://georgia.travel/adjika) describes its uses and variations; [Nena, “Adjika”](https://nena.ge/en/product/52-Adjika) supports the English product spelling proposed as an alias. The GI's pepper variation is an internal recipe variant, not substitution for the finished condiment.
