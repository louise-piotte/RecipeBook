# WP04 Section P — frozen breaded fish sticks product identity review (R221)

- **Review date:** 2026-10-16.
- **Recommendation:** Add identity-only **frozen breaded fish sticks / bâtonnets de poisson panés surgelés** as **ingredient-ref-frozen-breaded-fish-sticks**, category **PROTEIN**.
- **Boundary:** A species-neutral group for commercially prepared frozen breaded fish sticks. Include sticks made from minced fish or fish fillets. Keep raw fish cut into sticks at home, battered fish portions, breaded fillets, fish burgers, fish cakes, fish balls, surimi products, and species-specific fish references separate.
- **Status:** Submitted proposal accepted and integrated on 2026-10-16; see the R219–R225 gap-review integration README. No app tests or emulator checks were run because the accepted changes add database JSON data and documentation under `AGENTS.md`.
- **Proposal-local source namespace:** **WP04-SECTIONP-FROZEN-BREADED-FISH-STICKS-R221-20261016-S01** through **S11**. The prefix and cited source URLs were free in the ledger and archives at review time. The eleven proposal-local IDs are now allocated on the accepted ledger record.

## Why this is a distinct open product gap

Section P still has open finfish and prepared-seafood coverage. Current Québec grocery listings show a recurring frozen breaded fish-stick product class in the same aisle as fish fillets, burgers, and other formed seafood. The 700 g Metro High Liner value-pack listing is bilingual under the same product number; Metro reports 28 pieces and identifies the fish as minced fillets of pollock or haddock. Loblaws also currently lists frozen breaded fish sticks in French. The class therefore has a clear market identity even though the exact species and formulation vary among products.

The active catalog already distinguishes such neighboring products as fish balls, fish tofu, crab-flavoured pollock flakes, and frozen cuttlefish balls. None represents commercial breaded fish sticks. Keep this group separate from raw species and cuts: CFIA’s fish-label guidance explicitly notes that breaded portions may be made from slices or minced fish, and the Metro package specifies minced fish. A fish-stick name does not settle either the species or the structure of its fish portion.

The recommendation is restricted to breaded sticks sold frozen. Do not include battered fish portions, whole breaded fillets, tempura fish, fish fries, fish burgers, or homemade breaded strips under this identity. The standards and terminology sources discuss battered and breaded products together, but the reviewed current Canadian retail examples for this proposed row are explicitly breaded.

## Bilingual name and alias decision

Use the exact current Québec retailer wording **frozen breaded fish sticks / bâtonnets de poisson panés surgelés**. TERMIUM records **fish stick** and **bâtonnet de poisson**, and its frozen-food record gives **frozen fish stick / bâtonnet de poisson congelé**; the entry describes battered or breaded whitefish products. OQLF’s GDT entry says the English borrowing *stick* is often rendered as *bâton* or *bâtonnet* and gives *bâtonnet de poisson* as one example. This supports Québec French wording generally; the exact full frozen-product name is supported by retailer sources.

Add no aliases. The complete English and French product phrases are the display names and both are directly searchable. Keep **fish fingers**, **bâtonnet de poisson congelé**, **poisson pané**, **fish fillets**, and species names out of aliases: some are broader than the reviewed breaded-stick class, while others would imply a species or product form not established for every retail stick. Do not alias this prepared product to raw pollock, haddock, cod, or another fish.

## Recipe, amount, and substitution findings

- **Bundled demand:** A scan of all **114 manifest-listed recipe files and 1,123 structured ingredient rows**, including recipe text, found no fish-stick, fish-finger, or French bâtonnet-de-poisson match. Add no bundled recipe link.
- **Retail package:** The bilingual Metro listing for product **061763023163** is **700 g / 28 sticks**. This is one SKU’s package mass and declared count, not a reusable stick-to-mass mapping. The Janes manufacturer page reports 480 g and 1.1 kg formats; those format sizes are also brand-specific. Do not derive a universal count, yield, or serving from either product.
- **Product standard:** Codex Standard 166 describes frozen fish sticks and portions as a class that may use minced fish, fillets, or a mix, and may use one species or a mix of similar fish. Its 20–50 g coated-stick criterion is a product-standard observation, not a recipe measure or a Canadian package conversion. No density, unit mapping, count conversion, or active measure is proposed.
- **Homemade recipes:** Metro’s bilingual “Homemade Fish Sticks” recipe uses 680 g (1½ lb) of raw tilapia or cod for four servings, then cuts and breads the fish at home. Choose Canadian Seafood’s recipe uses 1.14 kg of raw fish fillets for 24–28 homemade pieces and lists recipe-specific alternatives. These amounts and alternatives describe home preparation from raw fish, not the commercial frozen breaded-stick product.
- **Substitution:** No source supports replacing a recipe quantity of frozen commercial fish sticks with raw fillets, fish balls, fish cakes, crab-flavoured pollock flakes, or another species at a general ratio. The homemade recipes’ species choices apply only to those recipes’ raw-fish preparation. Add no global or contextual substitution.

If accepted, the catalog delta is identity-only: **ingredient-ref-frozen-breaded-fish-sticks**, **PROTEIN**, the two display names above, empty aliases, null density, and empty unit mappings. The ledger should mark identity verified, measurement research **researched_no_supported_value**, and substitution research **researched_no_supported_substitute**, retaining package and recipe amounts only as source-specific observations.

## Disposition

Advance the identity-only frozen breaded fish-stick group with the exact bilingual displays and no aliases, forms, recipe links, active measures, conversions, yields, or substitutions. Preserve each SKU’s own species, ingredient declaration, package size, and stick count as source-specific evidence. Keep homemade fish sticks, battered fish portions, full fillets, fish balls, fish cakes, and surimi separate. The shared recipe corpus has no matching demand; the current Canadian/Québec retail evidence supports representing this recurring prepared-fish product class on its own.
