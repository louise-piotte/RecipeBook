# WP02 water-chestnut flour and extracted-starch boundary review

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-09
- **Status:** research proposal; only this proposal file is changed.
- **Coordinator decision:** **reject one unqualified `water chestnut flour` identity or alias; keep WP02 B3 open.** Accept the evidence-backed boundary that whole-corm flour and extracted corm starch are different products. Do not integrate either under the current water-chestnut vegetable reference. Re-scope future candidates by botanical source and processing, then obtain independent Québec French review.
- **Scope:** water-chestnut flour/starch identity, processing, English and Québec French naming, aliases, measurements, substitutions, and current catalog/recipe matches for WP02 Section B.

## Recommendation and decisions

“Water chestnut flour” is not a stable, single product name in the sources reviewed. It can name flour ground from dried *Trapa* fruits, and is also used in Chinese-market labeling for *Eleocharis dulcis* products that research identifies as extracted starch. Primary food-science research separately defines whole-corm flour and extracted starch from *E. dulcis*. The current English/French root reference adds a further naming collision: Québec sources use “châtaigne d’eau” for both the Chinese sedge and *Trapa natans*.

| Question | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Add generic `water chestnut flour` / `farine de châtaigne d’eau` as one global reference or alias? | **Reject.** | The English name covers more than one botanical source and processing class. The French phrase is not a verified fr-CA product name and collides with the active root reference and current Québec plant nomenclature. |
| Merge flour and extracted starch, or represent either as a form of the raw vegetable? | **Reject.** | For *E. dulcis*, a primary study prepares flour by drying and milling peeled corm slices, but prepares starch by aqueous extraction, filtration, precipitation, drying, and milling. The study reports distinct composition and pasting behavior. These are separate `FLOUR_AND_STARCH` product identities, not preparation forms of the vegetable or of each other. |
| Create a product identity for *Trapa* flour under the unqualified English name? | **Defer.** | India’s official eNAM commodity entry defines “water chestnut flour” as ground dried nuts of *Trapa bispinosa* or *T. quadrispinosa* and gives the common name `Singhare ka atta`. This is distinct from *E. dulcis*, but its global English and fr-CA display names remain unresolved. |
| Add English or French aliases such as `water nut`, `water caltrop`, `singhara`, `singoda`, `ma tai`, `ma ti`, `mah tai`, `pi tsi`, or `kuwai`? | **Reject for this proposal.** | These terms are not product-specific synonyms across the sourced botanical and process boundaries. OQLF explicitly marks several transliterations as criticized associated terms; none is justified as a general flour/starch alias. |
| Add density, unit mappings, conversions, or a substitution rule? | **Reject for this proposal.** | The reviewed sources do not support a reusable catalog density or volume conversion. Cookie formulation trials are species-, recipe-, and method-specific and do not establish a general replacement ratio. |

The future research split should be: (1) whole-corm *Chinese water chestnut flour* from *Eleocharis dulcis*; (2) extracted *Chinese water chestnut starch* from *E. dulcis*; and, if WP02 intends to cover it, (3) dried-fruit *Trapa* flour, source-named as `Singhare ka atta` / `singhara flour`. These are candidate scopes, not approved display names or reserved IDs. Keep the fr-CA product names unresolved until a Québec terminology reviewer approves a qualified naming strategy. Do not use bare `chestnut flour`, which can also refer to tree-chestnut (*Castanea*) flour.

## Identity and process evidence

The strongest direct process evidence is Panid Rujirapisit’s study of *Chinese water chestnut (Eleocharis dulcis Trin.) flour and starch*. The paper says both are prepared from the edible corms, then documents different preparations: flour is made by washing and peeling the corms, slicing, tray-drying, milling, and sieving; starch is made by grinding peeled corms with water, filtering and re-extracting the residue, settling starch from the filtrate, drying, milling, and sieving. Its abstract and composition table distinguish the products, and its discussion reports different gelatinization and pasting behavior. This directly supports a product boundary, but only for the study’s sample and method. The reported laboratory composition and viscosity values are not catalog measurements.

The Indian eNAM specification defines its `WATER CHESTNUT FLOUR` commodity as a product obtained by grinding clean, sound, dried nuts of *Trapa bispinosa* or *Trapa quadrispinosa* and names it `Singhare ka atta`. It is a different botanical scope from the Chinese-sedge corm product. The entry is an Indian market commodity definition, not a global definition for every label using `water chestnut flour`.

The 2020 *Foods* study identifies *E. dulcis* as the Chinese water chestnut and, in its Materials and Methods §2.1, lists a commercial sample as “water chestnut starch (Pan Tang Brand, Guangzhou, China).” This establishes that a commercial Chinese product is researched and sold under the starch class. It does not show that every package labelled “water chestnut flour” is extracted starch or establish a French product name.

## English and Québec French names

- **English:** `Chinese water chestnut` and `Chinese water chestnut flour` / `Chinese water chestnut starch` are explicitly used for the *E. dulcis* corm products in the primary study. The qualifier `Chinese` is needed in research and catalog proposals to distinguish them from *Trapa* flour. Bare `water chestnut flour` is rejected as a global alias because eNAM uses it for dried *Trapa* nuts, while Chinese-source material uses “water chestnut flour” and “water chestnut starch” for separately processed *E. dulcis* products.
- **Québec French root name:** OQLF’s older entry gives preferred `châtaigne d’eau` for the edible tuber of Chinese sedge and English `water chestnut`; it also lists `water nut` and marks several transliterated terms as criticized. However, the current Gouvernement du Québec flora page gives `châtaigne d’eau` / `water chestnut` for *Trapa natans*. OQLF has a separate `châtaigne d’eau` entry translated as `water caltrop`, with `water chestnut` only as an associated English term. These are direct, conflicting taxonomic scopes for the same French name.
- **Québec French product names:** No authoritative Québec source located here establishes a preferred fr-CA name for the flour or extracted starch products. `farine de châtaigne d’eau chinoise`, `fécule de châtaigne d’eau chinoise`, and `farine de singhara` are only possible qualified working strings; they remain **unresolved and must not be added as names or aliases**. A Canadian retailer lists `Singoda` with the French phrase `farine de châtaigne d’eau`, and a French retailer lists `Farine de Châtaigne d'eau`, but those retail labels do not establish botanical source, extraction method, or Québec terminology. Do not copy either as a canonical fr-CA name.
- **Existing-reference conflict to route separately:** the active `ingredient-ref-water-chestnut` has English `water chestnut` and French `châtaigne d'eau`, based on the older OQLF Chinese-sedge entry. The current Québec Government page assigns that French/English pair to *Trapa natans*. This proposal does not edit or resolve the existing WP03 root reference; the coordinator should route that bilingual identity conflict to its owner before relying on the French name for a new WP02 identity.

## Measurement and substitution evidence

- **Density and unit mapping:** no reusable `g/ml` density or ingredient-specific cup/volume mapping is supported here. Process-study moisture, composition, particle, gelatinization, viscosity, and blend values describe particular laboratory samples or tests; they are not a catalog density or conversion. Keep `defaultDensity` null and `unitMappings` empty for any later candidate until a matching product/method source supports a value.
- **Substitution:** a 2016 primary cookie study identifies its flour source as dried *Trapa natans* fruits, ground and sieved, and tests *Trapa* flour/wheat-flour blends in cookie formulas. A 2024 study tests *Trapa natans* flour with wheat flour in cookies and reports acceptable 15–30% formulations in that study. These findings are recipe-specific evidence for *Trapa* flour, not for *E. dulcis* flour or extracted starch; they do not establish a universal 1:1 substitution or a general flour-to-starch conversion. No rule, ratio, confidence, or rounding policy is proposed.

## Exact catalog and recipe audit

The audit followed `normalizeIngredientSearchKey` in `app/src/main/java/app/recipebook/domain/localization/IngredientSearch.kt:9-20`: lowercase using `Locale.ROOT`, Unicode NFKD, fold `œ`/`æ`/`ß` and `&`, remove combining marks, replace non-ASCII-alphanumeric characters with spaces, then collapse whitespace. Candidate phrases were checked with the app’s complete-word-phrase-first search and substring fallback behavior in lines 113–140. A read-only Python reproduction of that exact normalization and phrase matching was used; no runtime picker or tests were run.

The 37 phrase queries covered English, French, regional/transliterated, and false-match terms: `water chestnut`, `Chinese water chestnut`, `water chestnut flour`, `Chinese water chestnut flour`, `water chestnut starch`, `Chinese water chestnut starch`, `water chestnut powder`, `Chinese water chestnut powder`, `water nut`, `water nut flour`, `water caltrop`, `water caltrop flour`, `singhara`, `singhara flour`, `singhare ka atta`, `singhara atta`, `singoda`, `singoda flour`, `singoda atta`, `singhada flour`, `ma tai`, `ma tai fen`, `ma ti`, `ma ti fen`, `mah tai`, `pi tsi`, `kuwai`, `chestnut flour`, `châtaigne d'eau`, `farine de châtaigne d'eau`, `farine de châtaigne d'eau chinoise`, `fécule de châtaigne d'eau`, `fécule de châtaigne d'eau chinoise`, `amidon de châtaigne d'eau`, `amidon de châtaigne d'eau chinoise`, `farine de singhara`, and `farine de singoda`.
