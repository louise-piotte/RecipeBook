# WP06 Spanish-speaking Caribbean cuisine gap sweep proposal

**Status:** Source-review proposal for coordinator review. It recommends one new pulse identity, two pepper identity candidates, and several scoped alias or compound candidates; the remaining mappings are held. No seed or shared catalog data changed.
**Research date and access date:** 2026-10-11.
**Scope:** Cuba, Puerto Rico, and the Dominican Republic, with source-specific examples rather than claims of a single Spanish Caribbean cuisine.

## Scope and live-state audit

This is the bounded Spanish-speaking Caribbean sweep named under Section 05 / WP06. The sources benchmark ordinary savory dishes, festive food, breads and sweets, seasonings, and beverages where the reviewed material made those relevant. They do not establish a universal recipe, ingredient vocabulary, or complete survey of any island.

The current split seed package was read before drafting: **114 recipes and 1,123 ingredient rows**, **954 active ingredient references**, and **53 ingredient forms**. There are 1,036 active alias strings across 428 references. The active legacy-import identity file contains 75 candidates. The research ledger has 1,223 records, 3,679 source entries, and 2,993 distinct source IDs. The exact snapshots audited were:

Source-attested names that are already **existing exact identities** include rice, black beans, plantain, `malanga` (`ingredient-ref-malanga`), and `culantro` (`ingredient-ref-culantro`). Bitter orange is also an active exact identity (`ingredient-ref-bitter-orange`) with existing English `sour orange` and French `bigarade` / `orange de Séville` aliases. The active cassava-root identity is `ingredient-ref-cassava-root` (French label `manioc`), but the Cuban source's `yuca` / `yucca` is not an active exact alias and has no exact match in the refs, forms, legacy candidates, or ledger; it is therefore listed as an alias candidate below. No regional relink is proposed for the ordinary rice, bean, or plantain lines.

This benchmark reaches savory meals in all three cuisines; a Cuban cassava bread name; Puerto Rican Christmas food and drink; and Dominican sweets, festive baking, and regional stew examples. Preserved-food coverage is thin: alcaparrado appears inside one Puerto Rican recipe as a prepared olive/caper mixture, but no independent product standard was reviewed. Cuba's named drinks and Puerto Rican coquito are cultural or table beverages in the cited material; no new cooking-beverage ingredient is established. Other islands, regions, diaspora practices, and cuisine categories remain open.
