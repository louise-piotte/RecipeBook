## Candidate decision

Recommend these two candidates for coordinator review. They describe different culinary requests even though some egusi crops are botanically watermelons. The evidence supports `egusi` as a stable regional food-category identity spanning multiple cucurbit species; it does not support treating every watermelon seed product as egusi or assigning a single species to the category. The National Academies chapter and recent Ghana-focused research explicitly describe this multi-species/landrace usage, and a 2025 food-science paper calls egusi a food category rather than one taxon. [National Academies Press](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/11763/chapter/10), [Boakye et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44403-025-00035-y), [Northern Ghana study](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2025.1446681/full)

## Identity boundaries and regional names

| Name or product | Evidence and scope | Catalog treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Egusi** | The National Academies describes `egusi` as a generic West African name for food seeds from several Cucurbitaceae, including sources reported as *Citrullus lanatus*, *Cucumeropsis mannii*, *Lagenaria siceraria*, and *Telfairia occidentalis*. Recent northern Ghana research documents four local landraces with differing growth habits, colors, and seed coats, with no official botanical classification for the surveyed taxa. The term is therefore stable as a culinary/category request while remaining taxonomically broad. | One `egusi seeds` reference is supportable. Do not populate a canonical scientific name or assume uniform shell, composition, or species. |
| **Akatoa/Agushie/Tijapuro; Wrewre/Neri/Niri/Inabe; Guna shanu/denne nai/ibara; avrouda** | A 2025 food-science paper reports these as regional names for egusi types. A separate Northern Ghana study uses some names for specific local landraces and says the taxa are not officially classified. An older National Academies account associates `avrouda` with a fermented seasoning, while the 2025 paper lists it among seed names. | Preserve in research with source, locality, and product scope. Do not make them unqualified aliases. In particular, the different `avrouda` usages remain unresolved. |
| **Egusi-itoo / égousi-itoo / égousi / gousi / white seed melon** | PROTA’s species account uses these names for *Cucumeropsis mannii* (also listed there under the synonym *Cucumeropsis edulis*) and distinguishes it from watermelon egusi. | Species-scoped terms, not aliases for the generic egusi category. A later species-level identity may use them if the catalog has evidence and recipe need. |
| **M’bika / mbika** | A FAO trade study calls egusi, m’bika, and “graine de courge” generic commercial names used for several cucurbit genera, and reports m’bika in Brussels and Congolese-run Paris shops. | `m’bika` is a possible source-scoped market alias for egusi, not an EN/fr-CA display. Do not erase its locality and trade context. |
| **Graine(s) de courge / pumpkin seed(s); melon seed(s)** | FAO documents `graine de courge` as one trade name for mixed Cucurbitaceae products. In wider retail usage these expressions can refer to other cucurbits, dessert-melon seeds, or pumpkin seeds. | Too broad for unscoped aliases. Keep generic `melon seeds` and `graines de melon` unresolved unless a source specifies the crop and food product. |
| **Watermelon seeds / dried watermelon seed kernels** | USDA SR28 has a distinct food record named `Seeds, watermelon seed kernels, dried` with *Citrullus lanatus*. Some egusi sources also include watermelon crops, but a shared species name alone does not equate a general dried kernel product with a regional egusi request. | Use the narrow dried-kernel candidate only for an explicit kernels/dried request. Do not alias it to `egusi`; do not map unshelled snack seeds to it without a recipe/product match. |
| **Ground egusi / flour / meal** | Regional sources describe dehulled seeds ground for soups and stews, and commercial flour. The National Academies also describes defatted, low-hull flour for food processing, which is a different product from ordinary ground seeds. | If recipe demand appears, represent dry ground seed as a preparation form only when the seed base and shell state are known. Treat defatted meal/flour as a separate derived product when requested; do not alias every `egusi flour` to whole seeds. |
| **Paste, spread, fermentation, oil, dishes** | PROTA describes *C. mannii* kernels ground into a pale paste. The National Academies separately describes pounded roasted seed spread, fermented wrapped seed seasoning, and oil. Recipes also distinguish soups such as egusi soup. | Do not treat paste, roasted spread, fermented seasoning, extracted oil, defatted cake, or egusi soup as generic seed aliases. Their ingredients and processing are not interchangeable. A future plain ground-seed paste form would need recipe-specific evidence about added liquid and processing. |

The shell boundary is not uniform. Sources describe dehulled kernels and meal, but recent Ghana samples include Akatoa seeds both unshelled and shelled, while Neri is generally used without shelling. PROTA describes roasted seeds both with and without the integument for *C. mannii*. Do not assert that all egusi must be shelled, or that the term `whole seeds` consistently means in-shell or kernel-only. [Springer sample details](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44403-025-00035-y), [PROTA species account](https://plantuse.plantnet.org/fr/Cucumeropsis_mannii_(PROTA))

## Substitution findings

- **Egusi:** one 2015 food-science study prepared soup with melon-seed flour, watermelon-seed flour, and a 50:50 blend. It reports no significant difference in appearance, taste, thickness, or overall acceptability between the melon-only soup and the 50:50 blend, and concludes that watermelon-seed flour can replace 50% of melon-seed flour in that egusi-soup preparation. This supports one narrow partial-blend observation (`researched_supported`), not a general species equivalence, a replacement for every egusi product, or a simple one-target runtime rule. The paper’s botanical/product labels and sample context do not settle generic market identity. [Akusu & Kiin-Kabari, 2015](https://ccsenet.org/journal/index.php/jfr/article/view/49928)
- **Egusi to sunflower seeds:** a Nigerian recipe source presents sunflower-seed egusi soup as an alternative and says to use the same method. Its alternative uses 2 cups (290 g) sunflower seeds; the linked fried egusi version uses 4 cups (500 g) egusi seeds. The recipes do not establish equal measures, equal yield, or a general ratio. Keep this as a recipe-specific qualitative alternative; do not create a global or contextual substitution rule from it. [Sunflower-seed soup](https://www.allnigerianrecipes.com/soups/sunflower-seeds-egusi-soup/), [classic fried egusi soup](https://www.allnigerianrecipes.com/soups/fried-egusi-soup/)
- **Dried watermelon seed kernels:** no supported replacement for the exact dried-kernel product was found in the reviewed authoritative and recipe evidence. A secondary substitution page suggests equal-volume pumpkin/sunflower seeds for larger watermelon seeds eaten cracked like a snack; that is a different usage/form and is not evidence for substituting dry kernels in a recipe. Recommend `substitutionResearchState: researched_no_supported_substitute` for the narrow USDA product. Do not transfer the egusi-soup flour blend to this identity.

For the generic egusi identity, retain the supported scoped evidence above but add no active substitution rule. A future rule would need to preserve the soup context, seed/flour state, direction, and amount or mixture basis. Ingredient-to-ingredient equivalence is not implied by both products belonging to a broad seed category.

## Sources and source-to-claim map

All sources below were accessed 2026-10-09. IDs are proposal references only and have not been added to the shared research ledger.

- **WP03-S233 — National Research Council, [“8 Egusi,” *Lost Crops of Africa: Volume II: Vegetables*](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/11763/chapter/10)** (2006; DOI 10.17226/11763): generic West African seed-category use; example species; whole seed, dehulled kernel, ground flour, roasted paste/spread, fermented seasoning, trade names, and the limitations of taxonomic certainty. Primary broad identity and product-boundary source.
- **WP03-S234 — Boakye et al., [“Chemical and physical characterization of ‘egusi’ melon seeds and prediction of fat content by NIR Spectroscopy”](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44403-025-00035-y)** (*Food, Nutrition and Health*, 2025): describes egusi as a food category across varied Cucurbitaceae; records Ghanaian and Nigerian names; samples Akatoa and Neri seed types, including shelled/unshelled preparation differences. Peer-reviewed evidence for regional variation and limits on a single species/form assumption.
- **WP03-S235 — [“Potential of egusi for food and nutrition security in northern Ghana”](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2025.1446681/full)** (*Frontiers in Sustainable Food Systems*, 2025): four surveyed Ghanaian landraces, local names, seed-coat/color/shape variation, absence of official taxonomic classification for the surveyed types, and use of egusi as a broad food category. Regional peer-reviewed evidence.
- **WP03-S236 — PROTA, [*Cucumeropsis mannii*](https://plantuse.plantnet.org/fr/Cucumeropsis_mannii_(PROTA))** (French species account): species-scoped names including egusi-itoo/égousi-itoo; distinguishes it from watermelon egusi; describes roasted seeds, dehulled kernels, paste, oil/cake, 1,000-seed weight, and species-specific kernel-weight ratio. Do not generalize species measurements or names to the category.
- **WP03-S237 — FAO, [*Évaluation des échanges des produits forestiers non ligneux entre l’Afrique subsaharienne et l’Europe*](https://www.fao.org/4/x6612f/x6612f.pdf)** (French trade report, section 3.3.5): documents `egusi`, `m’bika`, and `graine de courge` as generic commercial names for multiple Cucurbitaceae and reports market-specific naming by city/community. It explicitly cannot determine whether traders sold whole seed or flour from appearance alone.
- **WP03-S238 — USDA ARS, [National Nutrient Database for Standard Reference Release 28, Food Group 12](https://www.ars.usda.gov/ARSUserFiles/80400535/Data/SR/SR28/reports/sr28fg12.pdf)** (2015; NDB 12174): exact record `Seeds, watermelon seed kernels, dried`, species *Citrullus lanatus*, common-measure values 1 cup = 108 g and 1 oz = 28.35 g, plus the record’s `63% Hard seed coat` refuse note. Supports only that USDA item’s portion observation and requires shell-state care.
- **WP03-S239 — OQLF, [melon d’eau](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/2297115/melon-deau)** (updated 2021): preferred Québec term `melon d’eau` and accepted `pastèque` for the fruit; does not define a seed product. Supports only the fruit component of the proposed descriptive translation.
- **WP03-S240 — Akusu & Kiin-Kabari, [“Comparative Studies on the Physicochemical and Sensory Properties of Watermelon and Melon Seed Flours Used in ‘EGUSI’ Soup Preparation”](https://ccsenet.org/journal/index.php/jfr/article/view/49928)** (*Journal of Food Research*, 2015): tests melon flour, watermelon-seed flour, and a 50:50 blend; reports similar sensory results for melon-only and blended egusi soups and concludes the watermelon flour can replace 50% in this soup context. Supports only the scoped experimental blend observation.
- **WP03-S241 — All Nigerian Recipes, [Sunflower Seeds “Egusi” Soup](https://www.allnigerianrecipes.com/soups/sunflower-seeds-egusi-soup/)** (publication date not shown): calls sunflower seeds an alternative and directs use of the same preparation method; lists 2 cups/290 g sunflower seeds. Regional recipe evidence only; no general amount ratio.
- **WP03-S242 — All Nigerian Recipes, [Nigerian Egusi Soup (Fried Method)](https://www.allnigerianrecipes.com/soups/fried-egusi-soup/)** (publication date not shown): names egusi/melon seeds and lists 4 cups/500 g in one recipe; supports the separate recipe amount observation, not a matched substitute ratio.
- **WP03-S243 — FoodSubs, [watermelon seeds](https://foodsubs.com/ingredients/watermelon-seeds)** (secondary substitution reference): describes larger watermelon seeds cracked and eaten like sunflower seeds and suggests equal-volume pumpkin/sunflower seeds. Excluded from support for the dried-kernel candidate because the source frames a different snack form and use.
