## Recommendation

Propose identity-only **miso-zuke (salted vegetables pickled in miso)**. MAFF classifies *misozuke* as salted vegetables subsequently pickled in miso, distinct from soy-sauce pickles, sake-lees pickles, rice-bran pickles, and other preservation methods. The National Institute of Health and Nutrition's food-list appendix separately records daikon and yamagobo miso-zuke entries; those examples support that the term is not a daikon-only identity, but they do not establish one universal vegetable set or formula. [S01–S02]

Keep this candidate vegetable-only and method-defined. It is not the miso ingredient or white miso paste, and it is not a global synonym for every food preserved with miso. Preserve individually named foods and other pickle-method identities. The French-Canadian wording is a provisional description, not a standardized name or alias.

If accepted for later integration, add one identity-only `SAUCE_AND_CONDIMENT` reference. Add no aliases, forms, density, unit mappings, reusable measures, recipe links, conversions, yields, or active substitutions. No numerical statement about general vegetable salting is specific enough to become a miso-zuke default.

## Identity and bilingual boundary

MAFF's national pickles entry defines the *miso-zuke* method class as salt-preserved vegetables pickled in miso, and lists it separately from soy-sauce, sake-lees, koji, vinegar, rice-bran, mustard, and other pickle methods. Its examples are named foods within that classification, not evidence that those names are interchangeable. [S01]

The NIBN food-list appendix separately includes `Daikon (Miso-zuke: pickled with miso)` and `Yamagobo (Miso-zuke: pickled with miso)`. This corroborates more than one vegetable substrate in that food-list context. It does not specify a universal vegetable range, recipe, seasoning ratio, fermentation outcome, or Québec terminology. [S02]

Use a qualified English display to make the preserved-vegetable scope visible. There is no Québec authority or reviewed French-language source standardizing a French name for this generic Japanese pickle class; keep the French display descriptive and provisional. Add no aliases, including `miso`, `miso paste`, `Japanese pickles`, or names of separately classified products.
