# WP05 Section T — Yeast extract identity and boundary review

- **Packet type:** proposal-only identity, Québec bilingual naming, exact-collision, and recipe-demand review.
- **Prepared and source-access date:** 2026-10-11.
- **Plan:** Section T, “MSG, nutritional yeast, yeast extract, bouillon powder, seasoning salt, and mushroom seasoning.”
- **Scope:** generic yeast extract as a culinary ingredient. Yeast-extract spreads, nutritional yeast, autolysed extract, monosodium glutamate, yeast extracts used as components of seasoning blends, and baker’s yeast are reviewed as boundaries only.
- **Status:** integrated on 2026-10-11 as one identity-only `yeast extract / extrait de levure` reference. Aliases, forms, recipe links, and numeric behavior remain empty. The separate yeast-extract-spread candidate stays held.
- **Source IDs:** `WP05-T-YEAST-EXTRACT-20261011-S01` through `S13`. The prefix was checked against the live ledger and existing proposal/batch text before assignment; no collision was found.

## Coordinator recommendation

Recognize generic **yeast extract / extrait de levure** as a food-ingredient identity, proposed ID `ingredient-ref-yeast-extract`, with category `BAKING_AND_SPICE` to match the existing catalog treatment of nutritional yeast and monosodium glutamate. Canadian government material uses `yeast extract(s)` and `extraits de levure` for the generic food ingredient, while current Canadian ingredient suppliers list yeast-extract products in powder, paste, and liquid presentations [S01–S04]. The generic bilingual name is source-supported, although the reviewed OQLF entry is for the more specific `extrait de levure autolysée`, not a generic standalone headword [S05].

Keep aliases empty. In particular, do not attach `yeast`, `levure`, `yeast extract powder`, `yeast extract paste`, `yeast extract liquid`, `autolyzed/autolysed yeast extract`, `autolyzed yeast cells`, `nutritional yeast`, `Marmite`, or `Vegemite`. These terms are either broader, format- or process-qualified, or refer to distinct products. Do not create a reference for yeast-extract spreads here; the existing `candidate-yeast-extract-spread` remains held pending its separate generic French display review [S05–S09].

The proposed row is identity-only. The suppliers’ presentation lists do not establish interchangeable formulas, common concentration, recipe use, or physical-form conversions. Add no recipe link, form, density, unit mapping, package yield, conversion, or substitution. The complete recipe bundle has no yeast-extract demand.

### Exact-key and search-collision limits

The full proposed phrases are unowned in the current active catalog and forms. Single-word queries remain broad: `yeast` can return multiple baker’s-yeast identities and nutritional yeast; `levure` can return yeast identities as well as baking powder and self-rising flour. Do not add those one-word terms as aliases. The held spread candidate also has a known phrase overlap: Metro’s Marmite page displays `Yeast Extract` / `Extrait de levure` for a prepared branded spread, while its English ingredient panel lists yeast extract with salt, vegetables, and spice extracts [S07–S08]. This is product-specific retailer wording, not proof that unqualified yeast extract means a spread. Preserve the generic extract identity and held spread candidate as distinct concepts; do not give the spread candidate the unqualified phrase as an alias.

## Identity and product boundaries

| Product or wording | Evidence-backed boundary | Catalog disposition |
|---|---|---|
| **Generic yeast extract** | Canadian government material treats yeast extracts as food ingredients, and Canadian ingredient suppliers list yeast extract as an ingredient family [S01–S04]. | Add one generic bilingual identity only: `yeast extract / extrait de levure`. Do not encode a supplier, source strain, composition, or application. |
| **Powder, paste, and liquid extract** | Canadian supplier portfolios list yeast extracts in these presentations [S03–S04]. | Leave the formats out of aliases and forms. They are useful product descriptors, but the sources do not establish a shared conversion, concentration, or recipe equivalence. |
| **Autolysed/autolyzed yeast extract** | OQLF records `extrait de levure autolysée / autolysed yeast extract` and describes extracted yeast material; Health Canada lists autolysed yeast extracts among other glutamate sources [S05, S10–S11]. | Preserve the process qualifier. Do not alias it to the generic row or create a separate active identity without recipe demand and a clearer decision about whether process-specific extracts need their own catalog identity. |
| **Nutritional yeast** | OQLF defines `levure alimentaire / nutritional yeast` as inactive yeast sold as flakes or powder and used as food; the existing catalog already has this identity [S06]. | Keep separate. It is not yeast extract and receives no alias or conversion to the proposed identity. |
| **Marmite/Vegemite-type yeast-extract spreads** | Metro’s Canadian English/French pages name a branded Marmite product with “yeast extract” / “extrait de levure” wording and list additional ingredients; the manufacturer calls Marmite a yeast-extract spread [S07–S09]. | Keep the existing spread candidate held. Brands are examples, not aliases. A spread is a prepared food, not a powder/paste/liquid form of generic extract. |
| **Monosodium glutamate and other glutamate sources** | Health Canada distinguishes directly added monosodium glutamate from naturally present glutamate and other sources, including soy sauce, autolysed yeast extract, and hydrolyzed plant protein [S10–S11]. The catalog already has a separate monosodium-glutamate reference. | Keep MSG/GMS, broad `glutamate`, soy sauce, hydrolyzed proteins, and yeast extract separate. Shared savoury or umami function does not make them aliases or supported substitutions. |
| **Bouillon powder and other seasoning mixtures** | The current Lee Kum Kee Canadian English and French product pages identify a chicken bouillon powder whose ingredient list includes yeast extract among several other ingredients. The two page declarations differ, so they are used only to show that extract may be a component of a blend, not as a formula or bilingual-SKU crosswalk [S12–S13]. | Keep bouillon powder and yeast extract as separate identities. A blend that contains extract is not itself extract; do not add bouillon aliases or reconstitution guidance to the extract row. |
| **Baker’s yeast** | The active baker’s-yeast parent and subtype identities cover leavening yeast; the recipe bundle’s three yeast rows belong to those products, not extracts. | Keep baker’s yeast and all its subtypes separate. Do not add generic `yeast` / `levure` aliases to the extract row. |
