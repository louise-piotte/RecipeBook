## Boundary notes

- **Belly is not one cured product.** Canadian bacon and OQLF's Quebec `bacon` entry describe belly-style bacon; pancetta is specifically salted, rolled, and dried belly. Their common cut does not make the names aliases.
- **Jowl is a different cut from belly.** The regional guanciale evidence is for pork jowl, not pancetta. Use `guanciale` as the source name; defer a fr-CA descriptor review rather than substituting fresh `bajoue de porc` or general pork cheek.
- **Ham is not one curing style.** The Canadian common name identifies a hind-leg product. Prosciutto is an Italian dry-salted raw ham in the Québec terminology source. Spanish sources separately specify Jamón Serrano and Jamón Ibérico naming. Preserve those named products and keep them out of the generic `ham / jambon` alias set.
- **Salt pork needs a concrete product scope.** The available Québec terminology entry and the North American pantry name overlap in wording, but the cited Québec note describes a salted-and-cooked product. Hold a general catalog row until a source or recipe establishes which market product is intended.
- **No curing or food-safety conclusions are drawn.** Similar names or shared cuts do not imply equivalent preparation, curing, cooking, storage, or raw-consumption behavior.

No forms, recipe relinks, numeric conversions, yields, or substitutions are proposed.
