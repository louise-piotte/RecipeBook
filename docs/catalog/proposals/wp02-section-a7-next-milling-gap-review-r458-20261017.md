## Recommendation

**Hold `spelt grits` / `spelt grit` unallocated.** Current manufacturer evidence establishes a real spelt-grits product class: MEGRO lists `Spelt grits – medium` separately from spelt grains and flakes, and an Ecoagri product specification names `Organic Spelt Wholegrain Grits` for bakery, boiled, and baked use [S01–S02]. The evidence does not establish a Québec-facing, product-matched French display. OQLF records `gruau` in both a technical grits/milling sense and the familiar Québec/Canada cooked-dish sense, but neither record names spelt grits [reused S01–S02]. The current Québec grain source uses `épeautre` for the cereal and describes whole-grain use; it does not name a grits product [S03].

The Canadian pages checked describe adjacent whole spelt groats/kernels, not grits [S04–S05]. The manufacturer pages also leave the processing boundary between `wholegrain grits` and `medium grits` underspecified: neither publishes a sieve range or other shared particle-size standard. Do not route these phrases to the active whole-kernel spelt row, and do not construct `gruau d'épeautre` from a base-species translation plus an old general milling term.

## Evidence limits

- MEGRO is a current German commercial mill source and directly lists `Spelt grits – medium`, but it does not give a mill specification, export availability, consumer label, or French name [S02].
- Ecoagri's source is a primary supplier specification issued 2019-04-01 and marked valid until further notice. It establishes that supplier's 500 g wholegrain product wording and use, but current SKU availability, production status, and a shareable grits definition were not independently verified [S01].
- The current Grains du Québec page is regional grain-sector information, not a retail-product census. It supports the local base term and whole-grain use, but does not rule out a Québec mill selling grits under another name [S03].
- The Canadian Oak Manor retailer page had a displayed product-expiry date of 2026-10-09, already past on the review date; the Canadian farm's separate spelt-groats listing showed `Sold out`. Both are used for wording and the adjacent whole-kernel boundary only, not current stock [S04–S05].
- OQLF's technical `gruau` entry is dated 1983 and scoped to malt crushing; it gives a general grits translation, not a spelt label. Its separate 2002 entry treats `gruau` as a Québec/Canada cooked dish and allows that dish to contain other cereals. Neither verifies `gruau d'épeautre` for dry spelt grits [reused S01–S02].
- The archive's R42 Swiss composite-ingredient phrase is evidence that `gruau d'épeautre complet` occurs outside Québec; it is not an independent current Québec product source and is not reused as an alias.
