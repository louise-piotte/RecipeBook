# WP06 Section Z — West African fermented locust-bean condiment boundary review

- **Date:** 2026-10-10
- **Status:** Proposal only. Keep the current `soumbala` reference unchanged and do not add global `iru` / `dawadawa` aliases or new rows. No shared catalog, ledger, plan, roadmap, recipe, code, or test changes are made.
- **Scope:** `iru`, `dawadawa`, `soumbala`, and the source-attested close form `soumbara`; direct product/process boundaries only.
- **Evidence access:** 2026-10-10
- **Decision owner:** Catalog coordinator. Repeat the live-package collision and recipe scan before any later integration.

## Recommendation

Treat `iru`, `dawadawa`, and `soumbala` as **regional names within one broad fermented African locust-bean condiment family when the source identifies the same seed substrate**. They are not three unrelated base ingredients. Research uses `iru` and `dawadawa` for closely related Nigerian/Ghanaian products and describes soumbala as the Burkina Faso/Mali name for the fermented *Parkia biglobosa* seed condiment. The broad family relationship is well supported.

Do **not** treat the unqualified words as globally interchangeable product aliases. Local processes, added fermentation starters, fermentation extent, texture, drying, and seed source vary. A Benin study explicitly compares `afitin`, Beninese `iru`, and `sonru` made from the same *Parkia biglobosa* seed, but calls them three different condiments: the processes use different additives and durations and produce visibly different products. Nigerian `iru wooro` and `iru pete` also distinguish partially fermented, distinct-bean product from a softer, paste-like product. In addition, literature calls products made from *Parkia filicoidea*, soybeans, *Treculia africana*, and Bambara groundnut `dawadawa` or dawadawa-type condiments. The local name alone therefore does not guarantee one botanical substrate or product state.

The catalog has no alias-level country, language-community, substrate, or product-state scope. The recipe corpus has no matching demand. Keep the accepted `ingredient-ref-soumbala` as the one source-bounded identity already present; record the relationship in research notes if integrated later, not in unqualified global alias arrays. Do not create separate `ingredient-ref-iru` and `ingredient-ref-dawadawa` rows in this pass.

## Product, process, and botanical boundaries

| Name / boundary | Evidence and limit | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| **Shared family: `iru`, `dawadawa`, `soumbala`** | Regional food-science literature groups these as fermented seed condiments. Sources identify *Parkia biglobosa* for soumbala in Burkina Faso and for many iru/dawadawa studies in Nigeria and Ghana. This supports a shared product family and regional name relationship, not one standard recipe or invariant finished product. | Treat as related regional products within the same family. Keep local names and product-specific process differences in source context. |
| **Iru process and forms** | An Ilorin study identifies *P. biglobosa* and distinguishes market `iru woro` (partially fermented, soft but with distinct beans) from `iru pete` (more completely fermented, softer and paste-like). The Benin study’s `iru` is a different local process: its second-stage cooked cotyledons receive `iku-iru`, a fermented hibiscus-seed additive, before about 48 h fermentation. | Do not collapse `iru woro`, `iru pete`, and Beninese `iru` into a universal texture or process. They are not separate global identities in this pass because there is no recipe demand or locality-aware catalog field. |
| **Soumbala process** | Burkina Faso sources describe alkaline fermentation of *P. biglobosa* seeds, with locally variable processing; one surveyed process boils and dehulls seeds, cooks again, ferments, then dries and molds them into balls. The same source says production conditions affect the finished product. | Keep the existing row source-bounded to the named fermented *P. biglobosa* condiment. Do not infer a standard moisture, ball size, texture, salt content, or preparation from this process description. |
| **Dawadawa substrate ambiguity** | A Ghana study makes dawadawa from *P. biglobosa*, *Glycine max*, and *Treculia africana*. A separate Nigerian study explicitly names fermented soybean condiment `soy-daddawa`. These are not evidence that all dawadawa products are one botanical ingredient. | A `dawadawa` query must not silently map a source-specified soy, Bambara-groundnut, *Treculia*, or other-seed product to *P. biglobosa* soumbala. Keep qualified products separate. |
| **Parkia species boundary** | *Parkia biglobosa* is accepted by Kew POWO. A Nigerian dawadawa process study identifies its substrate as *P. filicoidea*, which Kew also accepts as a separate species. Older common-name use of “African locust bean” is therefore not enough to assert one species across every dawadawa record. A 2024 Nigerian `iru` paper labels both *P. biglobosa* and *Prosopis africana* as iru in its abstract, but its Methods calls the *Prosopis* samples `Okpehe`; treat that paper as internally inconsistent evidence, not proof of synonymy. | Use *P. biglobosa* only where the product source names or reliably identifies it. Do not widen the current soumbala identity to all Parkia species or to *Prosopis* from a common name alone. |
| **`soumbara`, `daddawa`, and product-qualified spellings** | `Soumbara` is explicitly listed as a regional name in the Burkina Faso food-science literature. `Soy-daddawa` is explicitly a soybean product. Sources differ in their naming coverage and do not establish that every near spelling has the same local scope. | Retain only exact source spellings in their cited contexts. No spelling-variant alias cluster is proposed. |
| **Ogiri and ogiri-egusi** | The active `ingredient-ref-ogiri-egusi` is a Nigerian fermented melon-seed condiment. The existing `ingredient-ref-egusi-seeds` is a raw regional seed-category identity. Neither is a Parkia-seed condiment. Generic `ogiri` remains broader and unresolved. | Keep all three separate. Do not add `ogiri`, `ogiri-egusi`, `egusi`, or melon-seed names as iru/dawadawa/soumbala aliases. |
| **Groundnut paste / peanut butter** | Active `ingredient-ref-peanut-butter` describes roasted peanut paste/spread; the earlier WP06 review found that generic `groundnut paste` does not identify one uniform product state. The West African peanut-stew line is explicitly peanut butter, not locust beans. | Keep groundnut and peanut products separate from fermented Parkia seeds; no substitution or alias follows from regional use in soups. |
| **Red-palm preparations** | `ingredient-ref-crude-palm-oil` is an oil from oil-palm fruit, while iru/dawadawa/soumbala are fermented seeds. The earlier Section Z review keeps generic red-palm oil and zomi terms source-local because they do not consistently encode processing state. | Keep red-palm oil, red-palm preparations, zomi, and crude palm oil separate from the fermented-seed condiment family. |

## Measurement, process, yield, and substitution disposition

- **Density / household conversions:** none of the reviewed papers supplies a reusable bulk density or a kitchen unit mapping. Nutrient composition and lab sample values are not density evidence.
- **Process numbers:** fermentation times, cooking times, substrate ratios, temperatures, and measured food composition are source- and protocol-specific manufacturing/research data. Do not turn them into recipe measures or general product forms.
- **Yield:** an older *P. filicoidea* controlled-process paper reports 1.0 kg raw beans to 1.3 kg processed substrate and then 1.2 kg dawadawa at stated moisture levels. This is a specific mass balance for cleaned/boiled/dehulled beans fermented at 37°C in static fermenters, not a reusable household yield for *P. biglobosa*, soumbala, or unspecified dawadawa. Do not add a yield relation.
- **Conversions / substitutions:** no source compares these finished products as recipe substitutions or establishes that one can replace another across forms and dishes. Shared family membership is not substitution evidence; add no rule.
- **Recipe links:** zero current candidate rows; add none.
