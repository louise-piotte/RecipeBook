# WP06 Section 05 — bounded Oceania animal-food gap sweep

**Date:** 2026-10-12  
**Status:** Proposal-only, source-led regional sample. It does not complete the Australia, Aotearoa/New Zealand, or Pacific checklist.  
**Scope:** Meat, finfish, shellfish, other animal foods, and animal-food dish compounds only.  
**Local source IDs:** `WP06-OCEANIA-ANIMAL-20261012-S01`–`S22`.

## Result and scope limits

This packet samples Australian game meats and barramundi; Aotearoa/New Zealand hoki, green-lipped mussel, whitebait, huhu grubs, and tītī; and Fijian walu as named in a kokoda recipe. It identifies eight source-supported identity candidates, three unresolved product scopes, two context-sensitive bare-alias holds, and two existing-owner or prior-proposal handoffs. It makes no canonical edits and proposes no recipe links, forms, measurements, densities, conversions, yields, or substitutions.

The regional plan item remains open. Eggs and dairy are still in scope but are not meaningfully sampled here; no finding is made that region-specific egg or dairy products do not exist. Broader Australian First Nations animal foods, Aotearoa/New Zealand species and customary foods, other Pacific island cuisines, and product-specific Québec French terminology remain explicit follow-up work.

## Measurements, forms, and substitutions

No bundled row names any candidate, so no form, measurement, recipe link, package yield, conversion, or substitution can be attached to a live recipe. FSANZ’s emu/kangaroo/crocodile cuts are sample descriptions. Fiji Tourism’s `150 g fresh Spanish mackerel (walu)` belongs to its one kokoda formula. The source also says snapper or wahoo may be used in that kokoda recipe; it does not establish a general substitution for other fish recipes. Add no forms or substitution rules from these observations.

The green-lipped mussel and hoki sources identify products/species but provide no recipe-linked kitchen measures. Whitebait’s source-specific fritter context does not justify a generic fish, egg, or flour conversion. Te Ara’s varied tītī preservation methods are distinct source products, not automatic catalog forms or cooking directions.

## Coverage reached and remaining misses

This is a bounded animal-food sample, not a full regional cuisine inventory. It reaches Australian commercial game-meat examples and one fish species; an Aotearoa/New Zealand named fish, shellfish, whitebait product class, and Māori foods; and one Fijian fish name from a dish recipe. The scan of all 114 recipes found no exact term demand for any of these candidates.

Still open: Australia’s other native and commercial game species, species-qualified seafood, eggs, dairy, and regional animal products; Aotearoa/New Zealand regional fish and shellfish beyond hoki and green-lipped mussel, further customary foods and preserved seafood, eggs and dairy; Fiji beyond walu/kokoda; and animal foods from Samoa, Tonga, Cook Islands, Tokelau, Niue, Tuvalu, Vanuatu, Solomon Islands, Papua New Guinea, Kiribati, French Polynesia, New Caledonia, and other Pacific communities. This packet did not source-review regional egg or dairy names. The overall WP06 Section 05 item must remain unchecked.
