# WP04 Section R — Plant dairy replacements and egg replacer boundaries

- **Review date:** 2026-10-12
- **Status:** Proposal only; no canonical or shared integration files changed.
- **Scope:** Plant cream and whipping alternatives, plant yogurt, butter/spread alternatives, cheese alternatives, and egg replacers. Plant beverages and coconut product boundaries are excluded because they were reviewed and integrated separately.

Source IDs in this review are local to this proposal and have not been added to the shared research ledger.

## Recommendation summary

- Consider a bounded, identity-only `plant-based yogurt substitute / substitut de yogourt végétalien` candidate under `DAIRY_AND_ALTERNATIVE` if catalog coverage is desired. OQLF supplies current Québec terminology and defines the product as fermented and made only from plant ingredients. Keep it separate from the active plain and Greek-style dairy yogurts and from plant beverages. The corpus has no exact recipe line for it.
- Consider a bounded, identity-only `vegan cheese substitute / substitut de fromage végétalien` candidate. OQLF defines this narrower class as made exclusively from plant or fungal ingredients and lists English `plant-based cheese substitute` and `plant-based cheese alternative`. Do not alias unqualified cheese/fromage, `fauxmage`, or generic dairy cheese identities to it. The corpus has no exact recipe line for it.
- Hold generic plant cream, whipping alternative, plant butter, and plant spread identities. Current Canadian products use product-specific labels and multi-ingredient formulas; plant cream and butter wording alone does not establish one composition, function, or Québec bilingual common name. Keep any future exact product row tied to its package name and intended use.
- Keep the six existing margarine recipe links as margarine. Canadian standards allow margarine to contain milk-derived ingredients, and a current Canadian plant-butter maker distinguishes its plant butter from its own margarine. Add no `plant butter`, `vegan butter`, or dairy-free aliases to margarine or dairy butter.
- Keep the legacy `egg substitute` candidate unresolved. CFIA's general common-name guidance uses that phrase for an egg-white product with added ingredients, while a Canadian retail baking powder calls itself a vegan egg replacer. Neither wording is a universal identity for aquafaba, flax or chia mixtures, fruit purées, or every formulated alternative. There is no recipe demand.
- Add no recipe links, forms, measurements, conversions, yields, or substitution rules. Brand-specific usage claims, including manufacturer-stated 1:1 replacements, do not define category-wide behavior.

## Findings and recommendations

| Product boundary | Evidence-supported naming and composition limit | Recommendation and alias boundary | Recipe link |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Plant yogurt substitute** | OQLF's 2025 entry prefers `substitut de yogourt végétalien` and `substitut de yogourt végétal`, with English `plant-based yogurt substitute`, `plant-based yogurt analog/analogue`, and `vegan yogurt substitute`. It defines the product as a fermented food imitating yogurt and made exclusively from plant-origin ingredients. OQLF notes that `yogourt végétal/végétalien` and `plant-based/vegan yogurt` are used in some contexts, but commercial use of unqualified yogurt wording may be restricted. [S01] | **Candidate if coverage is accepted:** `plant-based yogurt substitute / substitut de yogourt végétalien`, category `DAIRY_AND_ALTERNATIVE`. If aliases are needed, limit them to OQLF's preferred substitute terms. Hold the contextual terms `vegan yogurt`, `non-dairy yogurt`, and `yogourt végétal` from automatic aliases pending label-specific evidence. Keep separate from dairy plain and Greek-style yogurt, plant beverages, and natural soy/oat ingredients. No plant source, sweetening level, Greek-style qualifier, recipe behavior, or form is inferred. | None. |
| **Vegan cheese substitute** | OQLF's 2025 entry defines `substitut de fromage végétalien` as a cheese substitute made exclusively from plant or fungal ingredients. It lists English `vegan cheese substitute`, `plant-based cheese substitute`, and `plant-based cheese alternative`. OQLF notes that bare `fromage/cheese` may face Canadian commercial-label restrictions and that `fauxmage`-type forms are not commonly used in commercial Québec labels. Its broader `substitut de fromage` entry also covers products where milk or milk components are only partly replaced. [S02–S03] | **Candidate if coverage is accepted:** `vegan cheese substitute / substitut de fromage végétalien`, under `DAIRY_AND_ALTERNATIVE`. The two OQLF-listed English substitute terms are bounded alias candidates. Keep `vegan cheese`, `plant-based cheese`, `fauxmage`, and bare `cheese/fromage` out of aliases; the source treats them as contextual terms or warns of labeling confusion. Restrict this candidate to products with evidence of exclusively plant/fungal ingredients. Do not merge with conventional cheese, cream cheese, nut/seed butter, or spread references. A product labelled only `dairy-free` needs its own composition review before classification as vegan. | None. |
| **Plant cream / whipping alternative** | Current Canadian Violife pages use `Dairy Free Whipping Cream / Crème à fouetter sans produits laitiers` for one named product. Its displayed ingredients include lentil preparation/protein, coconut-and-canola oil blend, sugar, starch, emulsifiers, and gums. Its manufacturer-specific whipping/cooking claims describe that product, not all plant creams. [S07] | **Hold a generic plant-cream identity and aliases** such as `plant cream`, `vegan cream`, `non-dairy cream`, or `crème végétale`. No OQLF product-class entry was located in this review. A future exact recipe or SKU can use a source-backed product label; distinguish whipping alternatives, cooking alternatives, creamers, whipped toppings, and natural coconut products by their exact name and use. Do not infer a general formula or common behavior from one brand. | None. |
| **Plant butter and other spreads** | Becel's current Canadian English/French pages use `Plant Butter / beurre végétal sans produits laitiers` for named SKUs and list a multi-ingredient oil/protein formula. The maker explicitly distinguishes the taste and consistency of its Plant Butter from its own margarine. The CFIA standard defines margarine as a water-in-fat emulsion using fats/oils not derived from milk, but it permits skim-milk powder, buttermilk, whey solids, and protein. [S04–S06] | **Hold a generic plant-butter or plant-spread identity.** Do not alias the Becel product name or `beurre végétal` to `margarine`, the active `butter / beurre` identity, or natural nut/seed butters. Keep unqualified recipe `margarine` on its existing reference; the standard does not make margarine equivalent to vegan or dairy-free margarine. Do not use broad `plant spread` because it can include natural nut/seed butters and other formulated spreads. | None beyond the six existing margarine links. |
| **Plant cream-cheese spread** | Daiya's Canadian English product page presents `Dairy-Free Original Cream Cheese`; its French page title is `Tartinade onctueuse nature` while its description uses `fromage à la crème sans produits laitiers`. Both are branded formulated products. Their exact product names and ingredient-list presentations are not a standardized bilingual term pair. [S08] | **Hold a separate generic cream-cheese replacement identity and aliases** until an exact recipe or product is in scope. It may be a product-specific member of the broader cheese-substitute family when its ingredients meet that definition, but do not add `cream cheese`, `fromage à la crème`, or `tartinade` as aliases to a general vegan cheese reference. Follow the exact product label and assess baking, spreading, or cooking claims for that SKU only. | None. |
| **Egg replacer** | CFIA's current common-name guidance says liquid egg whites with added ingredients that match the nutritional qualities of shell eggs can be described as `egg substitute`. This is distinct from plant-based alternatives. CFIA's plant-egg-alternative page is explicitly **proposed guidance** from a closed consultation; it recommends that a common name describe the food and make clear that it is not an egg product. Bob's Red Mill Canada currently titles one baking powder `Succédané d’œuf sans gluten` and describes it as a vegan egg substitute for baking; its English product page calls it `Gluten Free Vegan Egg Replacer`. Its starch/psyllium formula is product-specific and it does not perform all egg roles. [S09–S11] | **Keep legacy `ingredient-ref-egg-substitute` held.** Do not promote its generic English label or malformed French `substitut d œuf` as a universal product identity. The manufacturer's bilingual names may support that exact SKU if a recipe later requests it; they do not establish a general formula or all-purpose equivalence. Keep formulated replacers separate from aquafaba, flax/water or chia/water preparations, and fruit purées. Do not add an egg alias, replacement link, or ratio. | None. |
| **Natural plant ingredients and recipe preparations** | The active catalog separately holds ingredients such as flax seeds, chia seeds, aquafaba-related pulse products, and nut/seed butters. Some can be used in particular food preparations, but ingredient identity is not the same as a formulated replacement product. WP08's existing aquafaba observation is bounded to selected baked goods and remains unavailable for automatic use. [S12] | Keep these natural ingredients under their own identities. Do not treat `flax egg`, `chia egg`, or aquafaba as aliases or forms of egg, or merge plant ingredients with plant-based dairy products because a manufacturer uses them in a formula. Any future substitution requires the exact dish role, source, quantity basis, and rule-model support. | None. |

No forms, measurements, unit mappings, yields, conversions, or substitutions are proposed. Manufacturer statements that one named product can replace one named dairy product in certain recipes remain product-specific claims, not global ingredient rules. Product formulas and allergens vary; do not store an allergen-free, vegan, or dairy-free property on a broad identity from one SKU's label.

## Unresolved cases and limits

1. The corpus has no exact recipe demand for any plant cream/yogurt/butter/cheese alternative or egg replacer. The two OQLF-bounded candidates are identity-coverage proposals only.
2. This source search did not locate a current OQLF entry for a general plant cream or plant butter product class. That does not establish absence of market use; current products use specific marketed names and formulas.
3. `Plant cream`, `whipping alternative`, and `cream-style spread` may refer to products with different bases and uses. A matching exact recipe line, retailer listing, package label, and intended function are needed before assigning aliases or behavior.
4. The Becel source demonstrates a named plant-butter line distinct from its own margarine; it does not establish that all market plant butters are compositionally identical or that all margarines contain dairy. The CFIA standard only allows the listed milk ingredients; it does not require them in every margarine.
5. OQLF's term `substitut de fromage végétalien` has the tight exclusive plant/fungal definition. The broader cheese-substitute class can include partial milk replacement, so do not classify a product solely from the words `alternative` or `dairy-free` without its label.
6. Daiya's English and French product pages use different front-facing names and different ingredient-list presentations at access time. Verify the retail package before attaching a SKU-specific bilingual identity or composition claim.
7. The CFIA egg-alternative page is proposed guidance, not a final binding standard. Use it only as label-description context. The current generic legacy `egg substitute` could also mean a product containing egg whites.
8. The old WP08 aquafaba finding does not supply an accepted egg-replacer identity or an active substitution rule; the runtime cannot enforce the source's count-to-volume basis and selected recipe-function scope.
9. No numeric behavior or broad allergen property is established for any product class. No tests or emulator checks were run for this proposal-only documentation task.
