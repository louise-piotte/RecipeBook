## Recommendation

Propose an identity-only generic **nukazuke (vegetables pickled in rice bran)** reference. The Japan Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries (MAFF) names `nuka-zuke` as a typical pickle and classifies vegetables pickled in rice bran separately from vegetables pickled in vinegar, soy sauce, sake lees, or miso. A primary study defines `nukazuke` as the pickled vegetables and `nukadoko` as the fermented rice-bran bed used to make them. Together, these sources support a generic vegetable-preserve identity; they do not support collapsing the bed ingredient, rice bran, or every regional rice-bran pickle into one formula. [S01–S02]

Use **`nukazuke (vegetables pickled in rice bran)`** as the English display. For fr-CA, use the provisional descriptive display **`nukazuke (légumes fermentés au son de riz)`**. No Québec authority or reviewed French-language source standardizes this preserve name, so keep the borrowed term and descriptive French wording provisional and add no French alias. Add no aliases overall; in particular, do not add `nukadoko`, `rice bran`, `takuan`, `pickled radish`, or broad `Japanese pickles` as aliases.

Scope the candidate to **vegetables** pickled in fermented rice bran. MAFF’s broader wording can include foodstuffs beyond vegetables, and rice-bran-pickled fish is separately documented; do not use this row for fish, meat, tofu, or the pickling bed itself. Keep the active `takuan` row as a more specifically named daikon pickle even though MAFF includes it in the rice-bran-vegetable category. No rows are changed or merged.

If accepted for later integration, add one identity-only `SAUCE_AND_CONDIMENT` reference, with no aliases, forms, density, unit mappings, reusable measures, recipe links, conversions, yields, or active substitutions. Keep laboratory results, vegetable-specific observations, and all preparation numbers within their exact source contexts.

## Identity and bilingual boundary

MAFF’s overview identifies `nuka-zuke` as a pickle made by placing foodstuffs in rice bran and separately lists **vegetables pickled in rice bran** as a category. It places takuan and other named foods in that category. It also classifies vinegar-, soy-sauce-, sake-lees-, miso-, salt-, and rice-bran-pickled vegetables separately. This supports the generic method-based preserve class and its separation from the already modeled neighboring pickle methods. MAFF is a descriptive government overview, not a legally binding ingredient standard or a Québec naming authority. [S01]

Sugiura et al. distinguish `nukadoko`, the fermented rice-bran bed, from `nukazuke`, the vegetables pickled in that bed. Their study used cucumber, cabbage, and Japanese white radish, supporting that the culinary identity applies across different vegetable substrates. The authors’ affiliations include both a pickle-industry research institute and Kyushu University; this review uses their multi-vegetable identity boundary, not a named commercial product or a transferable product claim. The study’s microbiota and processing results remain sample-specific. [S02]

The proposed English display retains the source-attested Japanese food name but clarifies its vegetable scope. The fr-CA parenthetical is a transparent description, not an official term or alias. Keep `nukadoko` separate from `nukazuke`; keep `rice bran` separate as an input ingredient; and do not merge `takuan` or any other named preserve into this generic row. Rice-bran-pickled fish and roe are also excluded from the vegetable-only identity.

## Numeric, form, and substitution evidence limits

No reviewed source supports a transferable household measure, mass-per-unit value, density, unit mapping, conversion, finished-food yield, serving amount, or universal salt concentration for nukazuke vegetables. Sugiura et al. prepared one experimental bed with **1,800 g** of raw or roasted rice bran, **2 L** of water, and **200 g** of salt, then fermented it at **24 °C for 40 days** before pickling the study vegetables. These are that study’s experimental conditions for the `nukadoko` bed, not a home formula, vegetable amount, catalog measure, or product-wide process. The paper also reports a **120 °C / 2-hour** roasting treatment for its roasted bran; that preparation is likewise study-specific. Do not calculate or store a generic concentration from these sample values. [S02]

MAFF gives a general discussion of salting vegetables for pickles, including a broad salt-concentration statement, but does not assign that number specifically to nukazuke. Do not transfer it to this identity. The MAFF source’s named takuan and regional pickle descriptions remain scoped to those products. [S01]

No form is established beyond the broad class of vegetables pickled in rice bran. Do not infer universal whole, cut, drained, cooked, dried, packaged, toasted-bran, or matured forms. Use `defaultDensity: null` and `unitMappings: []`; add no forms.

No reviewed source recommends replacing nukazuke vegetables in a specified dish, role, method, and amount. Keep `substitutionResearchState: blocked_pending_evidence` and add no active contextual or global substitution. Do not nominate takuan, pickled radish, pickled mustard greens, nanohana-zuke, nozawana-zuke, narazuke, fukujinzuke, pickled fish, fresh vegetables, or rice bran as a replacement based only on shared substrate or preservation language.

If integrated, use `identityState: verified`, `measurementState: researched_no_supported_value`, and `substitutionResearchState: blocked_pending_evidence`. The current bundled recipes supply no line-specific substitution evidence.
