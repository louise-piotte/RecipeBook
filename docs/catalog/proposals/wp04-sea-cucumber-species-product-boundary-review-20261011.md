- **Packet type:** identity and boundary review, integrated as a held research-ledger candidate.
- **Research date:** 2026-10-11.
- **Scope:** the open Section P sea-cucumber item; Québec/Canadian names, species scope, Canadian product examples, recipe demand, and identity-versus-measurement boundaries.
- **Recommendation:** hold an active generic `sea cucumber / concombre de mer` reference, aliases, forms, and recipe links until a recipe or product import identifies the intended species and edible product part. ## Recommendation and contingent names

Do not add a generic active reference, aliases, forms, or recipe link now. The OQLF GDT entry places `concombre de mer` in the food/fishery-products domain, pairs it with English `sea cucumber`, and lists `bêche-de-mer`, `bêche de mer`, and `biche-de-mer` as preferred French terms. It also says the common name is used for several holothurian species. CFIA’s Fish List likewise assigns “sea cucumber” and `concombre de mer` to multiple species and families. The broad name is valid terminology, but the evidence does not identify one species or a single processed ingredient product. [S01, S03]

For a future recipe or import that specifically identifies the East Coast species *Cucumaria frondosa*, current OQLF terminology provides a more bounded Québec/Canadian naming candidate: **orange-footed sea cucumber / holothurie touffue**, with **North Atlantic sea cucumber / concombre de mer du Nord** also listed. Seafood from Canada independently describes a Canadian East Coast sea-cucumber product as *Cucumaria frondosa*. These sources support reviewing that species-specific candidate when requested; they do not make its name an alias for every sea cucumber. [S02, S04]

Do not add the following as global aliases without a matching recipe or package: `bêche-de-mer`, `bêche de mer`, `biche-de-mer`, `sea slug`, `trepang`, `sea cucumber skin`, or `sea cucumber meat`. OQLF includes several broad or alternate-language terms under the generic entry, while Fisheries and Oceans Canada documents “dried skin” and “frozen muscle strips” as separate products for its British Columbia species. Those product parts and names should not be collapsed into one global alias group. [S01, S05]

The dynamic CFIA Fish List includes both an *Apostichopus californicus* entry (`California Sea Cucumber / Holothurie de Californie`) and a *Parastichopus californicus* entry (`Sea Cucumber / Holothurie`, with a separate `California Sea Cucumber / Concombre De Mer` row). The current DFO profile uses *Apostichopus californicus* for Giant Red Sea Cucumber. Preserve those source-scoped names; do not turn the genus spellings or species-associated common names into aliases on a broad ingredient row. This report does not make a taxonomic ruling. [S03, S05, S06]

### Measurement, conversion, yield, and substitutions

No reviewed source supports a household measure, density, unit mapping, count weight, package yield, edible-portion factor, drying/soaking yield, or cooking yield for a recipe-compatible product. The Seafood from Canada page’s nutrition values “per 100 grams” are a nutrient-reporting basis, not a volume mapping or serving weight. The DFO split-pound commercial catch basis and its processing descriptions are fishery/product information, not recipe measures. [S04–S06]

Activate no density, unit mapping, package conversion, raw/frozen/dried/reconstituted form rule, or yield. In particular, do not infer the dry-to-rehydrated ratio from “dried is more commonly used,” or infer edible yield from net package mass. Keep any future observation tied to its exact species, part, preservation/preparation state, product/brand, market, original quantity, and source method.

No global or contextual substitution is proposed. The available evidence does not establish equivalence among species, dried skin, frozen muscle, whole animal, other sea cucumbers, abalone, sea urchin, mollusks, or generic seafood. Similar culinary category or shared market naming is not evidence of an interchangeable amount, cooking result, safety, or recipe role.

## Proposal-only disposition

1. Hold generic `sea cucumber / concombre de mer` from the active reference catalog until a recipe or imported product names the species and intended edible product.
2. Do not add `bêche-de-mer` variants, `sea slug`, `trepang`, species names, or processed-part names as aliases on a generic row.
3. If a future request identifies *Cucumaria frondosa*, reopen with the species-scoped candidate `orange-footed sea cucumber / holothurie touffue` or `North Atlantic sea cucumber / concombre de mer du Nord`; verify the exact product wording before selecting display and aliases.
4. Keep frozen muscle, dried skin, whole animal, dried, and rehydrated products distinct until a recipe/package defines the intended part and state. Add no forms, measures, density, conversions, package/edible yield, or substitutions from this review.
5. Leave WP04 Section P open.
