# WP02 Malt Extract and Diastatic Malt Flour — Coordinator Recheck

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-09
- **Purpose:** Independent recheck of [`wp02-malt-extract-diastatic-boundary-20261009.md`](wp02-malt-extract-diastatic-boundary-20261009.md) against the current package, recipe corpus, catalog taxonomy, and source wording.
- **Disposition:** Accept the current identity separations and the proposal's no-alias/no-measurement/no-substitution boundary. Defer new catalog rows, forms, and Section E closure: no bundled recipe currently uses these products. Two points need explicit coordinator judgment before any row is created: the proposed malt-extract category, and whether enzyme activity should be represented as a form or product-qualified identity. The extract activity claim is supported only at the product/process level, not as a universal state of commercial extracts.
- **Plan:** Section E item 1113, `Diastatic versus non-diastatic malt, malt extract, and composition-known dough improvers`, remains open.

## Claim-by-claim decision

| Proposal claim | Recheck | Coordinator recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keep malted barley flour / `farine d'orge maltée` as a separate identity with EN alias `malt barley flour`. | **Supported.** The live row matches exactly. OQLF pairs its preferred French name with the English term `malt barley flour`; CFIA separately lists malted barley flour and malted wheat flour in its flour standard; an Ontario mill describes barley germinated, dried, and ground as its malted barley flour. | **Accept existing row unchanged.** Preserve its sole English alias and identity-only data. Do not broaden to `malt flour`, `malt powder`, extract, or syrup. |
| Keep malted wheat flour / `farine de blé maltée` distinct, with no aliases. | **Supported.** The current row is separate and alias-free. CFIA distinguishes malted wheat from malted barley flour; the existing WP02 identity record documents the Government of Canada terminology pairing and milling-product evidence. | **Accept existing row unchanged.** No alias, numeric data, or form is warranted by this recheck. |
| Keep roasted wheat malt flour / `farine de malt de blé torréfié` separate and alias-free. | **Supported with a narrow limit.** The live row is separate. The bilingual Diversi Foods declaration lists wheat malt flour and roasted wheat malt flour as separate ingredients; it does not establish the latter's detailed process. | **Accept existing row unchanged.** Do not infer a roasting process, broad `malt flour` alias, measurement, or substitution. |
| Diastatic and nondiastatic malted flour are product/activity distinctions, not global synonyms. | **Supported.** Briess describes enzyme-active diastatic and enzyme-inactivated nondiastatic flour products, and gives cereal-specific wheat/rye examples as well as barley. OQLF's technical entry supports `pouvoir diastasique` as a term, but does not standardize the proposed full product labels. | **Accept the boundary; defer forms.** Preserve an explicitly sourced activity qualifier when a recipe names one. The proposed French phrases remain provisional pending Québec review. Before using `IngredientForm`, check whether enzyme activity fits that state model; it is a functional property, not plainly a preparation state. Do not infer activity from `malted flour` alone. |
| Candidate generic identity `malt extract` / `extrait de malt`, ID `ingredient-ref-malt-extract`. | **Name pair supported; creation deferred.** OQLF (2019) identifies `extrait de malt` as preferred and gives English `malt extract`; it describes a malt-wort concentrate and liquid or powder presentation. Briess describes a soluble concentrated extract from malted grain, distinct from milled flour. But the OQLF entry is in food chemistry/brewing, no recipe requires the identity, and Briess says the unqualified name refers to barley under U.S. CFR convention while other cereal extracts are qualified. Canadian bread standards list names but do not define the source-grain convention. | **Defer active row creation.** If a recipe later requires a generic row, the bilingual name and empty aliases are supported. Record source cereal in the exact product/line wording when known; do not treat wheat or other cereal extracts as aliases for a barley product. The candidate ID remains unallocated. |
| Candidate extract category `BAKING_MIXIN_AND_PANTRY`. | **Valid taxonomy value; source-neutral category is not established.** The enum exists. Briess calls extracts nutritive sweeteners used for yeast food, browning, flavor, and color. Current syrup references are classified as `SUGAR_AND_SWEETENER`, while baker's yeast is `BAKING_MIXIN_AND_PANTRY`. | **Defer category assignment until row creation.** The proposal's value is plausible, but coordinator should decide whether the identity follows its primary sweetener use or its baking-ingredient role. Do not report this category as a sourced fact. |
| Malt extract can be liquid or dry/powder; activity is a separate qualifier. | **Supported at the presentation/product level; activity needs tighter wording.** OQLF says liquid or powder. Briess lists liquid and dry/powder product examples. Its technical page says adjusted extraction can retain alpha-amylase and make diastatic malt extract possible; a Briess product sheet describes its listed extracts as nondiastatic. Those statements support product-specific activity, not a universal set of extract forms or the claim that all commercial extracts include both states. | **Accept the no-forms-now boundary.** If a recipe later needs forms, retain exact source wording (`liquid`, `dry`, or `powder`) and French labels such as `liquide`/`en poudre` with Québec review. Do not add liquid-to-powder conversion or infer activity. |
| Do not make `malt syrup` / `sirop de malt` a global alias for malt extract. | **French name supported; equivalence unresolved.** OQLF's 1964 entry gives `sirop de malt` as the preferred French term and `malt syrup` as English. CFIA lists malt syrup, malt extract, and malt flour separately as permitted bread ingredients but does not define equivalence. The 1996 FDA guide records historical interchangeability and a U.S. barley-only convention; Briess also repeats a U.S. CFR convention that 100% barley extract can be called malt syrup. These are not Canadian definitions. | **Accept the bilingual name pair; defer identity/alias mapping.** Keep both phrases unassigned pending an exact Canadian product label and composition evidence. Do not add a cross-alias from U.S. guidance alone. |
| Do not add `malt powder` as an alias. | **Supported; add one ambiguity to the record.** There are no active hits. Briess uses powder for malt extract products, while OQLF and the flour sources distinguish powdered extract from ground malted flour. Briess also describes malted milk powder as a compound product, another possible reading of a loose `malt powder` phrase. | **Accept no alias.** Keep exact product wording. Do not merge malted milk powder into pure malt extract or flour if it appears later. |
| Keep generic dough/bread improver out of malt/extract aliases and defer a general identity. | **Supported.** Current catalog and recipes have no hits. Puratos Canada describes complete multi-use and modular improver products; the Québec Food Directory example separately lists malted barley flour and a dough improver composed of enzyme and ascorbic acid. OQLF's bread-improver entry dates to 1987 and describes oxidizing flour-maturation agents, so it is not a complete current product definition. | **Accept no generic alias or identity.** A named, composition-known improver can be reviewed as a separate compound/product only if an exact recipe and ingredient declaration support it. Current supplier French `améliorant à pain` is evidence of usage, not a universal composition or catalog synonym. |
| Do not add density, kitchen measures, unit mappings, activity-dose defaults, liquid/dry conversions, or substitutions. | **Supported.** None of the current product identities has numeric catalog data or forms. Sources give manufacturer-specific Lintner values, use rates, packaging, or product composition; none establishes a general kitchen conversion, density, or replacement ratio. | **Accept no numeric data and no substitution rules.** Revisit only for a named product, measurement method, and recipe context. |

## Sources checked and limits

1. [OQLF, `extrait de malt` (GDT, updated 2019)](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/2086260/extrait-de-malt): preferred French term, English equivalent, malt-wort definition, liquid/powder presentation. Brewing/food-chemistry context; no baking dose, source-grain rule, or enzyme threshold.
2. [CFIA, Canadian Food Compositional Standards](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/about-cfia/acts-and-regulations/list-acts-and-regulations/documents-incorporated-reference/canadian-food-compositional-standards-0), Volume 12, sections 12.1 and 12.2.1(f): names malted barley/wheat flour separately and lists malt syrup, malt extract, and malt flour in standardized bread. It does not define extract/syrup as synonyms or specify their equivalence.
3. [CFIA, Food composition and quality claims](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/food-labels/labelling/industry/composition-and-quality), “malted”: adding malt extract alone does not make a food malted; the carbohydrate must have been modified by malt diastase. This is labeling-claim guidance, not a catalog rule or enzyme-activity threshold.
4. [OQLF, `farine d'orge maltée` (GDT)](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/2497648/farine-dorge-maltee): French preferred term and English `malt barley flour`; the entry is old and is used here for terminology, not processing or product behavior.
5. [OQLF, `pouvoir diastasique` (GDT)](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/2088387/pouvoir-diastasique): French technical term for diastatic power. It does not certify the proposal's full French labels for specific flours.
6. [OQLF, `sirop de malt` (GDT, 1964)](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/10491445/sirop-de-malt): preferred French term and English `malt syrup`; the entry supplies terminology but not a definition or equivalence to malt extract.
7. [OQLF, `améliorant de la fermentation` (GDT, 1987)](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/2297239/ameliorant-de-la-fermentation): preferred terms include `améliorant de la fermentation` and `améliorant de la panification`; its note focuses on oxidizing flour-maturation agents. Treat as historical terminology, not a modern compound-product definition.
8. [U.S. FDA, CPG 515.200 (August 1996)](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/cpg-sec-515200-malt-extract-malt-syrup-malted-cereal-syrup-liquid-malt-dried-malt): historical U.S. policy says unqualified extract/syrup applies to barley and records occasional interchangeability. It cites historical correspondence; it is not a current Canadian standard.
9. [Briess, Malting & Roasting](https://food.briess.com/capabilities/malting/): activity distinction for malt/flour, other cereal examples, extract cereal qualifiers, and the possibility of diastatic extract when extraction preserves alpha-amylase. This is manufacturer process and product guidance, not a universal formulation or Canadian labeling rule.
10. [Briess, What is Malt Extract?](https://food.briess.com/capabilities/extraction-drying/): soluble concentrated extract, liquid or powder output, source-grain qualification, and coextract boundaries. Manufacturer description; not a general dose or conversion.
11. [Briess, Malt Extracts](https://food.briess.com/products/natural-malt-sweeteners/) and its [product list](https://food.briess.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Briess_ProductList_MaltExtractsMMP.pdf): product examples in liquid, dry, and powder forms; the product sheet describes its listed extracts as nondiastatic. These are supplier-specific products and the sheet's footer identifies a 2021 revision.
12. [Briess, Malted Flours](https://food.briess.com/products/briess-specialty-malt-flours/): product examples distinguish diastatic and nondiastatic flours and list composition/activity for named Maltorose blends. Brand-specific product facts; no general bakery formula or home dose is imported.
13. [Puratos Canada, `Améliorants à pain`](https://www.puratos.ca/fr/boulangerie/categories/bread-improvers): current Québec supplier wording and a portfolio described as complete multi-use and modular solutions. Does not define one universal composition.
14. [Québec Food Directory, Simply Grain Bread Mix White](https://www.repertoirealimentsquebecois.gouv.qc.ca/Produit/Index/8201535): one ingredient declaration lists malted barley flour separately from `dough improver (enzyme, ascorbic acid)`. The agency page says the product information is company-supplied and MAPAQ is not responsible for its accuracy; it does not disclose proportions.
15. Existing name support and limits for malted wheat and roasted wheat malt flour are recorded in [`malted-wheat-flour-identity.md`](../batches/malted-wheat-flour-identity.md) and [`roasted-wheat-flours-identity.md`](../batches/roasted-wheat-flours-identity.md); the latter relies on a bilingual manufacturer ingredient declaration that distinguishes the two named flours but does not document process details.

## Coordinator recommendation

1. Accept and preserve the three current flour rows exactly as audited; do not duplicate them or add broad malt aliases.
2. Defer a new malt-extract row until a bundled recipe or exact source product requires it. The proposed display pair is supported; keep aliases, density, mappings, forms, and substitutions empty if a row is later authorized. Decide the category at that time; `BAKING_MIXIN_AND_PANTRY` is a valid but not source-determined choice, and current syrups use `SUGAR_AND_SWEETENER`.
3. Keep `malt syrup`, `sirop de malt`, `malt powder`, cereal-qualified extracts, and malted-milk powder separate or unresolved unless a specific label and recipe establish the intended product. Do not use the U.S. barley naming convention as a Canadian rule.
4. Preserve diastatic/nondiastatic activity only when explicitly documented. Defer form modeling and the proposed French flour labels pending a recipe need, data-model fit review, and Québec French review.
5. Keep improver products unresolved at family level. If one appears in a recipe, retain its exact product/composition and classify it independently; do not alias it to malt.
6. Keep all measurement, conversion, dose, yield, density, and substitution fields empty. Section E line 1113 stays open; this recheck does not complete wider Section E work.

No catalog, ledger, plan, workflow, recipe, code, test, or generated file was changed for this recheck.
