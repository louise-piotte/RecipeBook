## Recommendation

Accept a distinct source-named culinary identity for rocoto, proposed ID *ingredient-ref-rocoto-pepper*, with English display **rocoto pepper**, provisional Québec French display **piment rocoto**, and category *VEGETABLE_AND_AROMATIC*. For Peruvian cultivation and food use, the botanical evidence consistently associates rocoto with *Capsicum pubescens*. Record that as a source-supported taxonomic note; keep the catalog display name culinary and do not imply that every regional Capsicum name denotes rocoto. Kew accepts *C. pubescens* as a species native to Peru and Bolivia and records it as used for food; INIA and PROMPERÚ explicitly connect Peruvian rocoto to this species. [S01–S04]

This identity is distinct from generic pepper, red pepper, chili/ají, and other named Capsicum products. PROMPERÚ profiles rocoto separately from ají amarillo (*C. baccatum*), ají limo (*C. chinense*), and charapita (*C. frutescens*). Another Peruvian source says that Andean usage distinguishes the specific name ro’qote/rokoto for rocoto or locoto from uchu, a broader term for other pepper groups. These taxonomic and culinary boundaries are strong enough for a separate row. [S03, S06]

## Identity, aliases, and unsafe generalizations

The safe alias set should be limited to complete names that the reviewed Peruvian sources directly map to *C. pubescens*: *locoto*, *rokoto*, *ro’qote*, *chile manzano*, *chile de cera*, and *chile perón*. INIA’s 2023 agronomic manual explicitly says rocoto (*C. pubescens*) is also known as chile manzano, chile de cera, locoto, or perón. PROMPERÚ’s bilingual resource ties ro’qote/rokoto and locoto specifically to rocoto (*C. pubescens*) in its account of Andean terminology, and separately lists chile manzano, chile perón, and locoto under its rocoto profile. Preserve these as source-backed names, not as proof that the terms are universal across all Spanish-speaking regions. [S02, S06]

Use only the complete forms *chile manzano*, *chile de cera*, and *chile perón*; do not add bare *manzano*, *cera*, or *perón*. The bare terms are less specific. *Locoto*, *rokoto*, and *ro’qote* are accepted only as the exact source-named regional spellings. If a future catalog model can attach language/geographic scope to aliases, retain the cited regional notes there; the current alias fields have no scope metadata.

The PROMPERÚ 2016 rocoto profile describes its sample as red, but the bilingual 2015 resource distinguishes both red and yellow rocoto varieties. Therefore, red is not a defining identity condition; neither *red pepper* nor *ají rojo* is a safe synonym. [S03, S06]

## Source-grounded evidence

Kew’s Plants of the World Online accepts *Capsicum pubescens* Ruiz & Pav. and gives Peru–Bolivia as its native range; its overview says the species is used for food. INIA’s agronomic manual and PROMPERÚ’s Peruvian pepper profile call rocoto *C. pubescens*. This convergent botanical and crop evidence supports recording the species as an identity note for the source-named Peruvian food. [S01–S04]

PROMPERÚ’s 2016 product profile describes rocoto as used fresh in dressings and sauces and sold natural, as paste, and as powder. Its export list names fresh, whole dehydrated, powder, canned/preserved, and paste presentations. This is useful market and product evidence; it is not a processing standard or a recipe for those presentations. The Spanish and English profiles give separate entries for rocoto, ají amarillo, ají limo, and charapita and associate the latter three with other species. [S03, S04]

INS’s 2023 food tables distinguish “Rocoto fresco” (code B76) from “Rocoto fresco, molido con cáscara, en agua sin sal” (code B117). This is direct support for a fresh rocoto product and one narrowly specified fresh-ground product. It does not make the shorter “rocoto molido” name equivalent to that food-table entry. The Ministry of Production’s *Recetario Pota mía* lists *rocoto molido* separately from *pasta de ají amarillo* in the tiradito de pota recipe, but the recipe does not define the rocoto preparation. [S05, S07]

The PROMPERÚ cultural guide distinguishes broad *uchu* from the Andean-specific *ro’qote, rokoto* for rocoto or locoto (*C. pubescens*). On the next page it distinguishes red and yellow rocoto, describes qacha as rocoto split, deseeded, and sun-dried, and defines uchu kuta as ground ají or rocoto with vegetables, salt, and a little water. These are source-specific cultural/product descriptions; do not turn generic uchu or the mixed condiment into rocoto aliases. [S06]

Peru’s MINCETUR inventory identifies rocoto relleno as a typical Arequipa gastronomic preparation and describes rocoto used as the pepper shell. This is culinary-use evidence and confirms that the dish name should not be treated as the ingredient’s identity. [S08]
