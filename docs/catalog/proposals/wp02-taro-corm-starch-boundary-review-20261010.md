## Recommendation

**Keep a distinct extracted taro-corm starch candidate on hold until a Québec/Canadian French product name is accepted.** Primary food-science sources establish that starch isolated from *Colocasia esculenta* corms is a product distinct from whole-corm flour. The current WP02 package already has separate generic taro and taro-flour references, so treating starch as another form or alias of flour would erase a real processing boundary. The evidence does not establish a current Québec food label for the extracted starch.

The English phrase `taro starch` is used for the isolated product in primary research. OQLF identifies `taro` as the Québec French name for the plant/food and explains that `fécule` is used more specifically for starch in tubers or other underground organs. A historical French report also records `fécule de Taro` for starch extracted from colocase tubers in Martinique. Those sources make `fécule de taro` a plausible descriptive draft, but none establishes a contemporary Québec label. Do not activate it or `amidon de taro` as a display or alias yet. [S01–S04]

## Identity and Section B2/B3/B9 boundaries

The candidate product is the separated starch fraction extracted from *C. esculenta* corms. Primary studies explicitly analyze taro flour and isolated starch as separate samples. A 2025 comparative study washes, peels, homogenizes corms with water or other extraction liquids, separates and washes settled granules, then dries and powders the starch. It also studies corm starch from *Colocasia*, *Xanthosoma*, *Alocasia*, and *Amorphophallus* species and reports that extraction method and botanical source affect measured properties. This supports a source-qualified identity; it does not establish a generic “aroid starch” equivalence or standard market specification. [S05–S06]

Keep the boundary narrow:

- **Separate from active `taro flour / farine de taro`:** flour is the milled whole-corm product; isolated starch has non-starch material removed during extraction. Do not map flour to starch, or starch to flour.
- **Separate from active `taro / taro`:** a fresh corm/root is not extracted starch.
- **Exclude:** taro leaves; unspecified taro powder; cooked, instant, or flavored taro powders; whole-corm flour; starch-rich corm powder that has not been isolated; and starch from *Xanthosoma*, *Alocasia*, *Amorphophallus*, or another aroid unless a future source defines that product separately.
- **Do not use as global aliases:** `dasheen starch`, `cocoyam starch`, `eddoe starch`, or `amadumbe starch`. OQLF’s `dasheen` is an English equivalent for the plant/food, while research and regional sources use overlapping aroid common names. The 2025 aroid study confirms that several genera can yield starch with materially differing properties. Keep source-specific terms attached only to a source-defined product.
- **No forms:** native, modified, pregelatinized, instant, and precooked states are not proposed as catalog forms. The studies’ extraction or laboratory treatment conditions do not define consumer product forms. No bundled line supplies a form distinction.

This is a candidate under B3’s “other regional starches” and an example for B9’s whole-root-flour versus extracted-starch boundary. The open checklist also asks for exact Québec naming. This proposal does not complete B2, B3, or B9 as a whole.

## Québec/Canadian French naming review

- OQLF’s GDT entry, last updated in 1995, lists French `taro`, `colocase`, and `colocasie`, English `dasheen`, `taro`, and `old coco-yam`, and Latin *Colocasia esculenta*. It names the plant/food, not its flour or isolated starch. [S01]
- OQLF’s `amidon` entry, updated in 2026, gives `amidon` as the preferred general term and `fécule` as a context-specific term for starch in tubers or more generally underground plant organs. This supports the construction `fécule de taro` linguistically, not as an attested product term. [S02]
- TERMIUM Plus records the same general French distinction between `fécule` for tuber/root starch and `amidon` for starch from grain. It does not provide an exact taro-starch entry. [S03]
- The 1878 international-exposition report says that starch extracted from colocase tubers was called `fécule de Taro` in Martinique. This is historical Caribbean wording, not Québec usage; the report’s botanical nomenclature is old. It is evidence that the construction has existed in French, not enough to settle the fr-CA display. [S04]
- Targeted searches of OQLF/TERMIUM and Québec/Canadian product wording for `taro starch`, `fécule de taro`, `amidon de taro`, and colocase/colocasie equivalents did not surface a current Québec terminology entry or a standalone Canadian/Québec food-product label for isolated taro-corm starch. Search results are not exhaustive; the exact local product name remains unresolved.

Do not add `colocase starch`, `colocasie starch`, `amidon de colocase`, `fécule de colocase`, `amidon de colocasie`, `fécule de colocasie`, or unqualified `arrowroot` phrases as aliases. The French names for the plant do not automatically name its isolated starch, and historic `arrow-root` wording is not a safe current crosswalk to the existing broad arrow-root starch identity.

## Measurements, forms, and substitutions

**Do not add a default density, unit mapping, measurement conversion, yield, or substitution.** Current evidence is laboratory/process-specific:

- Parmar and Roghelia (2025) report one study’s taro-root starch bulk density as `0.530 ± 0.026 g/mL` and tapped density as `0.663 ± 0.015 g/mL`. The method puts 1 g of sample in a 20 mL graduated cylinder and manually taps it 3 times for bulk density or 300 times for tapped density. The accessible source describes raw-market taro but does not establish the species; this is not a kitchen cup measure and cannot be assigned to the narrower *C. esculenta* candidate. [S07]
- The 2025 aroid comparison reports that both density values vary with starch source and extraction method. Its measurements characterize laboratory powders and material handling, not household scoop behavior. Extraction yields and solvent/process ratios likewise are not recipe conversions. [S06]
- No reviewed source provides a kitchen cup/tablespoon weight, a product-specific mass/volume conversion, or a recipe-scale yield for a Québec-market *C. esculenta* starch product. Keep all such values unavailable.
- No reviewed source tests a taro-corm starch replacement ratio or direction against cornstarch, potato starch, tapioca starch, arrowroot, taro flour, or another starch in an actual recipe. Functional-property comparisons and edible-film experiments do not justify either a general or contextual culinary substitution.

If the identity is accepted after naming review, keep aliases, forms, `defaultDensity`, unit mappings, yields, conversions, and substitution rules empty until recipe-relevant evidence is found.
