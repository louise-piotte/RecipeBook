# WP05 Section V — coffee, tea, and powdered beverage boundary review

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-12
- **Status:** Proposal only. No canonical package, ledger, legacy, plan, roadmap, workflow, or shared integration file was changed. No tests or emulator checks were run.
- **Scope:** Coffee as dry beans/ground product, brewed coffee, espresso, instant coffee, tea types, the already integrated matcha reference, cocoa beverage mixes, and malted drink powders.
- **Plan state:** Section V remains open at `Coffee beans/grounds/brewed/espresso/instant, tea types, matcha, cocoa beverages, and malted drink powder.` The matcha portion is already integrated under Section W and is not reopened here.

## Recommendation

Keep `ingredient-ref-coffee` for the brewed beverage use evidenced in the recipe corpus. Do not attach dry whole-bean or ground-coffee terms to it. If the global catalog adds dry coffee coverage, add a dry roasted-coffee identity, with whole-bean and ground as product forms, then add instant/soluble coffee as a distinct processed identity. The OQLF defines coffee separately as a seed product and as an infused beverage; this distinction is material to the current reference's ownership.

Treat espresso as a prepared beverage with an extraction method. Keep it separate from the active `espresso powder` reference, whose exact recipe line calls for a dry baking product. Do not add a brewed-espresso reference without an ingredient line or another approved catalog need. Keep hot/strong/regular coffee as recipe wording or preparation attributes, not separate identities.

For tea, source evidence supports the dry *Camellia sinensis* leaf/bud product and clearly named black and green tea products. A follow-up identity-only tranche can add `black tea / thé noir` and `green tea / thé vert` with empty aliases, or keep them as held candidates until recipe demand warrants more tea scope. Do not duplicate the already integrated `matcha / matcha` reference. Keep white and oolong tea terminology held for separate product/category evidence; do not treat herbal infusions as tea aliases.

Keep hot-chocolate/cocoa drink mixes as compound products, distinct from the existing unsweetened, natural, Dutch-process, and black cocoa powder identities. Keep malt drink mixes distinct from malted milk powder, malt extract, and malted cereal flours. Both beverage-mix families have current Québec retail naming evidence, but no matching ingredient line; defer new references until the catalog owner chooses broader identity-only coverage or a recipe names the product.

No recipe changes, links, forms, aliases, densities, kitchen measures, conversions, yields, preservation guidance, or substitutions are proposed in this review.

## Identity and form boundaries

### Coffee

OQLF has two distinct *coffee* senses: the coffee seed at several processing stages and the brewed beverage made by infusing ground coffee. The live `ingredient-ref-coffee` links only to the latter use. Keep this owner stable for the two linked beverage rows. Its broad display remains potentially ambiguous when searching for dry coffee, so a future dry-coffee reference should receive an explicit dry-product name instead of receiving aliases on this beverage owner.

If broad dry-coffee coverage is accepted, use a separate **roasted coffee / café torréfié** candidate and treat whole-bean and ground states as product forms under that dry identity: current Canadian manufacturer wording pairs `Whole Bean Coffee / Cafés en grains` and `ground coffee / cafés moulus`, and OQLF defines `café moulu / ground coffee`. Do not make `brewed coffee`, `café filtre`, or espresso forms of the dry identity. They are drinks produced by extraction. Keep `hot`, `strong`, or `regular` as recipe-specific preparation/strength wording.

The OQLF defines instant/soluble coffee as roasted coffee that has been dehydrated into powder or granules and dissolves in hot water. Treat `instant coffee / café soluble` as its own processed product candidate, with `soluble coffee` and `café instantané` as source-supported synonyms. It is not an alias for ground coffee. Keep powder/granule wording within this soluble product class; no drying-method subtype is justified by current recipe demand.

OQLF defines espresso as a drink prepared by short, high-pressure percolation of ground coffee. This is separate from espresso powder. The current espresso-powder identity is active but has no ledger evidence; this review makes no name change or alias addition. A King Arthur product page describes that company's baking espresso powder as ground, brewed, and dried coffee and places it in baking use. Its editorial source states that the product is different from instant coffee; this evidence is product-specific and does not establish a universal French display or universal composition for every product sold as espresso powder.

### Tea and matcha

CFIA defines tea as dried leaves and buds of *Camellia sinensis*. OQLF similarly defines `thé / tea` as dried tea leaves and notes the black/green distinction. CFIA has separate black- and green-tea standards, while OQLF supplies the Québec pairs `thé noir / black tea` and `thé vert / green tea`. If included in a bounded identity-only pass, these should be separate product identities with empty aliases, rather than aliases that erase their type. Tea bags, loose-leaf presentation, brewed tea, hot/iced serving, and infusion method do not create new identities here.

White tea and oolong are current Québec product/menu terms (`thé blanc / white tea`; `oolong`) but this review found no comparable current Canadian compositional standard entry or OQLF English/French definition for the complete product class. Keep them held for a later term and taxonomy check. A prior 1982 OQLF entry mentions the black, green, and partially processed oolong group, but its dated cuisine-specific notes are not treated as an exhaustive modern tea taxonomy. Herbal teas/tisanes, rooibos, and plant infusions do not fall within the cited *Camellia sinensis* definition; do not add them as generic `tea / thé` aliases.

Keep `ingredient-ref-matcha` exactly as WP06 integrated it: `matcha / matcha` with `thé matcha` as its only French alias. WP06 treats matcha as a powdered tea product and excludes generic green tea, other tea powder, flavor products, sweetened mixes, and prepared drinks. No additional aliases such as `green tea powder`, no separate `matcha powder` reference, and no changes to its identity state are proposed.

### Cocoa and malt beverage powders

Retail sources show `hot chocolate mix / mélange à chocolat chaud` as a named powder product, with cocoa and other formulation ingredients. A separate current Ovaltine product is labeled `Malt Drink Mix / Mélange de boisson maltée`; the listed SKU contains cereal/malt extract, sugar or glucose syrup, dairy ingredients, cocoa, and other additions. These are formulation-specific retail examples, not universal formulas.

If an exact future recipe calls for either mix, use a compound-product identity with the exact supported product family and bilingual display. Do not attach `hot chocolate`, `chocolat chaud`, or `cocoa beverage` as aliases to unsweetened cocoa powder. Do not alias malt drink mix to `malted milk powder / lait malté en poudre`, malt extract, malt syrup, or any malted-flour reference. WP04's federal-standard review establishes malted milk powder as a separate milk-plus-barley-malt product; the name is not a generic term for every malt-based drink powder. No drink-mix aliases or identities should be integrated from one branded formula alone.

## Bounded next tranche and explicit holds

1. **Dry coffee and instant coffee:** If broader catalog coverage is desired, add a separate dry roasted-coffee identity, with whole bean and ground as forms, plus a separate soluble/instant-coffee identity. Preserve the current `coffee / café` beverage owner and its two recipe links. The exact proposed search terms are `whole bean coffee / cafés en grains`, `ground coffee / café moulu`, and `instant coffee / café soluble`; `soluble coffee` and `café instantané` can be aliases of the instant identity. No recipe links or numeric data are supported.
2. **Common leaf teas:** Consider identity-only `black tea / thé noir` and `green tea / thé vert` rows, empty aliases. Keep unqualified `tea / thé` as a source-defined dry-leaf concept only if the catalog owner wants a generic tea identity. Hold white tea, oolong/wulong, pu-erh, yellow tea, flavored/scented teas, tea powder other than integrated matcha, and herbal infusions for separately scoped reviews.
3. **Coffee beverage and espresso:** Retain existing coffee beverage ownership. Hold separate brewed espresso identity pending exact recipe demand. Keep espresso powder separate and source review pending; do not merge with instant coffee.
4. **Cocoa and malt drink mixes:** Hold generic `hot chocolate/cocoa beverage mix` and `malt drink mix` references pending exact recipe demand. If added later, use `hot chocolate mix / mélange à chocolat chaud` and `malt drink mix / mélange de boisson maltée` as product-family candidates with empty aliases; keep branded names and formula details source-scoped. Keep malted milk powder in the separate WP04 disposition.

No aliases are recommended for instant espresso, hot cocoa, drinking chocolate, Ovaltine, Milo, Horlicks, `malted milk`, or generic `malt powder` in this packet. These terms either refer to a different prepared/product class or lack enough reviewed product scope here. No measurements, preservation advice, conversions, or substitutions are included.
