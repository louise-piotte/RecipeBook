## Why this reassessment changes the earlier hold

The earlier [species and product-boundary review](wp04-sea-cucumber-species-product-boundary-review-20261011.md) correctly held the generic name because it covers multiple species and product parts. It also identified *Cucumaria frondosa* as a possible future species-scoped candidate, but said to reopen only when the species and edible product were matched. This review supplies that match from current Québec naming and product sources, a Québec fishery document, and a Canadian processing study.

The evidence supports one **narrow identity**: *Cucumaria frondosa* (orange-footed sea cucumber; OQLF-preferred Québec/Canada French `holothurie touffue`) with a **dried, gutted body-wall form**. It does not resolve the generic sea-cucumber category, every edible part of this species, or a household recipe amount. The species is the identity; dried gutted body wall is a distinct product form/part specification.

## Proposed catalog boundary

1. **Support a species-scoped identity:** *Cucumaria frondosa*, English `orange-footed sea cucumber`, Québec/Canada preferred French `holothurie touffue`, category `PROTEIN`. OQLF supports `concombre de mer du Nord` as a species-specific French term and lists `Atlantic sea cucumber`, `northern sea cucumber`, and `North Atlantic sea cucumber` as English terms. Keep `Cucumaria frondosa` available for disambiguation. Do not activate generic `sea cucumber` / `concombre de mer`, nor market terms such as `bêche-de-mer` or `trepang`, as aliases. [S01, S02]
2. **Scope the supported edible product form narrowly:** dried, gutted body wall (`paroi corporelle`), only where a source, package, or recipe identifies that product. The current sources do not establish that the dried body-wall product is interchangeable with the whole animal, muscle bands, or another sea-cucumber species. [S03, S04]
3. Keep the broad candidate `sea cucumber / concombre de mer` unresolved. Do not add generic `sea cucumber`, generic `concombre de mer`, `bêche-de-mer`, `bêche de mer`, `biche-de-mer`, `trepang`, `sea slug`, `meat`, or `skin` as aliases on this species-scoped row. OQLF’s generic term record and CFIA’s multi-species list show why those names do not identify this product by themselves. [S01, S02]
4. Keep *C. frondosa* muscle bands/meat separate from dried gutted body wall. Leave frozen products unresolved by part: Québec and Canadian sources describe frozen products but do not consistently establish whether an item is whole, body wall, or muscle. Do not collapse those product states into one form. [S03, S05, S07]
5. Do not create recipe links. The 114-recipe corpus has no exact-name, species, part, or synonym match.

## Numeric and recipe behavior

This review supports no count weight, household measure, unit mapping, density, package conversion, edible-portion factor, or drying/rehydration yield. Although the St. Lawrence study estimates fishery processing recovery, it reports dependence on capture depth, season, stock, body condition, and processing; its purpose is resource and commercial-yield analysis. Those rates are not a culinary conversion. A Québec seafood page describes rehydration taking days, but it does not establish a transferable mass or volume ratio. [S04, S05]

No global or contextual substitution is proposed. The available sources do not establish equivalence among dried body wall, muscles, frozen/whole products, *Apostichopus californicus* products, and other holothurians.

## Remaining evidence limits

- No bundled recipe specifies *C. frondosa* or dried gutted body wall, so the candidate has no recipe mapping to resolve.
- The Québec retail/product listings reviewed use broader French labels such as `concombre de mer séché`; they do not print the species and body-wall state together on the same consumer-facing SKU. The narrow candidate is supported by combining independent sources, not by a single exact package label.
- The sources also document edible muscle bands and frozen products, but do not establish one shared form, preparation state, or measurement basis for them. Keep those open.
- The evidence does not support mapping the generic sea-cucumber name, dried product aliases, other species, commercial pack quantities, or rehydration claims into recipe behavior.
