# WP02 A7 R951: sorghum-germ milling-fraction hold

## Review record

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-19.
- **Status:** Proposal only; no shared catalog, ledger, plan, roadmap, or checklist files were changed.
- **Open-plan anchor:** [WP02 Section A7](../../RecipeBook-global-ingredient-catalog-plan.txt#L2289) leaves other grain meals and milling products open.
- **Candidate reviewed:** A separate human-food identity for the germ fraction removed from sorghum grain, informally “sorghum germ.”
- **Decision:** Hold outside the active catalog and research-candidate ledger. Primary evidence establishes that a sorghum germ fraction can be separated, but the reviewed use evidence is pet food, while human-food processing sources describe germ retained in edible sorghum flour or co-occurring with bran/offal. The reviewed evidence does not establish the separate fraction as a culinary ingredient.

## Bounded identity and hold condition

The candidate is the separated germ (embryo and scutellum) of grain sorghum, *Sorghum bicolor*. It would be distinct from whole sorghum grain, sorghum bran, flour, grits, semolina, starch, and puffed sorghum. A separated germ fraction is also distinct from **sprouted or malted sorghum grain**: the latter names a germination process applied to the whole grain, not the grain’s anatomical germ.

Do not assign feed-oriented `sorghum germ meal`, `sorghum germ cake`, or mixed sorghum mill-feed to a prospective human-food row. Feedipedia describes sorghum germ meal/cake as a sorghum-starch-processing by-product made from germ after part of its oil has been pressed; this feed-oriented description does not establish a human-food identity [S03]. Do not infer a separate culinary use from the nutritive composition of germ tissue or from its presence in whole-grain flour.

Reopen only when a primary human-food source directly identifies separated sorghum germ as an ingredient in a food preparation and clarifies whether it is whole/fat-containing germ, defatted meal, a flour, or a mixed milling fraction. A source that reports feed use, animal-food extrusion, whole-grain germination, or germ carried in bran is not sufficient by itself. If the identity later becomes supportable, review a Québec French display separately; `germe de sorgho` remains only a possible literal rendering here.

## Remaining uncertainty

1. A separate sorghum germ can be produced by milling, but the reviewed direct use evidence is for pet food; a human-food ingredient role remains unestablished.
2. Human-food milling may leave germ in whole or edible grain fractions or remove it with variable bran/offal. No reviewed source defines a consistent standalone culinary germ fraction.
3. Sorghum germ meal/cake terms apply to feed-oriented oil-extraction by-products and should not be used as synonyms for edible germ.
4. There is no bundled recipe demand, household measure, or supported Québec French term for the proposed identity.
