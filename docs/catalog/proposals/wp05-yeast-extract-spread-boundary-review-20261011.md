# WP05 Section Z — Yeast-extract spread identity boundary review

- **Packet type:** proposal-only identity, bilingual terminology, and collision/demand review.
- **Prepared and source-access date:** 2026-10-11.
- **Plan:** Section Z, “Marmite/Vegemite-type yeast extracts, malt extracts, gravy mixes, and regional seasoning concentrates.”
- **Scope:** Marmite/Vegemite-type savory yeast-extract spreads only. Malt extract is cross-referenced to the existing WP02 proposal; gravy mixes and other seasoning concentrates are out of scope.
- **Status:** Coordinator recorded a held savory yeast-extract-spread candidate; active identity remains pending an exact, Québec-reviewed generic French display. The product class is distinguishable from yeast-extract powder and nutritional yeast. No recipe link is warranted.
- **Source IDs:** `WP05-Z-YEAST-SPREAD-20261011-S01` through `WP05-Z-YEAST-SPREAD-20261011-S10`. The prefix was checked against the live ledger and existing proposals/batches before use; no collision was found.

## Coordinator recommendation

1. **Recognize a generic ingredient candidate:** savory yeast-extract spread, represented by products such as Marmite and Vegemite. These are branded examples, not generic ingredient names. Marmite and Vegemite must not be stored as aliases for the generic candidate.
2. **Hold active row creation until the French display is settled.** A current Canadian Metro listing for the same Marmite SKU uses English `Yeast Extract` and French `Extrait de levure`; the manufacturer describes the product as a spread. OQLF supports `tartinade` for “spread,” but the reviewed Canadian label and terminology sources do not give an exact generic French equivalent for “yeast extract spread.” `Tartinade à l’extrait de levure` is a transparent candidate for Québec review, not an attested term to activate in this packet.
3. **Keep generic yeast extract broader than this spread.** Yeast extract occurs as powder, paste, or liquid; the spread product is a prepared, savory, spoonable condiment made with yeast extract and other ingredients. Do not alias unqualified `yeast extract` / `extrait de levure` to a spread identity, and do not make powder, paste, or liquid variants forms of Marmite/Vegemite-type spreads.
4. Keep nutritional yeast, baker’s yeast, malt extract, gravy mixes, and blended seasoning bases distinct. No product-to-product conversion or substitution is established.
5. Add no aliases, forms, densities, unit mappings, package yields, conversions, recipe links, or substitutions. Do not allocate a reference ID while the row is held.

## Repository audit and demand

The audit read the bundled reference and form arrays, legacy-import candidate array, all records in the research ledger, the 114 manifest-listed recipe files, and relevant proposals/batches. It used the app’s search-key normalization (lowercase, Unicode decomposition/accent folding, punctuation-to-space, and whitespace collapse) and checked complete phrases plus obvious English/French variants: `Marmite`, `Vegemite`, `yeast extract`, `yeast extract spread`, `concentrated yeast extract`, `yeast extract powder`, `nutritional yeast`, `extrait de levure`, `levure alimentaire`, `malt extract`, and `extrait de malt`.

- The bundled catalog contains 1,030 references and 72 forms. No reference or form has a complete yeast-extract, Marmite, Vegemite, nutritional-yeast, or spread phrase. The broad `ingredient-ref-yeast` row contains `yeast` / `levure` and bakery-specific aliases; that one-word overlap does not establish a match for `yeast extract` or a spread. The osmotolerant baker’s-yeast row also has no matching phrase.
- None of the 75 legacy-import candidates matches the searched phrases.
- No recipe text or ingredient line in the 114 manifest-listed recipes matches Marmite, Vegemite, yeast extract/spread/powder, nutritional yeast, or their French equivalents. There is no recipe-level demand and no recipe link is proposed.
- The ledger contains existing malt-flour and malted-barley-flour decisions that explicitly keep malt extract separate. It has no yeast-extract-spread identity or candidate record.
- The existing [`WP02 yeast-products boundary review`](wp02-yeast-products-boundary-review-20261009.md) treats nutritional yeast and yeast extract as outside baker’s-yeast aliases. It integrated baker’s-yeast subtypes, including an instant-yeast recipe link, but does not add nutritional yeast or yeast extract.
- [`WP02 malt-extract coordinator recheck`](wp02-malt-extract-coordinator-recheck-20261009.md) supports the candidate name pair `malt extract` / `extrait de malt` but defers its active row and category pending broader review. Keep that candidate separate; the Section Z umbrella item is still open for malt extract, gravy mixes, and regional seasoning concentrates.

## Identity and term boundaries

| Product | Evidence-backed boundary | Catalog recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Marmite/Vegemite-type spread | Marmite’s manufacturer calls it a dark, thick yeast-extract spread made from concentrated yeast extract; its FAQ says its major ingredient is extract from brewer’s yeast. Vegemite’s manufacturer describes its product as a spread developed from brewer’s yeast and says the label changed from “Pure Yeast Extract” to “Concentrated Yeast Extract” for precision [S03–S05]. The current Canadian Marmite listing identifies one product as yeast extract in English and extrait de levure in French; its ingredient list also includes salt, carrots, spice extracts, and onions [S01–S02]. | Generic candidate is a prepared savory spread. Do not treat it as pure yeast extract or equate different brands’ recipes. Hold the active bilingual reference until the generic French display is reviewed. |
| Generic yeast extract, including powder | A manufacturer of yeast-extract ingredients describes extract as available in powder, paste, or liquid form [S09]. A yeast-extract spread therefore cannot serve as a universal identity or alias for those other forms. | Keep `yeast extract` and `extrait de levure` unassigned to the spread candidate. Powder may warrant its own identity when a recipe requires it; do not infer that a spread is a powder form or add a conversion. |
| Nutritional yeast | OQLF defines nutritional yeast / `levure alimentaire` as inactive yeast sold as flakes or yellowish powder, commonly sprinkled onto food; it has no leavening properties [S07]. | A separate food product and use from a yeast-extract spread. It has no matching active reference or recipe demand in the audited seed. Do not alias it to a spread or baker’s yeast. |
| Baker’s yeast | The active catalog names the baking-scoped identity `baker’s yeast` / `levure de boulangerie` and has separate active-dry, instant, compressed, and osmotolerant baker’s-yeast identities. Existing yeast research describes nutritional yeast and yeast extract as outside that parent [S08]. | Keep all commercial leavening yeast separate from the spread. Do not map the spread into any baking-yeast subtype or substitution. |
| Malt extract | OQLF’s `extrait de malt` entry defines a malt-wort concentrate obtained before fermentation [S10]. The existing WP02 review separates that extracted cereal product from malted flour and defers a generic active row. | Do not alias yeast-extract spread to malt extract or malt syrup. Carry the unresolved malt-extract work under the WP02 review. |
| Gravy mixes and regional seasoning concentrates | These are broader multi-ingredient products. A yeast extract spread may be used in gravies, but a use suggestion does not make a spread equivalent to a gravy mix or stock/seasoning base [S03, S05]. | No identity, alias, or substitution is proposed for these out-of-scope products in this packet. Keep the remaining Section Z review open. |
