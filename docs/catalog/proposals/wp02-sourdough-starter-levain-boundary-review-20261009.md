- **Scope:** maintained live sourdough starter, recipe-built levain, discard, dried starter, hydration/activity descriptions, and the boundary from commercial baker’s yeast.
- **Status:** research recommendation for coordinator review. Section E remains open.

## Coordinator recommendation

1. **Add one live sourdough-starter identity candidate, separate from commercial baker’s yeast.** Suggested EN display: `sourdough starter`. The French choice is unresolved: OQLF’s preferred term is `levain`, while Québec baking education and local product pages also use `levain naturel` for a maintained, naturally fermented culture [S01–S03, S08]. OQLF’s older definition also includes flour dough that may be mixed with baker’s yeast and lists `levain de pâte` as context wording, so do not activate every bare `levain` phrase as a global alias. The provisional fr-CA display candidate is `levain naturel`; retain `levain` as a terminology candidate pending bilingual review.
2. **Treat recipe-built levain as a build/pre-ferment derived from starter, not a new global identity or automatic starter alias.** King Arthur describes a levain as a fed offshoot of a starter, used in one recipe, whose flour/water proportions can differ from the maintained starter [S04–S05]. English `levain` can also mean sourdough bread. Keep `levain build`, `levain de pâte`, `levain chef`, and English `levain` unresolved as aliases; a recipe must provide enough context to determine whether it means the maintained culture or a particular build.
3. **Treat discard as a maintenance state of the same starter, not a separate ingredient identity.** It is the portion removed during routine feeding; it is typically unfed/unripe, but discard can be saved, fed later, used in recipes, or used to start a new culture. It is not necessarily biologically inactive, although King Arthur notes its discard recipes often use it mainly for flavor rather than leavening [S04–S06, S10]. Do not add `sourdough discard` as a subtype alias if that would lose the recipe’s state. If needed later, retain `unfed/discard` as an explicit recipe preparation or a narrowly scoped form.
4. **Treat dried, reactivatable sourdough culture as a future form of the live starter identity.** The reviewed King Arthur method dries a fed, active culture for storage and later rehydrates and feeds it; a Québec product is sold as `levain déshydraté` / `levain en poudre` and is described as a culture of bacteria and yeasts for starting a culture [S07–S08]. Require product evidence that the material is intended to reactivate before applying a dried-starter form. Do not globally alias `sourdough powder`, `levain en poudre`, or `levain déshydraté`: a separate manufacturer sells a dehydrated, devitalized levain with selected yeast for direct dough use [S09]. That product is not a reactivatable starter.
5. **Keep hydration and activity as recipe/product properties, not identities or default forms.** Hydration is water weight divided by flour weight; a 100% hydration culture is common in one source but not universal, and levain builds may use different proportions [S04–S06, S11]. “Ripe,” “fed,” “unfed,” “falling,” and “discard” describe condition or timing. Preserve a value or state only when the recipe/source specifies it; no default hydration or activity claim is supported for this catalog.
6. **Do not equate starter with the existing baker’s-yeast identity.** King Arthur describes starter as a living flour/water culture of yeast and bacteria that requires feeding, distinct from instant or active dry commercial yeast [S10]. Existing `docs/catalog/batches/baking-substitutions.md` already documents a narrow source-specific use of starter as part flour/liquid accounting and rejects a general starter-to-commercial-yeast dose conversion. This packet activates no conversion or substitution.

### Québec terminology decision

OQLF’s preferred term is **levain**, but its GDT definition includes flour dough that has fermented or has been mixed with yeast; it also lists **levain de pâte** as a context term. The record is dated 1983 and maps to English **sourdough** with a U.S. usage label, rather than precisely to a maintained “starter” [S01]. Current Québec baking education uses **levain naturel** when teaching people to start and maintain their own culture, and Puratos Canada describes **levain** as a natural water-and-flour fermentation product [S02–S03].

For coordinator review, use `sourdough starter` as the EN candidate and `levain naturel` as the provisional fr-CA candidate. Decide whether **levain** should be the canonical French display or a search alias only after bilingual review. No standardized Québec French term for **sourdough discard** or **levain build** was established; the descriptive candidates above remain inactive. Do not use `levain de pâte`, `levain chef`, `levure de levain`, `levain-levure`, or literal translations as global aliases without recipe and product context.

## Hydration and activity evidence (inactive)

- **Hydration basis:** King Arthur defines hydration as total water weight ÷ total flour weight × 100. It gives 80 g water with 100 g flour as an 80% hydration example [S05]. This is a formula property, not a volume conversion.
- **100% starter observation:** King Arthur’s discard guidance says its usual starter is equal flour and water by weight. In that source’s 100 g example, that starter contains 50 g flour and 50 g water [S06]. The existing baking-substitutions batch separately records King Arthur’s 227 g starter example as 113 g flour plus 113 g liquid [existing `docs/catalog/batches/baking-substitutions.md`, S7]. Both are source-scoped observations, not a catalog default.
- **Feeding ratio is not a universal hydration setting:** One King Arthur test used 1:1:1, 1:2:2, and 1:4:4 starter:flour:water feeds. In that test, 60 g starter + 60 g flour + 60 g water at about 78°F reached its observed peak after about 4–6 hours; the source says results vary with starter health and kitchen temperature [S11]. These figures describe one feeding/test, not every starter’s state or readiness.
- **Activity is not binary:** King Arthur describes ripe starter as fed, risen, bubbly, and doubled or tripled; another source reports a ripe starter beginning to fall after its peak [S10–S11]. Discard means the removed portion is not fed during that maintenance step, not that it is dead. A starter’s timing and leavening strength vary with feed schedule, temperature, flour, and culture condition.
- **Dried-culture recovery is product/method-specific:** King Arthur’s home method uses 1 oz dried starter with 2 oz lukewarm water to soften it, then 1 oz flour; later feedings and warm resting are part of that method [S07]. This is not a dry-to-live starter conversion. The Québec product page provides a 30 g package and says it is a bacterial/yeast culture for activation, but does not establish the King Arthur revival ratio [S08].

All these observations stay inactive. Do not set starter hydration, density, unit mappings, a dried-to-live yield, a ripe-time threshold, or a sourdough-to-yeast substitution. Existing partial flour/liquid accounting in `baking-substitutions.md` is not a leavener conversion and should remain scoped to its source and conditions.
