Légis Québec’s current Annex I for field vegetables pairs “Cerfeuil tubéreux” with *Chaerophyllum bulbosum* and lists leafy “Cerfeuil (feuille fraîche, séché)” separately as *Anthriscus cerefolium*. This supplies an exact Québec French name and a useful species/plant-part boundary; the annex is a crop list, not an OQLF terminology ruling or evidence of retail prevalence. [S01]

The English name “Chervil, turnip-rooted” is paired with *C. bulbosum* in the U.S. root-and-tuber vegetable crop group. Kew accepts *Chaerophyllum bulbosum* L. as a distinct species. An Acta Horticulturae research paper explicitly describes its root as edible as a vegetable. Together, these support the generic identity and edible-root scope, not a relationship to turnip or to leafy culinary chervil. [S02–S04]

Keep this row separate from active chervil, which is *Anthriscus cerefolium* and categorized as HERB, as well as from turnip (*Brassica rapa*). Do not assign the bare words “chervil”, “cerfeuil”, or “turnip” as aliases; the Québec annex already uses cerfeuil for a different species’ leaves. Do not include leaves, seeds, other *Chaerophyllum* species, wild-foraging advice, or any edible-part claims beyond the root. The existing leafy-chervil review also explicitly kept *C. bulbosum* distinct rather than adding it to the herb identity. [S01, S05]
