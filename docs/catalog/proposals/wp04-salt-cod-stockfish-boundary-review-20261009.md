# WP04 Section P: salt cod and stockfish product-boundary review

- **Review / source access date:** 2026-10-09.
- **Disposition:** Recommend **separate product identities** for salt cod and stockfish. Do not merge salted cod with unsalted dried stockfish or assign stockfish to one cod species. Keep regional cures and species-specific products open for separate recipe-driven review.
- **Scope:** Identity, English/Québec French naming, measurement, and substitution research for the Section P candidates `salt cod` and `stockfish`.
- **Proposal-time live state:** 414 ingredient references, 16 ingredient forms, 99 legacy candidates, 579 research-ledger records, and 114 bundled recipes / 1,123 ingredient lines.

## Decision

The evidence supports a product-state boundary: an FAO commodity classification separately labels **cod, dried, unsalted (stockfish)** and **cod, salted and dried (klipfish)**, with corresponding French and Spanish descriptions [WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S07]. Norway's fish regulations define stockfish as fish dried naturally or industrially **without added salt or other additives**. That stockfish designation covers cod, haddock, saithe, ling, blue ling, and tusk, with a possible exceptional extension to other dried species [WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S08]. Therefore, stockfish is not a synonym for salt cod and is not inherently one fish species.

Recommend two identity candidates if WP04 later integrates this Section P pair:

| Candidate | Proposed display | Category if accepted | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salt cod | **salt cod / morue salée** | `PROTEIN` | Cod preserved with salt. Keep dry, lightly cured, heavily cured, and region-specific products from being silently treated as compositionally identical. Do not assign a cod species unless a source or recipe names it. |
| Stockfish | **stockfish / stockfish** (French display provisional pending Québec review) | `PROTEIN` | A named unsalted dried fish product. The Norwegian legal definition includes multiple species, so the product identity must not imply Atlantic cod or any other one species. No cod-specific French display or alias is proposed for the full product scope. |

`Morue salée` is a supported Québec French name for salt cod: Québec recipes use it directly, Parks Canada's French recipe describes salted and dried cod as `morue salée et séchée`, and OQLF records regional forms such as `morue salée du type Labrador` and `morue salée du type Gaspé` [WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S01–WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S04, WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S11, WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S15–WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S16]. OQLF also says Québec does not use the France-specific `morue`/`cabillaud` distinction to distinguish salted from fresh or frozen cod; retain the explicit qualifier `salée` in a fr-CA candidate [WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S03].

The generic salt-cod candidate does not specify a salt level, drying state, cut, source species, or curing tradition. The reviewed evidence distinguishes salted-but-not-dried fish from dried salted fish, and identifies more than one salted-cod cure [WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S01–WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S02, WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S06–WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S08]. Whether those states warrant separate identities, forms, or recipe-level product wording remains unresolved; do not flatten a source's more specific product into a generic row without a later scope decision.

No source-qualified Québec French display for stockfish was found. FAO's French commodity label `morue, séchée, non salée (stockfish)` describes its **cod** commodity record, but it cannot be generalized to Norway's broader species-defined stockfish product [WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S07–WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S08]. Keep the English product name `stockfish` in the provisional fr-CA display until an independent Québec reviewer confirms a suitable French label. Do not activate `morue séchée non salée` as a stockfish alias across species.

## Product boundaries and terminology

| Comparison | Disposition |
|---|---|
| Salt cod vs. stockfish | **Separate product identities.** The salted vs. unsalted distinction is explicit in FAO's cod trade classes; Norwegian regulation also defines stockfish as dried without added salt or additives [WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S07–WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S08]. No equivalence, alias, or substitution follows from their shared fish family. |
| Cod salted but not dried vs. salt cod / dried salted cod | **Unresolved within the salt-cod candidate.** FAO and CFIA distinguish salted fish, salted-and-dried fish, and other salted states [WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S06–WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S07]. The sources reviewed do not establish one catalog boundary covering all products sold or described as `salt cod`; preserve exact product qualifiers when present. |
| Salt cod vs. generic cod / `morue` | Keep distinct. Brazil's Ministry of Agriculture says `bacalhau` is a fish name used across species and presentations, including fresh or frozen fish; Spain's official commercial-name list uses `bacalao` for more than one cod species. The unqualified words alone do not identify salted cod [WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S09–WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S10]. OQLF notes that Québec's unqualified `morue` does not distinguish fresh/frozen from salted cod [WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S03]. Require a process qualifier such as `salt cod` / `morue salée` when that is the intended product. |
| `bacalao` / `bacalhau` | **No alias proposed.** FAO's source-qualified Spanish description for salted and dried cod is `bacalao, salado y seco`; its stockfish line is `bacalao, seco, sin salar` [WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S07]. Spain's official commercial-name list and Brazil's Ministry of Agriculture show that the unqualified word names fish species, not a unique salted product [WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S09–WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S10]. Treat those complete phrases as regional terminology evidence, not as English or fr-CA catalog aliases. |
| `klipfish` / `klippfisk` | **No alias proposed.** FAO uses `klipfish` for its salted-and-dried cod trade class, but Norwegian regulation allows the related salted-fish/klippfish designations for multiple cod-family species [WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S07–WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S08]. Do not attach the term to a generic cod identity without a market- and species-specific decision. |
| Gaspé and Labrador cures | Keep as named regional product candidates, not aliases. OQLF records `morue à la gaspésienne` / `morue salée du type Gaspé` as lightly salted, brined, dried cod with 34–36% moisture; it records `morue salée du type Labrador` / `Labrador fish` as heavily salted cod with about 47% moisture and 17–18% salt [WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S01–WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S02]. Their distinct names and specifications show variation within salt-cured cod, but the reviewed material does not decide whether a recipe book needs separate catalog identities or form/product qualifiers. |
| Portuguese traditional cured cod | Keep as a product-specific name, not a generic alias. Portugal's official product listing describes `Bacalhau de Cura Tradicional Portuguesa` as scaled, salted, matured, and dried *Gadus morhua* [WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S12]. This product's species and traditional process do not establish that every `bacalhau` or `salt cod` uses the same species or cure. The portal returned a 403 to full-page retrieval; this review relies only on the visible official listing/search abstract. |
| Desalted salt cod | Keep distinct from stockfish. Québec recipes explicitly soak salt cod and then call it `morue dessalée`; this is recipe preparation of salted cod, not evidence that it becomes an unsalted dried product [WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S11, WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S15–WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S16]. No form record is proposed here. |

## Alias and name proposals

- Candidate canonical terms: `salt cod` and `morue salée` for the salted-cod product; `stockfish` for the stockfish product. These are source-backed names, not proposed active rows.
- **No aliases proposed.** Do not add bare `cod`, `morue`, `bacalao`, or `bacalhau`; they do not uniquely identify salt cod. Do not use `dried cod` for stockfish because it omits the no-salt distinction. Do not add `klipfish`/`klippfisk`, which has a wider legal product scope in Norwegian regulation. Do not add `Labrador fish`, Gaspé terms, or `Bacalhau de Cura Tradicional Portuguesa` to a generic row; these are specific regional or process-qualified products.
- Do not create a stockfish species matrix. The product name is supported as a multi-species product category; the species used in any particular recipe or market remains unspecified unless the source names it.
- If bilingual sign-off is required before a future integration, obtain an independent Québec review of the provisional `stockfish` fr-CA display. No French term is invented in this proposal.

## Substitution evidence — separate from identity and measurement

No general substitution rule between salt cod and stockfish is supported. They have opposite salt-preservation states, and sources give no universal recipe conversion, desalt/re-hydration ratio, or culinary equivalence [WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S07–WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S08]. No substitute rule is proposed for either row.

Parks Canada's tested fishcake recipe is a limited contextual observation: the page describes the dish as based on salted-and-dried cod, lists cooked, broken dried cod with frozen filets as an option, and says leftover fried or boiled fish may replace the dried fish [WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S04]. This supports a recipe-specific option in that fishcake method only; it does not establish that stockfish substitutes for salt cod in other dishes, or vice versa. It supplies no replacement ratio. A Norwegian Seafood Council / Nielsen survey of Italian stockfish consumers reports that some respondents substitute other seafood, with fresh cod and `baccalà` among reported choices; it is consumer behavior data, not tested cooking guidance or a usable ratio [WP04-SALT-STOCKFISH-S14].
