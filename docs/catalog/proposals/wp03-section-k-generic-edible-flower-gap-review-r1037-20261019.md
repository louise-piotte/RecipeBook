The OQLF’s preferred Québec French botanical term is “monarde écarlate”; its entry identifies *Monarda didyma* and lists “Bee Balm” as an associated English term. UQAC’s Québec edible-flower page names “Monarde, *Monarda didyma*,” calls it a culinary flower, and describes flower uses in fruit juices, punches, lemonades, and iced tea. The proposed compound “fleurs de monarde écarlate” is descriptive fr-CA wording assembled from those locally supported names; the flower phrase itself is not an OQLF entry. [S01–S02]

Kew accepts *Monarda didyma* L. as a species. University of Illinois Extension independently identifies beebalm (*M. didyma*) flowers as edible. These sources support the culinary flower part for this species only. Do not extend the claim to other *Monarda* species or hybrids. UQAC also describes edible leaves, but leaf use is a separate part and is outside this identity. [S02–S04]

Keep the proposed flower identity separate from any *Monarda* leaf, stem, seed, whole-plant, or prepared tea identity. Do not add “bergamot” or “bergamote” as aliases: the live catalog assigns those names to *Citrus bergamia* fruit. OQLF’s “thé d’Oswego” and “Oswego Tea” refer to the plant’s tea-associated naming, not a flower ingredient alias. The generic words “bee balm,” “monarde,” and “flower” are not aliases; retain the species-qualified displays and empty alias arrays. [S01, S05]
