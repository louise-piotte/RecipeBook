- **Scope:** Exact Québec French naming for food-grade starch extracted from edible *Colocasia esculenta* corms. This follows the open WP02 B2/B3 residual and B9 whole-root-flour/extracted-starch boundary; it does not reopen other flour or starch candidates.

## Recommendation

**Keep the candidate on hold. No exact Québec or Canadian French product name for isolated *C. esculenta* corm starch was found.** The English working name `taro starch` is supported by technical literature and appears in a Canadian trademark goods statement. Neither source provides a Québec French label. The French construction `fécule de taro` remains a descriptive inference; `amidon de taro` appears in a French-language regional commercial classification but not in Québec or Canadian product evidence.

## Québec French naming evidence

- OQLF’s GDT entry lists `taro`, `colocase`, and `colocasie` for the plant/food *Colocasia esculenta*. It does not name flour or isolated starch. [S01]
- OQLF’s `amidon` entry, updated in 2026, prefers `amidon` as the general term and says `fécule` is used more specifically for starch in tubers or, more generally, underground plant organs. This makes `fécule de taro` linguistically plausible for a corm-derived starch, but the entry does not pair it with English `taro starch` or attest it as a product name. [S02]
- OQLF’s `fécule de manioc` entry defines that particular product as an industrial product obtained by wet extraction from tuberous roots. It shows a process-qualified French product entry, but is attributed to AFNOR and does not establish a taro term. `Fécule de manioc` / `tapioca starch` remains the separate cassava-derived product. [S03]
- Targeted TERMIUM Plus searches for `taro starch`, `fécule de taro`, and `amidon de taro` did not surface an exact taro-starch entry. The dynamic direct query page could not be fetched during this review; the search result is not exhaustive and does not prove that no entry exists. [S04]
- OAPI’s 2025 official bulletin includes `amidon de taro à usage alimentaire` in a Nice class 30 goods description. This confirms that the French wording appears in a contemporary commercial classification, but OAPI serves member states of the Bangui Agreement; the listing is not a Québec/Canadian product label and does not identify the botanical source. [S05]
- CIPO’s active Canadian trademark record lists `taro starch` among class 30 goods. Its claims state use in China, and the record provides no French wording or species specification. It is evidence that the English term occurs in a Canadian trademark goods statement, not evidence of Canadian retail use or an exact fr-CA product name. [S06]
- The inspected French-language Metro listing for `Taro Root` describes the fresh root, not extracted starch. Targeted Québec retail searches for the exact starch phrases did not surface a standalone French taro-starch product listing. This bounded search does not prove that none is sold. [S07]

Accordingly, do not choose between `fécule de taro` and `amidon de taro` for the catalog display, and do not activate either as an alias. The plant terms `colocase` and `colocasie` do not independently name its extracted starch. The historical French phrase `fécule de Taro` and the OAPI classification wording remain non-Québec evidence; the earlier proposal documents their limits.

## Product boundary

The product is the separated starch fraction from *C. esculenta* corms. A 2024 primary study analyzes taro flour and taro starch as separate products from two *C. esculenta* varieties. A 2025 primary extraction study lists *C. esculenta* samples separately from *Xanthosoma*, *Alocasia*, and *Amorphophallus* samples; it describes homogenizing the corm, filtering and washing granules, settling them, then drying the recovered starch. Codex defines native starch as starch separated from its raw material by source-specific processes. Together, these sources support a distinct, source-qualified candidate, but not a Québec name or equivalence among aroid starches. [S08–S10]

Keep the candidate separate from:

- Fresh `taro / taro` (`ingredient-ref-taro`) and whole-corm `taro flour / farine de taro` (`ingredient-ref-taro-flour`). Flour and extracted starch are not aliases or forms of each other.
- `malanga / malanga` (`ingredient-ref-malanga`), which is the separate *Xanthosoma sagittifolium* identity. Do not broaden the candidate to *Xanthosoma*, *Alocasia*, *Amorphophallus*, or unspecified “cocoyam” starch.
- Cassava flour, tapioca starch / `fécule de manioc`, cornstarch / `fécule de maïs`, and arrow-root starch. These have distinct botanical sources and active identities; do not use their names as taro-starch aliases.
- Taro leaves, unspecified taro powder, flavored or instant beverage powders, and whole-corm flour or meal. Those products do not establish isolated starch identity.

Do not create forms for native, modified, pregelatinized, instant, or cooked starch. The research process and laboratory treatments do not establish consumer forms for the catalog.

## Reopen trigger

Reopen the naming decision when a current Québec/Canadian source supplies the French name for a standalone food starch product and ties it to extracted edible *C. esculenta* corm starch. Useful evidence would be a Québec/Canadian package or product listing plus manufacturer specification, or an OQLF/TERMIUM entry explicitly pairing the French headword with `taro starch`. A classification from another jurisdiction, a translation example, a fresh-root label, or a `farine de taro` label does not close the gap.
