## Recommendation

**Hold waxy wheat starch outside active references and the research ledger.** Canadian primary sources establish waxy wheat cultivars and laboratory-isolated waxy wheat starch. Current Canadian evidence reviewed for this proposal identifies generic native wheat starch sourcing, not a current Canadian or Québec human-food product offered as a distinct waxy wheat starch grade. A current Canadian Grain Commission order lists CDC Vosk in the Canada Western Special Purpose class, and the University of Saskatchewan calls it waxy/low-amylose wheat adapted to central Canada; neither source is a starch product offer. The AAFC-affiliated study fractionates a waxy spring-wheat genotype into flour and starch, but describes experimental material and properties rather than a marketed Canadian product. [S01–S04]

The current Canadian supplier pages reviewed here quote generic native food-grade wheat starch and list waxy maize, rice, and tapioca starch, but do not list waxy wheat starch. Those are bounded supplier-page findings, not proof that no other Canadian supplier or future product exists. The Québec Food Directory shows generic amidon de blé as an ingredient in a finished donut; it does not identify the starch as waxy or offer it as a standalone product. [S04–S05; R192-S01]

OQLF’s amidon de grains cireux entry defines the general cereal-starch concept and gives maize and rice as source-qualified examples. It does not attest amidon de blé cireux as a wheat-specific product name. The current Québec-facing product entry and OQLF’s wheat terminology support generic amidon de blé / amidon de froment only. Keep amidon de blé cireux as an unverified descriptive construction, not a stored display or alias. [R189-S03, R189-S07, WP02-S108, S05]

Keep the existing generic and process/function-qualified wheat-starch identities unchanged. Add no row, candidate ledger record, alias, form, measure, density, mapping, recipe link, or substitution.

## Canadian and Québec evidence

The Canadian Grain Commission’s 2026 CWSP order includes CDC Vosk among designated varieties and is effective for the crop year beginning August 1, 2026 through July 31, 2027. It establishes current Canadian class status for the variety; it does not say that waxy wheat starch is sold as a standalone food ingredient. [S01]

The University of Saskatchewan fact sheet calls CDC Vosk “waxy” and low-amylose and states that it is adapted to spring-wheat-growing regions of Central Canada. It is cultivar and seed information. Its references to waxiness and grain-quality measurements do not establish starch extraction, a commercial starch grade, Canadian stock, a consumer package, or a Québec product name. [S02]

An Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada research paper reports that a waxy spring-wheat genotype was fractionated into flour and starch through roller and wet milling. It demonstrates that waxy wheat starch is a real research material distinct from the corresponding flour and from nonwaxy wheat starch. Its laboratory comparisons and food-function observations are not a current Canadian product listing, consumer recipe, package measurement, or general replacement recommendation. [S03]

Allzone Trading’s current Canadian wheat-starch page lists generic native food-grade wheat starch with quotation-dependent grade, packing, origin, and documentation. It does not state that the offered wheat starch is waxy. Its separate current waxy-starch page names waxy maize, waxy rice, and waxy tapioca as sourcing options, with product particulars confirmed by quotation; wheat is not listed on that page. This is evidence only about the pages reviewed, not a market-wide nonexistence claim or guaranteed stock position. Reuse the existing R192 source ID for the waxy-starch page rather than registering its URL again. [S04; R192-S01]

The Québec Food Directory’s current listing for Beigne gateau nature includes amidon de blé in the ingredient declaration and was last updated 2026-07-24. This confirms current Québec-facing French wording for generic wheat starch inside a finished food. It is not a standalone starch offer and does not identify a waxy cultivar or grade. [S05]

OQLF’s current amidon entry prefers amidon as the general cereal-starch term and specifically mentions wheat among cereal sources. Its older amidon de grains cireux entry defines a broad amylopectin-rich cereal-starch class, with maize and rice as examples. OQLF’s amidon de froment term remains attached to the existing generic wheat-starch row through WP02-S108. These entries support the terminology components and existing ownership, but none supplies a product-matched Québec name for waxy wheat starch. [R189-S03, R189-S07, WP02-S108]

The bounded product search used the phrases waxy wheat starch, waxy wheat, waxy wheat starch Canada, and amidon de blé cireux; it also checked the current Canadian supplier’s generic wheat-starch and waxy-starch pages, current Canadian wheat-class/cultivar material, and the Québec Food Directory. The reviewed results support the cultivar and research boundaries above but did not identify a current Canadian or Québec human-food product marketed as waxy wheat starch. This is a bounded non-discovery, not proof of nonavailability.
