## Recommendation

Recommend a narrow identity-only **FLOUR_AND_STARCH** reference for the source-named product **whole red kidney bean flour**. Celiac Canada's product finder, last updated July 2026, lists AGT Foods' exact title **Whole Red Kidney Bean Flour 20**. The finder says it is not updated in real time and notes that some listed items are ingredients available only to food manufacturers, so this is Canadian product-directory evidence, not proof of current retail availability. AGT's public pulse-ingredients list does not include kidney flour, and the only located IFANCA certificate naming AGT and this exact title expired in 2020. Current SKU status and technical specifications therefore remain unverified. [S01–S03]

Retain the title's **whole** scope in the English display. Do not interpret the suffix **20**: the current directory provides no explanation, while the historical certificate gives the product code **WKBF-020** without defining the number. Do not treat it as a percentage, grade, particle size, protein value, serving amount, or unit. [S01–S02]

Use **farine de haricots rouges entiers** as a provisional Québec French display. OQLF's preferred whole-bean term is **haricot rouge**, mapped to English **red kidney bean** and *Phaseolus vulgaris*; it discusses culinary use in chili and distinguishes dark- and light-red varieties. No reviewed authority or current Québec flour label supplies this exact flour compound. The proposed French display is a transparent construction from the OQLF bean term and the listed whole-flour product boundary, not an OQLF ruling or verified package wording. [S04]

## Product and processing boundary

The listed product title distinguishes this item from whole kidney beans and from AGT's separately listed whole black-, pinto-, navy-, and Great Northern-bean flours. It supports a named flour identity, but does not establish cultivar, colour class, source lot, dehulling, milling method, heat treatment, or whether the product is raw, precooked, extruded, roasted, or otherwise treated. Do not create any of those as forms or aliases from the listing. [S01]

A historical IFANCA certificate dated 2019 lists **Whole Red Kidney Bean Flour 20**, product code **WKBF-020**, and identifies AGT Foods in Regina and a Minot processing plant. The certificate expired on 2020-03-31. It corroborates the product wording and historical code only; it is not current certification or a technical specification. AGT's public pulse-ingredients list retrieved for this review names several other flour products but does not list kidney flour. That omission is not proof of discontinuation, but it leaves current product status unresolved. [S02–S03]

Research uses several flour preparations that cannot be assigned to the listed SKU:

- A 2026 noodle study dried red kidney beans below 12% moisture and milled whole flour through an 80-mesh sieve. This is the study's method, not a verified AGT method. [S08]
- A 2018 cupcake study evaluated precooked red-kidney-bean flours boiled for 0, 20, 30, or 40 minutes and dried at 80°C for 3 or 4 hours; its authors found 20 minutes boiling and 3 hours drying adequate for their precooked-flour cupcake application. This identifies a research preparation, not a commercial form for this candidate. [S09]
- A 2024 University of Guelph study investigated acidic and alkaline reactive extrusion of whole kidney-bean flour, showing that treatment can materially change the flour's antinutrient and flavour profile. The experimental extrusion conditions are not a product specification. [S10]

Health Canada's lectin guidance concerns dry whole red kidney beans: it notes high phytohaemagglutinin in raw or undercooked beans and gives preparation guidance for those dry beans. It does not establish the heat treatment or safe-use instructions for the listed flour. Do not represent this proposal as a preparation or food-safety instruction, and do not add a ready-to-eat, raw, or precooked form. [S11]

## Québec naming and whole-bean separation

OQLF gives **haricot rouge** as the preferred French term and **red kidney bean** as the English equivalent for *Phaseolus vulgaris*. Its note says Québec has dark- and light-red kidney beans and describes red kidney beans as a familiar ingredient in chili. It does not define a flour term or say which red-kidney colour the AGT title denotes. Keep the proposed French flour name provisional and the colour/cultivar unspecified. [S04]

Québec recipe publisher RICARDO uses **haricots rouges** for whole canned beans in its Louisiana-style red-beans-and-rice recipe, with a 540 mL can for four servings. The bundled classic-chili recipe likewise calls for 540 mL kidney beans, rinsed and drained, and links that line to the active canned/rinsed/drained whole-bean form. These are whole-bean examples, not demand for kidney-bean flour. [S05, current recipe corpus]

The Health Canada serving record for **Beans, kidney, red, canned, drained and rinsed / Haricots, rouges, conserve, égouttés et rincés** gives 125 mL = 83.5 g, 175 mL = 116.9 g, and 250 mL = 166.9 g. These figures belong to the canned whole-bean food code and cannot be copied to flour. [S06]

Keep the candidate distinct from:

- **ingredient-ref-kidney-beans** — whole kidney beans / haricots rouges, category **LEGUME_AND_PULSE**;
- **ingredient-ref-black-bean-flour** — black-bean flour;
- **ingredient-ref-pinto-bean-flour** — whole pinto-bean flour;
- **ingredient-ref-navy-bean-flour** — whole navy-bean flour; and
- **ingredient-ref-great-northern-bean-flour** — whole Great Northern bean flour.

The product finder lists the related whole-bean flour names separately. Do not use generic white-kidney/Cannellini wording to name red kidney flour. OQLF's dark/light red distinction is source evidence for possible bean varieties, not evidence that the AGT product has a particular one. [S01, S04]

## Recipe demand, numeric observations, and substitution

A scan of all **114** manifest-listed recipe files and **1,123** ingredient rows found **zero** flour mentions or exact flour links for red kidney bean flour, kidney bean flour, the proposed bilingual names, or likely whole-red-kidney-flour phrasing. The only nearby bundled use is recipe 034, ingredient 9: **540 mL kidney beans, rinsed and drained**, linked to the canned whole-bean form. It is not flour demand. [current recipe corpus]

External studies establish culinary research uses, not demand in the bundled library:

- The 2026 gluten-free noodle study tested red kidney bean flour at **10–50%** of its rice/glutinous-rice/tapioca composite flour and reported **20%** as the best balance within that noodle formulation. Its range and preferred level are specific to the tested product and are not a general replacement ratio. [S08]
- The 2018 gluten-free cupcake study evaluated precooked red-kidney-bean flour preparations; it does not establish an equivalent amount for wheat or other flour in everyday recipes. [S09]
- Siddiq et al. report **0.556 g/mL bulk density** for their red-kidney-bean-flour sample. This is a study-specific bulk-density observation, not a standardized household measure or a validated g/mL default for the listed product. The same study reports different properties for small-red-kidney and black-bean flour samples, reinforcing that the value is sample-specific. [S07]

No reviewed source gives the listed product's cup weight, spoon weight, household measure, product-specific density, or kitchen conversion. Whole-bean CNF portions do not apply. Keep **defaultDensity** null, **unitMappings** empty, and add no flour form, package yield, conversion, recipe link, or portion mapping.

No global same-ingredient form conversion or contextual ingredient substitution is supported. The noodle study's 10–50% experimental range is a formulation trial, not a reusable recipe substitution; the cupcake study used a separately heat-treated flour. Do not activate either study as a wheat-flour or whole-bean substitution rule. No bundled recipe line needs one.

## Evidence gaps and decision limit

The identity-only candidate is supported by an exact Canadian product-directory title and has a clear whole-bean owner to remain separate. **Current commercial status, a current manufacturer specification, and an exact Québec French flour label remain unverified.** The Celiac Canada listing is not real-time, the AGT public product list does not name kidney flour, and the located certificate is expired. This blocks stronger claims about availability, process, cultivar, measures, or substitution, but does not erase the source-named catalog identity. Keep the French compound explicitly provisional and seek a current package or manufacturer sheet before adding any product form or numeric behavior.
