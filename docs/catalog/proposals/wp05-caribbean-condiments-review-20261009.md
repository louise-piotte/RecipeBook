# WP05 Section Z: Caribbean condiment product-boundary review

- **Review and source-access date:** 2026-10-09.
- **Scope:** Jamaican jerk paste; Haitian epis and pikliz; and Trinidadian/Caribbean green seasoning.
- **Status:** integrated on 2026-10-09 after coordinator review; the broad Section Z item remains open.
- **Source IDs:** `WP05-CAR-S01` through `WP05-CAR-S17`. The `WP05-CAR-` namespace was checked in the current ledger and all existing proposals before assigning these IDs; no collision was found.

## Coordinator recommendation

Recommend four compound-identity candidates in `SAUCE_AND_CONDIMENT`, with regional names retained:

1. **Jamaican jerk paste** — recognize a wet paste seasoning product. Keep the relation to commercial dry jerk powder unresolved at catalog level: a source calls powder and paste commercial types of jerk seasoning, but does not establish a universal recipe, form conversion, or equivalence to every dry mix. Keep ready-made jerk marinades and jerk barbecue/table sauces outside the paste identity.
2. **Haitian epis** — recognize the fresh blended seasoning base as a Haitian product. Keep it separate from Trinidadian green seasoning, sofrito, and other seasoning bases even where sources compare their cooking role. A Québec Haitian product line sells a dried powder as a dehydrated version of that maker’s marinade; do not generalize that brand relationship into a global form or conversion.
3. **Haitian pikliz** — recognize a distinct pickled vegetable condiment, separate from epis, generic slaw, sauerkraut, and hot sauce. Sources describe varying household and commercial preparations; do not define a universal formula or assume every product follows the same fermentation process.
4. **Trinidadian green seasoning** — recognize the Trinidadian preparation as a distinct compound seasoning. Do not create one generic Caribbean-wide identity or alias: sources explicitly distinguish island and household versions, and distinguish green seasoning from Haitian epis and Jamaican jerk seasoning. Leave the wider Caribbean umbrella unresolved.

The identity evidence supports these four review candidates. **Measurement and substitution research remains incomplete for all four.** No density, mass-volume conversion, unit mapping, package yield, form conversion, or substitution rule is proposed. Keep dry jerk seasoning, powdered epis, Jamaican green seasoning, and other island-specific green seasonings as boundary notes or separate future review candidates; do not merge them by shared words, herbs, or culinary use.
