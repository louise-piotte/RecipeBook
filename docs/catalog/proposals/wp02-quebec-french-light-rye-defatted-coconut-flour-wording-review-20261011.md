- **Reviewed/source access date:** 2026-10-11.
- **Scope:** independent review of the two provisional fr-CA displays in [`wp02-flour-starch-residual-coverage-review-20261011.md`](wp02-flour-starch-residual-coverage-review-20261011.md). This note reviews wording only; it does not reopen the candidate identity or process boundaries. The accepted wording was integrated in [`the batch README`](../batches/wp02-a-b-residual-flour-boundaries-integration-20261011/README.md).
- **Disposition:** accept `farine de seigle claire` as the light-rye display, with `farine de seigle blanche` and `farine de seigle légère` as bounded search aliases. Accept `farine de noix de coco dégraissée` as the descriptive display for the narrow defatted product candidate, with no aliases.
- **Source-ID prefix:** `WP02-QC-FLOUR-FR-20261011-S01` through `S07`; checked against the research ledger and catalog documents before use.

## Evidence and boundaries

### Light/white rye flour

The OQLF entry for generic `farine de seigle` is useful for the base Québec term and notes that light and dark rye flours differ, but it was last updated in 1982 and does not name a light grade. The former Canadian General Standards Board specification paired **Light Rye Flour / Farine de seigle claire**. The Government of Canada catalogue says that standard was withdrawn in April 2020, so it is historical bilingual class/name evidence only; its former specifications must not be treated as current requirements. [S01–S03]

TERMIUM Plus marks `farine de seigle blanche` correct for **white rye flour** and describes that product as endosperm-only. Arva’s current French product page titles one item `Farine de seigle légère` and describes the same item as `farine de seigle claire, aussi appelée farine de seigle blanche`, made from rye endosperm. These sources support the two aliases for this candidate’s bounded light/white class. Arva is an Ontario mill, and its page is product evidence rather than an OQLF ruling. [S04–S05]

Use `farine de seigle claire` as the display because it has the closest direct bilingual pairing with English `light rye flour` and is the term Arva uses in its explanatory description. Keep `farine de seigle blanche` as an alias for the federally attested English/French white-rye pair. Keep `farine de seigle légère` as an alias because the current Canadian mill uses it as the very same product’s French title; its use remains manufacturer-attested rather than standardized. The alias set does not establish that all suppliers use identical extraction or milling thresholds. Do not absorb medium/dark rye, whole rye flour, rye meal, or pumpernickel meal.

### Defatted coconut flour

The proposed wording is understandable and grammatically agrees with `farine`. Current Québec retailer copy uses `farine de noix de coco` for a product whose description says its dried coconut flesh is defatted by pressing. Yupik’s French-language Canadian producer page uses the same broad title for its product and describes the coconut flesh as defatted, dried, and finely milled. Together these support the exact process adjective `dégraissée` in a more specific catalog display. They do not show that every item sold as `farine de noix de coco` is defatted, nor do they make the broad phrase a safe alias. [S06–S07]

The proposed display is an evidence-backed descriptive phrase, not a claim that OQLF or a Canadian labelling standard has established it as the preferred term. Retain no aliases, and preserve the candidate’s process boundary: coconut meat/flesh defatted before fine milling. Do not attach branded serving sizes, nutrition values, yields, conversions, or flour-replacement ratios to the generic candidate.
