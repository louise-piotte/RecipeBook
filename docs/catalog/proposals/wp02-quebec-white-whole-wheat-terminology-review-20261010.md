# WP02 independent Québec French wording review: white whole-wheat flour

**Review date:** 2026-10-10
**Status:** Proposal only; no shared catalog, ledger, plan, roadmap, or test files changed.
**Scope:** Independent Québec French terminology review for the active `ingredient-ref-white-whole-wheat-flour` identity, plus source-backed disposition of the adjacent mill descriptors left open in the WP02 plan and prior flour review.

## Recommendation

Retain the current bilingual display for the narrowly defined identity:

| Field | Current value | Review disposition |
|---|---|---|
| ID | `ingredient-ref-white-whole-wheat-flour` | Keep the existing ID; do not create a duplicate. |
| English display | `white whole-wheat flour` | Retain for flour explicitly identified as whole-grain white-wheat flour. |
| Québec French display | `farine intégrale de blé blanc` | Retain. It combines OQLF-attested `farine intégrale de blé` wording with the exact product wording and whole-grain claim of a Québec mill. This is well supported as a display for that identity, but the full compound is not an OQLF headword or a separately established generic standard. |
| English alias | `hard white whole wheat flour` | Leave as currently integrated; this review does not extend it. |
| French aliases | None | Keep none. In particular, do not add `farine de blé intégrale blanche` on the evidence below. |

The name is not a synonym for `farine blanche`, all-purpose flour, or every flour made from white wheat. In OQLF terminology, `farine intégrale de blé` denotes flour retaining every part of the grain, while `farine blanche` is wheat flour milled after the bran and germ have been removed [WP02-QCFT-20261010-S01, WP02-QCFT-20261010-S03]. The Québec mill’s exact `farine intégrale de blé blanc` product title says the product contains the whole grain [WP02-QCFT-20261010-S04]. Reading `blanc` as a descriptor of `blé` in this qualified whole-grain phrase is supported by that direct product label; it is not an OQLF ruling on the whole compound.

The source does not say it retains the whole grain; do not map it to white whole-wheat flour, all-purpose flour, or generic bread flour. “Blé blanc” and “très blanche” are separate wording in the product description. |
| `farine Manitoba` / `farine Manitoba Tipo 0` | Québec retailer pages attest a Caputo Manitoba Tipo 0 product and a separate Vecchio Molino Manitoba type 00 product [WP02-QCFT-20261010-S09, WP02-QCFT-20261010-S10]. | Hold as SKU/regional wording. These product labels do not establish a single Québec flour class or universal Manitoba alias, and their type numbers must remain product-specific. |
| `farine de blé intégrale blanche` | The phrase is quoted in a Québec trade interview, with no flour package, composition, or product identifier in the cited passage [WP02-QCFT-20261010-S07]. | Hold as a possible local variant phrase, not a French alias. Revisit only if a Québec manufacturer label or specification links the exact phrase to the same white whole-grain flour boundary. |

The original white whole-wheat boundary remains explicit: accept only flour that is identified as whole-wheat/whole-grain and as white wheat. A product described merely as white flour, white wheat flour, non-bleached flour, or flour for white bread does not meet that boundary.

## Bleaching and catalog-model limit

`Blanche` and `non blanchie` can appear together in Québec product wording, while OQLF’s `farine blanche` definition describes bran-and-germ removal. Therefore the color/refinement wording must not be used as an alias for `farine intégrale de blé blanc`. The active catalog already removed `farine blanche` from all-purpose aliases; this proposal recommends no change to that decision.

The current all-purpose search phrase `farine tout usage non blanchie` still does not preserve bleaching as a structured qualifier. That is a search/model limitation: this review does not split the all-purpose identity, create a bleaching form, or remove/change the existing phrase. Any future resolution should preserve the distinction between flour identity and product/process qualifiers.
