## Recommendation

**Hold the candidate outside the active catalog until the mill clarifies what “white” identifies and names the source-grain class or variety.** The current Québec manufacturer page and a Canadian retailer page verify a marketed flour product with “white” and “winter wheat” in its names. The manufacturer’s description only says the flour is milled from winter wheat. Neither page says whether “white” refers to the wheat kernel class or to the finished flour, identifies soft or hard wheat, names a variety, states grain origin, or assigns the product to Canada Eastern White Winter (CEWW).

The distinction matters. The current Canadian Grain Commission (CGC) class table describes CEWW as **soft white winter wheat**, but that class evidence does not assign La Milanaise’s product to CEWW. The product name and its 11.5% ± 1% protein claim are not a formal class or variety declaration. Do not infer CEWW from the Québec mill address, the word “winter,” the word “white,” protein, or the product’s use suggestions.

This is a bounded hold on a real commercial product phrase, not a claim that the product lacks a distinct identity. Reopen the candidate if La Milanaise identifies whether “white” refers to grain colour/class or flour appearance and supplies the source-grain class/variety and origin. A class-specific flour identity would also need the mill to name that class for this flour product; independent review should confirm the concise Québec French display before any French catalog label is activated.

## Québec French naming

La Milanaise’s current French page is the strongest Québec product-language source in this review. It titles the product **`Farine de blé Winter blanche non blanchie biologique`** and says it is milled from **`blé d’hiver`**. A Canadian retailer also lists **`Farine de blé blanche « Blé d’hiver » biologique à pain`** and describes it as `farine de blé d’hiver blanche`. These are product labels, not a Québec terminology ruling for a reusable ingredient-class name.

OQLF prefers `farine de blé tendre` for flour made from soft wheat and `blé tendre` for soft wheat. Those terms do not supply the missing softness/class fact. The CGC’s official French CEWW designation is `Blé blanc d'hiver, Est canadien (CEWW)`, but using this as a flour display would imply the product belongs to that class, which the mill has not said. Avoid converting the manufacturer’s English `Winter` into a class-bearing French alias or treating `farine de blé d’hiver blanche` as a validated generic Québec display. Hold the French catalog naming decision for a mill clarification and independent Québec review.

## Measurements, forms, and substitutions

- **Measures and density:** The manufacturer page provides no textual package size or household quantity; its format area is an image. The retailer lists a 20 kg bag and bulk pallet options. These are package sizes only. Neither source gives a cup, spoon, volume-to-mass conversion, default density in g/ml, or reusable unit mapping.
- **Product specifications:** La Milanaise lists protein at `11.5% ± 1%` and `Equivalent: T55`. Keep both as product-specific claims. They are not class-wide values, kitchen measures, or flour density.
- **Uses and forms:** The manufacturer recommends baguettes, breads, pasta, and describes pastry and bread use. The pages do not define a set of ingredient forms or a flour-form conversion. The product being sold as flour does not justify attaching a form to the wheat-berry row.
- **Substitutions:** “More flexible than traditional all-purpose flour” is a comparative product claim, not a quantified substitution instruction. No ratio, tested procedure, or context-scoped ingredient substitution is supported. Add no rule to or from all-purpose flour, bread flour, pastry flour, white whole-wheat flour, or soft-white wheat flour.
- **Yields and recipe links:** Package size and baking suggestions do not define a usable yield, recipe linkage, or ingredient-line substitution. Add none.

If the product is reconsidered, keep unsupported numeric and conversion data empty unless new direct evidence specifically supports a general flour measure or scoped substitution. Do not transfer the 20 kg format, protein percentage, or T55 statement to a broader wheat-flour class.
