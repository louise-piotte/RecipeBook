# WP04 Section P — smoked cod market-boundary review (R184)

- **Review date:** 2026-10-16.
- **Scope:** Canadian and Québec market wording for smoked cod products, and whether it supports an identity-only preserved-food reference.
- **Recommendation:** **Add** `smoked cod / morue fumée` as an identity-only market group, proposed ID `ingredient-ref-smoked-cod`, category `PROTEIN`, with no aliases, forms, measures, mappings, conversions, yields, recipe links, or substitutions. This follows the live catalog's `smoked trout / truite fumée` group: a market product identity can be useful without assigning every SKU a species, cut, or smoke process. The identity must not imply *Gadus morhua* for every item sold as `morue fumée`; keep `morue noire fumée` and other named species boundaries separate.
- **Integrated (2026-10-16):** The identity-only reference and evidence record are in the bundled catalog and research ledger; see the [R184 integration report](../batches/wp02-wp03-wp04-r184-gap-review-integration-20261016/README.md).

## Boundary decision

The open Section P preservation checklist explicitly includes smoked fish. Prior focused work covers smoked haddock, smoked salmon, smoked trout, smoked mackerel, smoked herring fillets in oil, and several other preserved seafood states. This review selects only the **smoked cod market product group**; it does not activate a universal smoked-fish form or reopen those decisions.

Current Québec-facing evidence now supports the bilingual market identity directly. Seafood Canada's French **Planchette fumée** identifies its morue component as fresh Gaspésie cod smoked with maple wood, salt, and brown sugar. The 200 g product is an assortment of four hot-smoked fish and is ready to eat once thawed. Seafood Canada says its products are sold in Sobeys stores in Québec and Ontario. [S07–S08] AfricanFish.ca, a seller located in Sainte-Catherine, Québec, lists a standalone product titled **Morue Fumée**, describes it as hot-smoked white fish, and specifies a 5 kg carton. It advertises use in salads, pasta, soups, and stews. [S09] Les Pêcheries Gaspésiennes in Gaspé lists **filets de morue fumée** among its smoked products, and Pêcheries M.C. Gaspésie lists partner-supplied `Morue fumée`; the latter says smoked-product selection changes with seasons and arrivals. [S10–S11] These establish current French product wording and more than one commercial presentation, including a Québec retailer listing that is not a composite board.

OQLF's entry for **gades fumés** defines the category as smoked haddock, cod, cusk, hake, pollock, or a mixture, and explicitly says that for a single fish the appropriate wording includes **morue fumée**. It distinguishes that single-fish wording from the mixed category. [S12] This reinforces `morue fumée` as an intelligible Québec product term; it does not establish one cod species, cut, cure, or preparation for all products using that name.

The correct scope is therefore a **finished-food market group**, not a taxonomic species row or a universal processing form. The evidence spans hot-smoked products and a Canadian cold-smoked cod fillet listing; the Québec product sources do not consistently name a species or define one shared smoke/salt process. That is comparable to the active identity-only `smoked trout / truite fumée` group, which was accepted from Québec listings despite species/process differences across products. The cod group should follow that precedent while retaining SKU-level specificity. The English Canadian retailer currently lists **Frozen Cold Smoked Cod Fillets**, smoked by Seabright Smokehouse, in one-pound packages. [S02]

OQLF's edible-fish entry pairs `morue` with `cod` and *Gadus morhua*. [S01] That terminology supports the ordinary French/English name pair, but is not evidence that every unspecified species in a retail listing is *G. morhua*. Separately, a current Montréal retailer sells **morue noire fumée tranchée**, an Organic Ocean product from British Columbia. `Morue noire` is already an active sablefish (*Anoplopoma fimbria*) search alias, while OQLF does not retain it as the preferred Québec fish-product term. Keep it owned by the sablefish identity and do not add it as an alias to smoked cod or infer the SKU's taxon from its title. [S04–S05]

## Ownership and picker audit

I checked the current plan, Section P proposals and batches, active references and forms, legacy-import identities, and research ledger for `smoked cod`, `smoked cod fillets`, `morue fumée`, `filets de morue fumés`, the proposed ID `ingredient-ref-smoked-cod`, and the proposal source-ID prefix. No exact owner or focused smoked-cod packet was found. Current snapshot counts are **1,561 active references, 122 forms, 61 legacy-import candidates, and 1,906 ledger records**. There is no exact smoked-cod reference/form/legacy owner, nor a ledger identity record for these terms.

The live phrase-first search is implemented in [`IngredientSearch.kt`](../../../app/src/main/java/app/recipebook/domain/localization/IngredientSearch.kt). In the active reference snapshot:

| Query | Current relevant results | Counterfactual with `smoked cod / morue fumée` | Boundary consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `smoked cod` | No result | One result | Exact English product wording is a singleton. |
| `morue fumée` | No result | One result | Exact Québec French product wording is a singleton. |
| `cod` | Arctic cod, Atlantic cod, Pacific cod, sablefish, salt cod | Those five plus smoked cod | Broad search remains multi-result. `Atlantic tomcod` is not an exact `cod` phrase in the current picker. |
| `morue` | Arctic cod, Atlantic cod, Pacific cod, sablefish, salt cod | Those five plus smoked cod | Broad search remains multi-result. |
| `morue noire` | Sablefish via its active search alias | Same | Preserve sablefish ownership. |
| `morue noire fumée` | No result | No result | The picker does not expand a shorter alias to a longer query; do not add this phrase to smoked cod. |

Keep aliases empty. In particular, do not assign `black cod`, `morue noire`, or `morue noire fumée` to the proposed row. Also do not add broad `cod`, `morue`, `smoked fish`, `poisson fumé`, `filet`, `fillet`, or process/cut variants as aliases: names already search across multiple active rows, and source-level product distinctions do not need to be encoded as synonyms.

## Bundled-recipe demand and numbers

The read-only corpus audit parsed all **114 manifest-listed recipe files** and **1,123 structured ingredient rows**, checking ingredient rows and decoded recipe JSON for `smoked cod`, `smoked cod fillets`, `morue fumée`, smoked cod portions/fillets, and relevant product-state combinations. It found **zero matching recipe files and zero matching ingredient rows**. There is no recipe link, recipe-line amount, or method to connect to the new identity. This is market-coverage support rather than demonstrated bundle demand.

The 200 g Seafood Canada board, 5 kg AfricanFish carton, and one-pound cold-smoked fillet package are source-specific sale packages. They do not establish a generic portion, recipe amount, drained or edible yield, density, mass-volume conversion, or smoked-to-raw yield. No reviewed source provides a reusable substitution ratio. Add no forms, measures, mappings, conversions, yields, recipe links, or substitutions between smoked cod, Atlantic/Pacific/Arctic cod, salt cod, sablefish, smoked haddock, or other smoked fish. Keep species, cut, salt/cure, smoke temperature/wood, storage, and ready-to-eat status at the product/SKU level.

## Recommendation

**Add** the identity-only market group `smoked cod / morue fumée`, proposed as `ingredient-ref-smoked-cod` in `PROTEIN`, with empty aliases and no forms, measures, unit mappings, density, conversions, yields, recipe links, or substitutions. Current Québec product listings and OQLF's explicit single-fish term establish a sufficiently clear market boundary, even though they do not establish one species or processing profile for every SKU. The active identity-only smoked-trout group is a directly relevant precedent for representing this product group without asserting one species/process. Do not equate the new group with *Gadus morhua* at the catalog level, and preserve the separate sablefish owner for `morue noire`. No bundled recipe calls for smoked cod, so attach no recipe quantity or substitution. Keep species, cut, cure, smoke process, packaging, storage, and readiness specific to each future product source.
