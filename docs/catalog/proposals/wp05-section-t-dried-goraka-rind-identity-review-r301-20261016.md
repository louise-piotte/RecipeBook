The original hold asked for corroboration of both species and edible part. Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew accepts *Garcinia quaesita* as a species native to Sri Lanka [S01]. A Sri Lanka country report identifies the species by the common name Goraka and describes its sun-dried fruit rind as a curry condiment and fish-curing ingredient [S02]. A study using authenticated *G. quaesita* material calls it Rathgoraka and states that the dried rind is used as a souring spice in traditional curries [S03]. A 2025 Sri Lankan study independently identifies commercially available dried *G. quaesita* rinds as Goraka [S04]. These sources resolve the dried-rind identity without assigning every Garcinia product or regional souring fruit to this record.

### Taxonomic and product boundaries

Some older Sri Lankan material used *Garcinia cambogia* and other names as synonyms for *G. quaesita*. Current Kew taxonomy accepts *G. quaesita* separately [S01], while Kew lists *G. cambogia* as a synonym of accepted *G. gummi-gutta* [S05]. The Sri Lankan cooking study also distinguishes *G. quaesita* from *G. gummi-gutta* [S03]. Therefore, do not use `Garcinia cambogia` or `Malabar tamarind` as goraka aliases. Keep dried kokum (*G. indica*), tamarind, and the compounded goraka paste candidate separate. The older IDB and Export Development Board sources describe rind use and a paste-product context but do not supply the botanical resolution; the earlier local recipe establishes one use of the name, not the species [WP06-SA-20261010-S22–S23, S38].

`pelure séchée de goraka` is a descriptive French display retaining the borrowed ingredient name. The OQLF searches returned no goraka terminology entry, and no Québec grocery wording for this culinary product was identified in the reviewed evidence; the display is not presented as an official term. The source-backed `goraka séché` phrase is retained as a French search alias.
