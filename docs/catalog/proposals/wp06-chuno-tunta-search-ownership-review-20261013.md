# WP06 Andean chuño and tunta: refreshed boundary and search-ownership review

- **Date:** 2026-10-16
- **Status:** Research refresh; the two supported identities are already integrated as `ingredient-ref-chuno-negro` and `ingredient-ref-tunta`. This memo proposes no catalog, ledger, recipe, plan, or roadmap edits.
- **Scope:** Recheck the black chuño / tunta boundary against the current WP06 plan and archive; distinguish both products from unqualified chuño, moraya, papa seca, generic dried potato, potato flour, and potato starch; verify current search ownership and recipe demand; retain Québec French wording as provisional where no authoritative term was found.

## Recommendation

Retain **black chuño** and **tunta / chuño blanco** as distinct, identity-only potato products. The existing names and boundaries are supported by Bolivian government material, Peruvian agricultural research and standards, procurement nomenclature, and food-table entries. Their processes differ: black chuño is described as frozen, trodden, and dried; white chuño/tunta includes a water-immersion step. These descriptions support separate identities, not a universal manufacturing recipe or a conversion rule. [WP06-CHUNO-TUNTA-20261009-S01–S06; WP06-CHUNO-TUNTA-RESCAN-20261013-S01]

## Product boundary and evidence limits

| Product | Supported boundary | Evidence limit |
|---|---|---|
| Black chuño | Bolivia’s Ministry calls it `chuño negro` / “normal” and contrasts it with the pale `chuño tunta`. INIA research describes black chuño as a freeze-dried product made by freezing, treading, and drying. The Peruvian National Institute of Health lists `papa chuño negro` separately. [WP06-CHUNO-TUNTA-20261009-S01, S02, S08] | The research describes regional processing variants; the ministry overview is not a product standard. The cited use as cooked food does not provide a universal preparation instruction. |
| Tunta / chuño blanco | INIA’s Puno paper explicitly calls the product `chuño blanco (tunta)` and describes one local process that includes freezing, water immersion, peeling, and drying. Peru Compras approved the commodity name `Tunta o Chuño Blanco`; an FAO cookbook names `white chuño (tunta)` in a quinoa-and-lamb broth; INACAL has tunta-specific standards. [WP06-CHUNO-TUNTA-20261009-S03, S04, S06, S10; WP06-CHUNO-TUNTA-RESCAN-20261013-S01] | The detailed INIA process is one field study and the INACAL standards are Peruvian standards; neither defines every regional use of `tunta` or `chuño blanco`. The source-specific process is not a household recipe. |
| Papa seca | Peru’s INACAL describes NTP 011.802:2017 as a papa seca standard and gives a route of selecting, washing, peeling, cooking, cutting, and drying potato. Its 2026 standards summary lists papa seca and tunta under separate technical standards. The Peruvian food table also has separate entries. [WP06-CHUNO-TUNTA-20261009-S05, S08; WP06-CHUNO-TUNTA-RESCAN-20261013-S01] | The cited standard scope is Peru-specific. It supports a product distinction, not a global definition of every market item sold as papa seca. Perú Travel’s carapulcra page is one dish-specific preparation; it does not establish a general conversion or preparation rule. [WP06-SOUTHAM-20261009-S03] |
| Unqualified chuño, moraya, generic dried potato | The sources use these words with varying scope. INIA distinguishes black chuño, white chuño/tunta, and moraya in one passage; another passage groups `tunta o moraya`. The Peruvian food table separately lists moraya products and identifies packaged `chuño de papa` as potato starch/fécule. [WP06-CHUNO-TUNTA-20261009-S03, S07, S08] | This vocabulary variation is insufficient to assign a broad alias or to equate products across regions. Generic drying language does not identify a single market product. |

Bolivia’s Ministry reports black chuño as cooked after soaking and commonly served with ground peanut or other flavorings; it also describes tunta as a food ingredient in western and eastern Bolivian cooking. FAO’s French technical chapter names chuño blanco in soups and stews and papa seca in carapulca. These are examples of culinary use only, not endorsed preparation directions or substitution evidence. [WP06-CHUNO-TUNTA-20261009-S01, S09]

## Display names and Québec French

No authoritative Québec French equivalent for either product was established in this review. As of 2026-10-16, targeted OQLF GDT and TERMIUM queries for `chuño`, `chuno`, and `tunta` did not surface a matching term entry. This is a limited search result, not proof that no Québec term exists. The OQLF defines `pomme de terre` as the generic tuber name and distinguishes `fécule` as starch in underground plant parts; neither supplies a product name for Andean chuño or tunta. FAO’s French technical text retains the Spanish product names `chuño blanco` and `chuño negro`. [WP06-CHUNO-TUNTA-20261009-S09, S11]

| Product | EN display | fr-CA display | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Black chuño | `black chuño` | `chuño negro` | Retain; French wording remains provisional and source-named. |
| Tunta / white chuño | `tunta (white chuño)` | `tunta (chuño blanco)` | Retain; French wording remains provisional and source-named. |

Use `chuño blanco` only as a scoped name for the cited tunta product pairing. Do not add bare `chuño`, `moraya`, `muraya`, `chuño de papa`, `papa seca`, `dried potato`, flour, or starch as aliases to either whole-tuber identity.

### Exact owners and live search behavior

- The only exact target-name owners in references are `black chuño` / `chuño negro` → `ingredient-ref-chuno-negro`. The full tunta display strings normalize to `tunta white chuno` and `tunta chuno blanco`; phrase search maps `tunta`, `white chuño`, and `chuño blanco` to `ingredient-ref-tunta`. Both integrated rows have empty alias arrays.
- Exact target-family keys found no match in the 122 forms or 61 legacy candidates. No form is attached to either target identity. The integrated IDs are present once each in the active reference catalog; there is no duplicate row or competing legacy owner.
- Live ingredient-reference search returns `chuño` and accent-folded `chuno` → both target IDs; `black chuño`, `black chuno`, and `chuño negro` → black chuño only; `tunta`, `white chuño`, and `chuño blanco` → tunta only. `moraya`, `muraya`, `chuños`, `tuntas`, and `chuño de papa` return no result. The broad `chuño` result is the display-name phrase match, not an alias or equivalence rule.
- Comparator exact owners are `papa seca` → `ingredient-ref-papa-seca`, `potato flour` → `ingredient-ref-potato-flour`, `farine de pommes de terre` → `ingredient-ref-potato-flour`, `potato starch` → `ingredient-ref-potato-starch`, and `potato` / `pomme de terre` → `ingredient-ref-potato`. Phrase search for generic `dried potato` currently returns papa seca because that phrase occurs in its English display `dried potato (papa seca)`; there is no standalone generic alias. Broad phrase search also returns sweet-potato flour for `potato flour`, and five results for `potato starch`: modified potato starch, raw unmodified potato starch, potato starch, sweet-potato starch, and sweet potato starch noodles. The exact-name owner should not be confused with the complete phrase-search result set.
- The proposed boundary terms have zero exact ingredient-row hits and the two integrated target IDs have zero bundled recipe links. `papa seca`, potato flour, and potato starch also have zero direct recipe-reference hits. Generic fresh `potato` has two current direct recipe-reference rows. No relink or recipe edit is proposed.
