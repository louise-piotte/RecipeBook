# WP05 Section X R607: satay-paste product boundary review

## Review record

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-18.
- **Status:** Integrated bounded hold; no shared catalog or ledger data changed.
- **Open checklist:** Section X continues to track satay paste separately from the integrated `satay sauce / sauce satay` identity and remains open in the [global plan](../../RecipeBook-global-ingredient-catalog-plan.txt#L9215).
- **Candidate:** General packaged-condiment wording `satay paste`; no single product or preparation is assigned.
- **Proposal-local source IDs:** `WP05-SATAY-PASTE-BOUNDARY-R607-20261018-S01` through `S04`. These IDs are not shared-ledger allocations.

## Recommendation

**Keep a general satay-paste identity unallocated pending a current Canadian/Québec product source that defines a coherent product scope and supports a matched French name.** Do not add an active reference, alias, legacy candidate, ledger decision, form, measure, recipe link, conversion, yield, or substitution.

Current product pages use the phrase for distinct preparations. Tungsan Food Industries in Singapore names a paste formulated for Satay Bee Hoon, describes it as a dipping sauce or cooking base, and provides one product-specific preparation that mixes the paste with water to make gravy [S01]. A separate European retailer lists a Healthy Boy/Dek Som Boon 240 g item as `Satay Paste`; its page uses Spanish product wording and does not establish Canadian distribution or a Québec French label [S02]. Umami Shop Canada has an indexed product title `Pho Satay Paste 14.11oz`, but its direct product page could not be retrieved during this review. The pho qualifier and missing ingredient/direction details do not establish that it is the paste used for satay skewers or Satay Bee Hoon [S03].

Québec terminology does not resolve that boundary. OQLF's `saté` / `satay` entry is in the food/dish domain and describes marinated meat or seafood threaded onto skewers and grilled; it does not name a paste product [S04]. Lee Kum Kee Canada separately labels its 220 g product `Sauce satay` in French and describes it as a sauce for marinades, dipping, or barbecue, consistent with the already active sauce row [reused `WP05-EASP-FU-20261010-S12`]. That sauce evidence does not make paste a synonym or establish a general French paste name.

The older Southeast Asian-condiments proposal held satay paste because the Prima Taste items reviewed there were complete sauce or kit products rather than pastes. R607 checks new direct paste-product evidence but does not widen that evidence into one global identity. Reopen only when a manufacturer, importer, or Canadian/Québec retailer identifies the specific product, provides composition or use detail sufficient to distinguish it from satay sauce and `pho satay` preparations, and supports a product-matched French display.
