## Recommendation

**Hold this candidate outside the active reference catalog and research ledger.** Current manufacturer and primary-research sources establish that pistachio kernels can be pressed for oil and the residue milled into food flour. They also show that partially and more fully defatted samples can differ materially. A current US maker sells a raw, partially defatted powder/flour and uses the phrase `partially defatted pistachio flour` among its product search terms. This confirms a distinct marketed product class and English wording, but it does not satisfy the prior review's explicit threshold for a Canadian/Québec product review before allocating a separate row and French display.

The Canadian evidence found in this pass is limited to CFIA's grouped import class `Farine, semoule et poudre de pistache`, OQLF's base nut term `pistache`, and a Canada-facing listing for unqualified Bronte pistachio flour whose ingredient is pistachios. None attests a Canadian or Québec product labelled as defatted/partially defatted pistachio flour, or establishes a Québec French product name for it. Live Oil by Lesna's current product is sold by a maker based in Illinois; its company page describes delivery throughout the United States, and neither page establishes Canadian availability or a Québec French label. Targeted searches for the qualified English and French product phrases returned no Canadian/Québec product-label source. This is a bounded search result, not proof that no such product is sold.

The French-market manufacturer evidence uses more than one construction: `farine déshuilée de pistache` in a French maker title, `poudre de pistache déshuilée` in its ingredient statement, and an Italian maker's `farine ... partiellement déshuilée`. These variants establish product usage in Europe, but not a stable Québec display. Huilerie Beaujolaise's product and category pages were unavailable on direct review; a stale search-index excerpt is retained only as a research lead and is not used to support current product or numeric claims. Since the prior pistachio review explicitly reserved this decision for Canadian/Québec review, keep the candidate unallocated until product-level regional evidence or independent Québec terminology review resolves the display. ## Numeric evidence and substitution screen

No reviewed source provides a reusable mass-per-volume or bulk-density value, a household cup/spoon measure, a package-to-recipe yield, or a conversion for defatted pistachio flour. The available numbers describe products or research samples:

- La Maison de la Pistache lists one 300 g product, states 30 g fat and 40 g protein per 100 g for that SKU, and suggests using 10–20% with wheat flour or adding it to cream preparations. Live Oil by Lesna lists a 454 g net package and broad culinary uses. Package masses and qualitative use lists do not give a household volume or density. Huilerie Beaujolaise's direct product and category pages were unavailable, so no cached package or recipe quantities from that listing are used here. [S04, S12]
- Pariani's 2025 catalog identifies partially defatted pistachio-flour product classes but does not establish a Canadian product or household measure. [S05]
- Ling et al. distinguish full-fat, partially defatted, and totally defatted pistachio-kernel flour. In their samples, lipid was 51.84, 21.90, and 0.59 g/100 g dry matter respectively. The paper ties those samples to different oil-removal pathways; these values show process variation but do not define a commercial threshold. Its sorption study does not provide a household measure or volume conversion. [S06]
- Mendoza-Pérez et al. report that extraction system and pretreatment affect flour characteristics; their hydraulic-press sample had water absorption capacity up to 2.75 g/g, while single-screw samples were around 2 g/g. Water absorption capacity is water mass absorbed by flour mass, not flour bulk density. [S07]
- TwoCups lists 105 g per US cup for **unqualified** pistachio flour, but does not identify a product, oil-removal state, grind, or reproducible fill method. Do not apply it to this candidate or import it as a density or unit mapping. [S10]

For substitution, the 2021 Guardianelli et al. study tested one Argentine oil-industry pistachio by-product flour at 5, 15, and 25 g per 100 g wheat flour. It reports changes in dough hydration and rheology and warns that high levels could affect breadmaking; it does not establish a source-neutral replacement ratio for other recipes or products. [S08] Puratos uses `poudre de pistache déshuilée` in one specific chocolate coating and says it is required there for gloss and tempering performance; that is a confectionery role and formulation, not a general flour substitution rule. [S09] Live Oil by Lesna describes baking and other culinary applications but gives no replacement ratio. [S12]

Keep all numeric reference fields empty and substitution rules untouched. If a future regional product review allocates a separate identity, measure research must still be assessed independently and against that product/process. The current ordinary pistachio-flour ledger's generic 105 g/cup estimate is not evidence for a defatted product.
