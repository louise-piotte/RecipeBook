# WP04 Section R — Plant Beverage and Coconut Product Boundaries

Date: 2026-10-12  
Status: proposal only; no canonical or shared integration files changed  
Scope: oat, soy, almond, rice, and coconut beverages; culinary coconut milk; coconut cream; and sweetened cream of coconut.

This review covers the open plant-beverage and coconut-product checklist items. It does not decide plant cream, whipping alternatives, plant yogurt, butter or cheese alternatives, egg replacers, or other beverage bases. Source IDs are local to this proposal and are not entries in the shared research ledger.

## Recommendation summary

- Treat each plant beverage as a product identity distinct from its source ingredient. Proposed Canadian/Québec names are oat beverage / boisson d’avoine, soy beverage / boisson de soya, almond beverage / boisson d’amande, rice beverage / boisson de riz, and coconut beverage / boisson de noix de coco. Use the existing DAIRY_AND_ALTERNATIVE category if accepted.
- Do not add a catch-all plant-based beverage identity. OQLF’s umbrella term describes a product family; source plant and formulation remain meaningful product differences. A packaged beverage may include water, oil, salt, flavouring, stabilizers, or added vitamins and minerals, and therefore is not an alias for oats, soybeans, almonds, rice, or coconut flesh.
- Keep the existing coconut milk identity for the thicker culinary product. Retain its canned coconut milk match for the three exact recipe lines. Do not map carton coconut beverage wording to that identity.
- Keep the existing cream of coconut reference separate from a candidate coconut cream / crème de coco reference. For the sweetened cocktail product, propose crème de coco sucrée as a clearer French display candidate. Do not use the unqualified French phrase crème de noix de coco as an automatic alias for either product because current Québec sources use it for both product classes.
- Add no forms, recipe substitutions, conversions, densities, yields, or other numeric behavior. The corpus contains no beverage or coconut-cream ingredient line.

| Sweetened cream of coconut | Grace Foods Canada says its Coco López product is sweetened for piña coladas and cannot replace coconut cream in Thai or Southeast Asian cooking. Propose crème de coco sucrée as a clearer French display candidate if this reference is intended for the sweetened cocktail/pastry product class. The label guidance from CFIA says consumer prepackaged common names are bilingual and should identify the food specifically and accurately [S09]. It supports using source-specific beverage names and qualifiers where needed; it does not itself establish the individual OQLF product terms.

Sweetened and unsweetened plant beverages should remain product qualifiers when a source or recipe explicitly names them. OQLF notes that commercial plant beverages may be flavoured or enriched, and the two retailer/manufacturer examples show that packaged beverages can contain multiple ingredients. No universal ingredient list, nutrition profile, behaviour, or substitution can be inferred from the source plant alone. No sweetened/unsweetened beverage form is justified by the current corpus.

## Unresolved cases and limits

1. There is no recipe demand for the five plant beverages, coconut cream, or coconut-water-based drinks. Their proposed identities are catalog coverage candidates only.
2. The OQLF coconut-beverage term can sometimes describe a drink made from coconut water. Exact product scope and a safe short alias remain unresolved.
3. OQLF’s crème de coco definition and a retailer product label support the coconut-cream name, but retail products can differ in composition and processing. No general formula or recipe behaviour is established.
4. The French commercial wording crème de noix de coco overlaps between a sweetened cream-of-coconut product and products sold as coconut cream. A source-specific or qualified name is needed; the unqualified phrase should not route searches automatically.
5. The legacy almond milk and oat milk candidates have no sources and no recipe rows. Their migration into any new reference or search alias list requires a separate integration decision.
6. The current recipe 081 can note is already covered by prior package-count research. No brand, general can size, package yield, or edible-mass rule is inferred here.
7. No density, unit mapping, cooking conversion, yield, form, substitution, or numerical behaviour is supported. No tests or emulator checks were run for this documentation-only proposal.
