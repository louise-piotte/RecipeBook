# WP04 fish balls and surimi product-boundary review

- **Integration status:** Root accepted identity-only fish balls and added an unresolved surimi boundary candidate to the catalog/ledger on 2026-10-11.
- **Review date:** 2026-10-11.
- **Scope:** generic fish balls and surimi naming/product boundaries. Bonga dried-fish powder is excluded.
- **Disposition:** Add one identity-only `PROTEIN` reference for `fish balls / boulettes de poisson`. Keep it separate from surimi, fish cakes, and imitation seafood. Keep unqualified `surimi` unresolved because Canadian retail usage can refer to a finished ready-to-use product while technical sources use it for the fish-paste base. Do not model either relationship as an alias or same-ingredient form.
- **Corpus demand:** no bundled ingredient row names fish balls, surimi, surimi seafood, or imitation crab. Recipe 019 is titled “Crab Balls,” but its seafood ingredient is `1 lb jumbo lump crab meat`; the title does not establish fish-ball or surimi demand.

## Recommendation

Promote an identity-only `ingredient-ref-fish-balls` reference under `PROTEIN`, with English display **fish balls** and French display **boulettes de poisson**. OQLF's GDT pairs the preferred French term `boulette de poisson` with English `fish ball` [reused source `WP04-BONGA-POWDER-20261011-S08`]. A current Metro Québec listing uses the bilingual product labels **Frozen Fish Ball / Boulette de poisson surgelée**, with the same product number on each language page [S08]. This is enough to distinguish the formed fish-ball product class from surimi paste and actual crab meat; it does not establish one species, ingredient formula, or preparation state for all products called fish balls.

If integrated, keep the row broad and identity-only. Use `fish ball` and `boulette de poisson` as singular search aliases for the plural displays, but do not add `surimi`, `surimi fish balls`, `fish cake`, `fish cakes`, `imitation crab`, `crab ball`, `shrimp ball`, or `squid ball` as aliases. The identity should not default to a species or to fresh, frozen, raw, cooked, fried, stuffed, or ready-to-eat state. No form, amount, density, unit mapping, yield, conversion, or substitution is supported.

Keep `surimi` as a candidate rather than adding an unqualified reference in this batch. CFIA defines surimi as highly refined minced-fish paste and says finished products should use their generally known name with descriptors where appropriate; a finished surimi product imitating crab, shrimp, or scallop must be clearly identified as an imitation [reused source `WP04-DOYS-S04`]. OQLF separately distinguishes `surimi` (fish protein paste with cryoprotectants), `surimi brut` (fish protein paste without additives), and `pâte de surimi` (surimi mixed with other ingredients for imitation-seafood manufacture) [S01–S03]. Meanwhile, Québec-market listings call ready-to-use shredded imitation products `râpé de surimi`, `flocons de surimi`, or `bâton de surimi` [S10–S11]. Without a recipe line or a precisely named product, an unqualified `surimi` reference would not tell the user whether the recipe means the paste base or a finished imitation product. Do not use `surimi` as an alias or form of fish balls, `crab meat`, or imitation crab. Keep imitation crab / `similicrabe` separate from actual `crab meat / chair de crabe` [reused source `WP04-CRAB-MEAT-20261011-S03`].

At proposal time, the candidate IDs `ingredient-ref-fish-balls` and `ingredient-ref-surimi` had no active or ledger owner. The proposal-time normalized exact-key audit found no exact match for `fish ball(s)`, `boulette(s) de poisson`, `surimi`, `surimi paste`, `pâte de surimi`, `surimi brut`, `simili-fruits de mer`, `similicrabe`, or `imitation crab` in active reference names/aliases, form labels/match terms, or the legacy import identities; `crab meat / chair de crabe` was the only nearby active exact product key. Root has since added identity-only `ingredient-ref-fish-balls` and held `candidate-surimi-product-boundary`. The active fish-ball reference remains distinct from the actual crab-meat identity and all imitation terms; no active surimi identity was added. Search for the generic token `fish` may overlap more than one seafood result after integration, so preserve the full product qualifier in displays and never link an unqualified `fish` recipe line.

## Product and state boundaries

| Term or product | Evidence and boundary |
|---|---|
| **Fish balls / boulettes de poisson** | OQLF provides the Québec French/English term pair. Metro lists a frozen 180 g item as **Frozen Fish Ball / Boulette de poisson surgelée**; its page showed the product out of stock when reviewed, so it supports product wording and package declaration, not current availability or composition [S08]. Toronto manufacturer Ocean Food lists plain fish balls as one use of surimi, alongside crab balls, fried fish balls, and crab-flavoured fish balls; it says its fresh line is never frozen and its frozen line is pre-cooked [S05–S07]. These facts show product variation; they do not justify default forms or aliases for every fish-ball product. |
| **Assorted or modified fish balls** | T&T's 800 g assorted listing groups fish balls, fried fish balls, fish cakes, imitation-lobster fish balls, and veggie fish balls [S09]. Treat the assortment as a package of different labeled items, not one recipe ingredient identity. Keep fried, stuffed, crab-flavoured, imitation-lobster, vegetarian, shrimp, and squid products out of the generic aliases until a recipe or exact product requests one. |
| **Surimi base** | CFIA and OQLF describe a fish paste/raw ingredient base. Ocean Food, a Toronto manufacturer, says it sells its refined raw surimi paste for commercial and industrial use, made from a fish blend without preservatives; its standard bulk pack sizes are 5 lb and 20 lb [S05, S07]. The source does not identify species or disclose a complete formulation. Its “without preservatives” statement does not establish that the product has no additives, so do not equate it with OQLF's narrower `surimi brut` definition. |
| **Surimi-based finished products** | OQLF defines `simili-fruits de mer` as imitation shellfish made from textured, steamed surimi paste, and `similicrabe` as a surimi-based food imitating crab meat [S04 and reused source `WP04-CRAB-MEAT-20261011-S03`]. CFIA requires the finished name and an imitation descriptor when a product imitates more valuable seafood [reused source `WP04-DOYS-S04`]. These are separate finished foods, not aliases for the paste base or actual crab meat. |
| **Retail “surimi” products** | Metro lists Aquamar **Surimi shreds / râpé de surimi** (227 g) and Québec grocery listings use names such as **flocons de surimi** and **bâton de surimi** for ready-to-use products [S10–S11]. The retailer pages do not establish these names as synonyms for raw surimi paste. Keep the finished product's descriptor and composition attached to its own future identity if recipe demand requires one. |
| **Fish cakes / croquettes** | T&T lists fish cakes separately from fish balls in its assorted product, and Ocean Food describes separate kamaboko/fish-cake products [S06–S09]. OQLF's `croquette de poisson` has its own definition for a mixture shaped and fried; a fish cake or croquette is not an alias for a round fish ball [S12]. No fish cake or croquette identity is proposed here. |

## Recipe, measurements, and substitutions

The bundled package contains 114 recipes and 1,123 ingredient rows. An exact scan of `originalText` and `ingredientName` found no fish-ball, surimi, surimi-imitation, imitation-crab, or crab-stick ingredient line. Recipe 019's “Crab Balls” title is not a fish-ball line: its seafood ingredient is specifically jumbo lump crab meat. No recipe link should be added for the proposed fish-ball identity, and the absence of recipe demand leaves the unqualified surimi candidate unresolved.

Package declarations are not kitchen measures: Metro's 180 g fish-ball pack and Ocean Food's 5 lb / 20 lb bulk surimi packs do not provide pieces-per-weight, preparation yield, density, or a recipe serving basis. Product examples for soup, noodles, hotpot, or barbecue are usage suggestions, not a basis for conversion. Do not add count-to-mass or mass-to-volume mappings, densities, package yields, or conversions.

Do not add a global substitution among fish balls, raw surimi paste, surimi crab/imitation crab, actual crab meat, fish cakes, or other seafood balls. Although some fish balls are made from surimi, a paste input and a finished formed product are distinct ingredient identities; the manufacturer descriptions do not establish a culinary equivalence or safe cooking equivalence. No contextual substitute or warning rule is supported.
