# WP02 independent Québec French review: rye meal

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-11; sources accessed 2026-10-11.
- **Status:** Proposal only. This review changes no catalog data, ledger record, plan, workflow, recipe, code, or test.
- **Exact scope:** Québec French display `gruau de seigle` and the active French aliases `semoule de seigle` and `farine à pumpernickel` for `ingredient-ref-rye-meal`.
- **Out of scope:** Reopening the accepted rye-meal identity or its category, reviewing English display/aliases, adding rye grades, and assigning extraction, particle-size, measurement, conversion, recipe-link, yield, or substitution data.

The plan records independent bilingual review and rye extraction/grind research as open. This review addresses only the Québec French wording above; it does not resolve extraction/grind classes.

## Recommendation

| Catalog field | Disposition | Recommendation and evidence limit |
|---|---|---|
| French display `gruau de seigle` | **Accept** | TERMIUM lists it as the correct French term for *rye meal*. Current Québec product ingredient declarations also use the exact phrase, including a Bon Matin bread in the Québec food directory and a rye bread listed by Metro. OQLF’s headword `gruau` has other cereal-related senses, including the familiar cooked-oatmeal dish, but the full `de seigle` compound and ingredient-list context identify the milling ingredient. OQLF does not directly prescribe this exact compound. |

OQLF’s general `semoule` entry concerns wheat milling, so it is supporting lexical context rather than an OQLF ruling on rye. Keep the active identity as `rye meal / gruau de seigle`. This recommendation leaves its English aliases and the separate generic and whole-rye-flour identities as they are. The term review does not set a measurable grind threshold: TERMIUM’s qualitative `grossier` wording is descriptive and supplies no sieve size, particle distribution, or extraction rate.

## Evidence and interpretation

### Terminology authority

The TERMIUM Plus record is the only reviewed terminology-authority entry that gives these exact rye compounds. In its Food Industries / Breadmaking domain, it marks *rye meal* as correct, lists `gruau de seigle` and `semoule de seigle`, and uses `farine à pumpernickel` in its French context. The record says the product is made from whole-kernel rye, coarse and dark, for pumpernickel bread and rolls. It dates from 2001; it establishes a Canadian terminology record, not current Québec market prevalence or a technical grind standard. [S01]

The targeted OQLF search found no exact GDT entry for `gruau de seigle`, `semoule de seigle`, or `farine à pumpernickel`. The related OQLF entries clarify the limits of component-word evidence:

- `gruau` is a Québec/Canada term for a cooked-oatmeal dish in the food entry, whose note allows extension to dishes made with other cereals. A separate, older malt-crushing entry defines `gruau` as cereal particles and notes that some authors use it as a synonym of `semoule`. These are distinct domains; neither entry itself names rye meal. [S02–S03]
- `semoule` is defined in a cereal-milling entry for coarse particles from soft-wheat milling and durum-wheat pasta production. That definition cannot by itself establish `semoule de seigle`. [S04]
- `pumpernickel` is defined as a Westphalian dark rye bread, with `pumpernickel` and `pain de seigle noir` as preferred terms. The entry supports keeping the bare bread name distinct from an ingredient; it does not list `farine à pumpernickel`. Its English note describes the bread as made from coarsely ground whole rye meal. [S05]

### Québec product and culinary usage

The Québec government’s Répertoire des aliments québécois lists the Bon Matin Pain multigrain fin 600 g product, marked “Aliments préparés au Québec,” and identifies the maker in Laval. Its ingredient declaration places `gruau de seigle` in a cereal mixture with flaxseed, barley flakes, and cracked wheat. The record was last updated 2026-07-06. This is direct local ingredient wording, not a retail package of rye meal or a definition of its grind. [S06]

Metro’s French Canadian listing for a Natual Bakery rye bread also gives `Gruau de seigle` in its ingredients. Its Stonemill twelve-grain rye bread listing gives `Semoule de seigle biologique` and `Semoule de seigle` among the ingredients. These are market/culinary usage evidence for exact ingredient wording; they do not identify an independently sold rye-meal product, a universal bread formula, or a particle-size class. [S07–S08]

For comparison, Québec food retailer Mayrand lists a dark rye flour as `Farine de seigle foncée`. This attests that wording for a particular dark rye flour product; it does not establish that dark rye flour is synonymous with rye meal or with flour for pumpernickel. [S09]

## Boundary and uncertainty

- `gruau de seigle` has the strongest fr-CA support in this review: an exact TERMIUM term plus current Québec product declarations. Keep the full compound; do not add bare `gruau` as an alias, since OQLF documents both a common prepared-dish sense and other cereal-milling senses.
- `semoule de seigle` has both TERMIUM support and a Québec-market ingredient-label attestation. Retain it as a search alias, not as evidence for an unqualified semolina grind class.
- `farine à pumpernickel` is attested by TERMIUM in a context that equates it with this specific coarse whole-kernel product. Its Québec product-language evidence is weaker: the local examples reviewed name `gruau de seigle`, while the current dark-rye-flour retailer listing uses `farine de seigle foncée`. A bounded search that found no local example cannot prove the phrase is absent from Québec usage, which is why the recommendation is provisional rather than a claim that the term is wrong.
- Do not add bare `pumpernickel` or `pain de seigle noir` as ingredient aliases. The OQLF entry defines those as bread terms. Do not use `farine de seigle foncée`, `farine entière de seigle`, or whole-rye wording as aliases here; this review does not establish synonymy with the separate flour identities.
- No new rye extraction or grind classes, numerical thresholds, recipes, forms, conversions, or substitutions are proposed.
