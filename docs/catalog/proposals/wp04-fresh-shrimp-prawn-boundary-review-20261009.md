# WP04 Section P — fresh shrimp/prawn identity and preparation boundary

- **Packet type:** proposal-only identity research; no canonical catalog, ledger, recipe, plan, roadmap, code, generated file, or test was changed.
- **Review date:** 2026-10-09.
- **Scope:** the open generic fresh shrimp/prawn identity question, Québec English/French naming, and its boundary against dried shrimp, shrimp paste, stock/condiments, and preparation or market states.
- **Disposition:** hold/defer a global row. The Québec term pair is supportable, but a global shrimp/prawn scope and a safe search boundary are not established by the current recipe corpus or Canadian market-name evidence. Section P remains open.

## Recommendation

Do not add a generic fresh-shrimp or fresh-prawn identity in this pass. OQLF supports **shrimp / crevette** as the Québec English/French pair and lists English **prawn** as a United Kingdom usage. CFIA material shows that shrimp and prawn can overlap in English common usage, but the broad interchangeability note appears in an aquatic-animal-health fact sheet, not Canadian food-labelling guidance. CFIA’s Canadian Fish List is species-linked, and the recorded recipe corpus does not contain a shrimp or prawn request.

The open question is not whether those words can overlap. It is which culinary and market scope a single global recipe identity should cover. The live search index already returns the separate dried-shrimp identity for a bare “shrimp” query, and returns dried shrimp and Kapi for “crevette.” A new unqualified identity would therefore share short-query results with those distinct active products. This is not an exact normalized-key owner collision, but it is a real result-set overlap. With no recipe line requiring the broad identity, defer the row until a concrete request establishes its intended scope and the coordinator accepts the resulting picker choices.

If reopened, treat **shrimp / crevette** as the minimum terminology candidate, not “fresh shrimp” as a canonical identity. Do not activate **prawn** as a global alias solely from the overlapping usage: OQLF marks it as UK usage for generic *crevette*, and the CFIA interchangeability note is limited to animal-health context. Keep any eventual alias decision explicit and scoped. “Fresh,” “raw,” “cooked,” “frozen,” “previously frozen,” and “peeled/shell-on” describe separate product axes; the research supports no new form, conversion, measurement, substitution, recipe link, or preserved-product crosswalk here.

## Québec terminology and Canadian naming scope

OQLF’s GDT entry for **crevette** places it in edible-crustacean terminology, makes **crevette** the preferred French term, and gives **shrimp** as the English equivalent. It also lists **prawn** with the label Royaume-Uni. That is direct Québec terminology evidence for the pair, but it does not define one species or one worldwide commercial class [S01].

OQLF’s separate **crevette nordique** entry is a useful boundary example. It describes a Québec market name associated mainly with *Pandalus borealis* while noting that landings under the commercial name can mix *P. borealis* with *P. montagui*. It lists several English terms, including “northern prawn,” for that scoped entry and says other Québec market names were not normalized to avoid consumer-label confusion [S02]. These species- and market-specific names are not generic aliases for all shrimp or prawns.

CFIA’s guidance says its Fish List collects English and French names accepted for Canadian fish labelling and links names to species. Where a species is listed, only the common name(s) listed for that species should be used as its Canadian label name. CFIA also explains that the regulated common name can come from incorporated standards, other legislation, or—where no name is prescribed—the name generally known or a non-generic descriptive name [existing WP04-FINFISH-ID-20261009-S06; existing WP04-DOYS-S04]. This commercial-labelling scope does not dictate the recipe app’s taxonomy, but it is a reason not to treat a recipe alias as a universal Canadian species label.

CFIA’s aquatic-animal-health fact sheet lists examples with both terms, including “giant tiger prawn” and “white leg shrimp,” and states that “shrimp” and “prawn” can be used interchangeably. Because the fact sheet concerns animal disease rather than food labelling, use it as evidence of vernacular overlap only. CFIA’s separate susceptible-species page says its listed names do not replace the Fish List [S03, S05].

## Meaningful preparation and product boundary

This is a preservation/market claim, not a synonym for raw or uncooked [S04; existing WP04-DOYS-S04]. | Do not use “fresh” as the canonical identity or an alias that implies rawness. |

Generic seafood, scallops, dried shellfish, crab, lobster, crayfish, and langoustines do not become shrimp aliases by sharing aquatic or crustacean context. | Do not link generic seafood or other shellfish terms to this deferred candidate. |

### Current reference, form, and legacy audit

The active catalog contains 22 aquatic PROTEIN references: 14 species-scoped finfish; calamari; jumbo lump crab meat; and six preserved identities (dried bonito, dried oyster, dried scallop, dried shrimp, salt cod, and stockfish). It has no generic fresh shrimp or prawn identity. The aquatic condiment boundary includes fish sauce and Kapi; oyster sauce and vegan oyster sauce remain separate condiments. The five stock/broth references contain no shrimp stock.

No one of the 33 active forms is attached to a seafood or shellfish reference, and no active form term mentions shrimp, prawn, or crevette. The 86 legacy candidates contain no shrimp/prawn identity; the only seafood-adjacent candidate found is oyster sauce. The active Kapi row and dried-shrimp row are distinct and do not share identity IDs or categories.

The exact normalized-key audit followed normalizeIngredientSearchKey: lowercase with Unicode NFKD, fold œ/æ/ß and ampersands, remove combining marks, and normalize punctuation and spacing. Bare “shrimp,” “prawn,” and “crevette” have no exact normalized-key owner in active references, forms, or legacy candidates. Current search results are more specific: “shrimp” and singular “crevette” return dried shrimp; plural “crevettes” returns dried shrimp and Kapi; “prawn” has no current result. A new generic shrimp/crevette row would share these short-query result sets. That is a search-result overlap, not an exact-key collision.
