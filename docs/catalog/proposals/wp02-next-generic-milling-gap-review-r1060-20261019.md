# WP02 R1060 — Grass pea flour identity boundary review

- **Date:** 2026-10-19
- **Scope:** Proposal-only review of one generic pulse-flour gap under the open WP02 B2 alternative-flours checklist. The broader A7 review of grain meals and milling products remains open.
- **Disposition:** **Bounded hold; no active reference or ledger record proposed.**
- **Candidate:** Generic grass pea flour from *Lathyrus sativus* L. This is a crop-qualified identity, not a commercial product or product roster.
- **Provisional bilingual pair for review:** `grass pea flour / farine de gesse cultivée`. The French wording is attested in a Québec occupational-substance record, but the record is not a culinary label source and OQLF's `gesse` entry is scoped to animal feeding. Do not activate the French name or aliases on this evidence alone.

## Recommendation

Keep *Lathyrus sativus* flour as an unallocated research candidate. Food-use evidence supports it as a genuine generic ingredient: a Spanish food-safety committee describes flour use in human diets, and a primary Indian household study documents cultivation and consumption in its surveyed region. The candidate therefore is not being held as a study-only substance or rejected as non-food.

Hold an active recipe-catalog row pending a Québec-facing culinary name and a clearer human-food class boundary. The reviewed bilingual terms come from occupational or animal-feed terminology, and the safety report identifies β-ODAP as a relevant hazard without giving this review a transferable consumer flour/process class. Do not infer a safe serving, preparation process, or approval status from the sources below. This is a bounded hold, not a claim that grass pea flour is not used as food.

## Identity and bilingual boundary

| Item | Supported boundary | Keep out of this identity |
|---|---|---|
| Crop | Grass pea is *Lathyrus sativus* L. The AESAN report and the 2024 flour study use this taxon for the grass-pea food/flour discussed here. | Do not merge it with field/garden pea (*Pisum sativum*), chickpea, lentil, fava/faba bean, or other *Lathyrus* species. |
| Milling product | Flour from *L. sativus* is directly named in food-science work and in human-food-use sources. The 2024 study distinguishes native, dehulled, and germinated samples; these study inputs do not establish catalog forms. | Whole grass pea seed, split pulse, feed flour, mixed flour, and flour from another species are not aliases or forms established by this review. |
| English | `grass pea flour` is the preferred candidate name. `Chickling pea flour` appears in a CNESST occupational synonym list but is not proposed as an alias. | Keep `white pea flour`, `chickling vetch`, `Indian pea`, `khesari`, and `almorta` unassigned as search aliases; the reviewed sources do not establish their safe, Québec culinary scope for this row. |
| French | `farine de gesse cultivée` is a provisional translation candidate only. CNESST lists it alongside the Latin species in an occupational record. | OQLF's `gesse` entry is explicitly in animal-feed zootechny and describes flour use for pigs/cattle. It does not establish a Québec human-food display. Do not activate `farine de pois carré`, `farine de lentille d'Espagne`, or other regional plant names as flour aliases. |

The exact Latin species is the identity boundary. A broader `pea flour` or `farine de pois` alias would collide with existing *Pisum sativum* and pulse-flour results and would obscure the crop distinction.

## Evidence limits: measures, forms, and substitutions

- No transferable household measure, cup weight, density, or unit mapping was established in this review. Research-sample composition, flour particle size, functional properties, and β-ODAP measurements remain specific to their cited samples and methods.
- The 2014 household study's consumption observations describe the surveyed Maharashtra communities. They are not recommended portions or a safe-consumption limit.
- The 2024 study compares native, dehulled, and germinated flour samples. Its observed property differences do not establish three general catalog forms or a home-processing protocol.
- No general substitution ratio or directed form conversion is supported. Do not substitute for yellow/green pea flour, chickpea flour, lentil flour, fava/faba flour, or pea starch. A local dish or study formula would remain context-specific evidence, not a global rule.
- Do not add aliases, forms, measures, density, unit mappings, conversions, yields, recipe links, or active substitution rules.
