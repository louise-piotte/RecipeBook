# WP04 Section R — Plant-Protein Product Forms Follow-up

**Review date:** 2026-10-12

**Status:** Proposal only; no canonical catalog or recipe data changed.

**Scope:** Product shapes within the existing textured vegetable protein (TVP/PVT) identity; generic soy-curl terminology; and formulated plant-based mince and sausage products. This follow-up does not reopen tofu, seitan, natto, mycoprotein, plant beverages, or plant dairy replacements.

## Purpose and prior decisions

The accepted WP04 seitan/TVP review added one identity-only generic TVP reference. A later Section R review held generic soy curls, soy-specific textured protein/PST, and catch-all plant-based mince/sausage identities because no recipe line requested them and the available product labels did not support one shared product class. This review supplements those decisions with a focused check of TVP's named product shapes and current Quebec/Canadian product wording. It does not repeat or reopen the integrated tofu, seitan, natto, or mycoprotein decisions.

The current catalog identity is `ingredient-ref-textured-vegetable-protein`, displayed as `textured vegetable protein` / `protéine végétale texturée`, category `PROTEIN`. Its existing aliases are EN `TVP`, `texturized vegetable protein`, `texturised vegetable protein`; FR `PVT`. Keep the OQLF-defined narrower soy-only class, `textured soy protein` / `protéine de soya texturée` (`TSP` / `PST`), outside those aliases.

## Findings and recommendations

### 1. Retain the TVP identity; add only source-described shape forms

OQLF's current GDT entry defines generic PVT/TVP as plant material extruded into a dehydrated product with varied texture and shape. It expressly lists four market shapes: **flocons, petits morceaux, languettes, and boulettes**. It says the product is often used after rehydration, while it can also be eaten dry for texture. A Quebec manufacturer separately sells a soy-based product titled `PVT Lanières de soya` in French and `TVP Soy Curls` in English. That product corroborates a local strip presentation, but its label is product-specific and does not make every use of “soy curls” synonymous with generic TVP. [S01, S05]

**Recommendation:** retain the existing parent identity and propose four shape-only forms under it. These forms record commercial shape, not a new ingredient identity, source species, recipe behavior, or substitution. Use these bounded bilingual labels:

| Form code | Québec French label | English label | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| `flakes` | `flocons de protéine végétale texturée` | `textured vegetable protein flakes` | OQLF lists `flocons`. |
| `pieces` | `petits morceaux de protéine végétale texturée` | `textured vegetable protein pieces` | OQLF lists `petits morceaux`; do not equate every piece size with a separate grade. |
| `strips` | `languettes de protéine végétale texturée` | `textured vegetable protein strips` | OQLF lists `languettes`; the current Quebec product page uses the product label `PVT Lanières de soya` / `TVP Soy Curls`. Keep that exact product phrase product-scoped. |
| `balls` | `boulettes de protéine végétale texturée` | `textured vegetable protein balls` | OQLF lists `boulettes`; this describes shape only. |

The English form labels above are plain descriptive renderings of OQLF's French shape list; OQLF's entry supplies the bilingual generic headword, not a separate standardized English term for each shape. The Canadian manufacturer directly corroborates English `strips` and French `lanières` for its own soy-based TVP product. Do not add a `soy curls` parent alias or a form match term from this one product.

The current recipe corpus contains no shape-specific TVP line, so these forms receive no recipe links, quantities, measures, density, package yield, conversions, or substitutions. The recommendation is limited to a source-defined catalog description; it makes no claim of recipe demand.

### 2. Keep hydration and product grading outside the proposed forms

The OQLF defines TVP as dehydrated at the product-family level, then describes rehydration as a common preparation, with dry use also possible. The Quebec maker gives product-specific preparation instructions for its plain TVP and strip product, and its page describes adding TVP directly to sauces or using it dry in selected applications. Those instructions do not establish a universal hydration ratio, volume or mass yield, or mandatory hydrated state. [S01, S03–S05]

**Recommendation:** do not add generic `dry` or `rehydrated` forms to this TVP reference. Treat rehydration as recipe preparation when a future line requires it. Do not store a hydration ratio, conversion, measure, cooking result, or substitution from the product pages. Also hold `fine`/`granulated`, `crumbles`, and seasoned profiles such as `Greek style`, `Mexican style`, or `Bacon Bits` as generic forms or aliases: the current Quebec maker's own product names and fine-particle presentation demonstrate market-specific variants, not a shared product specification. [S03, S05]

### 3. Keep generic “soy curls” on hold, with the reason updated

Butler's official product page calls its branded product `Soy Curls™` and describes it as protein strips made from whole soybeans. Its FAQ says the beans are soaked, cooked, and dried at low temperature. The product page gives a rehydration method for that brand. [S03–S04]

Les Brutes, a Quebec producer, currently uses **`PVT Lanières de soya`** in French and **`TVP Soy Curls`** in English for its own soy product. It lists defatted soy flour as the ingredient and gives a product-specific hydration and pan-cooking method. [S05]

These two direct product sources establish current bilingual market wording, but they do not establish one generic recipe ingredient. The English phrase “Soy Curls” appears as Butler's brand name and as part of a different Quebec maker's product title; the sources describe different ingredient/process details. Their overlapping strip shape alone is not enough to normalize the Butler product to TVP or to assign a universal alias. **Hold a generic `soy curls` identity and all generic `soy curls` / `soya curls` aliases.** If a future recipe names a package or maker, represent that exact product only after reviewing its own label and recipe context. The local phrase `PVT Lanières de soya` may inform the proposed TVP `strips` form's descriptive notes, but should not be generalized as a synonym for Butler's trademarked product.

### 4. Keep formulated mince and sausage products product-specific

CFIA's bilingual guidance classifies simulated meat and poultry based on composition and appearance, including how a product is labeled or represented. It notes that these products may contain milk or eggs. Thus, `plant-based` alone does not prove vegan composition, and a common product label is not a universal ingredient identity. [S06]

Current Canadian Beyond Meat pages illustrate the product-specific wording: Beyond Beef uses `plant-based ground meat` / `viande hachée à base de plantes`; Beyond Sausage uses a named sausage line with flavor variants. The pages describe formulated products, not the generic extruded TVP family. [S07–S08]

**Recommendation:** retain the earlier hold on catch-all `plant-based mince`, `plant-based sausage`, `veggie mince`, and `veggie sausage` identities and aliases. Do not alias those phrases to ground beef, breakfast sausage, TVP/PVT, PST, soy curls, seitan, or mycoprotein. Do not add brand crosswalks, vegan claims, recipe links, or meat-substitution rules. If a future recipe names a product, review the exact package wording, formulation, variant, and recipe role before proposing a product-specific catalog record.

## Consolidated recommendation

1. Keep `ingredient-ref-textured-vegetable-protein` and its current bilingual identity/aliases unchanged.
2. Propose only the four generic TVP shape forms listed above: flakes, pieces, strips, and balls. Do not assign recipe links or quantitative behavior.
3. Keep TVP hydration state, fine/granulated products, seasoned products, PST/TSP, and generic soy curls outside the new forms or aliases. Hold `soy curls` as an ambiguous product name; the current Quebec French `PVT Lanières de soya` is product-specific.
4. Continue to hold catch-all formulated plant mince and sausage identities. Preserve the existing unqualified breakfast-sausage and meat rows as-is.
5. Add no measure, density, conversion, hydration ratio, package yield, cooking instruction, substitution, or safety-equivalence claim.

## Limitations

- This review is limited to the current bundled corpus and the named sources; it is not a full Canadian retail survey.
- The four shape forms are supported product presentations, not evidence of recipe demand. No recipe links are justified by the present corpus.
- English terms for the OQLF-listed form shapes are descriptive renderings; OQLF supplies the standardized bilingual identity but not a separate standardized English label for each shape.
- Product pages and labels can change. Brand names and preparation directions remain tied to the specific products reviewed.
- This packet does not add any source or decision to the shared evidence ledger, alter the canonical catalog, or run tests or emulator checks.
