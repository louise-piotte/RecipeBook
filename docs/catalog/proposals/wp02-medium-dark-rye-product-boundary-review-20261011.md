## Product-boundary findings

### Medium rye flour

The sources repeatedly treat medium rye flour as a named milling grade distinct from white/light rye and darker rye products. The BCcampus baking text says medium rye is straight flour after bran and shorts are removed, describes a light-grey product, and gives a textbook ash figure. Brant Flour Mills currently sells “Medium Rye Flour” as fine, partially sifted flour with bran flakes removed. Ardent Mills lists Ramsay Medium Rye alongside separate white and dark rye products and says its medium and dark products have more bran than white. King Arthur likewise describes its medium flour as having more bran than white rye and less of the dark product’s germ or bran. TERMIUM gives a medium-grade definition and the French equivalent. Together these support a distinct **middle grade label** rather than a synonym for generic rye flour, light rye flour, whole rye flour, or coarse rye meal. [S01–S05]

The descriptions do **not** support a precise universal recipe-facing definition. Brant’s partially sifted description, TERMIUM’s removal of some bran/germ, and the BCcampus text’s bran/shorts wording do not establish one retained-fraction threshold. Ardent and King Arthur give relative descriptions rather than common specifications. Keep the ash and protein figures in the BCcampus text as that text’s explanatory values only; they are not copied into the catalog. Do not add `straight rye flour` as an alias: one educational source’s comparison to straight-grade wheat flour is not evidence that the phrase is a market synonym across rye suppliers. [S01–S05]

No current Québec mill/retailer listing for medium rye flour was found in this review. TERMIUM supports the exact proposed French phrase, while OQLF’s rye entry supports only the generic `farine de seigle` and discusses light/dark colour differences without naming the medium grade. Because Québec commercial usage is unconfirmed and the phrase is not independently reviewed here, the French display remains conditional. [S03, S10]

### Dark rye flour

The product name is current in both English and Québec French. Brant Flour Mills labels a fine 100%-whole-grain flour “Dark Rye Flour”; Moulin de Charlevoix sells “Farine de seigle foncée biologique” and a bilingual brochure pairs it with “Organic Dark Complete Rye Flour”; Mayrand lists R.Fortin’s “Farine de seigle foncée 5 kg.” Those labels support current product-specific naming, not a shared grade specification. A distributor describes the Charlevoix SKU as retaining bran, germ, and endosperm, but that claim remains specific to the listed product. [S02, S06–S09]

The broader class is inconsistent. The BCcampus baking text compares dark rye with first-clear wheat flour and provides one ash/protein description, while TERMIUM’s record mixes a whole-kernel definition with a “clear flour” comparison and says dark flour is often, but not always, whole grain. King Arthur explicitly says dark rye differs by producer and lacks a standard classification; it may be whole grain, partially sifted, or contain little endosperm. That conflicts with treating the local “dark” label as one stable whole-grain identity, and whole-grain products already have the separate active `ingredient-ref-whole-rye-flour`. Keep `dark rye flour` source-specific unless a narrower class is proposed, such as a product explicitly labelled both dark and whole grain. Do not make `dark rye flour` a synonym for `whole rye flour`, `rye meal`, or `pumpernickel flour`. [S01–S03, S05–S09]

## Review and validation notes

- Recommendation is limited to Section A medium/dark rye-flour grades; it does not reopen rye meal, whole rye flour, light rye flour, malted rye flour, or toasted rye flour decisions.
- The medium identity uses `farine de seigle moyenne` after the independent reviewer accepted the exact federal bilingual terminology pair. That wording decision does not establish a uniform milling threshold.
- Dark rye’s exact Québec label is recorded as product evidence, but the general identity remains held due inconsistent milling scope. Reconsider only with a clearly bounded grade definition that separates dark flour from whole rye flour and rye meal.
- No measures, densities, unit mappings, forms, conversions, yields, substitutions, or recipe links are supported by this review.
- This packet was proposal-only before coordinator integration. The accepted medium identity is recorded in the batch README; this document retains its pre-integration snapshot and source analysis. No app tests were run because the accepted work only adds database JSON data.
