# WP06 Caribbean Cuisine Gap Sweep: Haiti and the French Antilles

**Status:** proposal-only; no catalog, ledger, seed, recipe, workflow, roadmap, code, or test changes are made by this packet.
**Prepared:** 2026-10-11
**Coverage:** a bounded review of Haiti and the French Antilles, with focused examples from Guadeloupe and Martinique. It does not describe one universal “Creole cuisine,” nor does it claim to cover the Caribbean as a whole.

## Scope and method

This sweep looks for ingredient identities, regional names, dish compounds, and evidence gaps in a small set of Haitian, Guadeloupean, and Martinican recipe and culinary sources. Names are kept in their source language and spelling. The review does not infer a global alias from one locality, translate dish names into raw ingredient identities, or treat a tourism overview or one publisher’s recipe as a canonical formula.

The audit compared source terms against current active ingredient references, ingredient forms, legacy import candidates, the research ledger, the 114 bundled recipes, and existing WP05/WP06 packets. The recipe benchmark checked all 1,123 ingredient lines for case-, accent-, punctuation-, and spacing-normalized occurrences of the regional dish and ingredient terms listed below. Generic ingredients such as rice, lime, plantain, or coconut were not counted as evidence that a Caribbean recipe is represented.

Online sources were checked on 2026-10-11. The source list records what each source supports and material limits. No recipe quantities or conversions are proposed. Quantities shown by publishers remain source-specific and were not used to infer defaults, density, serving behavior, or substitutions.

## Current-state audit and duplication check

At audit time the bundled package had 954 active ingredient references, 53 ingredient forms, and 114 manifest-listed recipes containing 1,123 ingredient lines. The legacy import file had 75 candidates. The research ledger held 1,223 records, 3,679 source entries, and 2,993 distinct source IDs. The regional phrase scan found no current recipe ingredient line naming *djon djon*, *mayi moulen*, *soup joumou*, *giraumon*, *lalo*, *colombo*, *bois d’Inde*, *cive*, *christophine*, *tourment d’amour*, or *kassav*.

This is a recipe-name and ingredient-line benchmark, not proof that no Caribbean food appears under a generic title. It shows that the bundled set has no visible recipe benchmark for these source-named dishes and terms. The recipe corpus therefore does not demonstrate coverage of this bounded regional scope.

Existing exact identities relevant to this review include `ingredient-ref-haitian-epis`, `ingredient-ref-haitian-pikliz`, `ingredient-ref-jute-leaves`, `ingredient-ref-chayote`, `ingredient-ref-malanga`, `ingredient-ref-cornmeal`, `ingredient-ref-green-onions`, `ingredient-ref-pumpkin`, and `ingredient-ref-allspice`. WP05 already reviewed and integrated Haitian epis and pikliz, so this proposal does not repeat those decisions; see [the WP05 Caribbean condiment review](wp05-caribbean-condiments-review-20261009.md), source IDs `WP05-CAR-S06` through `WP05-CAR-S12`.

An exact-name scan across active references, aliases, forms, and legacy candidates found usable existing names for the general ingredients above, but no exact identity/form/candidate for djon djon, Colombo powder, bois d’Inde leaves, or the other proposed holds. The exception for regional dish words is handled as a source-bound alias or compound candidate below; it is not an active alias merely because a recipe source uses the term.

The prior WP03 gourd review cites OQLF on *chayotte* and its regional names, including *christophine* (French Antilles/Guyane) and *mirliton* (Haiti), under source ID `WP03-L-GOURDS-OKRA-IDENTITY-20261009-S15`. The WP06 North American sweep already records Louisiana-scoped *mirliton/christophene* evidence. This packet reuses those findings and does not add another OQLF source record or broaden those aliases globally. See [the WP06 North American cuisine sweep](wp06-north-american-cuisine-gap-sweep-20261009.md).

## Coverage gaps and next research boundary

The sources offer a small but varied benchmark: Haitian rice, stew, staple porridge, festive soup, and baking; Guadeloupean fritters, cassava bread, and a Saintes pastry; and Martinican Colombo and Christmas-food contexts. The prior WP05 packet contributes Haitian epis/pikliz condiment coverage. The examples span everyday and festive dishes, savory cooking, grains, vegetables, aromatics, and sweets, but do not establish a comprehensive household or regional repertoire.

The recipe corpus has no regional phrase hits for this benchmark. Coverage remains open for additional Haitian household and provincial cooking, Guadeloupe’s distinct islands and community practices, and Martinique beyond tourism summaries and a single publisher’s recipe. This packet does not cover the English-, Dutch-, Spanish-, or Papiamento-speaking Caribbean; other French-speaking islands; French Guiana; or diaspora cuisines, except where an individual source happens to provide a comparison. It does not treat Haitian Creole, Guadeloupean Creole, and Martinican Creole as interchangeable or universal labels.

Suggested follow-up is limited to evidence gaps that could change identity decisions: a source that identifies *pois d’Angole* directly; a local producer or culinary institution defining *graines à roussir*; a Martinique/Guadeloupe source clarifying *cive* against the catalog’s green-onion identity; and a manufacturer label or technical source establishing what bouillon cube is meant in djon djon recipes. These are research holds, not approved catalog changes.

### Held source

A 2000 mycology paper, “Use of *Psathyrella cf. hymenocephala* (Coprinaceae) as a spice in Haiti,” is discoverable through its [publisher DOI page](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0269-915X(00)80032-4). ## Disposition

This packet proposes only scoped research follow-up for Haitian djon djon mushrooms, Colombo powder, and bois d’Inde leaves; source-bound alias review for *giraumon*, *lalo*, and *cive*; and unresolved holds for the terms listed above. Existing identities, prior WP05 decisions, and prior OQLF findings should be reused. The regional benchmark remains deliberately incomplete, with no claim that Haiti or the French Antilles share one uniform Creole ingredient vocabulary.
