## Recommendation

Propose identity-only **Jiangxi yancai**, a regional type named in peer-reviewed research on traditional Chinese fermented vegetables. Keep the Jiangxi qualifier in both displays. The source uses `Jiangxi yancai` for a studied regional type, while its introduction describes unqualified *yancai* much more broadly as salted pickles made from different vegetables across a wide area of China. Therefore, do not create an unqualified `yancai` row or alias, and do not infer that every salted or fermented vegetable from Jiangxi shares one formula.

Use `Jiangxi yancai` as the English display. For fr-CA, use the provisional borrowed name `yancai du Jiangxi (légumes salés et fermentés du Jiangxi)`. No reviewed source supplies a Québec-standardized French preserve name; the parenthetical is descriptive and must not be added as a search alias. Do not add aliases such as `yancai`, `Jiangxi pickled vegetables`, `Chinese pickled vegetables`, or `Chinese sauerkraut`. Keep `Yibin ya cai` distinct: its romanized name and GI-bounded identity are separately active, and the similar spelling does not establish a relationship.

If accepted for later integration, add one identity-only `SAUCE_AND_CONDIMENT` reference. Add no aliases, forms, density, unit mappings, reusable measures, recipe links, conversions, yields, or active substitutions. Keep sample counts, process descriptions, microbiology, and measured composition within the sources that report them.

## Identity and bilingual boundary

Xiao et al. identify Jiangxi yancai, Sichuan paocai, and Dongbei suancai as three traditional Chinese fermented-vegetable types. Their study collected five samples of each type from households in the respective provinces, including Jiangxi for Jiangxi yancai. This supports a bounded, source-named Jiangxi type and supports keeping it separate from the other two regional names. It does not show that all Jiangxi households make one standardized product. [S01]

The article’s introduction describes *yancai* broadly as a traditional salted pickle made from various vegetables over a wide area of China. That broader usage is exactly why `Jiangxi` must remain in the display and why bare `yancai` must not become an alias. The study does not provide a definitive ingredient list, a single botanical substrate, or a complete product specification for the Jiangxi type. It does not support aliases to generic pickled mustard greens, pickled radish, preserved mustard products, other region-qualified suancai/paocai, kimchi, or generic fermented vegetables. [S01]

The bilingual pair is therefore:

| Locale | Proposed display | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|
| English | `Jiangxi yancai` | The regional name is source-attested in the research article title and text. |
| French (Canada) | `yancai du Jiangxi (légumes salés et fermentés du Jiangxi)` | Provisional descriptive construction. No Québec authority or reviewed regional source standardizes this French name. The description is not an alias. |

The existing source `WP05-SECTIONW-DONGBEI-SUANCAI-R980-20261019-S02` belongs to the Dongbei record and must remain there. If this proposal is accepted, give the Jiangxi identity a new, candidate-specific source locator under `WP05-SECTIONW-JIANGXI-YANCAI-R1041-20261019-S01`; do not edit or repurpose the Dongbei source record.
