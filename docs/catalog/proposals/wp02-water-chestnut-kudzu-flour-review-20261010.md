The `kudzu flour` alias recommendation is deliberately limited to the exact English phrase that Japan’s Ministry of Education places under its starches and starch-products classification (code 02029). A separate 2026 bread study calls its material finely milled dried kudzu root and “kudzu root powder,” which preserves a clear source-backed distinction between powdered whole root and isolated starch. The Ministry of Agriculture’s account of Yoshino Hon-kuzu likewise describes a powder/flour obtained by extracting and refining kudzu-root starch. These sources support the existing identity boundary, but they do not resolve the catalog row’s broader botanical scope: one uses *Pueraria lobata*, while the current Québec invasive-species list labels kudzu as *Pueraria montana*. Keep that scope open as the existing regional-starches proposal directs.

The Canadian trademark wording “Japanese arrowroot powder for food [kudzu-ko]” and its French counterpart are useful evidence of a Canadian bilingual product phrase, not a reason to alias it to generic arrowroot. The live arrowroot row already has `arrowroot powder` as an alias; adding the longer Japanese phrase to either identity could make a broad `arrowroot` query return both. The source does not provide a standalone retail label or a generic equivalence standard.

## Product boundaries and terminology

### Water chestnut

The existing proposals [water-chestnut flour/starch boundary review](wp02-water-chestnut-flour-starch-boundary-20261009.md) and [independent water-chestnut review](wp02-water-chestnut-independent-review-20261009.md) establish three non-interchangeable products:

1. Whole *Eleocharis dulcis* corms, dried and milled as flour.
2. Starch extracted from *E. dulcis* corms.
3. Flour ground from dried *Trapa* fruits, called “water chestnut flour” in India’s eNAM commodity specification and “Singhare ka atta” there.

The primary *E. dulcis* study describes different processing for corm flour and extracted starch. The eNAM definition uses dried *Trapa bispinosa* or *T. quadrispinosa* nuts. Therefore the Canadian listing “Pan Tang - Water Chestnut Flour” (250 g) adds market evidence for the unqualified English phrase but cannot identify which of these product boundaries it represents. Its retailer description has no species, ingredient declaration, or process. Package net weight is not an ingredient measure.

Québec naming remains unsafe for product rows. The older OQLF Chinese-sedge entry gives `châtaigne d’eau` for the edible tuber and translates it as “water chestnut”; a separate OQLF record associates “water chestnut” with `water caltrop`. The current Gouvernement du Québec species list identifies `châtaigne d’eau` as *Trapa natans*. These are plant-name records, not processed-food labels, and the current Québec usage conflicts with relying on the older name as a product qualifier. Do not approve `farine de châtaigne d’eau`, `fécule de châtaigne d’eau`, `amidon de châtaigne d’eau`, or a `chinoise` qualifier as fr-CA catalog names on this evidence. Keep `Singhare ka atta` as source-specific Indian wording, not a universal alias.

### Kudzu/kuzu

- **English:** The active display `kudzu starch` is supported by the MEXT food table and existing starch literature. MEXT explicitly says this starch is “also called kudzu flour.” Accept that exact phrase as a possible EN alias of the active starch row. The 2026 primary study’s `kudzu root powder` is a different material: dried whole root milled to powder. Do not map `kudzu root powder` to starch, and do not create a separate row for that whole-root material in this proposal because it has no bundled-recipe demand and no reviewed Québec food name.
- **Québec French:** OQLF’s GDT has `kouzou` in the food-starch domain. It defines the term as a fécule extracted from a wild perennial root and attributes the source to Fondation Est-Ouest de Toulouse (1985). Keep the existing French display `kouzou`; this entry does not offer a French alias such as `farine de kudzu`, identify a Latin species, or specify whether all current products marketed as kudzu flour are extracted starch.
- **Canadian wording:** The Canadian Intellectual Property Office’s 2026 bilingual goods text records EN `Japanese arrowroot powder for food [kudzu-ko]` and FR `poudre d’arrow-root japonais à usage alimentaire [kudzu-ko]`. MAFF explains that `kuzu-ko` is an edible powder made by extracting and refining kudzu-root starch; the exact CIPO phrase is therefore source wording for a Japanese product class. Keep it as evidence only. It is not an OQLF recommendation, it carries the misleading-for-catalog `arrowroot` string, and it does not establish `kuzu flour` as an alias.
- **Botanical scope:** MEXT identifies *P. lobata* for its food-table entry. MAFF’s Yoshino product page describes kudzu without a Latin species, while the current Québec invasive-species list uses *P. montana* for kudzu. Do not resolve this nomenclature difference by inference; preserve the active row’s existing unresolved botanical note.

## Measurement, conversion, and substitution decision

- **Water chestnut:** No reusable density, volume-to-mass mapping, process yield, or generic conversion is supported. The Canadian retailer’s 250 g figure is a package size. Prior lab composition, pasting, and product-formulation observations are scoped to their specific *E. dulcis* or *Trapa* samples and methods. Do not turn them into a density or a flour/starch conversion.
- **Kudzu starch:** MAFF’s one-cup kuzuyu example uses 25 g Hon-kuzu with 180 cc plus 5 tablespoons hot water. This is a product- and recipe-specific ingredient amount, not a powder cup weight or density. The source provides no dry volume measure for the starch. Do not create a g/ml density, household unit mapping, generic yield, or mass-volume conversion.
- **Substitutions:** The 2026 bread study tests 3%, 6%, 9%, and 12% whole-root powder as replacements for wheat flour in its own formula. It reports changes in dough absorption and bread structure; this is not a general replacement ratio and it is not evidence about isolated kudzu starch. MAFF’s thickener/kuzuyu uses show culinary functions, not substitution ratios against cornstarch, arrowroot, or another ingredient. The earlier water-chestnut proposal likewise keeps sample- and recipe-specific *Trapa* cookie results out of global rules and has no transferable *E. dulcis* flour/starch conversion. Keep all substitution rules absent.
