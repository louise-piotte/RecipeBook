# WP02 Section A7 R1051: common buckwheat bran flour boundary review

**Date:** 2026-10-19
**Disposition:** Bounded hold; no catalog or ledger change proposed.
**Snapshot audited:** 2,058 ingredient references, 205 forms, 2,463 research-ledger records / 11,498 source entries / 9,658 distinct source IDs, 55 legacy candidates, and 114 bundled recipes / 1,123 ingredient lines.

## Scope and recommendation

The open WP02 Section A7 parent continues review of grain meals and milling products ([plan, line 2290](../../RecipeBook-global-ingredient-catalog-plan.txt#L2290)). R1051 reviews the possible generic milling identity **common buckwheat bran flour / farine de son de sarrasin commun**.

Keep this crop-qualified bran-flour candidate outside active references and the research ledger. Primary studies establish that common buckwheat milling samples have been described as bran flour or bran meal, and a separate study uses buckwheat bran flour in spaghetti. The evidence does not yet establish that the crop-specific common-buckwheat fraction and the species-unspecified food-study material are the same transferable culinary identity. Another common-buckwheat noodle study uses bran, without identifying it as a bran-flour grade. The existing common buckwheat bran identity already covers that broader fraction.

This is a bounded identity hold, not a claim that common buckwheat bran flour cannot be used as food. Reopen if direct human-food evidence identifies the material as common-buckwheat bran flour (or a clearly equivalent named fraction) and distinguishes it from unground bran and the already active Tartary-buckwheat bran flour identity.

## Identity boundary and evidence

Common buckwheat is *Fagopyrum esculentum*; Tartary buckwheat is *Fagopyrum tataricum*. The catalog already has generic **buckwheat bran / son de sarrasin** and a separate **Tartary buckwheat bran flour / farine de son de sarrasin de Tartarie** identity. Keep those owners distinct. Do not create a common-buckwheat flour identity by broadening the Tartary row or by treating the active buckwheat-bran row as proof of a transferable flour grade.

The principal boundary findings are:

- Martín-García et al. examine dehulled common buckwheat (*F. esculentum*), cultivar Darja, roller-milled in an experimental mill. The paper uses both “bran flour” and “bran meal” wording, describes the material as a by-product not commonly used in foods, and discusses possible food development rather than testing it in a recipe. Its sample, milling setup, and reported particle sizes do not define a general consumer grade. [S01]
- Mazahir et al. report fine flour, coarse flour, bran flour, and husk fractions for common and Tartary buckwheat samples. The study analyzes extracted milling samples; it does not test a food recipe or establish a transferable human-food use for common-buckwheat bran flour. Its chemistry and sample preparation remain study-specific. [S02]
- Manthey et al. use “buckwheat bran flour” in a spaghetti study, but the available abstract does not identify the buckwheat species. Its food use therefore cannot be assigned specifically to common buckwheat or used to bridge the species gap. The formula, hydration levels, extrusion, drying, and cooking results remain specific to that study. [S03]
- Liu et al. study **common buckwheat bran** in wheat dough and noodles. This supports food use of the already active bran identity; the article does not name the input as bran flour or establish a general milling grade. Its 4% test level and other noodle results remain study-specific. [S04]
- The older common-and-Tartary composition study already recorded on the Tartary-bran-flour row measures both species’ bran fractions. It is composition evidence, not proof of common-buckwheat bran flour’s independent culinary identity. Preserve its current source ownership. [R954-S03]

Keep the candidate separate from common buckwheat bran, Tartary buckwheat bran flour, buckwheat hull/husk, whole or light buckwheat flour, middlings, and bran-containing blends. The current evidence supplies no stable particle-size threshold or universal milling definition for the proposed common-bran-flour identity.

## Bilingual naming boundary

**Common buckwheat / sarrasin commun** is supported as a crop name by the Government of Canada’s TERMIUM Plus entry for *Fagopyrum esculentum*. OQLF’s existing GDT source supports the base term **sarrasin**; its existing **son** source is general bran terminology. These sources do not attest the complete Québec culinary name **farine de son de sarrasin commun**.

That French compound is a transparent descriptive candidate only. Because the ingredient identity remains held, add no French display or alias and do not present the compound as OQLF-preferred. Preserve **son de sarrasin** on the active buckwheat-bran row and **farine de son de sarrasin de Tartarie** on the Tartary-specific row.

## Numeric and substitution limits

No household measure, cup weight, density, unit mapping, cooking yield, or general milling yield is supported for common buckwheat bran flour. Martín-García et al.’s particle-size observations belong to one experimental mill and cultivar; Mazahir et al.’s samples and compositional results belong to its milling and extraction study. Do not turn any sample measurements into a retail grade, household quantity, generic nutrition claim, or processing specification.

The 2004 spaghetti study’s tested bran-flour proportions and hydration conditions are not a general recipe ratio, and the abstract does not establish species. Liu et al.’s 4% common-bran test and results concern its wheat-noodle formulation, not a general common-buckwheat-bran-flour replacement rule. No direct evidence supports substituting the candidate for wheat flour, buckwheat flour, bran, hull, or another ingredient. Add no measurement observation, density, mapping, unit conversion, yield, active or contextual substitution rule, or recipe-line substitution link.

## Decision

Keep common buckwheat bran flour held outside active catalog and ledger data. The evidence describes relevant milling samples and separate human-food uses, but does not yet establish that the exact crop-qualified bran-flour identity is a transferable generic culinary ingredient. Preserve active `buckwheat bran` and Tartary-buckwheat bran flour identities, their existing sources and scoped claims, and the open Section A7 parent.
