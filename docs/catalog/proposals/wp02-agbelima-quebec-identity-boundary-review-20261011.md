- **Inputs reviewed:** `AGENTS.md`; relevant `Requirements.md` ingredient-reference, form, conversion, and substitution rules; WP02 B2/B3 checklist; current references/forms, research ledger, legacy candidates, roadmap, related WP02 proposals, and every manifest-listed bundled recipe.

## Recommendation

**HOLD; propose no catalog delta.** Primary sources establish Agbelima as fermented cassava dough, meal, or mash, while a current Benin product register separately lists a fermented cassava flour product named `farine de manioc fermentée Agbelima`. The Québec-facing source located for this review lists one frozen item as `Pâte de manioc – Placali – Agbelima` and separately lists `Placali – 1Kg`; it does not say whether Agbelima is a synonym for Placali, a different dough, or a dehydrated product. The product exists, but the evidence does not settle one source-stable product stage or a standalone Québec French display. [S01–S06]

Do not treat this hold as rejection of Agbelima as a distinct regional food. It keeps open the possibility of a separate identity while the flour-versus-dough and local-name boundaries remain unresolved.

## Measurement, forms, and substitution limits

- **Density and unit mappings:** none proposed. No source provides a household volume-to-mass observation or density for Agbelima. The Québec title's `1Kg` is a package-title claim for that vendor item, not a recipe measure, cup conversion, or general mass mapping. [S05]
- **Forms and yields:** none proposed. S03 distinguishes market mash, a supermarket flour sample, and cooked dough; it reports oven-drying frozen mash at 65°C for 8 hours and milling it into flour. These document stages in one study, not a standardized catalog form vocabulary or the unspecified Québec frozen item. No general yield is established. [S03]
- **Conversions:** none proposed. S03's experimental protocol prepared 1 kg of cooked dough from 300 g of Agbelima flour and 900 mL of water, cooking for 20 minutes. Preserve that only as a study-specific recipe method; it is not a density, reusable mass-volume mapping, or general reconstitution/yield rule for other products or the Québec frozen listing. [S03]
- **Substitutions:** no global or contextual rule proposed. The reviewed studies characterize fermentation or quality; none tests Agbelima as an interchangeable substitute for Placali, lafun, fufu, amala, gari, attiéké, pupuru, cassava flour, or starch.
- **Recipe links:** none proposed. Exact recipe demand is zero in the current 114-recipe bundle.

## No-op boundaries and acceptance checks

This proposal changes no canonical or generated files. Do not add an active reference, aliases, forms, density, unit mappings, conversion/yield data, substitutions, or recipe links. Do not alias `agbelima` to `placali`, `farine de manioc fermentée`, `pâte de manioc`, generic cassava flour, tapioca starch, or another regional cassava product. Do not alter the integrated Placali row or the existing lafun evidence record.

Reopen for promotion only when evidence supplies all of the following:

1. A Québec product or producer source identifies Agbelima as a standalone item and specifies whether it is fermented mash/dough, dried flour, or another market form.
2. A Québec French label or authoritative Québec terminology supports a standalone display name and distinguishes it from Placali; a source naming both in one title is insufficient on its own.
3. Any proposed aliases are explicitly source-attested and do not collide with other fermented cassava products.
4. Any future measure, form conversion, yield, or substitution is supported separately for the exact product state and recipe context. No technical quality measurement is promoted to a kitchen mapping.

## Source register

All sources accessed 2026-10-11. Source IDs are local to this proposal and are not ledger edits.

- **S01** — W. K. Amoa-Awua, F. E. Appoh, and M. Jakobsen, [“Lactic acid fermentation of cassava dough into agbelima”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8880299/), *International Journal of Food Microbiology* 31(1–3), 87–98 (1996), DOI [10.1016/0168-1605(96)00967-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/0168-1605(96)00967-1). Primary fermentation study calls the product fermented cassava meal and examines dough fermentation. Supports identity and process, not a retail standard or Québec French name.
- **S02** — N. T. Dziedzoave, W. O. Ellis, J. Oduro, and J. H. Oldham, [“Quality Assessment of Fermented Cassava Dough (Agbelima) on Ghanaian Markets”](https://csirspace.foodresearchgh.org/bitstream/1/217/1/JAST_5_1_2_Dziedzoave_et%20al.pdf), *Journal of Applied Science and Technology* 5(1–2), 142–147 (2000). Primary assessment of 20 market samples across five markets; reports between-market differences in moisture, pH, and other quality measures. It does not provide a kitchen measure or universal product specification.
- **S03** — Lionnel Arnaud Chabi China et al., [“Food quality profile of fermented cassava dough: A tool for the value chain upgrading”](https://scijournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jsf2.70052), *JSFA Reports* 6(7), 280–287 (2026), DOI [10.1002/jsf2.70052](https://doi.org/10.1002/jsf2.70052). Primary study describes fermented mash cooked into dough, reports processing-time variation by country, and distinguishes mash, supermarket flour, and dough. Its 300 g flour + 900 mL water protocol yielding 1 kg cooked dough is one study method, not a general unit mapping or reusable yield; instrumental quality measurements likewise are not kitchen conversions.
- **S04** — Agence Béninoise de Sécurité Sanitaire des Aliments, [Répertoire des produits ayant reçu l’autorisation de mise sur le marché](https://agriculture.gouv.bj/abssa/images/documents/REPERTOIRE-DES-AMM-02_12_25.pdf), updated 2025-12-02, PDF p. 11, rows 182–183. The rows list `FARINE DE MANIOC FERMENTEE AGBELIMA` and `FARINE DE MANIOC FERMENTEE` separately; cited authorizations run through 2028. This is Benin product-register terminology, not Québec naming evidence or proof that all Agbelima is dry flour.
- **S05** — Afromarket, [“Pâte de manioc – Placali – Agbelima — 1Kg”](https://afromarket.ca/index.php/produit/pate-de-manioc-placali-agbelima-1kg/), Québec-facing French product page. Lists the item under frozen foods and shows Montréal-area phone numbers. The title and package quantity do not state ingredients, preparation, or whether the product is equivalent to the separately listed Placali item.
- **S06** — Afromarket, [“Placali – 1Kg”](https://afromarket.ca/index.php/produit/placali-1kg/), separate frozen product page; see also its [frozen-products listing](https://afromarket.ca/index.php/categorie-produit/congeles/), which displays both Placali products. The separate listing establishes a local product-name collision question, not Agbelima–Placali equivalence or difference.
- **S07** — Office québécois de la langue française, [“farine de manioc”](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/8432779/farine-de-manioc), GDT entry. Supports the generic Québec French term for cassava flour; it does not identify fermented Agbelima.
- **S08** — Office québécois de la langue française, [“fécule de manioc”](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/3287219/fecule-de-manioc), GDT entry citing an extracted wet-starch product definition. Used only to keep extracted manioc starch separate from fermented cassava dough or flour.
- **S09** — Agence Béninoise de Sécurité Sanitaire des Aliments, [2024-12-03 product register](https://agriculture.gouv.bj/abssa/images/documents/REPERTOIRE_DES_AMM_03_12_24.pdf), pp. 1–2, Alitech Industries entries. Pairs `farine de manioc fermentée` separately with `LAFUN` and `AGBELIMA`; the listed authorizations later expired. The existing lafun ledger record preserves this as dated regional evidence; it is not a Québec label authority.
