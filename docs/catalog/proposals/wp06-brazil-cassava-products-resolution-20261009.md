# WP06 Brazilian cassava flour and starch product-family boundary review

- **Date:** 2026-10-09
- **Status:** Research proposal; no shared catalog or ledger changes made.
- **Scope:** Resolve Brazilian `farinha de mandioca` groups, `polvilho doce` / `polvilho azedo`, and hydrated tapioca (`tapioca umedecida`) against the active edible cassava flour, tapioca starch, and cassava-root identities. Audit current forms, legacy candidates, normalized-name collisions, and all bundled recipe ingredient rows.

## Conclusion

Brazil's MAPA defines three **groups** under its official `farinha de mandioca` classification: Seca, d'Água, and Bijusada. They share a parent commodity name but are distinguished by processing and, for bijusada, its irregular flakes and low density. The product groups merit separate, source-named identity candidates. Keep unqualified `farinha de mandioca` as a family-level phrase, not as an alias for one group or for the active Codex-based `edible cassava flour`. [S01, S02, S06, S11]

MAPA distinguishes non-fermented `fécula` from `polvilho doce` and `polvilho azedo`, which commonly undergo fermentation to varying degrees; its guidance says fermentation is less extensive for doce than azedo. MAPA also says it has no repeatable, reproducible method to distinguish fécula, polvilhos, and mixtures, and that polvilhos are outside the scope of the cited quality standard. Accept separate **source-named identity candidates** for doce and azedo, but make no claim that their process or performance has a measurable universal cutoff. Keep them separate from active tapioca starch, and leave the French term provisional. [S03, S06, S12]

MAPA distinguishes dry tapioca granules and pearls from the higher-moisture product sold as `tapioca umedecida`, `massa para tapioca`, `goma para tapioca`, or `goma`. The latter is a separate hydrated product family, outside the dry-tapioca standard; it may be refrigerated or may contain other ingredients or stabilizing treatments. Accept one narrow identity candidate under `tapioca umedecida`. Do not globalize generic `goma`, `goma de mandioca`, or `goma de tapioca` as aliases. [S04, S05, S07]

The recommended set is six separate identity candidates: three farinha groups, two polvilhos, and hydrated tapioca. Use the Brazilian product names in both locale displays, with short EN/fr-CA descriptors where needed; mark the French descriptors provisional because the OQLF entries establish only generic manioc flour, manioc starch, and tapioca wording. The source-language terms belong in display names or provenance, not in `aliasesEn` / `aliasesFr` merely to store Portuguese spellings. Assign no forms, numeric data, conversions, substitutions, or recipe links.

The parent phrase `farinha de mandioca` is **deferred as a standalone catalog row or global alias**. MAPA uses it as the umbrella commodity and requires group identification in classification; the active `edible cassava flour` already covers a distinct generic whole-root flour definition. A recipe or package that only says `farinha de mandioca` needs source context before it can be linked to one group or the active generic flour.

Keep `tapioca granulada` (dry irregular granules) and `tapioca pérola` / `sagú artificial` (dry round granules) outside this candidate set. MAPA includes these dry products in its tapioca standard; they are distinct from the starch powder and the hydrated preparation. A future identity review should assess them separately rather than turn them into starch forms or alias them to `tapioca umedecida`. [S04, S05, S10]

## Product evidence and limits

| Product family | Evidence-supported boundary | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| Farinha de mandioca: Seca | MAPA defines the group by cleaning, peeling, comminuting, pressing, sieving, and drying cassava roots. It is the official dry group within the farinha standard. [S01] | The group name establishes a product boundary, not a universal regional flavor, roast degree, grind, recipe role, or equivalence to Codex edible cassava flour. MAPA's regulatory quality thresholds are not imported as ingredient data. |
| Farinha de mandioca: d'Água | MAPA defines this as predominantly fermented and identifies maceration in water with fermentation and organic-acid formation as the relevant process distinction. [S01] | This is a product definition only. No fermentation duration, household process, safety instruction, or use ratio is proposed. Regional consumption habits and acidity differ. |
| Farinha de mandioca: Bijusada | MAPA defines a low-density product processed/laminated into predominantly irregular flakes and separately classifies the Bijusada group. It requires packaging that preserves the flake shape and density. [S01] | Keep the group label attached. No particle-size, density, or package-yield value is inferred for the catalog. |
| Polvilho doce and polvilho azedo | MAPA defines fécula as extracted, non-fermented cassava starch and says polvilhos are commonly fermented to varying degrees; fermentation is less extensive for doce and more extensive for azedo. It notes expansion properties after further processing and their use in biscuits and pão de queijo. Embrapa lists fécula/polvilho doce and polvilho azedo as separate cassava derivatives. [S03, S06] | MAPA says no repeatable/reproducible method differentiates fécula, polvilhos, and mixed grades, and it does not register or fiscalize either polvilho under IN 23/2005. Therefore accept source names as culinary identity candidates, not as uniform chemical or performance specifications. Do not transfer MAPA's descriptions into a test, process, fermentation threshold, conversion, or substitute. |
| Tapioca umedecida / hydrated preparation | MAPA's 2021 guidance says dry tapioca in the standard is granular and covers granulated and pearl/sagu-artificial subgroups with moisture below 15%; it excludes the higher-moisture hydrated product. MAPA lists regional sales names `tapioca umedecida`, `massa para tapioca`, `goma para tapioca`, and `goma`. The 2026 FNDE page reiterates the dry/hydrated scope distinction. [S04, S05] | The hydrated product can contain other ingredients or stabilizing treatments and may be refrigerated. The guidance establishes a product family, not uniform composition, shelf life, refrigeration instructions, or recipe-level interchangeability. The source variants are regional labels, not safe global aliases. |
| Tacacá recipe context | The federal Fisheries Ministry describes tacacá as a Northern Brazilian dish and its recipe lists `goma de mandioca (ou polvilho azedo/goma de tapioca)` for the goma component. [S07] | This one recipe's “or” construction is contextual alternative wording. It does not resolve whether each listed item is the same retail product or establish a global synonym, form conversion, or substitution rule. |

## Active catalog comparison

The live package has four relevant active rows:

| Active row | Current display / category | Boundary to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| `ingredient-ref-cassava-root` | `cassava root` / `manioc`; `VEGETABLE_AND_AROMATIC` | Fresh root, not flour or starch. |

| `ingredient-ref-tapioca-starch` | `tapioca starch` / `fécule de manioc`; `FLOUR_AND_STARCH` | OQLF's GDT gives this bilingual pairing for extracted manioc starch; MAPA describes fécula as non-fermented. | `ingredient-ref-gari` | `gari` / `gari`; `FLOUR_AND_STARCH` | Retain its separate processed-cassava identity; no source reviewed here equates it with these Brazilian groups. |

OQLF's GDT gives generic `farine de manioc` / `manioc flour` terminology, and a generic `fécule de manioc` entry whose English terms include `tapioca starch`. The OQLF's 2025 `tapioca` entry describes an extracted manioc starch that can appear as powder, flakes, small balls, or pearls. TERMIUM adds an important context distinction: one Food Industries/Chemistry record says `tapioca starch` should not be used as a synonym for generic `cassava starch`, while a separate Recipes entry defines tapioca as a cassava-starch preparation in granular, flake, pellet, or flour form. Keep the active row's already adopted OQLF bilingual wording, but do not extend that wording to every cassava-derived starch or flour product. These generic terms do not name the Brazilian farinha groups, polvilhos, or hydrated retail product. The specific Brazilian names should remain visible in both locales; the fr-CA parenthetical descriptions above are proposed explanations, not OQLF-approved terms. Targeted OQLF and TERMIUM searches did not surface dedicated entries for `farinha d’água`, `bijusada`, or `polvilho`; this is a limited negative search result, not proof that no established French terminology exists. [S08–S10, S13]

## Recommendation and exclusions

Accept the six source-named products as separate identity candidates, subject to final English/French wording review. The evidence is strong enough for product distinctions and candidate identities; it is not strong enough for universal product composition, quality cutoffs, safety instructions, preparation behavior, conversions, or substitutes.

Keep the active cassava root, edible cassava flour, tapioca starch, and gari rows unchanged and distinct. Leave their alias lists empty for the source-language regional names. Do not add forms to the current 18-form package. Defer unqualified `farinha de mandioca`, generic `goma`, `goma de mandioca`, `goma de tapioca`, `tapioca`, and dry tapioca granules/pearls from this proposal's integration scope. The Ministry's tacacá recipe does not authorize cross-product aliasing or substitutions.

No tests, deployment, catalog/ledger/generated-code/test/roadmap/plan changes, or shared workflow edits were made for this review.
