## Recommendation and identity boundary

A primary food-science study separately identifies three emmer samples—two improved *T. turgidum* subsp. *dicoccum* genotypes and one emmer landrace—prepares their brans, and tests the bran samples in common-wheat bread. This is direct evidence for a species-qualified milling fraction with human-food use. It supports a generic emmer-bran identity without implying broad retail availability or everyday use. [R996-S01]

Keep the proposed identity scoped to the bran fraction from emmer. Keep it distinct from:

- emmer grain, emmer flour, and emmer semolina;
- generic wheat bran, spelt bran, einkorn bran, and bran from other cereal species;
- bran-rich flour, wholemeal flour, germ, middlings, and other milling fractions.

Some standards and taxonomies use “wheat bran” broadly enough to include emmer or other ancient wheats. This proposal does not claim that the scoped name is exclusive across every regulatory definition. Preserve the current generic `wheat bran / son de blé` and species-qualified bran rows unchanged; do not add `emmer bran` as an alias to any of them or merge existing identities. [R993 boundary review; current catalog]

The study’s improved cultivars and landrace, laboratory milling and grinding, sieve fractions, sample analyses, bread formula, and results remain specific to that study. In particular, its 20% flour-mass replacement experiment, water adjustment, 250 g sieve-analysis aliquot, and 1 g water-retention sample are not household measures, a generic particle-size grade, or transferable recipe instructions. [R996-S01]
