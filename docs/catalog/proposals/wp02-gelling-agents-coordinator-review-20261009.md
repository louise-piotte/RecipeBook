## Coordinator recommendation

Keep `ingredient-ref-gelatin` as the generic `gelatin / gélatine` identity. Its bundled recipe lines specify a mass quantity but no powder/sheet presentation, Bloom strength, Type A/B, or brand. Treat those as product qualifiers only when an exact recipe or product states them; do not turn Bloom or Type A/B into global forms or infer a default grade.

If an exact future recipe calls for agar, use a generic `agar / agar-agar` identity and preserve powder, flakes, or filaments as written product presentations. Do not automatically alias `gélose`: OQLF also uses it for acid-treated agar sold under that name. Pectin is a family with materially different HM, LM, LMA, and LMC product classes. “Standard” and “low-sugar” package wording does not identify one universal class; `NH Nappage` and `325NH95` are manufacturer product names and must remain product-qualified. Keep carrageenan generic only when the source is generic; if a recipe explicitly names kappa, iota, or lambda, preserve that subtype instead of merging it into a subtype-blind row.

Add no global gelatin↔agar, generic pectin↔pectin-type, or gelling-agent substitutions. Leave all density, unit mapping, dilution, strength conversion, powder-to-sheet conversion, and other recipe-quantity fields empty. The source measurements reviewed here are assay or product/application data, not reusable kitchen conversions.

## Measurement, data, and substitution limits

- The saved gelatin line quantity is already in grams. It does not give a volume conversion, density, sheet count, or sheet-to-powder equivalence. Bloom is a laboratory gel-strength result under a specified test method, not a recipe dose or an inferred conversion.
- Powder, flakes, filaments, leaves, and manufacturer pectin preparations are not interchangeable measurements. No source reviewed provides a product-independent kitchen mapping applicable to this corpus. No `defaultDensity`, `unitMappings`, form density, package yield, or substitution ratio is recommended.
- Cargill's pectin technical guide and Louis François' product pages describe class- and formulation-dependent conditions. Those use descriptions are not a global pectin dosage rule. Sure-Jell explicitly directs users to the exact product recipe and identifies its products as non-interchangeable; that supports product-scoped instructions, not a cross-brand conversion.
- The JECFA/FAO carrageenan monograph distinguishes kappa, iota, and lambda structures and notes that commercial articles can contain standardizing sugars, salts, or other formulation components. No general subtype blending or dose can be inferred from a generic `carrageenan` line.
- No reviewed source establishes a generic gelatin↔agar, gelatin↔pectin, gelatin↔carrageenan, pectin-type↔pectin-type, or agar-presentation substitution. Do not add one.
