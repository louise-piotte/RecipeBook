# WP02 B3 R182 — mango kernel starch product-boundary hold

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-16.
- **Status:** Root disposition recorded below; no shared catalog or ledger data was integrated.
- **Plan fit:** One distinct regional starch candidate under the still-open WP02 Section 04 B3 parent, “Water-chestnut flour and other documented regional starches.” The candidate is starch experimentally separated from the kernel inside the mango stone of *Mangifera indica*. It is not mango fruit-pulp starch, whole-kernel flour, mango powder, or mango-kernel oil/butter.
- **Proposal-local source-ID prefix:** `WP02-B3-MANGO-KERNEL-STARCH-R182-20261016-S`. The prefix is unused in the ledger and archive. The OQLF general `amidon` page is already cited under existing ledger source IDs and is reused below without reassigning those IDs.

## Recommendation

**Hold a separate mango-kernel-starch identity, display, aliases, and ledger candidate pending a current Canadian culinary product and a product-matched Québec French name.** Primary studies establish that researchers can isolate starch from mango kernels and characterize it for possible food use. The methods and materials vary: laboratory studies use wet milling, centrifugation, chemical washing, ultrasound, and different cultivars or maturity states. No current Canada- or Québec-facing source located in this review sells or specifies mango-kernel starch as a culinary ingredient. A Québec ingredient distributor lists corn, wheat, potato, rice, and pea starches, while a Canadian fruit-ingredient supplier lists mango pulp, purée, and concentrate; neither lists mango-kernel starch. These catalog searches are bounded negative evidence, not proof that no supplier exists. [S01–S05]

OQLF supports the general term *amidon* for starch and *mangue* for mango, but neither entry names this compound product. Do not promote a constructed display such as `amidon de noyau de mangue` or `amidon d’amande de mangue` as an established Québec product name. Keep candidate English phrases `mango kernel starch` and `mango seed starch` research-only, and do not activate `mango starch`, which could ambiguously refer to starch in mango flesh or the seed kernel. Reopen when a current Canadian culinary product source identifies the starting material and process scope and supplies or supports a matching Québec French label. [S06–S07]

## Numeric, process, form, and substitution boundaries

- **Extraction yield and purity:** One standardized-process study reports 48.43 ± 1.08% starch yield and 76.46 ± 0.83% purity for an optimized centrifugation route, while its chemical route reports 36.50 ± 0.58% yield and 92.03 ± 0.87% purity under a different alkali/acid treatment. Another study reports its highest 54% yield at 480 W ultrasound for 20 minutes. These are method- and sample-specific laboratory recoveries, not commercial ingredient composition, home extraction yield, package yield, or conversion factors. Do not select, average, or activate them. [S01–S02]
- **Composition and functionality:** The ultrasound study reports treatment-dependent changes to amylose, water/oil-holding capacity, solubility, swelling, and pasting behavior. These measurements characterize its prepared samples; they do not establish a standardized grade or a reusable household measure. [S01]
- **Forms:** Add none. Do not model starch as a form of mango fruit, mango kernel flour, whole kernel, amchur, or mango-kernel fat. If a culinary product is later documented, assess its specific native/modified status and processing independently. [S01–S03]
- **Density and measures:** No retail package, serving mass, volume measure, bulk density, unit mapping, or source-matched recipe quantity was established. Leave all unset.
- **Substitutions and recipe links:** The reviewed papers characterize extraction and functionality or discuss possible industrial/food applications; they do not establish a reproducible household replacement ratio for corn, potato, rice, tapioca, or other starches. No active global or contextual substitution, cooking yield, conversion, or recipe-line link is supported. The recipe corpus has no matching demand.

## Reopen criteria

1. Locate a current Canadian or Québec food ingredient product or supplier specification that identifies starch isolated from the mango kernel for culinary use.
2. Confirm the product’s plant part, whether it is native or modified, and any material extraction/processing qualifier. Keep fruit-pulp starch, whole-kernel flour/powder, and kernel oil/fat separate.
3. Obtain a product-matched Québec French label or authoritative terminology for the exact ingredient; do not infer a product name merely by combining *amidon* and *mangue*.
4. Repeat active-reference, form, legacy, ledger, source URL/ID, proposal/archive, picker, and recipe-corpus audits before catalog promotion.
5. Research measures, density, forms, recipe use, and substitutions separately against the supported product. Keep all laboratory extraction yields and functional-property measurements source-specific and inactive.

## Root integration decision

**Hold confirmed.** Mango-kernel starch remains outside active references and the research ledger. The reviewed evidence establishes laboratory-isolated starch, but no current Canadian culinary product or product-matched Québec French label. Keep it distinct from mango fruit, amchur, kernel flour/powder, pulp starch, and kernel oil. No recipe demand was found. The R182 plan and batch README record this decision; no shared data record was added.
