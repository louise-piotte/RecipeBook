# WP06 Section 05: Mexico and Central America ingredient-identity gap sweep

- **Review and source-access date:** 2026-10-09.
- **Status:** four named herb identities were integrated by the coordinator; all other recommendations remain proposal-only. This packet does not complete WP06 Section 05.
- **Scope:** source-grounded candidate review across Mexico and the seven Central American countries. This is a bounded gap sweep, not a quota exercise or a claim of full regional coverage.
- **Source IDs:** WP06-MEX-CENTAM-20261009-S01 through WP06-MEX-CENTAM-20261009-S31. The exact proposed IDs were checked against all source IDs in the live research ledger and all Markdown files in docs/catalog/batches and docs/catalog/proposals before assignment; no collision was found.

## Recommendation summary

The strongest near-term identity candidates are the separately named herbs and edible flower **epazote**, **hoja santa**, **chipilín leaves**, and **loroco**. Consider **achiote** as a spice candidate only after seed, powder, and paste boundaries receive product-specific review. The fresh and dried chile evidence supports keeping named products apart, but it does not settle whether each pair belongs in separate reference identities or a base identity plus form. Do not add aliases such as poblano/ancho, chilaca/pasilla, mirasol/guajillo, or jalapeño/chipotle.

The source set also supports review of compound products: Yucatecan recados by named subtype, salsa macha, and Costa Rican conserva de chiverre. Keep Guatemalan recados and Belizean recado scoped to their own named preparations; the shared word does not establish one Central American product. Do not create a generic mole, salsa, curtido, atole, or tiste ingredient identity from a prepared dish or beverage.

Any proposed English or fr-CA display wording for new candidates remains provisional unless the source register identifies direct Québec usage. Preserve each source’s Spanish or Indigenous-language name and spelling. No universal formula, alias, density, conversion, form mapping, yield, or substitution is proposed.

## Measurement and substitution evidence

Measurement and substitution were assessed independently from identity. This packet proposes no density, mass/volume conversion, unit mapping, package yield, form conversion, quantity ratio, or substitution rule. Recipe quantities remain in the source’s exact context and are not transferred to catalog data. For example, the Nicaragua source is a named Somoto/Madriz festival recipe, not a generic conversion or standard serving formula [S17].

The Yucatán cookbook includes a context-bound citrus fallback note [S06], but this packet does not promote it as a universal substitution. The same applies to recipe-level alternatives, forms of dried chiles, or serving suggestions. No replacement between masa harina and other maize products, fresh and dried chiles, local beans, prepared recados, or sour citrus is supported here.

## Recommended integration candidates and open questions
