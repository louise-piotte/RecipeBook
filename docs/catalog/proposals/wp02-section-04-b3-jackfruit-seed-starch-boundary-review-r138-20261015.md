# WP02 Section 04 B3 R138 — jackfruit seed starch product-boundary hold

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-15.
- **Status:** Proposal-only hold. This review changes no shared catalog JSON, research ledger, checklist, roadmap, generated file, recipe, or application file.
- **Checklist:** WP02 Section 04 B3, `[ ] Water-chestnut flour and other documented regional starches.` The parent remains open.
- **Candidate:** food starch isolated from seeds of accepted *Artocarpus heterophyllus* Lam. (jackfruit).
- **Proposal-local source-ID prefix:** `WP02-B3-JACKFRUIT-SEED-STARCH-R138-20261015-S`. A read-only check found no prior R138 prefix or focused *A. heterophyllus* seed-starch decision in the live references, forms, research ledger, legacy candidates, plan, or proposal/batch archive.

## Recommendation: hold, no delta

Keep jackfruit seed starch outside the active reference catalog and research ledger until a current Canadian or Québec human-food product source establishes a marketed, isolated seed-starch product and a product-specific Québec French name. Primary studies establish a separable starch fraction from jackfruit seeds and include food-application experiments. They do not establish a current Canadian pantry product, package specification, or Québec product label.

The Canadian and Québec-facing evidence found in this review identifies adjacent but different products: Grace Foods Canada lists a 540 mL canned `Fruit du Jacquier Vert` with water, salt, and citric acid; a Canadian jackfruit-flour seller describes flour made from green, unripe jackfruit. Neither source says the product is seed-derived or isolated starch. Exact searches for Canadian/Québec jackfruit seed starch, jackfruit starch, and French seed-starch wording surfaced research rather than a local standalone starch product. This is a bounded search result, not proof that no such product is sold through every channel.

The English research phrase `jackfruit seed starch` is precise enough to describe the studied fraction. OQLF supports `jaque` / `jacque` for the fruit *A. heterophyllus* and `amidon` as the preferred general starch term; it does not attest a compound for the seeds or their starch. `Amidon de graines de jaque` would be a transparent construction only, while `jacquier` is the OQLF tree term. Do not activate a French display or aliases from these component terms alone. A seed-derived starch should not be labeled `fécule` by analogy: OQLF reserves that more specific term for tubers or other underground plant organs. [S01–S03]

**No JSON delta is proposed.** Do not add or reserve an ingredient-reference ID or ledger candidate. Add no aliases, form, measurement observation, density, unit mapping, recipe link, conversion, yield, or active substitution. Reconsider if a current Canadian or Québec label or supplier specification identifies food-grade isolated *A. heterophyllus* seed starch and a Québec source supports its product name.

The results located primary studies and research reviews, but no current Canadian or Québec standalone human-food package or supplier specification for extracted seed starch. No source identified a product-specific Québec French starch name.

Two current-market references help bound the results without closing the candidate:

- Grace Foods Canada lists canned green jackfruit as `Fruit du Jacquier Vert`, 540 mL; its listed ingredients are fruit, water, salt, and citric acid. This is local bilingual product wording for fruit, not seed starch. [S07]
- Finch Foods Canada lists 500 g `Jackfruit Flour`, describes it as made from green, unripe jackfruit, and lists jackfruit flour as its ingredient. Its page does not identify the flour as seed-derived or as an extracted starch. It therefore cannot establish the candidate product. [S08]

These searches were for discoverable public retail or supplier evidence. A search result absence is not proof of market nonexistence, and a listing for whole fruit, seed flour, or a starch-containing blend would not alone establish an isolated-starch product.

## Measurements, forms, substitutions, and recipe demand

- **Study-specific extraction results:** Rengsutthi and Charoenrein report 10.55 g starch per 100 g seeds on a wet basis and 26.13 g/100 g on a dry basis for their one cultivar and extraction procedure. Tulyathan et al. report a 6% dry-basis starch concentration for a laboratory amylogram and a pasting temperature of 81 °C. These are lab extraction/sample and pasting observations, not bulk powder density, a household spoon/cup mass, a package serving, or a reusable extraction yield. Keep them out of active numeric fields. [S05–S06]
- **Forms:** Add none. Whole edible seeds, seed flour, and starch isolated from seeds are separate materials. Wet milling, alkali treatment, centrifugation, washing, and drying in the studies are extraction procedures, not a validated consumer form family or kitchen conversion. [S04–S06]
- **Substitution:** One primary study tested jackfruit seed starch and cornstarch separately at 1 g per 100 g in its laboratory chilli-sauce formulation, reported sauce performance, and concluded that jackfruit seed starch could substitute for cornstarch in that sauce. Preserve this as a bounded experimental observation only. It does not define a general 1:1-by-mass household rule, a substitute for other starches, or a catalog rule for sauces generally. No active substitution or recipe link is proposed. [S06]
- **Recipe demand:** A manifest-ordered scan of all **114 recipe files and 1,123 ingredient rows** found no content or ingredient-line hit for `jackfruit`, `jack fruit`, `jakfruit`, `jaque`, `jacque`, `jacquier`, `fruit du jacquier`, *Artocarpus heterophyllus*, `seed starch`, `jackfruit seed starch`, or the candidate French constructions. The bundle therefore supports no jackfruit seed-starch identity link, form, measurement, or substitution. This corpus-only result does not imply no wider market demand.

## Reconsideration conditions

1. Locate a current Canadian or Québec human-food label or supplier specification for isolated *A. heterophyllus* seed starch, distinct from whole jackfruit, seed flour, whole-fruit flour, research samples, cosmetic starch, and starch-containing blends.
2. Confirm a Québec French product name from a local label or authoritative terminology source. Do not activate `amidon de graines de jaque`, `amidon de graines de jacquier`, or a `fécule` construction from component terms alone.
3. Keep the candidate separate from `ingredient-ref-jackfruit` and *A. altilis* breadfruit flesh starch. Recheck whether a source-qualified English display would create acceptable `jackfruit` and `seed starch` query results before assigning any aliases.
4. Reassess numeric behavior and substitutions independently. Keep the sauce study's 1 g/100 g result scoped to its experimental chilli sauce and do not transfer extraction yield, pasting data, or seed-flour baking ratios.
5. Re-scan recipe demand and leave WP02 Section 04 B3 open; this review does not complete the regional-starch checklist item.
