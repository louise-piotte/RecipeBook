## Recommendation and ingredient boundary

Add generic `pearl millet bran` as the bran fraction separated from pearl millet (*Pennisetum glaucum*) during grain processing. A primary milling study distinguishes whole flour, semi-refined flour, and a bran-rich fraction; a second study explicitly identifies pearl-millet bran as a processing by-product and describes one parboiling, decortication, milling, polishing, and sieving route for obtaining it. These two accessible primary studies establish a distinct, source-grain-qualified milling ingredient with specialized food-component research use. The proposal also cites an institutional food-science report about bread development, but the report PDF could not be loaded during root review and was not used as active evidence. They do not establish broad household use, retail availability, or a standard bran grade. [R896-S01–S02]

Keep this ingredient separate from:

- pearl-millet grain (`ingredient-ref-pearl-millet`) and pearl-millet flour (`ingredient-ref-pearl-millet-flour`);
- the distinct milling fractions of proso/common millet, foxtail millet, finger millet, sorghum, triticale, buckwheat, spelt, wheat, rye, barley, oat, rice, and corn;
- the held pearl-millet-starch candidate and its extraction evidence;
- bran-rich fractions, which may contain broken grain, husk, or other material in addition to bran;
- extracted or hydrothermally treated dietary fibre, isolated polysaccharides, oil, or other derivatives.

### No-transfer boundary

Do not generalize any process, amount, measurement, composition, or performance result from the reviewed sources to every pearl-millet-bran ingredient:

- Suma and Urooj study a `bran rich fraction` obtained from two pearl-millet varieties. The study's varieties, milling steps, measured composition, and mineral-bioaccessibility findings remain tied to those samples. The source does not define pure bran for every mill. [R896-S01]
- Kaur et al. obtain bran from parboiled, decorticated kernels using a specified milling, polishing, and sieving process, then extract dietary fibre under laboratory conditions. The process, 74.5% extraction result, particle size, water/oil-holding values, phenolic results, and other assays are source-specific; none is a household measure, generic density, or transferable culinary property. [R896-S02]
- The proposal cites a CSIR-CFTRI bread-development report with separately processed pearl-millet bran/endosperm and a 20% wheat-replacement formula. The report PDF could not be loaded during root recheck, so this claim was not included in the active ledger or data; even if verified later, it would remain formulation-specific rather than a general substitution rule. [R896-S03]

No claim is transferred from pearl-millet flour, from another millet's bran, or from a branded product. Existing branded rows and evidence remain intact. A later use of product-specific evidence on a generic row requires a separate, explicit transferability review.

For Québec French, propose `son de millet perle` as a transparent descriptive compound based on the OQLF's source-grain term `millet perle`. The reviewed OQLF entry does not attest this bran compound, so keep the full French display provisional pending independent terminology review. Add no bare `millet`, `mil`, `son`, `bran`, crop alias, or scientific name. Reuse existing OQLF source record `WP03-S18`; do not duplicate its URL. [WP03-S18]

3. `son de millet perle` is a descriptive French construction from the OQLF crop name; the complete bran compound remains provisional pending Québec terminology review.
4. The cited 20% wheat-replacement note is unverified in this integration because its PDF could not be loaded. Extraction yields, laboratory measurements, sample compositions, and processing details remain source-specific. No generic measure, density, substitution, yield, or nutrition claim is proposed.
5. Further brand-, product-line-, flavour-family-, SKU-, or retailer-variant-only work remains out of scope because commercial-product counts do not add ingredient diversity.
