## Recommendation

**Keep `candidate-taro-corm-starch` unresolved.** This follow-up found no exact Québec or Canadian French product listing for isolated *C. esculenta* corm starch. `Fécule de taro` is a plausible construction from OQLF’s general terminology, while `amidon de taro` occurs in a non-Canadian French commercial classification. Neither is attested as a Québec/Canadian product name for this product.

The Canadian-accessible French retail evidence found in this pass names **farine de taro** for flour made from dried taro. It does not name an extracted starch. Keep that existing whole-corm flour identity separate. Do not add a reference or alias, form, measurement, conversion, yield, recipe link, or substitution from this evidence.

The technical boundary is consequential: whole-corm flour is dried and milled corm material, while the candidate is the separated starch fraction. A page or ingredient list that mentions taro, taro flour, generic `amidon`, or a taro-flavoured product does not establish that the item is extracted taro starch. Likewise, the OAPI wording does not specify *C. esculenta* and cannot serve as a Québec alias.

## Québec and Canadian retail search outcome

I searched current Québec/Canadian-facing retail results for `amidon de taro`, `fécule de taro`, `taro starch`, `taro starch powder`, and `Colocasia starch`, including grocery and Asian-specialty listings. Results either referred to fresh taro, taro flour, taro-flavoured products, other-source starches such as tapioca, or non-Canadian classification/trademark pages. No exact French retail item for extracted *C. esculenta* corm starch surfaced. This is a bounded search result, not proof that the product is unavailable or that no label exists.
