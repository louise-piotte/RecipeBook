# WP04 proposal — Acheta domesticus cricket powder identity and boundary review

**Scope:** one human-food ingredient candidate: whole-cricket powder from *Acheta domesticus*. This review does not cover insect feed or pet food.

## Repository and demand check

- The manifest lists **114 recipes**. A direct scan of all manifest-listed files counted **1,123 ingredient rows**. No ingredient row or any other recipe text matched `cricket`, `crickets`, `Acheta`, `insect`, `grillon`, `grillons`, `farine de grillon`, or `poudre de grillon` (case-insensitive).
- The same term-family scan found no matching active ingredient reference, form, or legacy import candidate. No exact catalog or legacy key collision was found.
- The ledger mentions African locust bean (`Parkia biglobosa`) in the unrelated `soumbala` record. It is a fermented seed condiment and supplies no insect identity or alias. No cricket, *Acheta*, or insect powder proposal was found in prior proposal/batch files.
- Existing catalog category values include `PROTEIN`. Existing whey powder remains `DAIRY_AND_ALTERNATIVE`, and textured vegetable protein remains `PROTEIN`; neither overlaps this species/product identity.

## Evidence and recommendation

Health Canada’s current non-novel determinations list has a dated 2016-07-18 entry for “Cricket (*Acheta domesticus*) powder,” described as powder made from whole crickets. The French list renders the same entry as **“Poudre de grillon (*Acheta domesticus*)”**. This determination supports this narrowly described food ingredient’s non-novel status; it is not a product approval or a general authorization for other species, extracts, formulations, uses, or health claims. Health Canada’s page also says listed determinations concern novelty only, are not safety evaluations, and do not exempt foods from other legal requirements.

The identity and naming boundary are supported independently. OQLF’s 2025 GDT entry pairs *Acheta domesticus* with English **house cricket** and Québec French **grillon domestique**, and says this species is sometimes reduced to powder and incorporated into other ingredients for consumption. C-fu Foods, a supplier that says its ingredients are available in Canada, describes its cricket flour and cricket powder as the same product made by roasting and grinding whole *Acheta domesticus* crickets. It reports one ingredient and approximately 65% protein for its product, but those are supplier-specific observations, not universal values.

**Recommendation: add an active, identity-only `PROTEIN` reference** for human-food whole-cricket powder from *Acheta domesticus*. Suggested displays are **house cricket powder / poudre de grillon domestique**. The scientific name should remain in the evidence/identity boundary rather than being an unqualified ingredient alias. The species-qualified display combines the exact Health Canada ingredient scope with OQLF’s Québec species name; it is an editorial display recommendation, not a verbatim source label.

Do not hold the species-specific identity for lack of Canadian status or bilingual name evidence. Keep unqualified market labels such as `cricket powder`, `cricket flour`, `poudre de grillon`, and `farine de grillon` out of global aliases for now. Health Canada lists whole-cricket powder for both *Acheta domesticus* and the distinct banded cricket *Gryllodes sigillatus*, with separate entries for banded-cricket flour and roasted whole insects. A broad alias would erase a documented species boundary and the corpus has no recipe line to resolve that ambiguity. Revisit those aliases only with an explicit scope rule or exact recipe/product evidence. Do not add a generic `insect powder`, `protein powder`, `Acheta`, `house cricket`, or `grillon domestique` ingredient alias: these either identify a broader/different product state or fail to name the powder.

## Regulatory and product boundaries

- **Human food:** Health Canada’s determination is specifically for powder made from whole *A. domesticus*. CFIA describes edible insect products, including powders and flour, as foods sold in Canada and says they must meet applicable requirements for foods; interprovincial trade, export, and import have the applicable Safe Food for Canadians Regulations requirements. This supports the human-food catalog scope, not blanket clearance for every product marketed as cricket powder.
- **Pet food, feeder insects, and livestock feed:** keep out of this identity. CFIA treats pet-food use, livestock-feed use, and human consumption as distinct end uses; insect-derived livestock feed ingredients may require source-specific pre-market approval and registration. PHAC says pet foods are not subject to the same regulations and testing as food intended for humans. A feeder or pet-feed label is not evidence of a human-food-grade ingredient.
- **Whole insects:** whole or roasted crickets remain a separate product state. The determination reviewed here concerns powder made from whole insects, not a whole-insect recipe identity. Do not link this candidate to whole crickets or roasted crickets.
- **Other insect powders:** do not merge banded cricket (*Gryllodes sigillatus*), mealworm, locust, or other insect powders. Health Canada’s separate *Gryllodes* determinations are direct evidence that “cricket powder/flour” can describe more than one species.
- **Extracts and formulated products:** C-fu distinguishes its whole-cricket flour/powder from textured insect protein and a higher-solubility insect protein concentrate. Keep protein isolates/concentrates, extracted or restructured proteins, and formulated foods out of this whole-powder identity.
- **Generic protein powder or supplement:** “protein powder” is a use/marketing description, not a unique ingredient name. Do not alias to whey, soy, pea, or other protein powders and do not infer a supplement identity, serving equivalence, nutrition claim, or substitution.

## Measures, forms, composition, and substitution review

- Do not add a separate `powdered` form: the proposed reference is already limited to the whole-cricket powder product. No whole-cricket reference or form is proposed in this packet.
- No global weight/volume measure, density, unit mapping, or conversion is supported. C-fu’s approximate protein percentage is brand-specific and gives no measure. Landish’s Canadian French page displays a 1 tbsp (10 g) use suggestion for its product, but it does not identify the species; do not attach that measure to *A. domesticus*.
- No general nutrient composition, processing specification, package size, or yield should be added. Manufacturer descriptions vary, and process, defatting, and insect species can change a cricket powder’s composition.
- Do not add a substitution to flour, whey, meat, or another insect ingredient. C-fu explicitly describes its cricket powder as a protein-rich product and gives baking/use examples, but no universal functional or quantity equivalence. Its product-specific IPC distinction also cautions against assuming water or baking behavior from a generic protein percentage.
- No recipe links or recipe edits are proposed.
