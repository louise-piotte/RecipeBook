## Candidate identity and decision

Quinoa bran is a generic milling fraction, distinct from whole quinoa and quinoa flour. Primary milling research separates quinoa bran from other grain fractions and describes the fractions as potential food ingredients. A peer-reviewed bread study also tests quinoa bran in gluten-free dough and bread. These sources establish a real generic milling fraction and food-research use, rather than a commercial roster item. [R902-S01–S03]

The consumer-use boundary is unresolved. A separate primary study of quinoa bran samples collected from five regions reports substantial saponin content and states that saponin removal is key to reducing the antinutritional issue before food-industry use. The study’s measured values describe those particular samples and cannot be assigned to all quinoa bran, but the finding is enough to prevent treating the unqualified identity as a ready-to-eat household ingredient. Its food-appropriate processing, acceptable residual saponin level, and relationship to the bread-study samples are not established by the accessible sources. [R902-S04]

Keep the candidate distinct from:

- whole quinoa, colored quinoa, quinoa flour, roasted quinoa flour, quinoa flakes, puffed quinoa, and the existing dry and cooked quinoa forms;
- quinoa hull, seed coat, germ, perisperm, bran-derived fibre-rich fractions, flour, protein concentrates, extracted saponins, and other processing fractions unless a separate review establishes an exact identity or equivalence;
- every other grain’s bran identity.

No alias or identity merge is proposed. Preserve all current generic and branded quinoa, cereal, and bran entries, along with their source records and product-scoped evidence.

### Precise no-transfer boundary

- Föste et al. examined five quinoa-bran milling fractions in a particular gluten-free dough and bread study. The reported 10% formulation result, 7.4% loaf-volume change, appearance and taste observations, rheological results, and material-specific outcomes stay within that experiment. Do not use them as a home-recipe ratio, generic bread performance claim, safe-use direction, density, measure, or substitution. The accessible abstract does not establish that its bran samples were saponin-reduced or consumer-ready. [R902-S01]
- Ray et al.’s 2021 dry-milling process and the 2023 laboratory-scale fractionation study describe their equipment, moisture conditioning, separation conditions, fraction labels, yields, composition, granulation, and functional properties. Those values belong to those research materials and methods. The 2023 fibre-rich fraction is described as coming from quinoa bran; do not make that fraction synonymous with all quinoa bran. [R902-S02–S03]
- Zhou et al.’s saponin results come from five collected regional samples. Keep the reported mean and range attached to those samples. Do not present them as a category-wide composition, threshold, or estimate for any recipe ingredient. The paper’s statement about saponin removal does not establish a household washing, cooking, roasting, or other de-saponification method. [R902-S04]
- Do not transfer any quinoa-bran measure or claim to whole quinoa, quinoa flour, another grain’s bran, a named manufacturer’s material, or a retail product. No manufacturer or retailer product was researched for this review.

The proposed French phrase son de quinoa is provisional only. The OQLF entry supports the Québec term quinoa and its classification as a pseudocereal; the existing OQLF entry for son supports the milling base word. Neither entry attests the compound son de quinoa. The existing CFIA bran guidance requires a grain-source name for bran from a source other than wheat; it supports the source-qualified English naming pattern, not the French compound, culinary suitability, or an active identity. [WP03-BLACKQUINOA-R151-20261015-S01; WP03-BARLEY-BRAN-QC-R89-20261013-S01; WP03-BARLEY-BRAN-20261013-R56-S03]

## Root review and disposition

Root review confirmed the evidence boundary. The TUM indexed abstract describes five quinoa-bran fractions in gluten-free bread research, but direct page access was unavailable; the accessible abstract does not identify the saponin state of the samples. The official CFTRI repository and indexed ScienceDirect record describe a 2021 dry-milling study that used dehulled, polished quinoa to reduce saponins and separated bran, germ, and perisperm as potential food ingredients. That process is study-specific and does not establish a general quinoa-bran specification. The opened 2023 Oxford Academic paper distinguishes its own bran-derived fibre-rich fraction and records its purchased sample, water conditioning, and laboratory milling; those details also remain study-specific. The indexed PubMed abstract for Zhou et al. explicitly identifies saponin removal as necessary to address the antinutritional concern in its sampled quinoa bran.

Given that source set, keep the gap on hold at the active catalog level: the materials support a named milling fraction and experimental food research, while they do not establish the food-appropriate processing state to expose as a generic recipe-picker ingredient. Do not generalize sample values or propose a household saponin-removal method. Reopen when source evidence establishes a usable, food-appropriate quinoa-bran identity and processing boundary, then review the Québec French compound. This is a generic evidence hold, not a commercial-product research task.

No brand, product-line, flavour, SKU, or retailer roster was pursued. Existing generic and branded quinoa and bran rows and all product-scoped evidence remain unchanged. No software tests, build, emulator, or deployment were run because this is a documentation-only hold.
