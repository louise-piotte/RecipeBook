# WP02 B4 — Regional root and fruit flour boundaries

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-10
- **Status:** Proposal only. This review creates no catalog, ledger, recipe, test, plan, or roadmap changes.
- **Scope:** Breadfruit flour, tigernut flour, yam flour, and taro flour. The open B4 checklist remains open for other regional root/fruit flours and independent Québec wording review.
- **Source IDs:** `WP02-REGIONAL-ROOT-FLOUR-20261010-S01` through `S22`. A repository search found no previous use of this proposal-local prefix; these IDs are not ledger records.

## Product identity and boundary review

### Breadfruit flour

The OQLF GDT gives `fruit à pain` and `fruit de l’arbre à pain` as preferred Québec terms for *Artocarpus altilis* [S01]. A Pacific Community leaflet issued for Pacific food use names `farine de fruit à pain` and describes it as dried breadfruit that is pounded or ground and sifted [S02]. This is direct French regional product evidence, not an OQLF ruling. Its separate traditional preservation route cooks and mashes the fruit before sun-drying; do not make that method universal. FDA's response to a Canadian University of British Columbia notification describes a dried and ground breadfruit-flesh product and identifies *A. altilis* plus *A. altilis × A. mariannensis* hybrids. FDA expressly says its response is not an endorsement of the name for ingredient labeling [S03]. A supplier product specification also names *A. altilis* and describes selected fruit being washed, peeled, dried, ground, sorted, and packed [S04].

Recommend the current catalog's narrower *A. altilis* fruit scope for the proposed flour reference. Do not add `breadnut flour`: Kew documents both the shifting breadnut common name and the separately recognized *A. camansi*, so a seed-bearing product or label may not identify the same flesh-only input [S05]. Do not add `ʻulu flour`/`ulu flour` as aliases in this pass: the name occurs on a Hawaiʻi product, but a product-local name need not be a global synonym [S06]. Hold flour made solely from the FDA-noted hybrid pending a species boundary review against the existing breadfruit reference.

### Tigernut flour

TERMIUM Plus defines tiger nut/tigernut as the edible tuber of *Cyperus esculentus*, and gives French common names including `souchet`, `souchet comestible`, `amande de terre`, `noix tigrée`, and `noix de tigre` [S09]. Tigernuts Traders lists conventional, fine, extra-fine, and defatted tigernut flours separately in its English and French product catalogs; its traditional product description says the flour is obtained by direct grinding and marks the product raw [S06–S08, S10]. This supports a generic ground-tuber flour candidate and a product boundary for defatted flour, but not a universal grade, roast, or fat specification.

The manufacturer's direct French catalog names `farine de souchet`; a Québec retailer sells a single-ingredient product titled `Farine de noix tigrées` and explains that its ingredient is a tuber rather than a nut [S10–S11]. Use `farine de souchet` as the display because it matches both the manufacturer's exact French product wording and the Canadian terminology entry. Keep `farine de noix tigrées` out of aliases pending independent Québec review of the nut-like market name. Do not alias whole souchet/chufa tubers, tiger-nut oil, tiger-nut butter, or defatted flour to the proposed whole-tuber flour.

### Yam flour

OQLF maps `igname` to the genus *Dioscorea* and explicitly distinguishes it from sweet potato, despite the North American use of `yam` for some sweet potatoes [S12]. Nigeria's Standards Organisation catalog lists NIS 457 (2004), titled “Standard for Whole and Composite Yam Flour”; the public catalog entry establishes that both whole and composite products exist under the standard's coverage, but does not expose enough text to define their composition [S13]. Abebi Foods names a `Yam Flour` made from selected yam species, calls it `elubo` in Nigeria, and says it is used to prepare `amala`; its separately named `Poundo Yam` is described as yam tubers with added edible starch [S14]. A Canadian retailer lists `Farine d’igname / Elubo / Yam flour`, while a Québec African-grocery page describes foufou as manioc or yam flour [S16–S17].

This supports a narrow yam-sourced flour identity and the Québec French display `farine d’igname`. It does not support `elubo` as a global alias: Abebi's single product uses that local name, while a food-science study describes `elubo` flour from yam, cassava, or unripe plantain, and calls the cooked paste `amala` [S15]. Keep `amala` as a prepared dish, not flour; keep `poundo yam` apart as its own instant/composite product family; and keep `fufu/foufou` unassigned because Québec-market usage spans manioc and yam. Composite yam flour also remains outside the proposed single-source identity until its ingredients are known.

### Taro flour

OQLF lists `taro`, `colocase`, and `colocasie` as preferred French names for *Colocasia esculenta* [S18]. TERMIUM Plus cautions that common `taro` usage is also applied to *Colocasia antiquorum* and *Xanthosoma* [S19]. Indonesia's current National Food Agency regulation (2026 amendment) recognizes taro flour as a flour milled from taro tubers, while its covered “talas” class includes both *Colocasia esculenta* and *Xanthosoma*; its process sketch includes peeling, cutting, soaking, drying, milling, and sieving [S20]. A Talaza Mitra Makmur product listing names `Tepung Talas Talaza – Talaza Taro flour`, identifies *C. esculenta*, and describes flour made from selected fresh tubers, sun-dried and finely milled [S21]. The directory page identifies the manufacturer, but its product entry is dated 2019; the manufacturer's profile showed activity in March 2026 when accessed. A peer-reviewed study confirms that taro flour is a food ingredient and that drying/precooking routes vary product properties, without establishing one universal process [S22].

Accept only the *C. esculenta* corm product supported by the manufacturer and current seed reference. Do not apply the wider Indonesian “talas” class to the global `taro` reference, since that class also includes *Xanthosoma*. Use `farine de taro` only as a transparent provisional fr-CA compound from OQLF's preferred base term; the exact flour phrase has not been found in an OQLF entry or Québec product listing. Keep `farine de colocase` and `farine de colocasie` inactive until species scope is confirmed for the source product. Taro powder may denote other food or beverage ingredients, so it is not an alias for the proposed flour.

| `ingredient-ref-taro` | `taro / taro`; category `VEGETABLE_AND_AROMATIC` | Keep the fresh corm and its flour as separate identities. Bare `taro` will match both. Avoid broader `cocoyam`, `malanga`, and `dasheen` terms because source evidence spans different taxa. |
