# WP04 Section P — octopus in Galician sauce product-boundary review (R233)

- **Review / source-access date:** 2026-10-16.
- **Scope:** One unfinished, named preserved-octopus product boundary under the open Section P preservation checklist.
- **Recommendation:** **Add** identity-only `octopus in Galician sauce / poulpe en sauce galicienne`, scoped to the source-supported market product. Keep it separate from generic octopus, the accepted octopus-in-olive-oil product, and other octopus preparations.
- **Proposal status:** Accepted and integrated as one identity-only `PROTEIN` reference with two French aliases and one English alias. No forms, active measurements, conversions, yields, recipe links, or substitutions were added. See the R233 batch README and research-ledger record.
- **Proposal source IDs:** `WP04-SECTIONP-OCTOPUS-GALICIAN-SAUCE-R233-20261016-S01` through `S09`, plus audit ID `WP04-SECTIONP-OCTOPUS-GALICIAN-SAUCE-R233-20261016-R01`. Root added R233 S10 for the Solfarmers French collection when correcting the attempted source-ID reuse.

## Recommendation and open checklist fit

Section P still has the open checklist item for smoked, salt-cured, dried, canned, oil-packed, water-packed, and fermented fish/seafood products ([current checklist](../../RecipeBook-global-ingredient-catalog-plan.txt#L6261)). The accepted [R181 octopus-in-olive-oil review](wp04-section-p-octopus-in-olive-oil-product-boundary-review-r181-20261016.md) explicitly retained Galician-sauce octopus as a separate product and did not review or allocate it. This review addresses that distinct product only; it does not reopen R181, the generic octopus identity, R229’s cooked-frozen-shrimp hold, or the excluded R221 fish-stick and R224 tempura decisions.

The manufacturer sells a named *Pulpo en Salsa Gallega* SKU (code `511110201`). Its own trilingual catalogue labels the same code `OCTOPUS IN GALICIAN SAUCE / POULPE EN SAUCE GALICIENNE`. Current Québec/Canadian retailer pages identify the Cambados product under corresponding English and French wording. That evidence is sufficient for a narrow finished-food market identity. It does not establish a cross-brand recipe, universal formula, common drained amount, or a general canned-octopus form.

## Product evidence and identity boundary

The official Conservas de Cambados product page names `Pulpo en Salsa Gallega OL-120 [GOURMET]`, reference `511110201`. For that SKU, the page lists octopus identified as *Octopus vulgaris*, sunflower oil, onion, paprika, natural flavour, and salt; its package format is OL-120, with 120 ml capacity and 111 g net weight. The maker describes manual preparation and addition of its seafood sauce. Its English/French product catalogue lists the same code with 111 g net and 67 g drained weight. These details identify one product; they do not define every octopus product in a Galician sauce. [S01, S07]

The Montréal retailer La Mer lists `Poulpe sauce Galicienne – 111 gr`; its English-language view calls the same Cambados item `Galician sauce octopus – 111g`. La Mer’s catalogue also lists its olive-oil octopus separately. Ottawa-based Solfarmers lists `Gourmet Octopus in Galician Sauce` and a French title `Poulpe Gourmand à la Sauce Galicienne`, each tied to Conservas de Cambados and a 111 g tin. These are Québec/Canadian retail wording observations for the same branded product, not independent formulas or evidence that stock is consistently available. [S03–S06]

The Cambados French storefront uses the SKU title `Poulpe à la galicienne` with the same product code. Its French copy appears to reuse olive-oil wording, so this review relies on that storefront only for the title and code; the Spanish manufacturer detail and the trilingual catalogue support product contents and the explicit sauce wording. [S01–S02, S07]

The maker and its Canadian retailers also list other seafood in Galician sauce, including mussels and small scallops. Those are separate ingredient identities and must not be folded into this octopus row. The proposed row is a narrow market identity, not a generic `Galician sauce` ingredient, a universal seafood preparation, or a claim about every product sold under that style. [S01, S07]

The manufacturer assigns *Octopus vulgaris* to this SKU. WoRMS lists *Octopus vulgaris* as an accepted species; the CFIA Fish List is the Canadian common-name/taxon reference and includes octopus names across multiple taxa. Keep this candidate species-neutral: neither the single Cambados SKU nor the taxonomic record assigns *O. vulgaris* to all octopus in Galician sauce. [S01, S08; reused `WP04-OCTOPUS-IDENTITY-20261012-S01`]

Codex CXS 192-1995 category 09.4 covers fully preserved fish products, including canned or fermented molluscs, crustaceans, and echinoderms. It is a food-additive category classification, not a product-identity standard or proof of this SKU’s thermal process, commercial sterility, or regulatory classification in Canada. Do not infer those properties from the can or the category reference. [S09]

## Québec French naming and alias scope

The manufacturer’s trilingual catalogue supplies the direct display pair **`octopus in Galician sauce / poulpe en sauce galicienne`**. The Montréal retailer’s exact product phrase `Poulpe sauce Galicienne` supports the narrow French alias `poulpe sauce galicienne`; its English page supplies `Galician sauce octopus`. The Solfarmers French collection lists the same separate SKU as `Poulpe à la sauce galicienne`, supporting that second French alias. Solfarmers’ longer product title adds the marketing descriptor `Gourmand`, which is not needed in the catalog alias. [S05–S07, S10]

OQLF’s food-mollusc entry lists `poulpe` and `pieuvre` as preferred French terms for English `octopus`; a separate 2025 OQLF entry says `pieuvre` is commonly used and well known in Québec for its species entry. The Québec product pages themselves use `poulpe`. Retain the observed product wording `poulpe` in this compound display without changing generic `octopus / pieuvre` or adding generic `poulpe` to this product. The compound phrase is supported by product listings; this review does not claim OQLF has approved it as a standardized product term. [reused `WP04-MOLLUSKS-20261009-S11`, `WP04-OCTOPUS-IDENTITY-20261012-S03`; S03, S05, S07]

Proposed aliases are limited to product-title variants that retain both octopus and the sauce qualifier:

- English: `Galician sauce octopus`.
- French: `poulpe sauce galicienne`; `poulpe à la sauce galicienne`.

Do not add `octopus`, `pieuvre`, or bare `poulpe`; they belong to the existing generic identity. Do not add `poulpe à la galicienne`: although that is the manufacturer storefront title for this SKU, it drops the explicit sauce boundary and can be read as a dish/preparation name. Do not add `canned octopus`, `tinned octopus`, `octopus in sauce`, `Galician sauce`, or `pulpo en salsa gallega` as aliases. The first three erase the product qualifier or overgeneralize the preservation state; bare `Galician sauce` would turn a finished octopus product into a sauce search term; Spanish is not an app display language and may refer to the Galician-style dish as well as the product title.

These are SKU/package statements only. Add no generic can measure, drained yield, count, household measure, or mass-volume mapping. [S01, S03–S04, S07]

- **Substitutions:** The recipe corpus contains no octopus request or dish context. Add no global or contextual substitution between this SKU and generic octopus, other octopus products, mussels, scallops, squid/calamari, cuttlefish, or another seafood ingredient.

## Final disposition and evidence limits

**Accepted and integrated.** Added the identity-only `octopus in Galician sauce / poulpe en sauce galicienne` product group with the three sauce-qualified aliases above. The official product code and trilingual catalogue establish a distinct finished product; La Mer and Solfarmers document bilingual Canadian/Québec-facing product wording. Broad `octopus` and `poulpe` queries remain multiple-choice, and the existing olive-oil product remains independently searchable.

Evidence is concentrated on one manufacturer SKU listed by two Canadian retailers. It does not establish prevalence across brands, one universal ingredient formula, a Canadian regulatory product identity, an OQLF-approved compound term, a general canned-octopus form, or recipe demand. Keep the recommendation limited to the names, aliases, and product boundary explicitly stated above. Do not infer related mussel/scallop rows from this octopus review.
