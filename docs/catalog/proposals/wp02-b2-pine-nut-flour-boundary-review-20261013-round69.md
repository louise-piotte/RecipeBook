# WP02 B2 — pine-nut flour product boundary review

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-13.
- **Status:** Proposal only. No shared catalog, ledger, plan, roadmap, checklist, code, recipe, test, or emulator state was changed.
- **Scope:** One previously unreviewed nut-flour boundary under the open WP02 B2 alternative-flour item: products marketed as pine-nut flour. This review does not reopen the active pine-nut or pine-nut-butter identities, other nut flours, or flour made from Brazilian pine seeds (*Araucaria angustifolia*).
- **Source-ID prefix:** `WP02-B2-PINE-NUT-FLOUR-20261013-R69`. This prefix was absent from the proposal and batch archive and the research ledger before drafting; IDs below are proposal-local, not ledger records.

## Recommendation

**Keep generic `pine nut flour` unallocated until the intended product process and a Québec-facing French flour name are established together.** The English phrase names products made from different materials: finely chopped whole kernels and flour milled from partially de-oiled press cake. A current Canada-facing seller’s Siberian listing says the raw nuts are “pressed and ground,” but does not specify oil removal or press-cake handling; an Italian maker’s current product is explicitly made from the partially de-oiled cake remaining after cold pressing. A primary study separately evaluates whole and defatted *Pinus pinea* meal and reports different functional properties. These sources support a real product family, but not one source-neutral row or a general measure or substitution. [S02–S05]

## Product and process boundary

The sources show why the unqualified English key is unsafe:

1. **Whole-kernel milling:** Frasada Food’s current Sicilian listing describes “pine nuts flour” made by finely chopping shelled pine nuts and lists untreated Sicilian pine nuts as its ingredients. Its product page could not be opened directly during this review; the indexed listing is retained only as a product lead, not as sufficient market-wide or Québec evidence. [S04]
2. **Partially de-oiled press cake:** Pariani identifies its `Partially de-oiled Italian Pine nut Flour P1` as finely ground cake remaining after cold-pressing pine nuts. It is a separate, explicitly named product and a 1 kg package. [S03]
3. **Unresolved Siberian process description:** Siberian Green Canada sells `Siberian Pine Nut Flour (Kernel Powder)` in a 454 g package. Its current page describes shelled, wild-harvested, ready-to-eat pine nuts from Altai, Siberia, says the flour is cold processed, and says raw pine nuts are “pressed and ground” into powder. It does not say that the product is defatted or partially defatted, identify press cake as the ground material, give a residual-oil specification, or publish a Latin species name. Do not infer any of those details from “pressed.” The seller lists a Pointe-Claire, Québec address. [S02]
4. **Study-defined distinction:** A primary study on Mexican pink pinion (*Pinus pinea*) analyzes whole pink-pinion meal and defatted pink-pinion meal separately; it reports different composition and functional properties. Those lab findings support a process boundary but do not establish a consumer flour standard. [S05]
5. **Different botanical source:** A primary Brazilian study makes `Brazilian pine seed flour` from dried and milled *Araucaria angustifolia* seeds and tests it in rice-flour cake blends. It is not a *Pinus* product and must not become an alias or scope expansion for pine-nut flour. [S06]

The product family also spans distinct pine species. OQLF’s entry is for `pignon` and lists *Pinus pinea*, while the Canadian Siberian product is source-named Siberian pine and the Pariani listing says Italian pine without publishing a Latin species name. Do not silently treat these product descriptions as proof that every pine-seed flour has the same botanical scope, fat content, grind, or baking behavior. [S01–S05]

## Québec French naming

- OQLF’s GDT entry lists `pignon`, `pigne`, and `noix de pin` as preferred Québec French terms for the seed and gives English `pine seed`, `pine nut`, and `pignoli`. It does not define a flour term. The entry was updated in 1991 and identifies *Pinus pinea*; it is authoritative for the base name, not for a flour product or every commercial pine species. [S01]
- `Farine de pignons de pin` is a transparent descriptive construction, not an OQLF-approved flour term. A current French-Canadian Faire discovery page includes that phrase, but it does not identify a particular package or processing method. [S07]
- A 2016 French wholesale catalogue names `farine de pignon de pin italien (dégraissée)` for a specific de-oiled product. This helps demonstrate French product wording and process qualification, but is France-market terminology and does not establish a Québec display. [S08]
- No exact Québec flour headword, Québec retail package label, or bilingual label pairing `pine nut flour` with a process-matched French flour name was located in the targeted searches. Therefore, do not activate `farine de pignons de pin`, `farine de pignon de pin`, `farine de noix de pin`, `poudre de pignons`, or `farine de graines de pin` as displays or aliases here.

## Recipe demand, measurements, and substitutions

The scan covered all **114 manifest-listed recipe JSON files and 1,123 ingredient rows**, searching the complete recipe JSON and ingredient rows for `pine nut flour`, `pine nuts flour`, `pine kernel flour`, `pine kernel powder`, `pine nut meal`, `pine nut powder`, `pine seed flour`, `pignoli flour`, `cedar nut flour`, `farine de pignons`, `farine de noix de pin`, `Pinus pinea flour`, `Pinus sibirica flour`, and `Brazilian pine seed flour`. There were **zero flour-candidate recipe-text matches and zero ingredient-row matches**.

The corpus has one adjacent whole-nut recipe line: recipe `102` `saucissons-de-chocolat-chocolate-sausage`, ingredient row 8, says `2 tbsp pine nuts` and links to `ingredient-ref-pine-nuts`. That line is demand for whole pine nuts, not a flour request; do not relink it, transfer its tablespoon amount, or infer a flour substitution.

No reusable numeric behavior is supported:

- Product package sizes (454 g and 1 kg) are package weights, not household measures or cup conversions. Pariani’s product nutrition panel and the lab studies are product/sample-specific and do not establish a default density, mass per cup, unit mapping, or recipe yield. [S02–S05]
- The *Pinus pinea* study reports whole-versus-defatted meal composition and functional-property differences, not a household conversion or universal baking behavior. Do not store its research values as active ingredient density or measurement. [S05]
- The Brazilian study tests *Araucaria angustifolia* seed flour in particular rice-flour cake blends at 12.5%, 25%, 37.5%, and 50% of the flour blend. Those formula-specific test levels are not substitution advice for *Pinus* pine-nut flour or a general wheat-flour replacement. [S06]
- Neither the product pages nor the cited studies provide a source-neutral substitution ratio between pine-nut flour and wheat, almond, or another flour. Do not create global or contextual substitution rules. The active global and contextual substitution arrays are empty; the package-yield file has no pine-nut entry.

## Integration sketch if reopened

1. Keep both active references (`ingredient-ref-pine-nuts` and `ingredient-ref-pine-nut-butter`), their names, and their categories unchanged.
2. Before proposing a new row, choose a product scope with a current product specification: whole-kernel milled pine seed versus a named partially/fully de-oiled press-cake product, and identify the botanical source where the source publishes it.
3. Obtain a Québec-facing French product name for that same scope. Treat `farine de pignons de pin` only as a descriptive candidate until a product label or authoritative Québec terminology supports it.
4. If a future decision accepts one or more source-defined products, create only the scoped identity/identities and directly supported aliases. Keep broad `pine nut flour`, `powder`, `meal`, and cross-species/pressing aliases unassigned unless independently established.
5. Add no form, density, unit mapping, mass/volume conversion, recipe link, yield, or substitution based on the present review.
