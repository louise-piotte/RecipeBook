# WP04 proposal — human-food mealworm identity boundary

**Review date:** 2026-10-11
**Scope:** Canadian human-food products made from yellow mealworm larvae (*Tenebrio molitor*): dried whole larvae and ground/powder/flour forms. This is a proposal-only review; no catalog, ledger, plan, roadmap, recipe, test, or code record is changed.

## Disposition

**Hold a general active mealworm reference pending a product-level species, form, and status match.** The sources support a distinct human-food mealworm product family in Canada and a Health Canada non-novel determination for a particular protein-rich flour from *T. molitor* larvae. They do not yet establish that a current Canadian human-food powder matches the flour description, whether that powder is whole-ground or extracted, or the status of whole dried larvae as a separate product.

This is a catalog-evidence hold, not a conclusion that other mealworm products are novel, prohibited, or unsafe. Health Canada says its public list contains most determinations since 2012 and automatically includes determinations requested after September 30, 2020; absence of a particular form from the list does not by itself establish its legal status. Listed determinations are specific to the described product/use, did not undergo a food-use safety assessment, and remain subject to other applicable food requirements.

The next useful evidence is a current human-food specification or package that names *T. molitor*, says whether the powder is ground whole larvae or an extracted protein, and can be matched to the relevant Canadian novelty scope. The available Québec French wording is useful naming evidence but is not a package-label image or species declaration for the powder.

The French list says “une farine riche en protéines à base de larves de ténébrions meuniers (*Tenebrio molitor*).” | This is a determination for the described flour, not blanket approval of every mealworm, dried larva, powder, flour, species, process, extract, supplement, or food use. The entry does not provide a current retail package, processing specification, quantitative composition, or common-name approval. Health Canada says listed items were not safety-assessed for food use and must meet other applicable rules. |
| Other mealworm-containing status | A separate 2016-07-18 entry covers C-fu™, a tofu-like, high-nutrient food made by extracting and restructuring proteins from yellow mealworm larvae, adult house crickets, and superworm larvae. | This is a named, formulated, mixed-species food. It does not establish generic status for mealworm protein extracts or concentrates. Health Canada’s list notes that extraction is not minimal processing; that note does not itself decide the status of every other extract. |

## Candidate identity and boundaries

If the missing product match is supplied, review a narrowly named human-food ingredient candidate, tentatively classified `PROTEIN`:

| Field | Candidate wording only |
|---|---|
| English display | `yellow mealworm flour` or `yellow mealworm powder`, selected to match the product specification |
| Québec French display | `farine de larves de ténébrion meunier` or `poudre de larves de ténébrion meunier`, selected to match the product and its form |
| Species boundary | *Tenebrio molitor* larvae; retain the Latin name in identity evidence, not as an unqualified ingredient alias |
| Aliases | None until a current food-grade product label/specification confirms the species and product state |

The candidate French constructions are editorial and species-qualified; they are not verbatim product labels or a CFIA-approved ingredient name. OQLF’s 2025 GDT entry prefers **ténébrion meunier** and says **ver de farine** designates that species in its larval form. TriCycle’s current French product page uses **Poudre de ténébrions** and **Ténébrions séchés nature**. CFIA’s French licence guide uses **larves de ténébrion meunier séchées** as an example. These sources support Québec French vocabulary, but none establishes a species-specific prescribed common name for every product.

Keep the following boundaries explicit:

- **Whole dried larvae versus powder/flour:** the Québec maker lists them separately. Do not merge whole dried larvae into a powder/flour reference or add a dried-to-ground form conversion. The 2023 Health Canada determination is for protein-rich flour, not whole dried larvae.
- **Whole-ground powder versus extracted protein:** do not treat powder/flour as an extract, isolate, concentrate, or textured/restructured protein. The separate C-fu determination describes extraction and restructuring in a named mixed-species food. The available current TriCycle page does not document its powder process, so that specific product remains unresolved.
- **Human food versus feed/pet products:** TriCycle separates its human-food dried/powder products from animal nutrition and live larvae for pets/birds. Keep intended use and food-grade product evidence tied to the product, not only to insect species.
- **Supplements and generic protein powders:** a protein-oriented product name or nutrition marketing does not make the item a generic protein powder or a natural health product. CFIA’s licence guide separately categorizes insect foods, multiple foods such as protein drinks/nutritional supplements, and natural health products. Do not alias a mealworm ingredient to `protein powder`, sports supplements, whey/soy isolates, or a supplement product absent product-specific classification evidence.
- **Other insect species:** keep mealworm separate from the active *Acheta domesticus* house-cricket powder and *Gryllodes sigillatus* banded-cricket powder. Do not add `insect powder`, `cricket powder`, or other species names as aliases. Health Canada’s separate C-fu entry also names *Zophobas morio* larvae; that does not make “mealworm” a synonym for superworm or other insect powders.
