# WP05 Section V — stock, broth, and concentrate boundary review

- **Packet type:** Proposal-only identity and recipe-demand review.
- **Review date:** 2026-10-12.
- **Plan area:** Section V, stocks, broth, consommé, demi-glace, dashi, stock concentrates, cubes, and powders.
- **Disposition:** Recommend a bounded vegetable stock/broth split, promotion of the legacy chicken-stock identity with corrected French wording, an identity-only fish-stock candidate, three flavor-qualified bouillon-cube candidates, and a branded Better Than Bouillon base family for the one exact branded recipe line. Keep product-specific concentrations and remaining dry formats held. Existing beef stock/broth, generic broth, chicken bouillon powder, Hondashi, and shiro dashi decisions remain intact.
- **Boundary:** This packet proposes identity/link decisions only. It does not propose ingredient forms, density, unit mappings, measurements, reconstitution conversions, yields, substitutions, or recipe quantity/note changes.

## Decision summary

1. Preserve the integrated distinction between `beef stock / fond de bœuf` and `beef broth / bouillon de bœuf`; all three beef-broth recipe links stay as they are.
2. Resolve the current exact French-key collision between the active chicken-broth reference and the legacy chicken-stock candidate by promoting the latter as `chicken stock / fond de poulet`. Keep the exact `low-salt chicken broth` recipe row on the active chicken-broth reference; the recipe qualifier does not make broth a stock.
3. Split the existing vegetable entries: change the current stock’s French display from `bouillon de légumes` to `fond de légumes`, remove its unsupported `vegetable broth` alias, add `vegetable broth / bouillon de légumes`, and relink only recipe `105#2` to that broth identity.
4. Add an identity-only `fish stock / fumet de poisson` candidate from the OQLF bilingual term record. No recipe line requests it. Do not add `fish broth / bouillon de poisson` from this evidence.
5. Add three identity-only, flavor-qualified bouillon-cube candidates—chicken, beef, and vegetable—using current Canadian Knorr English/French product families. No recipe lines request cubes; no cube-to-liquid conversion or cube-to-powder alias is supported.
6. Separate recipe `099#3` from the generic broth-concentrate reference. Remove `Better Than Bouillon` from the generic reference’s aliases and add a bounded brand-family identity `Better Than Bouillon base / base Better Than Bouillon`, then link only `099#3` to it. The source recipe itself says any Better Than Bouillon flavor may be used; this is family-level product wording, not a universal base formula or stock/broth synonym.
7. Keep `broth concentrate / concentré de bouillon` provisional for recipe `044#5` pending stronger evidence for an unflavored generic identity and the preferred Québec French wording. Do not map it to branded paste bases or stock concentrates.
8. Hold consommé, demi-glace, generic dashi, generic fish broth, fish broth bases, generic cubes, granules, stock powders, instant stock mixes, and unqualified stock concentrates unless a later exact recipe/product review supports them. Preserve existing Hondashi and shiro-dashi identities as separate named products.

## Limitations and follow-up

- The OQLF *fond*, *bouillon*, and *demi-glace* entries are authoritative Québec terminology sources but were last updated in 1982; their definitions do not establish current commercial recipes.
- Product sites establish current names and product-specific descriptions, not the composition of every product sold under a broader class. Ingredient lists and reconstitution directions can change. The Campbell stock-concentrate and consommé examples are foodservice SKUs, not evidence that a home recipe’s unqualified phrase means those products.
- A named cube family is source-supported, but the recipe corpus has no cube demand. In particular, the current chicken cube English/French pages disagree about reconstitution quantity; no numeric data should be derived from either page.
- `low-salt` in recipe `037#9` is not evidence for a normalized low-sodium category or a numerical sodium limit. The active display remains generic; further naming cleanup of its draft ID is outside this packet.
