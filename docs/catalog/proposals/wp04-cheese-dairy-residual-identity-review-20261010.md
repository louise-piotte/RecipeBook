# WP04 proposal: Residual cheese identity and product-boundary review

- **Research date:** 2026-10-10
- **Status:** coordinator proposal; no shared catalog, ledger, recipe, plan, roadmap, code, or test changes were made.
- **Scope:** the remaining cheese block in WP04 Section Q: cream cheese, ricotta, cottage cheese, mascarpone, quark, farmer cheese, paneer, queso fresco, feta, halloumi, cheese curds, cheddar, mozzarella styles, Parmesan/Parmigiano-Reggiano, pecorino, Gruyère, Emmental, Gouda, blue and soft-ripened cheeses, and regional cooking cheeses.
- **Boundary:** this packet reviews identities and product boundaries. It proposes no cheese forms, density defaults, unit mappings, edible-portion rules, or substitution rules.

## Recommendation

Complete this checklist with a small set of source-named identities and explicit hold decisions. Keep the active identities for cheddar, cream cheese, Gruyère, mascarpone, mozzarella, Parmesan, cheese curds, and queso fresco serrano. Correct a few bilingual display names where OQLF gives a clearer Québec form. Do not create a second identity for an already active cheese or silently turn a broad cheese name into a style-specific product.

Accept source-named identities for ricotta, cottage cheese, quark, paneer, generic Mexican queso fresco, feta, halloumi, Gouda, Emmental, pecorino, blue cheese, Brie, Camembert, and the separately named Mexican cooking cheeses queso Oaxaca, Cotija, and queso panela. None has an exact linked ingredient line in the current bundle. These are coverage decisions grounded in distinct product definitions; they receive no recipe links, amounts, densities, or substitutions in this packet.

Keep **farmer cheese** unresolved until a recipe or package gives the intended product. Canadian terminology records `farmer cheese (Colby type)` as `fromage farmer (type Colby)`. No exact recipe or bilingual source in this review identifies unqualified farmer cheese as that Colby-type product. `Fromage fermier` means a farmstead production method in Québec, not this cheese variety. Do not merge farmer cheese with cottage cheese, quark, or farmstead cheese.

Keep fresh Italian mozzarella and North American lower-moisture mozzarella distinct when a recipe names the style. The sole linked recipe says only `shredded mozzarella`; that does not establish a product specification. Keep the existing generic mozzarella identity for this line and do not invent a style-specific link.

Keep generic Parmesan separate from protected `Parmigiano Reggiano`. Keep generic Mexican `queso fresco` separate from the already active Peruvian `queso fresco serrano`, Québec cheese curds, and OQLF's `fromage frais`. Treat Brie and Camembert as distinct named cheeses inside the soft-ripened family; do not add a generic `soft-ripened cheese` ingredient. Treat `blue cheese` as a broad named category, not an alias for Roquefort, Gorgonzola, Stilton, or another named blue cheese.

Within the residual items reviewed, the current linked recipe corpus supports only already active identities. Breakfast Casserole is the one page that mentions specific alternatives, including feta, but its current bundled ingredient line is generic and disagrees with the live source's quantity and name. The adjacent active Manchego row also no longer matches its linked Turkey Meatballs page, which calls for Parmesan. Record both discrepancies for recipe reconciliation; do not add cheese links or substitution rules from them.

## Composition, aging, moisture, salt, and function

Canadian CFIA percentages below are regulatory limits for named Canadian standards, not measured values for every imported or artisanal cheese. They demonstrate product boundaries; do not copy them into recipe quantities, density fields, or universal nutrition claims. The CFIA variety table sets these maximum moisture / minimum milk-fat values for common names: cheddar 39% / 31%; blue 47% / 27%; Brie 54% / 23%; Camembert 56% / 22%; Emmentaler (Emmental, Swiss) 40% / 27%; feta 55% / 22%; farmer's 44% / 27%; Gouda 43% / 28%; Gruyère 38% / 28%; mozzarella (Scamorza) 52% / 20%; part-skim mozzarella 52% / 15%; Parmesan 32% / 22%. [S01]

| Boundary | Source-grounded distinction | Modeling result |
| --- | --- | --- |

OQLF notes cream cheese is sold in containers or bricks. [S01, S02] | Keep the current cream-cheese ID. The word `brick` is not an additional identity, and a spread is not an alias for plain cream cheese. No current recipe asks for spread. |

OQLF gives `cottage à la crème`; Canadian interpretation guidance labels it `fromage cottage à la crème`. Québec cheese curds are unaged chewy pieces, generally cheddar-style. ## Measures, edible portion, forms, and substitutions

- **Density and unit mappings:** unavailable for all reviewed cheese identities. No reviewed source supplies a stable, product-wide density, and the existing cheese references have `null` density and empty unit mappings.
- **Recipe-specific measure evidence:** Health Canada's Canadian Nutrient File gives cheddar code 119 as 47.8 g per 100 mL shredded, 119.4 g per 250 mL shredded, 55.8 g per 100 mL diced, 139.5 g per 250 mL diced, and 257.8 g per 250 mL melted, with 0% refuse. This supports that measures vary by physical preparation, not a generic cheese density. `016#3` asks for 1 cup shredded cheddar. The app's `cup` is 236.5882 mL; a straight-line volume estimate from the CNF shredded entry is about 113 g, but this is an inference and should not be installed as a default mapping because packed shredded-cheese volume varies. [S31]
- **Other checked CNF records:** code 111 is part-skim mozzarella (52% water, 16.5% milk fat) and lists 119.4 g per 250 mL shredded with 0% refuse; the bundled mozzarella line does not specify part-skim. Code 93 is shredded Parmesan and lists 105.7 g per 250 mL and 5.1 g per 15 mL with 0% refuse; the recipe lines say generic Parmesan or freshly grated Parmesan. Code 5583 is 1% unsalted cottage cheese and lists 238.8 g per 250 mL with 0% refuse, but no cottage recipe exists and the candidate doesn't specify this product. None is an exact-enough match for a new mapping here. [S32, S33, S34]
- **Edible portion:** those specific CNF products report 0% refuse. No current recipe asks to discard rind, brine, whey, or another part, so no cheese-wide edible-yield value is warranted. The CNF result must not be generalized to rinded cheeses.
- **Forms:** there are no active cheese forms. This identity packet adds none. `shredded`, `grated`, `fresh`, `softened`, and similar wording remains attached to the exact recipe line unless separately reviewed as a form; it does not define a universal cheese identity or conversion.
- **Substitutions:** no direction is justified globally. The current source of `016#3` says the author used cheddar and that any cheese may be used; it names no exact alternatives, so no rule follows. The Breakfast Casserole source offers regular/sharp white cheddar, pepper jack, feta, or another preferred cheese for that casserole only, but its current page conflicts with bundled `027#5` text/amount. Record as source-context evidence, not a global or recipe-linked substitution. [S35, S36]
- **All other replacements:** unavailable. No source-backed amount ratio or direction justifies substitutions among cream cheese, mascarpone, ricotta, cottage cheese, quark, paneer, queso fresco, feta, halloumi, mozzarella styles, hard cheeses, blue cheeses, or regional cooking cheeses.
