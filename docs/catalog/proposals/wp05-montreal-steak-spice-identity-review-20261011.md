- **Packet type:** Bilingual blend-identity, naming-boundary, and recipe-demand review.

- **Decision:** Add `Montreal steak spice / épices à steak de Montréal` as one identity-only blend, with aliases `Montreal steak seasoning` and `épices à steak Montréal`.
- **New source IDs:** `WP05-MONTREAL-STEAK-SPICE-20261011-S01` through `S05`; each was checked against the live ledger before insertion.

## Identity and boundaries

Current Québec retailer listings pair Firebarns `Montreal Steak Spice` and `Épices à steak Montréal` under the same product number 628176089362 [S01–S02]. A separate Canadian Club House listing uses `Montreal Steak Spice Seasoning`, supporting the English variant `Montreal steak seasoning` [S03]. A Metro recipe uses the locally natural phrase `épices à steak de Montréal` [S04].

The French display uses that full phrase; `épices à steak Montréal` is retained as a product-page alias. Keep brand names out of aliases. These are multi-ingredient blends: ingredients and proportions differ across products and recipe variants. Add no universal blend formula, MSG status, density, unit mapping, conversion, yield, package data, or substitution.

OQLF gives `épices pour bifteck` as the French term for English `steak spice` but notes that adding ingredients such as salt, flour, or vegetables shifts a spice mixture into the seasoning category [S05]. Do not use `épices pour bifteck` as an alias for this salt-containing, oil-containing Montreal seasoning blend. Keep it separate from seasoning salt, plain salt, generic steak rubs, barbecue sauces, marinades, and other steak-seasoning products.
