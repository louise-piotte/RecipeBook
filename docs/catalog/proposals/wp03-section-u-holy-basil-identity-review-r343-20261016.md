# WP03 Section U — holy basil / basilic sacré identity review (R343, 2026-10-16)

- **Status:** Accepted and integrated on 2026-10-16. See the [R343 integration README](../batches/wp03-section-u-holy-basil-identity-integration-r343-20261016/README.md).
- **Plan anchor:** Section U, “Fresh herbs botanical aromatics and regional flavorings,” whose open regional-herb checklist names holy basil; see also the hold in the [WP06 Southeast Asia gap-sweep proposal](wp06-southeast-asia-cuisine-gap-sweep-20261011.md).
- **Candidate:** Culinary holy basil, source-identified as *Ocimum tenuiflorum* L. and Thai `Kra Prao`.
- **Source IDs:** `WP03-SECTIONU-HOLY-BASIL-R343-20261016-S01` through `S08`, registered on the shared research-ledger record.
- **Disposition:** Advance one identity-only `HERB` reference, `holy basil / basilic sacré`, with only the bounded aliases listed below. New Québec culinary-product evidence resolves the earlier French-product-wording hold. The French display is supported by local product usage, not an OQLF ruling.

## Recommendation

Accept one culinary identity for *Ocimum tenuiflorum* L. The Thai Department of Agricultural Extension identifies a fresh food crop titled **Holy Basil (Sacred Basil)**, gives the Thai name `Kra Prao`, names *O. tenuiflorum*, and lists fresh, cooked, and processing uses. Kew accepts *O. tenuiflorum* and records food use. Current Québec-facing product pages use **basilic sacré** and pair it with **Tulsi**; one Québec microgreen seller describes fresh culinary uses. [S01, S03, S05–S07]

## Identity, naming, and overlap boundaries

| Term or product | Disposition | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Holy basil / sacred basil | Proposed English display and alias | The Thai agriculture authority uses `Holy Basil (Sacred Basil)` for *O. tenuiflorum*. Health Canada’s 2026 natural-health-product monograph lists `Holy basil` and `Sacred basil` as common names for *O. tenuiflorum* leaf. The monograph is not food-ingredient evidence; it is used only for the name-to-taxon claim. [S01, S04] |
| `basilic sacré` | Proposed French display | Québec-facing Tulsi Farm and Pépinière.ca product pages use this wording; Tulsi Farm describes it as a fresh culinary microgreen/herb, and La Société des Plantes uses it on a Québec seed listing with culinary context. These are local product attestations, not an OQLF preferred-term decision. [S05–S07] |
| `tulsi` | Proposed English and French search alias | Health Canada lists `Tulsi`; two French-language Québec product pages directly pair `Tulsi` with `basilic sacré`. Retain the unqualified source spelling only. Do not add `Rama tulsi`: the monograph lists it, but the Québec food-product sources do not identify that narrower name. [S04–S07] |
| `Kra Prao` | Proposed English search alias, stored as `kra prao` | The Thai Department of Agricultural Extension gives exactly `Kra Prao` as its Thai name for the *O. tenuiflorum* crop. Do not expand to other romanizations (`kra phrao`, `kaphrao`, `kraprao`) without direct evidence for those spellings. [S01] |
| Thai basil / `basilic thaï` | Keep on existing `ingredient-ref-thai-basil`; do not alias to this row | The live Thai-basil row is source-named and has no assigned species. The Thai agriculture authority separately describes Holy Basil / `Kra Prao` as *O. tenuiflorum* and Sweet Basil / `Ho Ra Phaa` as *O. basilicum*. Pépinière.ca lists `basilic thaï` separately as a companion plant to its *O. tenuiflorum* basilic sacré. These sources support keeping the market names separate; they do not prove that every product labelled Thai basil has one species. [S01–S03, S07] |
| `Ocimum sanctum` | Taxonomic synonym in evidence only; not a picker alias | Kew treats *O. sanctum* as a synonym of accepted *O. tenuiflorum*. A Québec seed listing uses the older name. Keep botanical nomenclature in evidence, not recipe picker aliases. [S03, S06] |
| `Rama tulsi`, other morphotypes, holy-basil extracts, medicinal drops, seed products | Keep outside this ingredient row’s alias/form scope | Health Canada’s cited monograph is for dry leaf in a natural-health-product context and expressly excludes food-like dosage forms. Québec horticultural and seed listings establish local names but are not packaged edible-leaf specifications. Do not infer cultivar, dose, safety, food form, or equivalence to extracts. [S04, S06–S07] |
| Thai fresh-vegetable commodity grouping | Do not use as an equivalence rule | Thailand Ministry of Public Health Notification 386 lists “Basil, Holy Basil, Sweet Basil” together with several *Ocimum* species, including both *O. basilicum* and *O. tenuiflorum*. It is a broad commodity-classification table, not evidence that these distinct crop names are synonyms. The crop-specific agriculture entries and Kew’s taxonomy retain the boundary. [S01–S03, S08] |

## Measurements, forms, and substitutions

No form or numeric behavior is proposed. The DOAE page’s crop information, the Québec grower’s microgreen price/serving guide, and the seed seller’s seed counts are source-specific and are not household measures for basil leaves. Fresh, dried, seed, and medicinal-extract products remain separate presentations until a product-level need and useful behavior are established. No bundled recipe line or source establishes a general substitute. Leave forms, density, unit mappings, conversions, yields, recipe links, and substitutions empty.

## Remaining limits

This proposal advances one Section U identity; it does not complete regional basil coverage. It does not assign the existing Thai-basil row a species, resolve every Thai transliteration, adopt medicinal nomenclature as food safety guidance, or add forms, measures, recipe links, conversions, yields, or substitutions. An independent Québec review may still assess the borrowed `basilic sacré` display; the current recommendation is grounded in Québec product usage rather than an official terminology ruling.
