## Recommendation

Retain the existing pair lotus root starch / amidon de racine de lotus as an identity display, while leaving the claim of a Québec-preferred term open. A Canada-wide foodservice distributor's French product page directly uses amidon de racine de lotus in an ingredient list for a commercial warabi-ko blend, and its site says it ships across Canada. This is direct Canadian French product-language evidence available to Québec customers, but the ingredient is inside a starch blend, the seller is based in Ontario, and the source is not a Québec-origin label or a standalone lotus-starch package. [S03–S04]

OQLF directly supports racine de lotus as the Québec French name for lotus root, and separately explains amidon as the general term for starch while fécule is used more specifically for starch in tubers or underground organs. OQLF does not list the complete compound amidon de racine de lotus or pair it with the English starch term. The exact retailer wording supports retaining the current French display; it does not turn that compound into an OQLF-approved headword. [S01–S02]

## Product and identity boundary

The previous B3 review accepted extracted lotus-rhizome starch as distinct from the edible rhizome and any whole-root flour or powder. The primary study isolates native starch from rhizomes of Nelumbo nucifera cultivars; this supports the product boundary and a botanical example, not a universal retail definition or a new catalog form. [S06]

Keep the existing whole-vegetable reference ingredient-ref-lotus-root separate from the starch reference. Keep ingredient-ref-lotus-seeds separate as well. Do not make the ingredient declaration in a warabi-ko blend evidence that all lotus powders, lotus seeds, lotus roots, or other lotus preparations are starch. The seller listing does not name the botanical species of its lotus-starch component; the prior primary study remains the source for the narrower research boundary.

## Québec French terminology: direct attestations and inference

- Direct Québec terminology source: OQLF's GDT lists racine de lotus for the edible vegetable and pairs it with English lotus root. It does not discuss isolated starch. [S01]
- Direct Canadian French product wording: Fun Foods Canada's French warabi-ko listing names amidon de racine de lotus in the product's ingredients. Fun Foods states that it serves customers across Canada and ships from Ontario; this establishes Canadian French wording that is available to Québec customers, not a Québec-origin label. [S03–S04]
- Independent English product wording: Gusta Supplies' listing for warabi-ko names lotus root starch in the same type of mixed product ingredient list. This independently corroborates the English phrase but does not add a French term or establish pure-starch equivalence. [S05]
- Canadian administrative wording: a French Canadian Trademarks Journal goods declaration also contains amidon de racine de lotus. A goods declaration is not an OQLF terminology ruling or evidence that a particular product is sold in Québec. [S07]
- Editorial inference: combining OQLF's preferred root term with the product's extracted-starch class yields a transparent phrase. Since a Canadian French ingredient declaration independently attests the exact compound, retain the current display while keeping the stronger claim of Québec lexical preference unresolved. [S01–S03]

Do not add amidon de lotus or fécule de lotus as broad synonyms. Do not map colloquial Japanese arrowroot or other regional starch labels to this row. The current evidence does not establish aliases, product grades, species breadth beyond the prior research scope, or a shared naming convention for flour, powder, and starch.
