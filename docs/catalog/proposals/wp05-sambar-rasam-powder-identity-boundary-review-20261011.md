## Identity and boundary findings

### Separate sambar and rasam powder identities

The university course text lists `Sambar Powder 01`, `Sambhar Masala 02`, and `Rasam Powder` as separate named South Asian spice blends. Its `Sambar Powder 01` section describes a blend used to flavor sambhar and ground to a fine powder. Its rasam section describes a powder blend used to season rasam, which it calls a soup or accompaniment [S01]. The course gives individual recipes, but those are examples rather than a shared standard; product ingredients and regional styles must remain product-specific.

Canadian retail evidence also presents the dry products as separate names: Swaadisht lists `Sambhar Powder` and `Rasam Powder` as different products, and The State Basket separately lists Eastern Sambar Powder and Eastern Rasam Powder [S05, S08]. The official Canadian MTR trademark record likewise enumerates sambar powder and rasam powder as separate named wares [S02]. These sources support separate catalog identities even though this recipe bundle currently has no direct demand.

### Québec-facing French wording and alias limits

`Poudre de sambar` is the exact French title on a Walmart Canada listing categorized under powders; that page describes its specific SKU as a spice mixture for sambar [S06]. French Canadian retail pages use `Poudre de Rasam` and the longer descriptive phrase `mélange d’épices pour rasam` for a specific dry seasoning product [S07]. A 2025 French-language Canadian trademark publication uses `poudre de sambar` and `poudre de rasam` and separately enumerates the corresponding pastes [S03].

Use `poudre de sambar` and `poudre de rasam` as the French displays. Keep French search aliases empty: the exact product names are already the displays, while broader descriptive phrases are not needed as catalog keys. Retail wording supports Québec-facing use but does not establish an official terminology preference. Do not translate `sambar` or `rasam` into generic words for stew or soup, since that would blur the named South Indian products with other dishes.

The English alias `sambhar powder` is limited to the full dry-product phrase. The reviewed sources do not support `sambaar powder`, `sambar masala powder`, or `sambhar masala powder` as general aliases, and the university text shows why unqualified `masala` does not settle whether a source means dry powder or paste [S01].
