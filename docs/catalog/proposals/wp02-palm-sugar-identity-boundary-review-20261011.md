# WP02 Section C — palm sugar identity-boundary review

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-11
- **Status:** Proposal only. This file proposes no shared catalog, ledger, plan, roadmap, recipe, test, or code changes.
- **Scope:** The generic Section C palm-sugar candidate, including species breadth, finished-product states, regional names, Québec French naming, recipe demand, measures, yields, and substitution evidence.

## Recommendation

Keep generic **palm sugar / sucre de palme** unresolved and out of the active ingredient catalog. The term is used for sweeteners from multiple palm species and, in one Canadian terminology entry, even includes cane juice. Those source materials do not establish one Canadian catalog identity with uniform botanical/process scope and a common finished state.

Keep source- and region-qualified products separate. Coconut sugar is now a distinct active identity with coconut-specific aliases; maple sugar, cane jaggery, panela, piloncillo, rapadura, Okinawa kokuto, and Chilean chancaca are also represented separately. The WP05 proposal for Kampong Speu palm-sugar syrup and the WP06 hold for gula apong/nipah remain source-specific work. None should be treated as aliases for a generic palm-sugar reference.

Do not add a broad palm-sugar entry, alias, form, density, unit mapping, recipe link, culinary yield, or substitution rule from this review. If a future recipe names an origin- or species-qualified product, review that product as its own candidate and preserve the source wording. The French term **sucre de palme** is documented in federal terminology, but it does not resolve the product boundary or justify a single catalog identity.

## Product boundaries and inference

**Raw source facts:** palm-sap sweeteners are reported from multiple species, including Cocos nucifera, Borassus flabellifer, Arenga pinnata, Nypa fruticans, Caryota urens, and date palms. Some standards and local sources define narrower named products. Finished products range from molded blocks and granules/powder to paste and syrup. The Indonesian agriculture report shows process yield and composition criteria varying by product state; the Kampong Speu specification also lists multiple named forms. No source reviewed defines the same composition, processing endpoint, or physical form for every product called palm sugar.

**Inference:** a common botanical-family umbrella does not make these market products interchangeable ingredient identities. Sap source, source-market name, processing, and water content may differ. The historical Indonesian SNI demonstrates that some jurisdictions have standardized a multi-palm product class, but its 1995 text is no longer current and does not establish Canadian usage or the scope of the current Indonesian standard. The broad English phrase palm sugar and French sucre de palme are useful discovery terms, but assigning them to coconut sugar, Kampong Speu sugar, gula apong, or cane jaggery would overstate the evidence.

### Boundaries to preserve

- **Coconut sugar:** keep coconut-specific OQLF terms and the active coconut-sugar identity. Its coconut palm sugar alias explains why the app’s phrase search currently returns coconut sugar for palm sugar; that result is one specific product, not a definition of the broader term.
- **Kampong Speu sugar:** keep the WP05 palm-sugar-syrup candidate scoped to the Borassus flabellifer GI product. Its syrup form is distinct from its granulated, paste, and block forms; do not remove the origin qualifier or merge it into coconut sugar.
- **Gula apong / gula nipah:** the Sarawak evidence identifies a Nypa fruticans sap product. Keep this separate from Kampong Speu and coconut products. The prior WP06 packet’s French terminology hold remains material.
- **Gula melaka:** do not use as an unqualified global alias. Malaysia’s food regulation defines it from coconut inflorescence, while NParks’s nipa species record also uses that name for a Nypa sap sugar. The usage conflict is source- and region-dependent.
- **Gula aren / gula kabung:** the sources identify Arenga pinnata and a source-specific sweetener. Do not use either as generic aliases for coconut sugar or other palm products.
- **Maple sugar:** keep separate as the active maple-source sweetener; neither botanical family nor product name overlaps.
- **Cane jaggery/gur, panela, piloncillo, rapadura, and chancaca:** retain their cane or jurisdiction-qualified boundaries. Do not add bare jaggery/gur or jagré to the palm candidate. The accepted cane-jaggery record explicitly has no generic jaggery/gur alias.
- **Date sugar and date-palm jaggery:** the active date sugar term belongs to powdered dried dates, while date-palm jaggery remains held for product scope and Québec naming. Palm sap or date-palm sugar wording does not justify changing those identities.

## Proposed disposition

1. Keep the open generic palm-sugar candidate unresolved; do not create a catalog identity or assign palm sugar / sucre de palme as aliases to a source-specific sweetener.
2. Keep coconut sugar, Kampong Speu palm sugar/syrup, nipa sugar/gula apong, Arenga/gula aren or gula kabung, and date-palm jaggery separate and source-scoped.
3. Do not add gula melaka, jaggery, gur, jagré, palm sugar, or sucre de palme to an existing narrow identity from this review.
4. Add no forms, measurements, density, unit mapping, conversion, culinary-yield rule, substitution rule, or recipe link.
5. Revisit generic palm sugar only if an exact recipe or verified Canadian product requires it, and resolve the botanical/product-state scope and fr-CA wording before integration.

The remaining uncertainty is not whether the phrase is used: Canadian terminology and retail sources confirm it. The uncertainty is which specific sap source and product state a bare recipe line would mean. With zero matching ingredient lines in the current corpus and no source establishing a shared Canadian scope across the named products, the generic candidate should remain held.
