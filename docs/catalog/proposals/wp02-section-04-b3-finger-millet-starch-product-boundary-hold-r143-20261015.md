# WP02 Section 04 B3 R143 — finger millet starch product-boundary hold

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-15.
- **Status:** Proposal-only hold. No shared catalog, research-ledger, checklist, roadmap, generated, recipe, or application file was changed.
- **Checklist:** [WP02 Section 04 B3](../../RecipeBook-global-ingredient-catalog-plan.txt#L2209), `[ ] Water-chestnut flour and other documented regional starches.` The parent remains open after R142.
- **Candidate:** starch isolated from finger millet, *Eleusine coracana* subsp. *coracana*.
- **Scope:** Isolated finger-millet starch, distinct from finger-millet grain, finger-millet flour/ragi atta, and finger-millet rava. This is not foxtail millet starch (*Setaria italica*, R142) or pearl millet starch (*Pennisetum glaucum*, R139).
- **Proposal-local source-ID prefix:** `WP02-B3-FINGER-MILLET-STARCH-R143-20261015-S`. The candidate name, a hypothetical reference ID, and this source prefix were checked against active references, forms, ledger, legacy identities, plan, recipes, and archive. No focused prior finger-millet-starch decision or allocated ID/source prefix was found.

## Recommendation

**Hold finger-millet starch outside the active catalog and research ledger.** Primary studies establish separately isolated *E. coracana* starch and measure research samples. Current Canadian-facing product pages reviewed identify finger-millet/ragi flour, not a packaged isolated starch product. The bounded search found no Canadian or Québec human-food listing for standalone finger-millet starch, and no reviewed Québec-facing source attests a product-specific French starch name. Flour and research samples do not establish the market identity of a household starch ingredient.

Health Canada’s French crop-group page uses **éleusine** for *Eleusine coracana* subsp. *coracana*; FAO’s French human-nutrition publication uses **éleusine cultivée** and identifies **ragi** among regional grain names. These are crop terms. The Canadian French-language flour listings reviewed use **farine de ragi** and **farine de millet des doigts** for flour only. OQLF’s general **amidon** entry supports that word for starch, but it does not attest a compound such as **amidon d’éleusine cultivée** or a finger-millet-starch product. That compound is only a possible descriptive construction, not a validated Québec product name. [S01–S03, S07–S08]

Do not add a reference, ledger candidate, aliases, forms, density, unit mappings, recipe links, conversions, yields, or substitutions. Do not merge the candidate with finger-millet grain, flour, rava, other millets, or a resistant/modified starch fraction.

## Canadian and Québec-facing product scope

The current Canadian-facing listings found are adjacent products rather than isolated starch:

- Spice Divine’s French-language Canadian listing is titled **Farine de ragi Laxmi (farine de millet des doigts) 2 lb**. Its description names ragi/finger-millet flour, and the listing was marked out of stock when reviewed. It does not identify an isolated starch product. [S07]
- Walmart Canada’s French listing **Farine de ragi Verka, 2 lb** gives **Farine de Millet (Ragi)** as the ingredient and describes flour made by grinding ragi grains. The page was marked out of stock when reviewed; it is product-language evidence for flour, not a starch label. [S08]
- Puremill Canada offers **Puremill Ragi Flour**, describes it as 100% ragi grain milled into flour, and lists in-store milling locations in Stoney Creek, Hamilton, and Burlington, Ontario. It does not list finger-millet starch. [S09]
- Targeted searches on 2026-10-15 for `finger millet starch`, `ragi starch`, `nachni starch`, `starch of finger millet`, `amidon d’éleusine cultivée`, and `amidon de millet des doigts` across Canadian/Québec retailer and product-directory results found research and flour listings but no standalone Canadian human-food starch package or supplier page. This bounded non-discovery is not proof that no such product exists. [S12]

The French crop terms **éleusine** and **éleusine cultivée**, and the retailer wording **millet des doigts**, should not be promoted into a starch label without a product-specific Québec source. `Amidon` is a supported general term, but a grammatical compound does not by itself establish a local product name. [S01–S03, S07–S09]

## Measurements, forms, and substitution limits

- The 2024 primary isolation study reports laboratory process parameters and sample-specific yield/purity (including a 1:6 flour-to-solvent ratio and roughly 61% yield under its optimized process). Those are extraction observations, not kitchen measures, bulk density, package servings, or cooking yield for a Canadian product. [S05]
- The Canadian-grown millet study reports starch physical and molecular properties across species; it does not provide a finger-millet-starch cup or spoon mass. The flour ledger’s density observations belong to flour samples and must not be copied to extracted starch. Add no `defaultDensity`, unit mapping, or measurement observation for this held candidate. [S04, S10]
- Laboratory isolation is not a catalog form conversion from grain or whole-grain flour. Add no forms, preparation yield, or form rule.
- A primary paper blended finger-millet starch and corn starch in a 1:1 ratio for biodegradable food-packaging film. That is a material formulation, not an edible recipe or culinary thickener replacement. Other flour-use evidence concerns flour, not isolated starch. No product-specific household recipe replacement was found; add no global or contextual substitution. [S06–S09]
- The bundled corpus has no ingredient line to link. Do not derive demand or measurements from finger-millet grain, flour, or rava.

## Exact proposed delta

**No JSON delta.** Keep finger-millet starch outside the active references and research ledger. Do not reserve `ingredient-ref-finger-millet-starch` or create a ledger candidate. Keep Section 04 B3 open.

Reconsider only after a current Canadian/Québec human-food source identifies a standalone finger-millet-starch product and a product-specific Québec French term is independently reviewed. Any future measurement, form, conversion, yield, recipe link, or substitution requires evidence specific to that product and culinary use.
