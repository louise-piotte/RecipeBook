# WP02 Section B — Amala / elubo flour identity and Québec naming review

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-11
- **Status:** Proposal only. This is the sole file created for this review.
- **Scope:** The open `amala / elubo` flour candidate under the WP02 regional starch checklist. This review distinguishes dry flour inputs from the cooked amala paste and from fufu/foufou products.
- **Inputs reviewed:** Current WP02 plan items; live ingredient references and forms; the yam-flour ledger record and legacy import candidates; relevant prior proposals; all current bundled recipe files.
- **Source IDs:** `WP02-AMALA-ELUBO-QC-20261011-S01` through `S07`; proposal-local IDs, not ledger entries.

## Recommendation

Keep the generic `amala flour` / `elubo` identity **held**. The evidence supports a source-qualified research concept—fermented yam flour called *elubo* in a particular Nigerian product tradition—but does not establish that generic `elubo` or `amala flour` names one botanical product. Current Canadian listings also vary in stated composition. Québec French listings support the existing `farine d’igname` display for yam flour, but they do not settle a separate name for a fermented yam-flour identity.

## Product and process boundary

The reviewed evidence supports these distinctions:

- **Amala is prepared food:** The 2014 study calls it a thick cooked paste and says its flour input may be from yam, cassava, or unripe plantain; the study itself examines sweet-potato flour and the paste made from it. Thus `amala flour` is not a reliable source-material identity. This is one study’s description of Nigerian usage, not a universal cultural definition. [S01]
- **Elubo is not a stable botanical alias:** The same study describes traditional elubo from yam and cassava and reports sweet-potato elubo; it also identifies unripe plantain flour as an amala input. This shows usage breadth, not that every flour has the same process or recipe behavior. [S01]
- **A yam-specific research product can be named precisely:** The 2026 study’s use of `fermented yam flour (elubo)` is specifically tied to two *Dioscorea* species. This makes a future source-qualified candidate intelligible; it does not establish `elubo` as an alias for every yam flour or prove that the local retail products reviewed here share that fermentation process. [S02]
- **Retail composition is inconsistent or incomplete:** MyChopChop Canada labels a listing “Yam Flour (Elubo)” and describes its source as yam and/or cassava. Africanada Foods describes its own “Elubo Yam Flour” as finely milled dried yam flour for amala, but its manufacturer page does not specify *Dioscorea* species or the fermentation process. These are product-page statements, not standards. [S05–S06]
- **Keep other products separate:** Do not fold cassava flour, unripe-plantain flour, sweet-potato flour, fufu/foufou blends, or pounded-yam products into the held generic term. The live catalog already has separate edible cassava, green-plantain, sweet-potato, and yam-flour identities; exact product composition still governs which applies.

No source reviewed supports a generic ingredient alias from `amala` to flour, or from `elubo` to a single root/tuber flour. No numeric behavior, recipe conversion, yield, household measure, density, unit mapping, or substitution is proposed.

## Québec French and status limits

OQLF’s GDT entry for `igname` maps English `yam` to *Dioscorea* and distinguishes it from sweet potato. It does not define yam flour, elubo, or amala. Marché Kaïa’s current listing uses `Farine d’igname Elubo` and advertises delivery in Grand Montréal. That is direct local product wording and supports retaining the existing French base display `farine d’igname`; it is not evidence of an OQLF decision on `Elubo` or on `farine d’igname fermentée`. [S03–S04]

Accordingly:

- **Accepted fr-CA display for the existing identity:** `farine d’igname`.
- **Local wording, not a general alias:** `Elubo` or `Elubo-isu` as part of a specific product title.
- **Provisional descriptive French for a future, source-qualified candidate:** `farine d’igname fermentée`; neither this exact phrase nor a dedicated OQLF term for the product was verified.
- **Unresolved:** a generic Québec French display for `amala flour` or unqualified `elubo`.

The distinction is evidence-level specific: OQLF is terminology evidence for the root name; Québec retailer pages attest current product wording; primary Nigerian food-science research describes process and food relationships. None alone converts local use into a formal OQLF ruling.

## Decision requested for later integration

Keep the plan item open and leave all catalog rows, aliases, forms, ledger records, recipe links, numeric data, and substitutions unchanged. If reopened, require a recipe/package source that identifies the botanical source and product state. A confirmed single-source yam flour can continue to use the existing `yam flour / farine d’igname` identity. A verified fermented *Dioscorea* flour may be considered as a separate source-qualified candidate only if the use case needs that process distinction and a Québec French display is reviewed. Keep unqualified `amala flour` and `elubo` held.
