## Recommendation

Add one identity-only `FRUIT` reference for **mulberry / mûre (*Morus*)**, limited to the edible fruit used as a food ingredient. OQLF’s food-domain entry identifies the French fruit term `mûre`, its English translation `mulberry`, and Latin `Morus`; it explicitly distinguishes the *Morus* fruit from the unrelated *Rubus* blackberry even though both can be called `mûre`. USDA and university-extension sources establish food use for white mulberry (*Morus alba*), and USDA also documents food use of red mulberry (*M. rubra*). These sources support a generic culinary mulberry identity without adding species, cultivar, or fruit-colour rows. [S01–S04]

The generic row represents fruit sold or used as mulberry. The reviewed food-use examples are specifically *M. alba* and *M. rubra*; do not infer edible use, safety, or handling for every *Morus* species from this review. Do not turn the source’s fruit-colour range into colour-specific identities. Leaves, shoots, bark, ornamental plant material, and silkworm-related uses remain outside the row.

### Measurement and density

- USDA SR28 Release 28 (2015), NDB 09190, describes **raw mulberries** and reports common measures of **1 cup = 140 g** and **10 fruit = 15.0 g**. Record these only as two source-published observations for that exact raw-food record. The report does not specify the cup's volume convention, packing method, or fruit size/variability; do not infer a metric volume, per-fruit average, physical density, or any measure for cooked, frozen, or dried mulberries. [S05]
- USDA's FPED 2009–10 guide separately assigns **145 g per cup equivalent** to raw/fresh berries including mulberries. It says these group weights were consolidated from common FNDDS 5 cup weights and rounded to the nearest 0 or 5 g. This is a standardized food-pattern assignment, not a second direct mulberry measurement. Keep it as a separate qualified observation; do not average it with the SR28 140 g raw-mulberry record or treat it as a conflicting kitchen measure. [S06]
- The two federal values have different bases: the SR28 value is on a named raw-mulberry food record, while FPED assigns a group-level fruit equivalent. Retain their provenance and scope independently. No default density, volume-to-mass mapping, cultivar/taxon-general conversion, cooking yield, edible-portion yield, or package yield is supported. Keep `defaultDensity` null and `unitMappings` empty. The English `cup` labels remain source-defined because neither locator establishes a metric cup volume.

- The [Martha Stewart page](https://www.marthastewart.com/ways-to-use-fresh-mulberries-11982713) quotes chef Traci Weintraub in the section on crisps, cobblers, and crumbles, calling fresh mulberries a “fantastic swap” for blueberries in the blueberry crumble her kitchen makes. Record one directional, source-bounded finding **blueberry → fresh mulberry in that blueberry-crumble context**. The source provides no replacement amount or mass/volume basis, so this is evidence-only: do not infer a 1:1 amount, reverse the direction, generalize to blackberries, jam, preserving, or all berry recipes, or create an active substitution rule or recipe-line link. [S07]
- A search-result page titled “Mulberry Substitute: Blackberries + 5 More, With Ratios” was reviewed but not relied on: it is an unattributed substitute roundup with unsupported numeric ratios and broad claims; its direct page open failed with a cache miss. No further replacement candidate or quantitative/context-general rule is supported by the reviewed material.

Its basis is the named-chef recommendation in S07; its replacement quantity is absent because the source gives no amount or equivalence basis. The finding does not support the reverse direction or an active rule.
