# WP03 jackfruit maturity, product-scope, and Québec terminology review — 2026-10-11

- **Status:** Coordinator integration completed. Sources and the unresolved maturity/product boundary are in the research ledger and plan. The existing jackfruit identity and aliases remain unchanged; no maturity form, processed-product identity, recipe link, measurement, or substitution was added. See `docs/catalog/batches/wp02-wp03-wp04-flour-jackfruit-lap-cheong-integration-20261011/README.md`.
- **Scope:** The existing `ingredient-ref-jackfruit` identity; source-qualified fruit maturity; edible seeds; canned/retorted and frozen fruit; flour and other processed products; current Québec/Canadian French usage; bundled recipe demand.
- **Source labels:** S01–S11 below are local citations for this proposal only; they are not research-ledger entries.

## Recommendation

Keep the active `ingredient-ref-jackfruit` reference as the single generic fruit identity for *Artocarpus heterophyllus*. Preserve its present displays `jackfruit` / `jaque` and spelling aliases `jakfruit` / `jacque`. Do not add a green/unripe identity, a maturity alias, or product forms now: the live bundled corpus has no jackfruit phrase hit in its 114 manifest-listed recipe files.

If recipe demand later appears, keep maturity on this same fruit identity and retain the source's actual stage wording. Do not globally merge `green`, `unripe`, `young`, `tender`, and `mature unripe`: government processing material lists these as different source descriptions, but does not define a shared, measurable maturity standard. Treat seeds as a separate edible-part ingredient scope. Treat canned, frozen, and dried products as exact, label-qualified forms only when the fruit part, maturity, and relevant medium/process are known. Treat fruit-bulb flour as a separate `FLOUR_AND_STARCH` product identity, distinct from both fresh jackfruit and seed flour.

## Proposed product boundaries

| Source wording or product | Identity/form boundary | Recommendation and limit |
|---|---|---|
| `jackfruit` with no stage | Same active *A. heterophyllus* fruit identity | Keep the current reference. Do not silently assign ripe or unripe state. Kew accepts *A. heterophyllus* and records food use. [S01] |
| `green`, `unripe`, `young`, `tender`, `slightly grown`, `mature unripe` | Same fruit identity; source-qualified maturity descriptions | No separate identity and no universal form/alias. The PMFME material lists tender, slightly grown, mature unripe, ripe sweet fruit, and seed separately, without a standardized threshold. If a recipe later requires one, preserve its exact stage phrase as a scoped form label; do not cross-match the labels. [S05–S06] |
| `ripe jackfruit` / ripe bulbs or arils | Same fruit identity; a possible ripe-state form | A clearly stated ripe stage is distinct from the unqualified fruit and from immature cooking fruit. Add only if recipe demand exists. Do not imply that every ripe-fruit product is fresh whole fruit: ripe bulbs may be sold fresh, frozen, dried, or packed in syrup. [S05–S06] |
| `green jackfruit` | Same fruit identity; not a reliable global synonym for one stage | Keep the exact source phrase. Green skin is used in maturity descriptions, but retailers also use “green” as part of a named product. The consulted sources do not establish that every green-labelled product equals tender, young, or mature-unripe fruit. This is a boundary inference from distinct source uses, not a formal maturity standard. [S05–S06, S07–S08] |
| Jackfruit seeds / seed kernels | Separate edible-part ingredient identity if needed | Seeds are edible after boiling or roasting and are treated separately from fruit bulbs by the sources. They are not a form or alias of the flesh identity. Keep boiled, roasted, and dried seed states source-qualified if future recipes need them. [S05–S06] |
| Canned/retorted tender or young jackfruit in brine | Exact preserved-product scope; possible form of jackfruit only when contents are explicit | Do not create generic `canned jackfruit`. A useful form label would need the source-stated stage and packing medium, plus drained/rinsed status when the recipe or label makes that relevant. A current Canadian retailer listing names “Young Green Jackfruit in Brine” and its ingredient list names young jackfruit, water, salt, and citric acid, but the same listing has a conflicting syrup claim in its description; use the ingredients/title as a product example, not as a canonical definition. [S06, S08] |
| Canned ripe bulbs in syrup | Separate preserved-product scope from canned tender fruit | Same source fruit, but different maturity, edible part, medium, and likely use. If recipe demand appears, model as a fully qualified form/product, not as the generic canned form and not as a conversion from young jackfruit in brine. [S06] |
| Frozen jackfruit | Preservation state, not maturity | A frozen form may belong to the fruit identity when the product is simply frozen fruit and its stage is known. Do not collapse frozen green/tender fruit and frozen yellow/ripe bulbs under an unqualified form. Retail catalog examples include both frozen green jackfruit and yellow jackfruit; these establish distinct market scopes, not a universal maturity mapping. [S09] |
| Jackfruit bulb/flesh flour | Separate processed-food identity under `FLOUR_AND_STARCH`, if needed | Government processing material describes flour milled from dried bulbs. This is a dried/milled ingredient with a different use and composition from fresh jackfruit, so it should not be a fruit form or an alias of `jackfruit`. Preserve the documented part/stage. [S06] |
| Jackfruit seed flour/powder | Separate product scope from fruit-bulb flour | The source describes seed powder separately from bulb flour; do not merge the two under `jackfruit flour`. If a seed-flour identity is later needed, scope it to seeds and its documented process. [S05–S06] |
| Freeze-dried, osmo-dried, chips, purée, pulp, jam, pickle, or other prepared products | Separate, exact product scopes if recipe demand appears | Sources describe multiple products made from different parts, stages, preservation media, and processes. Do not add them as aliases of fresh fruit or infer a universal jackfruit preparation form. [S06] |

The form-versus-identity choices above are catalog recommendations, not source terminology. They follow the existing schema's separation of a fruit identity from recipe-relevant preparation forms and from separate flour identities; see current `breadfruit` and `breadfruit flour` references for a local precedent. That precedent supports the category boundary, not an automatic jackfruit-flour entry. [S11]

## Québec and Canadian French terminology

The current active French display `jaque` is supported by the OQLF Grand dictionnaire terminologique (GDT), which lists `jaque` and `jacque` as preferred terms for the fruit, gives English `jackfruit` and `jakfruit`, and identifies *Artocarpus heterophyllus*. Its entry was last updated in 1990, so it verifies the base fruit name but does not settle newer commercial product terminology. The OQLF's separate `jaquier` entry defines the tree and gives `jaquier` / `jacquier`; keep those tree terms out of the fruit identity. [S02–S03]

The English word `jack` appears in the OQLF translation list, but the prior integration's local matching audit found a whole-phrase collision with the inactive Monterey Jack candidate. Keep it out of the English aliases. Keep `jakfruit` and `jacque` as the existing spelling aliases. [S02, S11]

No OQLF or TERMIUM record for `jaque vert`, `jaque jeune`, `jaque mûr`, or `jaque non mûr` was located in the targeted terminology searches on 2026-10-11. Treat these as literal/provisional descriptive translations only, not validated Québec stage names. A Canadian retailer's French listing uses `jeune jacquier vert en saumure` in the title and `jeune fruit de jacquier` in the ingredient list; this supports observed commercial usage, not an OQLF-preferred term. The same page's product-description text inconsistently says syrup, while its ingredient list says water, salt, and citric acid, which is another reason not to elevate the listing's full wording to a product standard. [S08]

Other current product pages also use `fruit du jacquier` and `farine de jacquier` in Canadian retail French. These phrases are useful search/source wording, but the OQLF distinction between the fruit `jaque` and the tree `jaquier/jacquier` means they should not silently replace `jaque` or be added as global aliases. For any future product-specific French display, prefer a Québec terminology check; until then, retain the supplier's source wording in evidence and keep the base reference unchanged. [S03, S08, S10]

## Bundled recipe demand

A read-only exact-text scan for the jackfruit phrase family and `ingredient-ref-jackfruit` found no matches in the current 114 manifest-listed recipe files. It covered jackfruit/jack fruit, `jakfruit`, `jaque`/`jacque`, `jacquier`, `fruit du jacquier`, `farine de jaque`/`farine de jacquier`, and *Artocarpus heterophyllus*. Thus the corpus currently supports no jackfruit identity relink, maturity form, seed identity, canned/frozen form, flour reference, measurement, or substitution. This is a finding about the bundled recipes only; it does not imply that the products lack market demand. The preceding WP06 integration record also reports no recipe hit in its 114-recipe / 1,123-row audit. [S11]

## Disposition

Retain the current `jackfruit / jaque` reference and its aliases. Keep maturity and processing boundaries documented but inactive until a recipe or exact product request appears. Use `jaque` for the generic fruit; treat stage wording and fruit-derived product names as provisional/source-qualified in Québec French. This review proposes no active catalog or recipe change.
