# WP02 A7 — Malted pearl millet flour boundary review (R50)

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-13
- **Status:** Proposal only. This packet recommends keeping malted pearl millet flour unresolved and unallocated. It changes no shared catalog, form, legacy, ledger, plan, roadmap, recipe, or batch file.
- **Open checklist:** WP02 A7 at [the active catalog plan](../../RecipeBook-global-ingredient-catalog-plan.txt#L1444), which calls for review of other grain meals, malted/toasted flour identities, and gluten-flour equivalence.
- **Proposal-local source IDs:** `WP02-A7-MALTED-PEARL-MILLET-FLOUR-20261013-R50-S01` through `S09`. These IDs are unique to this proposal and are not ledger records.
- **Non-overlap:** This is a species-specific pearl-millet candidate. It does not reopen R32’s malted finger millet flour, R25’s micronised toasted millet flour, R44’s millet meal, R49’s toasted glutinous rice flour, or the A6 hold for sprouted pearl-millet flour.

## Recommendation

Keep **malted pearl millet flour** as an unresolved A7 candidate. Do not create an active reference, alias, form, legacy identity, ledger record, measure, recipe link, or substitution from this packet.

Peer-reviewed studies directly make and analyze flour from malted or germinated pearl millet, including a study that explicitly names a 500 μm-sieved experimental product “malted pearl millet flour” [S01–S02]. That establishes a real research material, not a stable Canadian/Québec product class. The targeted current-product search found Canadian-facing listings for plain pearl-millet flour, but no product page specifying malted pearl millet flour. It also surfaced an English “Activated pearl millet flour” listing; its description does not specify what “activated” means or say that the flour is malted [S05–S06, S09]. This bounded search is not proof that no malted product exists.

OQLF supports Québec names for the **grain** pearl millet, while Codex supplies the existing French product name for plain pearl-millet flour. Neither source names a malted flour [S03–S04]. Do not infer a display such as “farine de mil chandelle maltée” or “farine de millet perlé maltée” from the base terms. Keep the current plain pearl-millet flour identity and its existing aliases unchanged.

## Measurement and substitution limits

- The primary malting study uses a 1 kg lot of one Ex-Borno pearl-millet variety, with study-specific steeping, germination, kilning, milling, and a 500 μm sieve [S01]. Those process settings identify that laboratory sample; they are not a household measure, package conversion, reusable density, or standard product specification.
- A separate study used a different germination and drying process for its pearl- and finger-millet flour samples [S02]. The variation is a reason not to infer one commercial malted-flour grade from a research phrase.
- The active plain pearl-millet flour row has null `defaultDensity` and empty `unitMappings`. Its ledger retains a study-only bulk-density observation of **0.63 ± 0.01 g/mL** for whole pearl-millet flour measured by a tapped-cylinder method; this is not malted flour and is not a kitchen cup mapping [S07]. Plain-flour package weights are package masses, not volume conversions [S05–S06]. The S&V “activated” page shows both “2 lb” in its offer text and “1 kg” in its product metadata; neither is a recipe measure or evidence of activation [S09].
- A separate plain-flour wheat-bread study evaluated several pearl-millet-flour proportions in one formulation and reported one formulation-specific outcome [S08]. It does not test malted pearl-millet flour or support a reusable replacement ratio. No general flour substitution, yield, or cooking rule is proposed.
- Do not transfer a density, measure, process yield, hydration ratio, or substitution from plain pearl-millet flour, finger millet, generic millet flour, or a composite study blend.
