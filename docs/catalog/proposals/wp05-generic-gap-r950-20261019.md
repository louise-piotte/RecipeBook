## Current ownership and prior hold

Earlier WP06 and Section W reviews held sinki because they lacked a Québec-facing French name and wanted to determine whether a standalone identity was justified. The reviewed primary sources now support a bounded named identity: sinki is a traditional fermented-radish food, distinct from fresh radish and the generic salt-and-vinegar pickled-radish class. The French naming gap remains; the proposal keeps the borrowed name provisional instead of inventing a descriptive translation.

At the audit snapshot, the live seed contains **1,967 ingredient references and 205 forms**; the legacy-import file contains **55 candidates** and the research ledger **2,372 records**. Case-, punctuation-, and accent-folded searches for `sinki`, `sinkhi`, `sinki pickle`, `sinki pickles`, `fermented radish`, `fermented radish tap root`, `fermented radish taproot`, and `सिंकी` found no owner in active reference names or aliases, form labels or match terms, or legacy candidates. The proposed ID `ingredient-ref-sinki` is unowned, and there is no dedicated sinki ledger record.

The term does appear as contextual evidence under the existing `ingredient-ref-pickled-radish` ledger record. Its cited study is used there to distinguish sinki from generic pickles; the accepted decision explicitly keeps fermented radish preserves outside the vinegar-pickle identity. Preserve that evidence and decision without changing or reusing the pickled-radish row as the sinki owner. No other prior proposal assigns this identity.

## Identity and evidence limits

Tamang and Sarkar’s primary study identifies sinki as a traditional fermented radish-taproot food, consumed as a soup base or pickle in parts of northeastern India, Nepal, and Bhutan. Its abstract also reports one experimental optimization for a glass jar and a 12-day fermentation at 30°C. Those experimental conditions characterize that study; they are not transferable process instructions, measurements, forms, or catalog defaults. [S01]

A 2025 Nepal agriculture survey lists Sinki, glossed there as “fermented radish,” among preserved foods in its Kohalpur-2, Banke field report. A separate Nepal government-hosted indigenous-food-practices report describes sinki in terms that include radish taproot and leaf. Together these support the regional name and fermented-radish identity, while warning against imposing one plant part or universal preparation. The reports are geographically bounded and do not define a national standard or product specification. [S02–S03]

Use `sinki` in both language fields as a conservative borrowed display. Targeted OQLF searches did not surface a sinki-specific Québec term. Do not add `fermented radish`, `radis fermenté`, or `sinki pickle` as aliases: these are broad descriptions or use contexts, not verified Québec names for the named food. The borrowed fr-CA display is provisional, not an OQLF-standardized term. No manufacturer, retailer, or product-specific evidence is needed for this generic identity.

Keep sinki separate from raw radish, salt-and-vinegar pickled radish, fermented bamboo shoots, and gundruk. A study’s use of sinki as a soup base or pickle does not establish substitution equivalence with those ingredients. Add no global or contextual substitution rule. Do not carry over any experimental time, temperature, acidity, or other values as generic ingredient data.
