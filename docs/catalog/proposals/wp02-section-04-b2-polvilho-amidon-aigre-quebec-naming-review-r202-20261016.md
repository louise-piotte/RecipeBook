## Recommendation

**Hold `amidon aigre de manioc` outside the active aliases for now.** Keep the existing French display `polvilho azedo`, English display `sour cassava starch (polvilho azedo)`, and accepted Québec retailer alias `farine de polvilho azedo` unchanged. Do not create a new reference, assign the candidate to generic cassava/tapioca starch, or alter the sweet polvilho row.

The Canadian Pinduca product page directly pairs the candidate phrase with the product title `Sour Cassava Starch` and names `polvilho azedo` in its URL. At access, the listing was sold out, its web interface was English/Spanish, and the retailer identifies its physical store as being in Kingston, Ontario. The current Chambly, Québec French-language listing for Amafil uses `Farine de Polvilho azedo premium`; that exact Québec wording is already the active alias. OQLF documents the general French term `fécule de manioc`, not this candidate phrase or the fermented Brazilian product. CIRAD uses `amidon aigre de manioc` in technical writing for a Latin American product family and links the Brazilian name `polvilho azedo`, but that is not Québec usage evidence. Together these sources show that the phrase is credible and product-related, while leaving its local consumer wording unresolved. [R202-S01–S03; WP02-B2-POLVILHO-AZEDO-QUEBEC-ALIAS-20261012-S01; WP02-S24]

MAPA’s guidance supports keeping `polvilho azedo` distinct from non-fermented cassava fécula and `polvilho doce`; it also cautions that the products and mixtures lack a repeatable/reproducible distinguishing method. This establishes the product boundary, not a universal specification or a reason to assign generic cassava-starch wording to the row. [WP02-POLVILHO-LABELS-20261010-S01]

## Product identity and current Canadian/Québec wording

Rapidito Latin Market currently lists a 500 g Pinduca product under `Pinduca, Sour Cassava Starch (Amidon Aigre de Manioc)`. The URL includes `polvilho-azedo`; the title is direct pairing evidence for this exact product listing. At access, the product was marked **sold out** for $6.05 CAD. Rapidito’s own store description identifies its physical store as being in Kingston, Ontario. The product page offers English and Spanish navigation, so this is Canadian evidence but not a French-language Québec retail context. The title supports Canadian market use of the phrase, not a Québec naming preference or an availability claim. Its 500 g is package quantity, not a recipe measure. [R202-S01, R202-S03]

Marché Brésil’s current French-language page titles the Amafil product `Farine de Polvilho azedo premium 1 kg`. Its description says it is made from manioc fécules, and the listing identifies the retailer’s pickup location as 729 Rue Saint-Pierre, Chambly, Québec, with Montréal-region delivery. The page presents an add-to-cart option at access. It supports the exact locally used phrase `farine de polvilho azedo`, already assigned as an alias; it does not use `amidon aigre de manioc`. The 1 kg package size is not a household measure. [WP02-B2-POLVILHO-AZEDO-QUEBEC-ALIAS-20261012-S01]

OQLF’s GDT defines `fécule de manioc` as a product obtained by wet extraction from manioc roots and lists `manioc starch`, `cassava starch`, and `tapioca starch` as English terms. It does not list `amidon aigre de manioc` or define the Brazilian fermented product. This is useful Québec terminology context for generic starch only; it neither validates nor rejects the candidate phrase as a product-specific retail alias. [WP02-S24]

The CIRAD/IRD French-language cassava-processing publication uses `amidon aigre de manioc`, identifies it parenthetically with Brazilian `polvilho azedo` and Colombian `almidón agrio`, and describes specific Latin American breads. It is technical/regional evidence outside Québec. Its process and baking discussion does not supply a general retail label, household measure, or substitute ratio. [R202-S02]

Brazil’s MAPA guidance distinguishes cassava fécula from polvilho products and describes fermentation as varying among products; it says a repeatable/reproducible method for differentiating the products and mixtures was not available. Grupo PQ’s manufacturer page separately pairs `Polvilho Azedo` with `Sour Cassava Starch`. These sources support retaining the product qualifier and not folding `amidon aigre de manioc` into a generic tapioca or cassava-starch row. [WP02-POLVILHO-LABELS-20261010-S01; WP02-POLVILHO-LABELS-20261010-S05-AZEDO]

## Picker behavior

`IngredientReferenceSearchIndex` in `domain/localization/IngredientSearch.kt` normalizes accents, case, punctuation, and spacing, then returns complete word-phrase matches before falling back to substring matching. The current seed reference and form terms were projected through that algorithm. No app test or emulator run was performed for this proposal-only review.

## Measures and substitutions

The Pinduca listing’s 500 g figure is package mass. Grupo PQ’s separate manufacturer page labels a 5 g serving as one teaspoon and lists its own 500 g, 1 kg, and 5 kg package sizes. These are package/label-specific observations, not a transferable Pinduca serving, density, gram-per-volume mapping, or recipe conversion. No reusable measurement data are proposed.

The CIRAD publication discusses the use of sour cassava starch in specified Latin American breads and the process-related variability of baking properties. It does not test a substitution between polvilho azedo and generic tapioca starch, another starch, or a flour. Do not add a global form conversion or contextual substitution rule from these sources.

## Reopen conditions

Reopen this exact phrase only when a current Québec product/package listing or Québec terminology authority uses `amidon aigre de manioc` for a product clearly identified as Brazilian `polvilho azedo`. Confirm that the term does not refer to generic cassava/tapioca starch or another regional product class. If that evidence arrives, consider a French search alias on the existing azedo row only, record the accepted wording decision on its current ledger record, and rerun exact and broad picker projections. Keep the existing display, sweet polvilho row, generic tapioca-starch row, forms, measures, and substitutions unchanged unless separate evidence supports a separate decision.
