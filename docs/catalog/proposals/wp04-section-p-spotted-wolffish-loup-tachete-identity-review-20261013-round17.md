# WP04 Section P — Spotted wolffish / loup tacheté identity review

- **Review and source-access date:** 2026-10-13.
- **Packet type:** proposal-only finfish identity, Québec naming, picker, conservation/product boundary, ownership, and recipe-demand review.
- **Disposition:** **Add identity-only** for spotted wolffish / loup tacheté (*Anarhichas minor*), category `PROTEIN`, with no aliases. This recognizes the species-qualified food-fish identity supported by OQLF and CFIA and the limited seller evidence for farmed product. It does not claim general Québec availability or authorize wild capture, possession, or sale.
- **Integration:** completed in [the round-17 integration README](../batches/wp02-wp03-wp04-round17-gap-review-integration-20261013/README.md). No app or emulator tests were run because the shared catalog change only adds database JSON data.

## Candidate and decision

The Section P finfish work remains open. The accepted Atlantic-wolffish row covers only *Anarhichas lupus*; its review explicitly leaves spotted wolffish (*A. minor*) and northern wolffish (*A. denticulatus*) separate. A search of the active plan, proposal and batch archive found no dedicated spotted-wolffish identity decision or integrated row. This packet considers only *A. minor*.

| Proposed reference ID | Category | English display | Québec French display | Taxon | English aliases | French aliases |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ingredient-ref-spotted-wolffish` | `PROTEIN` | **spotted wolffish** | **loup tacheté** | *Anarhichas minor* | None | None |

Add only this species-qualified pair. OQLF places `loup tacheté` in both ichthyology and edible-fish terminology, identifies *A. minor*, and pairs it with English `spotted wolffish`. CFIA independently assigns both names to *A. minor*. A current DFO Gulf plan lists the taxon separately from Atlantic and northern wolffish. Together, these establish a distinct Canadian/Québec food-fish identity, not a generic wolffish group or a claim that wild fish can be retained.

Current food-product evidence is limited. A supplier reported on 2025-05-27 that its land-based PEI farmed spotted wolffish was being received by its Montreal buyer, Poissonnerie La Mer, but also said supply remained limited during an early production phase. That is seller evidence for one farmed supply channel, not proof of routine Québec retail availability, sustained inventory, wild-product availability, or a species-wide market. La Mer’s currently indexed online fish assortment does not list spotted wolffish; that omission cannot rule out in-store or fresh-arrival sales. Keep the catalog identity neutral and species-qualified, without a provenance form, source claim, availability note, or market-wide alias.

## Name, species, and regulatory boundary

- **Preferred Québec product name:** `loup tacheté`, with English `spotted wolffish`, for *Anarhichas minor*. OQLF’s entry was last updated in 2011. Its terminology is authoritative for the Québec display choice; its note that the fish had “commercial potential in Québec” is not current retail evidence. [S01]
- **CFIA name-list scope:** the Fish List record 171342 for *A. minor* includes `Spotted Cat Fish` / `Loup Tacheté`, `Spotted Wolffish`, `Spotted Sea Cat`, and bare `Wolffish`. CFIA’s record for *A. lupus* also includes bare `Wolffish`. This confirms that `wolffish` is not species-specific; it does not make each listed phrase a safe catalog alias or prove food-market availability. [WP04-P-ATLANTIC-WOLFFISH-20261012-S02]
- **Excluded names:** keep bare `wolffish`, `catfish`, `spotted catfish`, `spotted cat fish`, `spotted sea cat`, `spotted sea-cat`, `leopardfish`, `petit loup de mer`, `poisson-chat`, `chat de mer`, and `loup de mer tacheté` out of aliases. OQLF says `petit loup de mer`, `poisson-chat`, and `chat de mer` were not retained by the Fish Products Terminology Committee. CFIA/OQLF’s English `spotted catfish` and `spotted sea cat` contain broad picker terms; adding either would broaden the existing `catfish` result set. The Mayrand item headed `loup de mer tacheté` lacks a scientific name and its English page says `Spotted Sea Bass`, so it cannot override OQLF or confirm this taxon. [S01, CFIA source, Mayrand source]
- **CITES:** I checked the official CITES Appendices valid from 2026-03-05. Neither *Anarhichas minor*, *Anarhichas*, nor family `Anarhichadidae` appears in the current appendices. This is a narrow finding about CITES listing; it does not displace SARA, fisheries rules, or other domestic requirements. [S02]
- **SARA and DFO fisheries handling:** the 2026 DFO Gulf of St. Lawrence IFMP lists *A. minor* as Threatened under both COSEWIC and SARA, Schedule 1. In the IFMP’s covered Gulf groundfish fisheries (NAFO 4RST and subdivisions 3Pn/4Vn), fish at risk must be returned to the place of capture with least harm; this is a fisheries-area and licence context, not a statement about every Canadian fishery or every farmed fish product. DFO Québec’s 2026 recreational groundfish notice sets zero retention for spotted wolffish in the specified Division 4T fishery. Do not turn that recreational order into a general commercial-market ban. [WP04-P-ATLANTIC-WOLFFISH-20261012-S04, S03]
- **Current science and landing limits:** DFO’s July 2025 assessment treats the three Canadian Atlantic wolffish species separately, while noting that some reported wolffish landings are unspeciated and that observer coverage affects species-level catch estimates. Those totals cannot establish *A. minor* retail supply or a 4T stock trend. The DFO/Gulf sources support the species-level listing and the specific regional handling rules above; no stock-specific harvest claim is proposed here. [WP04-P-ATLANTIC-WOLFFISH-20261012-S03]
- **Wild versus farmed product:** DFO’s current wild-capture measures require release/return of spotted wolffish in the covered fisheries. The supplier’s PEI aquaculture account is separate, limited product evidence. This proposal neither determines the legal treatment of a particular aquaculture lot nor treats the seller’s statement as legal advice. A catalog identity is not permission to catch or possess a wild specimen.

## Measures, product states, and substitutions

- **No candidate-specific CNF serving measure was found.** Health Canada’s current CNF serving page returned for this family is food code 3091, `Fish, catfish (wolffish), atlantic, raw` / `Poisson, loup de l’Atlantique (loup de mer), cru`, explicitly *A. lupus*. Its `1/2 fillet = 148.4 g`, `1 food guide serving = 90 g`, and 73% total refuse are specific to that Atlantic-wolffish record and cannot be transferred to *A. minor*. A search for *A. minor* / spotted wolffish did not surface a separate CNF serving record; that search result is not proof that no such record exists in any CNF release. [reused CNF source ID]
- A Mayrand Quebec product page shows an approximate `4.54 kg` unit and `$19.99/kg` for `Loup de mer tacheté`, but all named stores show `Bientôt disponible`; the English page calls the item `Spotted Sea Bass`. It has no scientific-name or provenance evidence, so neither the species nor a package/yield measurement can be assigned to this reference. Treat this page as an ambiguous retail label, not a candidate unit conversion or a current-stock confirmation. [S06]
- Amar Seafood’s 2025 seller account gives no serving, package size, fillet weight, edible yield, or conversion. The PEI farmed origin is not an ingredient form. Add no `farmed`, `fresh`, `frozen`, `whole`, `fillet`, `cooked`, smoked, dried, salted, canned, powdered, or other form from these sources. Keep density null and unit mappings empty.
- DFO biological lengths, weights, survey catch, and landings are not culinary amounts. The DFO 2025 report’s unspeciated landings cannot be converted to an *A. minor* market amount.
- No source or recipe supports replacing spotted wolffish with Atlantic wolffish, channel catfish, cod, or another finfish. Shared genus, similar preparation, vendor flavor claims, and broader market names do not establish a safe or useful substitution. Add no global form conversion or contextual ingredient substitution.
