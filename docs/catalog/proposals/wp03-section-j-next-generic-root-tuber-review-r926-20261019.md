The OQLF assigns *crosne du Japon* to *Stachys affinis* and says the edible portion is the successive swellings on its tuberous rhizomes. Its English entry gives `Chinese artichoke` and `Japanese artichoke` and describes crisp edible tubers eaten raw or cooked. Kew accepts *Stachys affinis* and lists *S. sieboldii* and *S. tubifera* among its synonyms. The candidate is therefore the specific vegetable of this species, not a generic *Stachys* root or a member of the artichoke genus. [S01–S03]

Keep this identity separate from the active globe artichoke (*Cynara*) and Jerusalem artichoke (*Helianthus tuberosus*) references. OQLF notes a taste resemblance to artichoke, salsify, and potato; this is descriptive, not a shared identity or substitution claim. Do not map crosne to those vegetables, potato, salsify, or their flours and starches. The plan’s broader arrowhead, eddoe, true-yam, and regional-tuber boundaries remain as previously reviewed. [S01]

## Measurement, preparation, and substitution limits

OQLF and FAO Ecocrop support raw or cooked use; FAO also names boiling and frying. Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada describes the crop’s tuber appearance and dimensions and reports cultivation for raw consumption in China and Japan. Those are culinary-method or crop observations, not household portions. Do not convert the agronomic lengths, diameters, or yields into per-piece weights, density, cup measures, or unit mappings. No reviewed source establishes a reusable kitchen measure or a tested substitution. Flavor comparisons alone do not justify replacing crosne with globe/Jerusalem artichoke, salsify, potato, or another root. Leave forms, measurements, conversions, yields, and substitutions unset. [S01, S03–S04]
