# WP06 African regional gap review R484 — grains of Selim

- **Date:** 2026-10-17
- **Status:** Proposal only; no shared catalog, form, ledger, recipe, plan, roadmap, manifest, or generated-file changes made.
- **Scope:** One source-named West African spice identity for the culinary fruit of *Xylopia aethiopica*, commonly sold as grains of Selim. This is distinct from the active grains of paradise identity and from the prior WP06 additions for baobab leaves and dried *Gnetum* leaves.
- **Recommendation:** Add one identity-only **BAKING_AND_SPICE** reference for **grains of Selim / grains de Selim**. Use only the English aliases **Uda seeds** and **Selim pepper**, and the French alias **poivre de Sélim**. Do not add forms, numerical data, substitutions, or recipe links.

## Open-plan fit and recent-work boundary

Section 05 of the global ingredient catalog plan still leaves North, West, Central, Horn, East, and Southern African coverage open after its bounded six-region sweep, R478, and R481. This is one West African spice review, not a claim of country-level or continental completeness. Section Z remains separately open for African sauces, pastes, fermented seasonings, red-palm preparations, groundnut products, and related condiments; this single spice does not close or alter that checklist.

R476 concerns a held foxtail-millet-flakes name; R477 covers smoked Greenland halibut; R478 covers dried baobab-leaf powder; R479 adds steel-cut-oat aliases; R480 covers lobster meat; and R481 covers dried *Gnetum* leaves. None represents this fruit spice. R475 Voatsiperifery is another distinct pepper-like spice from Madagascar. The WP07 peppercorn review already establishes the boundary between *Xylopia aethiopica* and the active grains-of-paradise identity for *Aframomum melegueta*; this proposal preserves that distinction.

Health Canada lists **Grains of Selim (*Xylopia aethiopica*)** as a spice commodity and lists **Grains of Paradise (*Aframomum melegueta*)** separately [reused source WP07-PPC-20261009-S01]. Kew accepts *X. aethiopica* and places its native range in tropical Africa. FAO’s West African tree-food appendix lists it under spice and condiment use. Those sources establish a distinct regional culinary identity, but do not standardize every local name or preparation.

The proposed French display uses the Canadian French-language product phrase **grains de Selim**; **poivre de Sélim** is retained as a narrow alias because a Canadian French retail guide uses it for this spice. These are source-backed display choices, not OQLF-approved terminology. No OQLF culinary entry for Selim surfaced in targeted searches. TERMIUM Plus does have a Botany record for *Xylopia aethiopica* with **graines de Guinée** and **piment noir de Guinée**; that record does not establish a culinary commodity name. The exact phrase **graines de Guinée** is already an active alias on ingredient-ref-grains-of-paradise, so do not add it to this row or duplicate its picker result.

## Québec French and retail evidence

The Québec-facing evidence is a current NaturoSendey product listing whose page identifies its service area as Ahuntsic-Cartierville, Montréal. It is headed **Uda seeds**, marked **In stock**, priced at **CAD 14.67**, and also says that online purchase is unavailable. It lists the item as 50 g, reports Côte d’Ivoire origin, names *Xylopia aethiopica*, describes one fibrous pod with five to eight seeds, and mentions cooking and Touba coffee. The 50 g is the seller’s package amount, not a recipe measure.

Two additional Canadian French-language sources support wording and boundaries but do not prove a Québec terminology standard. Maison Téranga’s Café Touba listing calls the spice **poivre de Guinée, aussi appelé grains de Selim**; the page is a ground coffee blend, currently marked sold out, and its 200 g, 400 g, and 1 kg choices are blend package sizes. Savanna Select’s Calgary blog uses **poivre de Sélim**; its Taro-condiment listing separately labels **Rondelle** as *Afrostyrax lepidophyllus* and Selim berries as another blend component. Use the French words only to identify the spice; do not use either compound listing for measures, preparation rules, or a substitution.
