## Recommendation

**ACCEPT / RETAIN** the exact display `farine de fonio noir` for `ingredient-ref-black-fonio-flour`.

| Field | Current value | Review disposition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| English display | `black fonio flour` | No change in scope |
| French display | `farine de fonio noir` | Accept as a clear species-qualified French product construction |
| Species boundary | *Digitaria iburua* | Preserve explicitly in the identity record; this is a distinct species from white fonio, *D. exilis* |
| Aliases | none | Keep none; no Québec/Canadian flour label supports another regional display |

Canadian terminology evidence standardizes `fonio noir` as the French common name for *D. iburua*. Adding the ordinary product-class noun `farine de` yields a clear and grammatical ingredient display. This is not an OQLF-approved flour term or an attested Canadian/Québec package label: the evidence reviewed establishes the species name and the local use of `farine de fonio` for other fonio flours, but no regional seller located here labels flour made from *D. iburua* [S01–S06].

The word *noir* identifies the species’ established common name; it should not be read as a guarantee that the milled flour itself is black. One botanical product reference notes that the species is called black fonio for its dark spikelets while its grain is white [S06]. Retain the display because it follows the standardized name and preserves the species distinction. Do not change it to `farine de manne noire` or `farine d'ibourou`: those are standardized taxon synonyms in TERMIUM Plus, but the reviewed Canadian/Québec food-product sources do not show them used as flour names [S01, S04–S05].

## Québec and Canadian terminology findings

- **TERMIUM Plus:** The Government of Canada’s *Digitaria iburua* entry lists French `fonio noir` as a standardized masculine name, alongside `manne noire` and `ibourou`; its English side lists standardized `black fonio`. The record says these designations were standardized by ISO in 2013. This directly supports `fonio noir` for the species, not for a flour product [S01].
- **Health Canada:** The French crop-group list separately names `Fonio blanc (Digitaria exilis)` and `Fonio noir (Digitaria iburua)`. This is current Canadian government French usage and directly confirms the species boundary [S02].
- **OQLF:** The GDT entry for `fonio blanc` is an OQLF-authored food/cereal term, lists *D. exilis*, and gives the preferred French forms `fonio blanc`, `fonio`, `acha`, `fundi`, and `findi`. I found no OQLF GDT entry specifically for `fonio noir` in the targeted search. The OQLF entry is useful evidence that the color-qualified pattern is established in Québec terminology, but it covers *D. exilis* only and does not itself approve `fonio noir` or the flour phrase [S03].

No current Canadian or Québec retail label for flour explicitly made from *D. iburua* was located in this review. The Québec and Canadian product examples establish normal flour phrasing and the naming of the more common *D. exilis* product; they do not provide a direct black-fonio flour label.

## Boundary and alias decision

Retain the existing French display exactly as `farine de fonio noir` and keep it attached only to flour made from *Digitaria iburua*. Keep the following identities distinct:

- `ingredient-ref-black-fonio-flour` — `black fonio flour / farine de fonio noir`, species *D. iburua*;
- `ingredient-ref-black-fonio` — `black fonio / fonio noir`, grain identity for *D. iburua*;
- `ingredient-ref-fonio-flour` — `fonio flour / farine de fonio`, current flour identity scoped by its existing research to *D. exilis*;
- `ingredient-ref-white-fonio` — `white fonio / fonio blanc`, grain identity for *D. exilis*.

Do not add `farine de fonio`, `farine de fonio blanc`, `farine de manne noire`, `farine d'ibourou`, or `farine d'iburu` as aliases to the black-fonio flour reference. The first two belong to or can collide with the existing *D. exilis* flour identity; the latter forms lack reviewed Canadian/Québec flour-product usage. No alias change is recommended.
