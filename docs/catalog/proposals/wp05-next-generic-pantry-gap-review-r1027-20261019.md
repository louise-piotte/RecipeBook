## Recommendation

Propose one identity-only **chonggak-kimchi**, the named Korean kimchi made with whole young radishes and their leaves. Keep it separate from the generic kimchi reference and from other named kimchi types. Use `chonggak-kimchi` as the English display and use the provisional fr-CA display `chonggak-kimchi (kimchi de jeunes radis entiers)`. The parenthetical is a descriptive construction based on Korean government English and French descriptions; it is not an OQLF-standardized subtype term. Add only the narrow English aliases `chonggakkimchi` and `whole radish kimchi`, both attested by the Korea Tourism Organization (KTO). Add no French aliases.

The evidence supports a source-named generic food identity, not a fixed recipe or universal formulation. Do not add a raw-radish form, measurements, conversions, yields, recipe links, or active substitution rules.

## Identity and bilingual naming

KTO names the food **Whole Radish Kimchi (총각김치 / Chonggakkimchi)** and describes it as a kimchi made from whole radish with leaves. Its culinary description notes a tender leafy end and serving with rice or noodles. This supports a distinct prepared-food identity and the English alias `whole radish kimchi`; its page does not provide a standardized ingredient formula. [S01]

The Republic of Korea’s Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs lists chonggak among named kimchi types alongside baechu and gat kimchi. This corroborates the named-type boundary, not a complete taxonomy. The MAFRA URL already appears in the ledger under `ingredient-ref-kkakdugi` and `ingredient-ref-gat-kimchi`; keep those locators and source IDs in place. The proposal assigns its own source ID and chonggak-specific locator rather than moving or reusing either record. [S02]

Korea.net’s French-language pages use `chonggak kimchi` and the descriptive phrase `kimchi de radis`; one also identifies it as made with young radishes. OQLF’s existing `kimchi` entry supports the Québec French parent term but does not name this subtype. The proposed French parenthetical `kimchi de jeunes radis entiers` combines those government descriptions with KTO’s whole-radish description. It is therefore a source-derived fr-CA gloss, not an established Québec term. Keep the borrowed subtype name first and add no broad French alias such as `kimchi de radis`. Do not use the Korea.net page’s literal explanatory phrase `kimchi des vieux garçons` as a display or alias. [S03–S05]
