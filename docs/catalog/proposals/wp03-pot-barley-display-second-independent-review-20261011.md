# WP03 pot barley: second independent Québec French display review

## Review record

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-11.
- **Status:** proposal-only independent review; no shared catalog, ledger, checklist, plan, or code was edited.
- **Scope:** the French display for the existing pot-barley identity and its boundary from hulled and pearl barley. This is a terminology review, not a new identity or numeric-data proposal.

## Recommendation

**Hold final Québec bilingual approval. Retain `orge en pot` only as the current provisional French display.** The phrase is attested on Canadian French product listings, but I found no OQLF or TERMIUM approval for that exact term and no Québec-specific bilingual source that resolves the naming conflict. Current Québec retailer pages often use `orge mondé` for English-labelled pot barley; OQLF uses `orge mondé` for *hulled barley*, which is already a separate active row.

There is no safer evidence-backed alternate in this review. Replacing the display with `orge mondé` would reuse the active hulled-barley name; `orge entière` is used in a federal French purchasing specification for the English hulled-barley class, but is not established here as the Québec retail name for pot barley. Keep the human bilingual approval item open. Do not add French aliases.

## Live catalog and demand check

The active reference is `ingredient-ref-pot-barley`, displayed as `pot barley / orge en pot`, with English alias `Scotch barley`, no French aliases, no density, and no unit mappings. The separate active row `ingredient-ref-hulled-barley` is `hulled barley / orge mondé`; pearl barley is `pearl barley / orge perlé`. The research ledger marks pot-barley identity verified, while measurement and substitution research remain not yet researched.

A scan of all 114 bundled recipe files on 2026-10-11 found no barley, pot-barley, hulled-barley, or French barley-name references. There is no bundled recipe demand to settle the display or to support a measurement or substitution claim.

The shared WP03 batch still asks for independent Québec approval of the market-attested `orge en pot` display. The plan also explicitly says to keep the term provisional because current Québec usage collides with OQLF's hulled-barley term. This review supports keeping that open item open.

## Display and alias disposition

| Term | Disposition |
| --- | --- |
| `pot barley` | Retain as the English display for this distinct processing identity. |
| `orge en pot` | Retain only as the current provisional French display. It is market-attested, but this review does not approve it as the preferred Québec term. |
| `Scotch barley` | Keep only as the existing narrow English alias; the federal purchasing spec pairs it with pot barley, and TERMIUM marks it rare. |
| `orge mondé` / `orge mondée` | Do not add to pot-barley aliases. OQLF assigns `orge mondé` to hulled barley, and current retailers and federal translations also use it for pot barley. This is a collision, not grounds to merge the English identities. |
| `orge entière` | Do not add as a display or alias. The reviewed federal French spec uses it for English hulled barley, and no reviewed Québec retail source uses it to identify pot barley. |
| `orge légèrement polie` / `orge partiellement polie` | Do not add. These may describe processing, but no reviewed Québec source attests either as the product name. |

## Measurement and substitution limits

The active reference has no density or unit mappings, and the ledger leaves measurement and substitution research open. Retail package weights are net package sizes, not volume equivalents. The process descriptions do not establish a grams-per-cup value, cooking yield, or safe substitution between pot, hulled, and pearl barley. Keep all numeric and substitution data empty pending separate evidence.
