# WP03 Section G — Prepared chapati identity review

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-11

**Scope:** Chapati/chapatti as a finished prepared flatbread ingredient. This review separates the bread from atta flour and its existing “farine à chapati” search term.

**Disposition:** Integrated one narrow, identity-only chapati bread reference. It remains separate from atta, roti, naan, paratha, pita, and the broad pain plat family. The bundled recipe corpus has no chapati ingredient line, so no recipe was relinked. Sources S01, S02, and S08 are registered in the shared research ledger.

Use chapati itself for both displays; Québec terminology and local French recipes support the borrowed name, so a translation such as “pain plat indien” is unnecessary [S01, S06–S07]. Do not add pain chapati, chapati bread, roti, chapati flour, chappati flour, farine à chapati, naan, paratha, pita, flatbread, or pain plat as aliases.

The retail evidence reveals regional naming overlap: a current Canadian chapati product listing also describes chapati as roti [S08]. The proposed row is therefore scoped to the specifically named chapati product documented by the OQLF, not to a claim that chapati and roti are universally exclusive culinary classes. Keep the existing roti row independent, omit roti as a chapati alias, and add no substitution rule.

## Identity boundaries and limits

- **Bread, not flour.** The OQLF defines chapati as a prepared flat bread based on atta. Its separate atta entry lists farine à chapati as a rare French name for the flour and chapati flour / chappati flour in English; it also describes atta as an input used to prepare chapati, nan, and paratha [S01–S02]. The active ingredient-ref-atta is correctly categorized as FLOUR_AND_STARCH, with farine à chapati, chapati flour, and chappati flour attached to that flour record. Keep those terms there.
- **Roti overlap is real and bounded.** Canadian retail copy sometimes calls chapati “also known as Roti” [S08]. That usage does not establish a safe global alias: it conflicts with the narrower OQLF chapati definition and with the project’s existing separately named roti bread reference. Do not claim a universal taxonomy, merge the records, or let a roti-only recipe line resolve to chapati.
- **Naan and paratha remain separate.** The OQLF describes nan/naan as a leavened Indian flatbread baked in an oven and separately names chapati, nan, and paratha as products prepared with atta [S02, S04]. This supports distinct named identities, not a common flour formula or equivalence.
- **Pita remains separate.** The OQLF has a distinct pita bread entry, and the Québec government’s family food guide lists chapati, pain naan, and pain pita as separate bread choices [S05–S06]. The guide is serving-context evidence, not a product standard.
- **Pain plat and flatbread are family terms.** The OQLF’s 2026 pain plat entry includes chapatis and some pitas as examples across the flatbread family. That family relationship does not make pain plat or flatbread a chapati synonym [S03].
- **No forms or numeric behavior.** Do not infer whole-wheat, fresh, frozen, raw, cooked, or other global forms from an individual recipe or retail package. Source recipe quantities, servings, and retail pack sizes remain specific to those sources; they establish no standard piece mass, count, yield, conversion, density, or unit mapping [S06–S08].
- **No substitutions.** Similar flat shape, meal role, or service with curry does not support chapati↔roti, chapati↔naan, chapati↔paratha, chapati↔pita, or generic-flatbread substitution.
