## Recommendation

Propose identity-only **Sichuan paocai**, a regionally named Chinese preserved-vegetable food. The identity is the Sichuan-qualified class, not an individual vegetable or universal recipe. Sources describe the class with varied fresh vegetables and with salted or brined processing; the traditional paocai evidence identifies mild lactic fermentation as characteristic. Keep the place qualifier so this row does not become a catch-all for every Chinese food translated as “pickled vegetables.”

Use `Sichuan paocai` as the English display. For fr-CA, use the provisional borrowed name `paocai du Sichuan (légumes fermentés du Sichuan)`. The reviewed sources do not provide a Québec-standardized French preserve name; the parenthetical is descriptive, not an official term or search alias. Add no aliases. In particular, do not add unqualified `paocai`, `Sichuan kimchi`, `Sichuan pickles`, or broad `pickled vegetables` aliases: those phrases can encompass other vegetables or preparations and would weaken the regional boundary.

Propose no forms, measures, recipe links, conversions, yields, or active substitutions. Keep source-specific vegetable mixtures and fermentation conditions scoped to their studies or standards.

## Identity and bilingual boundary

The current Sichuan local standard is explicitly titled **Sichuan Paocai Production Specification** (DB51/T 1069-2025). Its registered scope applies to paocai made primarily from fresh vegetables, with optional additional ingredients, using either salt-salting or salt-water soaking; it permits multiple later treatments. This supports a regionally defined preserved-vegetable category and cautions against assigning one mandatory vegetable or process form. Only the standard’s title, status, and generic scope are used here; the registry’s drafting-organization list is not ingredient evidence. [S02]

Liang et al. describe paocai as a traditional mildly and lactic-acid-fermented vegetable food, especially associated with Sichuan, and report side-dish and appetizer use. Their article discusses multiple vegetables in the general description. Its own study used a particular vegetable mixture and brine, temperature, and fermentation schedule; those particulars are not definitions for all Sichuan paocai. [S01]

Xiao et al. name Sichuan paocai as one of three major traditional Chinese fermented-vegetable types alongside Jiangxi yancai and Dongbei suancai. The existing ledger owner is `ingredient-ref-dongbei-suancai` and its current locator treats the three as distinct regional types. If this identity is later integrated, add a Sichuan-specific source locator under a new record-local source ID while preserving the Dongbei source ID and interpretation. Do not treat the shared article as support for equivalent formulas, same substrate, or replacement behavior. [S03]

Use the borrowed regional name first in French: `paocai du Sichuan`. The display gloss `légumes fermentés du Sichuan` conveys the broad food class from the reviewed sources but is provisional; no Québec authority or regional source reviewed here standardizes a French preserve name. Do not add a French alias for generic `légumes fermentés`, `légumes marinés`, or `choucroute chinoise`.

The proposed identity is neither generic Chinese `泡菜` nor a catch-all for all fermented vegetables. It is also distinct from Dongbei and Bashang suancai, Jiangxi yancai, Tianjin dongcai, Hakka mei gan cai, Korean kimchi, and the separately named zha cai and Yibin ya cai. Shared vegetables, salt, acidity, or fermentation do not establish synonymy or substitution.

## Numeric, form, and substitution evidence limits

No reviewed source supplies a transferable recipe measure, mass per household unit, density, unit mapping, conversion, finished-food yield, or universal salt percentage, temperature, or duration. The 2016 J-STAGE study reports one experimental sample made with cabbage, cowpea, carrot, and selected aromatics, submerged in **6% w/v NaCl brine** and held at **20–25 °C for 30 days**; these are that study’s sample conditions only. Its introduction also summarizes a broader salt-brine range, but the statement does not create a stable product-wide recipe rule. Do not enter either the experimental values or the review range as catalog behavior. [S01]

The current standard’s optional salt- or brine-based production pathways and optional post-processing steps describe a manufacturing scope, not ingredient forms for recipe entry. Do not infer cut, drained, canned, cooked, seasoned, sterilized, package, or serving forms from that scope. No recipe line in the bundled library supplies a target amount or state.

No reviewed source recommends replacing Sichuan paocai with another ingredient for a specified dish role, method, state, and amount. Do not activate substitutions to generic pickled vegetables, pickled mustard greens, pickled radish, kimchi, Dongbei suancai, Bashang suancai, Tianjin dongcai, zha cai, Yibin ya cai, or raw vegetables on the basis of a shared ingredient or fermentation method.
