## Recommendation

**Hold `pili pulp flour` outside active references and the research ledger.** Philippine government and university sources establish human-food use of flour made from the edible pulp of *Canarium ovatum* fruit, separately from flour made from the edible nut kernel. They describe research or process-development products, including full-fat and differently oil-pressed samples. The current Canadian/Québec product review found whole pili nuts and nut-derived products, but no current Canadian standalone pulp-flour product or Québec French product label for this flour. The Québec-facing `noix de pili` name refers to the edible nut/kernel; it does not attest `farine de pulpe de pili`. [WP02-B2-PILI-PULP-FLOUR-R186-20261016-S01–S05; WP02-B2-PILI-NUT-FLOUR-R177-20261016-S02, S04; WP03-I-PILI-IDENTITY-20261012-S01, S05]

Do not add `ingredient-ref-pili-pulp-flour`, aliases, a legacy candidate, or a ledger candidate now. Keep the R177 `pili nut flour / farine de noix de pili` identity scoped to the kernel. The 2009 research report itself recommends pilot testing for commercialization, so its food trials do not establish a current retail flour product. [WP02-B2-PILI-PULP-FLOUR-R186-20261016-S05; WP02-B2-PILI-NUT-FLOUR-R177-20261016-S02]

## Product and taxonomy boundary

The candidate is flour made from the fleshy edible pulp of the *Canarium ovatum* fruit. The Philippine Department of Agriculture describes a fruit with pulp, shell, and kernel layers and reports the pulp as edible after cooking. It separately discusses nut processors and exports; this does not identify a Canadian pulp-flour SKU. [WP02-B2-PILI-PULP-FLOUR-R186-20261016-S01]

The 2023 University of the Philippines Los Baños thesis names `pili pulp flour` and prepares it from pulp remaining in pili-nut and oil processing. A 2025 University of the Philippines Diliman thesis distinguishes full-fat pulp flour from two defatted samples made using different oil-removal methods. The older Philippine utility-model record also describes separating the pulp mesocarp and exocarp, removing oil from the mesocarp, and drying/milling resulting materials. These sources support a distinct pulp-flour research product, but do not establish one current retail process shared across the market. [WP02-B2-PILI-NUT-FLOUR-R177-20261016-S02; WP02-B2-PILI-PULP-FLOUR-R186-20261016-S02–S03]

Keep these materials separate:

| Material or wording | Disposition |
|---|---|
| `pili pulp flour` | Source-attested research name for flour from fruit pulp. Hold as a candidate until current Canadian product scope and matching Québec French wording are established. |
| `pili nut flour` / `farine de noix de pili` | Existing `ingredient-ref-pili-nut-flour`; flour from the edible kernel. Do not merge with pulp flour. |
| Whole pili fruit, edible cooked pulp, or pulp powder | Not proven equivalent to dried and milled pulp flour. No alias or form is proposed. |
| Pili-pulp oil, seed/kernel oil, shell, coloring powder, or flour blends | Separate products or materials. No alias or substitution is proposed. |
| `pili flour`, `pili powder`, `pili meal`, `farine de pili` | Unqualified and scope-ambiguous. Leave unassigned. |
| `pili-pili` | OQLF defines this as an extremely hot African red pepper. Keep it out of all pili-nut and pili-pulp aliases. [WP03-I-PILI-IDENTITY-20261012-S07] |

## Canadian and Québec product and language evidence

The Québec-facing ALOCAL listing uses `noix de pili` for activated, dehydrated whole pili nuts; it currently reports the item out of stock. The same page does not name fruit pulp or flour. TERMIUM Plus pairs English `pili nut` with French `noix de pili` for the edible kernel, not pulp flour. A current Philippine processor page lists in-shell pili nuts, kernels, pieces, and nut butter; it does not list pulp flour. These sources establish adjacent nut wording and boundary only. [WP03-I-PILI-IDENTITY-20261012-S01, S05; WP02-B2-PILI-NUT-FLOUR-R177-20261016-S04]

Searches on 2026-10-16 for `pili pulp flour Canada`, `site:.ca "pili pulp flour"`, `site:.ca "pili flour" -nut -nuts`, `"farine de pulpe de pili"`, and `"poudre de pulpe de pili"` did not locate a current Canadian/Québec standalone pulp-flour listing or a French package/product title. Search absence is not an exhaustive market survey. In particular, the Department of Agriculture's report that pili processors export pili to Canada does not establish that this flour is among the exported products. [WP02-B2-PILI-PULP-FLOUR-R186-20261016-S01; WP03-I-PILI-IDENTITY-20261012-S05]

English `pili pulp flour` is directly named in Philippine research. For French, `farine de pulpe de pili` would be a literal descriptive translation only; I found no Québec product label, Québec recipe ingredient declaration, or OQLF/TERMIUM entry for that flour phrase. The adjacent local wording `noix de pili` is kernel-specific, and Health Canada's crop wording `noyer de pili` is not a food-product name. Do not store the literal French compound as an active display or alias without a separate Québec language review. [WP02-B2-PILI-PULP-FLOUR-R186-20261016-S05; WP03-I-PILI-IDENTITY-20261012-S01, S05]

## Measures, density, yield, and substitutions

The 2023 UPLB abstract reports a **10.8–12.2% flour preparation yield** from its pulp samples and **0.546–0.566 g/mL bulk density** for its study flour. These are sample- and method-specific research results, not a recipe cooking yield, household cup/spoon measure, or market-wide density. The 2025 UP Diliman abstract reports **0.36 g/mL bulk density** and **0.54 g/mL tapped density** for its selected maceration-plus-manual-pressing defatted sample. The difference in product state and study result does not support a shared default density or unit mapping. [WP02-B2-PILI-NUT-FLOUR-R177-20261016-S02; WP02-B2-PILI-PULP-FLOUR-R186-20261016-S02]

No reviewed source supplies a package serving or grams per household volume for a current Canadian/Québec flour product. If the candidate is later accepted, retain the reported research numbers only as distinctly described, source-specific ledger observations; keep `defaultDensity` null and `unitMappings` empty. Do not copy any quantity from whole pili nuts to pulp flour.

The 2009 cookery report describes ten trial food products, seven using pili-pulp flour, and recommends home and small-scale consumption and food preparation. The 2023 thesis says the flour may be considered as a flour substitute or composite-flour ingredient, but neither abstract provides a portable replacement ratio. No global, contextual, or recipe-line substitution rule is supported; the bundled corpus has no recipe to link. [WP02-B2-PILI-PULP-FLOUR-R186-20261016-S05; WP02-B2-PILI-NUT-FLOUR-R177-20261016-S02]
