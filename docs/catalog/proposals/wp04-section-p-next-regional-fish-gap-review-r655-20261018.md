## Source findings and market evidence

The CFIA Fish List has the *Catostomus commersonii* (TSN 553273) rows **White Sucker** with no French label, **Common Sucker / Meunier Noir**, and **Mullet**. It separately lists Longnose Sucker / Meunier Rouge for *C. catostomus*, bare Sucker and Largescale Sucker / Meunier à grandes écailles for *C. macrocheilus*, and other sucker-family names. The list pairs names with taxa for Canadian fish labelling; it does not establish culinary equivalence among these species. [S01, reused S11]

OQLF’s GDT fiche 1199912 provides a direct Québec French and English pair: preferred **meunier noir**, English **white sucker**, and Latin *Catostomus commersonii*. It places the term in both ichthyology and edible-fish domains and calls the fish a Québec freshwater species with commercial potential. Its note does not establish present-day commercial fishing or retail stock. OQLF also lists `carpe à cochon`, `carpe noire`, and `catostome noir` as terms used in some contexts, then says these were not retained for Québec fish-product commercialization because of potential consumer-label confusion. Keep the preferred term distinct from those unretained alternatives. [S02]

Québec’s fish-consumption and health page, updated 2025-11-21, lists **Meunier noir, cru** in its omega-3 table and attributes the data to Health Canada’s Fichier canadien sur les éléments nutritifs. This is current Québec-facing human-food and nutrition evidence, not a retail SKU or proof of current commercial supply. The Québec freshwater sport-fish consumption guide also lists generic **Meunier** among fish with a four-meal monthly default when there is no site-specific recommendation; its guidance is for wild sport-caught fish and varies by site, species, and size. It supports consumer use while leaving `meunier` broad as a picker query. Do not transfer its dietary guidance or nutrient figures into catalog measures or serving defaults. [S06, S07]

Fisheries and Oceans Canada’s species profile says White Suckers are widespread across Canada and “not usually fished except for bait.” The profile was last modified in 2016, so it is dated national context, not a 2026 market survey. A current Québec bait retailer lists **Ménés de meunier noir** and describes the frozen minnows as bait for ice fishing; its listing provides no scientific identification and is not a human-food product. This retailer page is product evidence for bait only and does not override the separate Québec nutrition and consumption guidance. [S03, S04]

Scott and Crossman’s 1973 *Freshwater fishes of Canada* describes the species’ flesh as edible, discusses food preparations, and reports historical commercial catches. The same account says white suckers and other suckers were usually marketed as **mullet** and that the fish was not highly favored commercially. This establishes historical Canadian food and fishery use, but its broad market label is not a safe species-specific alias and the 1973 evidence does not establish present Québec retail supply. [S05]

A July 2019 response in Québec’s environmental assessment registry summarizes MAPAQ’s 2017–2018 commercial fishing plan for the Saint-Laurent sector between the Pont Laviolette and Île d’Orléans. Its table lists **Meunier noir / Catostomus commersonii** among the species targeted by commercial fishing in that sector. The row’s asterisk marks the species as observed during the project’s environmental characterization; the response does not report a commercial landing or sale for the species. [S08]

UQAC’s 2009 *Commercialisation du meunier noir* report documents a prefeasibility project that prioritized North American human consumption and recorded interest from fish wholesalers. The report also describes the market as a low-demand niche and concluded that fishing was not profitable under the conditions it studied. This is evidence of a Québec human-food market evaluation and limited buyer interest at that time, not a current retail product or current market measure. [S09]

Targeted product searches on 2026-10-18 for `"white sucker" "Catostomus commersonii" food fish market Canada`, `"meunier noir" poisson filet vente Québec`, and `"meunier noir" filet de poisson épicerie Québec` did not surface a verifiable current Canadian or Québec retail SKU. A separate query, `"Catostomus commersonii" edible fish Canada commercial catch`, surfaced the 1973 account below. The lack of a current SKU is a bounded search observation, not absence of human-food evidence: Québec’s current nutrition and sport-fish guidance, the 2017–2018 fishing-plan listing, and the older market report document food use, commercial-plan coverage, and historical buyer interest within their stated scopes. [R01, S02, S05, S06, S07, S08, S09]

## Exact proposed data delta

**Hold active picker admission; add no reference or ledger row now.** Keep `ingredient-ref-white-sucker` and `candidate-white-sucker` unallocated. CFIA/OQLF and the Québec food and fishery sources support a bounded species-level food identity, so this hold is not a claim that the fish lacks culinary use. Do not add **white sucker**, **meunier noir**, *Catostomus commersonii*, **common sucker**, **mullet**, or any non-retained alternate as a new reference, alias, form, or accepted decision until search behavior can preserve the broader query boundary.

The source-backed name pair identifies one fish species, but adding a row with those canonical names would make the unqualified query `sucker` return this one species because `sucker` is part of the English display. CFIA applies `Sucker` and `Mullet` to additional taxa; the Québec sport-fish guide also uses broad `Meunier` wording. The French term `meunier noir` is species-specific, but the one-word query `meunier` would return this single row under current search behavior. No recipe in the bundled corpus names this fish, so the current picker would silently resolve a broad query without a recipe line that identifies the intended species. Hold active admission until a picker change can retain multiple species or the query boundary can otherwise be preserved.

Add no form, measure, density, unit mapping, recipe link, conversion, yield, or substitution. Do not transfer bait-product state, historical food use, fish-consumption guidance, nutrition values, or dated commercial-potential evidence into the ingredient catalog as a current product, serving, or sourcing claim. Reopen if the picker can preserve broad `sucker` / `meunier` scope; bundled recipe demand would also provide an explicit species-specific use case.

## Recommendation and remaining scope

**Hold White Sucker / meunier noir outside active catalog data for now.** A bounded food identity is supported: CFIA and OQLF pair *Catostomus commersonii* with English `white sucker` and preferred Québec `meunier noir`; current Québec nutrition guidance lists `Meunier noir, cru`, while its sport-fish guide separately uses generic `Meunier`; the 2017–2018 commercial-plan response includes this species, and the 2009 market report records historical human-food interest. Those sources do not amount to a current retail SKU or present-day supply evidence. More specifically, the current picker would make the unqualified broad query `sucker` and generic Québec query `meunier` resolve to this single row if its canonical names were added. CFIA applies sucker names to several taxa, and the sport-fish guide itself uses generic `Meunier`; the bundled corpus also has zero matching recipes to clarify intended species. Keep the hold for these query-boundary and demand reasons, not because there is no human-food evidence. Reopen when picker behavior can preserve broad terms or a recipe provides an explicit species-level use case.

This hold does not allocate `mullet`, reopen any neighboring fish decision, or close Section P’s broad finfish and regional-fish item. No form, measure, density, unit mapping, recipe link, conversion, yield, or substitution is proposed.
