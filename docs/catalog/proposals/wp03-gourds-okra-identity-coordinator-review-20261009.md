# WP03 Section L coordinator review: gourds, okra, and green jackfruit

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-09
- **Status:** Proposal only. This review writes only this file; shared catalog, ledger, recipe, plan, roadmap, code, and test files were not edited by this task.
- **Scope:** Section L’s remaining third bullet: bitter melon, winter melon, bottle gourd, ridge gourd, snake gourd, chayote, okra, and green jackfruit.
- **Coordination boundary:** The active tomato/cucurbit integration is outside this review. Chayote’s appearance in the earlier summer-squash terminology source is noted only to prevent a false alias; no tomato, cucumber, zucchini, squash, pumpkin, eggplant, or pepper decision is repeated. Fungi and finfish scopes are not included.

## Recommendation

Add seven identity-only `VEGETABLE_AND_AROMATIC` candidates, using the exact IDs and provisional bilingual labels below. Keep the existing jackfruit reference as the owner of the fruit and treat green/unripe/young/tender wording as an unresolved maturity or product-form boundary, not as a second identity or base-fruit alias. The current 114-recipe bundle contains no ingredient row naming any of the eight items, so no recipe relink or recipe-specific measurement is supported.

The most important alias boundaries are species and product distinctions: winter melon (*Benincasa hispida*) and bottle gourd (*Lagenaria siceraria*) are different foods even though an Ontario government crop guide lists “Chinese Preserving Melon” for both; ridge gourd (*Luffa acutangula*) is distinct from smooth sponge gourd/loofah (*L. aegyptiaca/cylindrica*) and from okra; and “melon serpent” in Québec’s field-vegetable list refers to a *Cucumis melo* melon, not snake gourd (*Trichosanthes cucumerina*). Hold these ambiguous phrases out of single-owner aliases.

Health Canada’s 2015 CNF supplies a few source-specific raw and boiled-drained portions for bitter melon and okra. They are candidate observations only: do not derive a density or universal unit mapping, and recheck the newer CNF resource before integration. One test-kitchen source supports a narrow qualitative use of chayote in place of some summer squash; a culinary-professional article describes unripe jackfruit as a meat substitute. Neither supplies a suitable catalog ratio. No forms, measurements, conversions, or substitutions are activated by this proposal.
