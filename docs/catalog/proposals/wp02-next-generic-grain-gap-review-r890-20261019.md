## Recommendation and identity boundary

Add generic `foxtail millet bran` as the bran fraction obtained from foxtail millet (*Setaria italica*) during grain polishing or milling. A 2024 primary study separately collected husk, bran, and millet fractions from ten foxtail-millet cultivars; its discussion identifies bran as a potentially useful food resource. A separate bakery study prepared a foxtail-millet bran-rich fraction and incorporated it into buns. These sources support a distinct grain fraction and a documented human-food research use, adding species-level milling diversity beyond existing generic bran references. They do not establish that foxtail bran is commonly retailed or used in everyday recipes. [R890-S01–S02]

Keep the row narrowly scoped to foxtail-millet bran. Distinguish it from:

- foxtail millet grain and foxtail millet flour;
- proso millet bran and other grain-specific brans, including oat, rice, rye, barley, corn, sorghum, triticale, buckwheat, spelt, and wheat bran;
- the outer husk and intact millet kernel;
- a bran-rich fraction containing bran plus broken grain and husk;
- any flour, extract, oil, fiber isolate, or laboratory-prepared bran powder.

The bakery study’s material was a **bran-rich fraction** made from true bran, broken grain, and husk. Its foxtail inclusion levels, bun formula, sensory scores, shelf life, nutritional findings, and outcomes apply to those study samples and recipes only. They do not define pure bran, a generic recipe proportion, a household measure, a substitution, or a nutritional claim. The 2022 bran-powder study reports that particle-size processing changes laboratory properties, and notes that the material remains underused. Neither study supports a standard grade, density, yield, or universal milling boundary. [R890-S02–S03]

The OQLF lists `millet d’Italie` as a preferred French term for *Setaria italica*, alongside `millet des oiseaux`. Since the latter also carries an explicit bird-feed association and none of the reviewed Québec sources attests an exact human-food bran compound, propose `son de millet d’Italie` only as a transparent, provisional descriptive display. Do not add `millet d’Italie`, `millet des oiseaux`, `son`, `bran`, `Setaria italica`, or study terms as aliases. [WP03-S175; R890-S04]

## Limitations and next review boundary

1. The generic identity has primary milling-fraction evidence and a documented research use in a bran-rich baked-food fraction, but there is no evidence here of broad retail availability or commonplace culinary use.
2. The bun study’s fraction includes husk and broken grain as well as bran. Its formula or outcomes cannot be transferred to pure foxtail-millet bran.
3. The proposed French display is descriptive and provisional; OQLF supports the source-grain name, not the exact bran compound.
4. The studies do not support a generic particle grade, density, unit mapping, serving amount, milling yield, package relationship, cooking yield, substitution, or health claim.
5. Further brand-, product-line-, flavour-family-, SKU-, or retailer-variant-only work remains out of scope because commercial-product counts do not add ingredient diversity. Preserve existing branded rows and product-scoped evidence; require an explicit transferability review before applying any product-specific claim to a generic identity.
