## Product boundary

Fresh and dried describe different finished pasta states; neither is a shape. OQLF's general pasta entry defines the food as shaped semolina/flour dough with water or eggs, notes that shapes vary, and identifies fresh and dried pasta as types within that food class. It says dried pasta takes longer to cook and lasts longer; drying lowers moisture to about 12%, leaving pasta rigid and brittle. Its fresh-pasta entry says fresh pasta cooks faster and has a shorter storage life. These are real culinary and storage differences, but the source does not give one universal cook time, shelf life, or weight conversion.

The state is distinct from ingredients and dietary composition:

- **Egg pasta:** Canadian regulation separately names egg alimentary paste and requires at least 4% egg-yolk solids on a dry basis for that product designation. Current Canadian product examples include refrigerated fresh egg pasta and dried egg pasta. Egg content therefore cannot be inferred from a dried/fresh state. Conversely, OQLF's Québec `pâte fraîche` entry defines its common concept with flour and eggs, while CFIA guidance permits `fresh pasta` as a label distinction from dehydrated pasta subject to specified preservation treatments, and CFIA import categories include fresh wheat pasta not containing eggs. The Québec term and the product-state classification do not line up as a simple composition-neutral alias.
- **Whole-wheat and gluten-free products:** These are composition distinctions. Catelli's current whole-grain spaghetti lists whole-grain durum-wheat semolina; Barilla's Canadian gluten-free spaghetti is made with corn and rice flour. A shape can be sold with different grain composition. Do not make whole-wheat or gluten-free a fresh/dry state, and do not infer either from generic pasta or a shape.
- **Named shapes:** Spaghetti and the other shape references remain product identities scoped to shape. Fresh and dried products can use different shapes; a state term does not rename spaghetti, macaroni, or another shape.
- **Cooking state:** Fresh/dried is not the same as cooked/uncooked. Do not infer cooked-form measures or recipe yields from a dried or fresh package label.

## Why forms remain on hold

The evidence would support treating fresh and dried as distinct *product states* in a future catalog model. However, activating them now under the broad generic row would leave two material scope questions unresolved:

1. `ingredient-ref-pasta` is not explicitly wheat-only. CFIA's current licensing categories list pasta as fresh, frozen, or dried; its 2024 import-code update separately classifies those states across wheat, corn, rice, and other-grain products, including non-egg products. A pair of forms on generic pasta would therefore read more broadly than the plan's wheat-pasta subtask unless the row's scope were clarified.
2. The form and recipe-line models associate one `ingredientFormId` with one `ingredientRefId`; forms are looked up within that identity. The active egg-pasta and shape identities do not inherit generic pasta forms. A fresh egg-pasta line or fresh named shape cannot carry a generic-pasta state form while preserving its more specific identity. Duplicating the same fresh/dried search phrases across those identities could create exact-key ambiguity. The OQLF fresh-pasta definition also makes the Québec display term composition-sensitive, while CFIA classifications show that fresh pasta is not necessarily egg-containing.

With no matching recipe line, there is no concrete identity/state pair that resolves these scope questions. Keep this as a research hold. Do not add separate fresh-pasta or dried-pasta ingredient identities. Reopen the form decision when a recipe line needs a specific product, or after a Québec terminology review defines how the `pâtes fraîches` label should behave beside the already active egg-pasta identity and the wheat/whole-wheat/gluten-free reviews. A future form proposal must keep state terms on the exact identity they describe and must not imply wheat, eggs, whole wheat, gluten-free status, shape, or cooked yield without its own evidence.

## Measurement and substitution evidence

Health Canada's Canadian Nutrient File lists one dry whole-wheat elbow macaroni food code with source-specific measures: 100 mL = 44.4 g, 250 mL = 110.9 g, and 26.9 g of dry product to make 125 mL. This is a food-code-specific portion record for **dry whole-wheat elbows** and a stated preparation amount; it does not establish a bulk density for generic pasta, a fresh-pasta measure, or a dry-to-fresh ratio. The manufacturer pages give product-specific package sizes and cooking instructions, not paired state measurements: Olivieri's fresh angel hair is a 300 g refrigerated product with a listed 30-second cook time; Filotea's dried egg tagliatelle is a 250 g product whose Québec distributor reports 30% egg and slow drying. These are different shapes and formulations; they cannot be used as a direct paired comparison.

No current source reviewed here provides a general grams-per-cup value for fresh pasta, a shared mass/volume basis across fresh and dried pasta, a cooking-yield factor between the two states, or a universal substitution ratio. OQLF's relative cooking-time and storage-life notes and the manufacturer package instructions do not authorize an amount conversion. No generic fresh-to-dried or dried-to-fresh substitution rule is supported. Keep all reference/form densities, unit mappings, package yields, conversion rules, and substitutions empty.
