# WP05 Section S — residual oil identities and product boundaries

- **Review date:** 2026-10-12
- **Disposition:** proposal only; one narrowly named candidate is newly supported, other reviewed boundaries stay held or covered
- **Scope:** sesame-oil roast wording, deodorized/refined coconut oil, red palm oil, and remaining named-oil candidates in the Section S plan
- **Data changes:** none; no canonical JSON, ledger, checklist, workflow, or roadmap file was edited

## Recommendation

Propose one identity-only addition for **deodorized coconut oil / huile de noix de coco désodorisée**, in category `FAT_AND_OIL`, with the French search alias `huile de coco désodorisée`. Use no English aliases. Current bilingual pages from Montréal-based Cha’s Organics name the ingredient as organic deodorized coconut oil in both languages, distinguish it from virgin coconut oil, and explicitly identify cooking and baking use. This independent product evidence narrows the previous hold, which relied in part on a conflicting bilingual Maison Orphée ingredient declaration. The existing unqualified coconut-oil recipe line remains on generic `coconut oil`; it does not request the deodorized product. [S04–S06]

Do not promote `refined coconut oil`, `refined and deodorized coconut oil`, `RBD coconut oil`, `neutral coconut oil`, `copra oil`, `cold-pressed coconut oil`, `unrefined coconut oil`, or `virgin coconut oil` as aliases of this candidate. One Canadian brand describes its own product as refined and deodorized, but that does not establish a general equivalence between the separate words `refined` and `deodorized`. The OQLF lists `huile de coco`, `huile de noix de coco`, and `huile de coprah` as broad coconut-oil terms, without grade or process distinctions. Keep active `virgin coconut oil` separate, and keep unqualified `coconut oil` on its generic owner. [S04–S06]

For sesame oil, preserve the current split: unqualified `sesame oil / huile de sésame` belongs to `ingredient-ref-sesame-oil`; explicit `toasted sesame oil / huile de sésame grillé` belongs to `ingredient-ref-toasted-sesame-oil`. Do not interpret the generic row as untoasted, and do not attach `untoasted`, `unroasted`, `raw`, or `virgin` as aliases. A Québec producer sells separate virgin sesame and toasted sesame products, including a product named “Virgin Toasted Sesame Oil”; this confirms that `virgin` and `toasted` are independent product qualifiers. A Japanese producer describes raw-seed and roasted-seed sesame-oil products, but its product names do not establish a universal bilingual or Québec taxonomy. No bundled recipe names untoasted, unroasted, raw, or virgin sesame oil. [S01–S03]

Keep the WP06 red-palm decision unchanged. The active crude-palm-oil identity already preserves fruit-pulp origin and a crude qualifier. OQLF notes that natural palm oil is reddish or orange; CFIA has sampled imported products labelled red palm oil. Neither source equates every red palm oil to the active crude product or resolves whether a given label names a product, colour, or process state. Add no `red palm oil`, `red oil`, `huile de palme rouge`, `zomi`, `dzomi`, or `amidze` identity or global alias. No bundled recipe uses these phrases. [S07–S10]

No additional oil identity is proposed from the remaining plan candidates. Mustard, groundnut/peanut, extra-virgin olive, grapeseed, avocado, walnut, hazelnut, pumpkin-seed, safflower, rice-bran, palm-kernel, soybean, sunflower, corn, canola, generic vegetable, generic sesame, toasted sesame, virgin coconut, crude palm, garlic-infused, and the existing chili-oil products already have active owners. Continue to hold toasted pumpkin-seed oil, high-oleic safflower oil, palm olein/stearin and palm-kernel fractions as separate named products without recipe demand or an established need for new rows. Do not restore generic `oil`, `huile`, or neutral/mixed-oil aliases. [S09–S11]

Add no recipe links, forms, measurements, density, unit mappings, conversions, yields, or substitutions. The plan’s remaining oil/fat-state items—solid, melted, whipped, and liquid states; oil versus emulsified spread; and clarified versus water-containing fat—remain separate open work.

## Limitations and next review boundary

- The proposed deodorized-coconut identity is supported by product-market evidence, not by a dedicated OQLF grade entry or a Codex named-oil standard. Québec product usage supplies the French ingredient wording; the OQLF entry supports the coconut-oil base term only.
- Retail/manufacturer labels may use `deodorized`, `refined`, and `neutral` differently. This proposal accepts only the narrower term directly stated as the product ingredient in a current bilingual Canadian source.
- The sesame sources demonstrate product variants, but no source establishes a universal `untoasted sesame oil` identity or a preferred Québec French phrase for it. No recipe line asks for one.
- The CFIA red-palm survey confirms Canadian retail use of the name, but not a stable crosswalk to crude palm oil or a French culinary display. Preserve WP06’s product-local decision until a recipe supplies its source and exact product context.
- This proposal does not close Section S. Solid/melted/liquid/whipped states, emulsified spreads, clarified fats, cooking sprays, and any future source-qualified oil demand remain outside its recommendations.

No tests or emulator checks were run for this proposal-only review.
