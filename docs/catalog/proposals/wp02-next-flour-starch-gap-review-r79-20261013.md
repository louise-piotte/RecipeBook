# WP02 B2 watermelon-seed flour review — R79

## Review record

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-13.
- **Status:** Proposal only. The only authorized write is this proposal; no catalog, form, ledger, checklist, roadmap, generated, or application file was edited.
- **Open checklist item:** Section 04, B2, line 1832 of the [global ingredient catalog plan](../../RecipeBook-global-ingredient-catalog-plan.txt#L1832): “[ ] Almond flour versus almond meal; hazelnut, coconut, chestnut, peanut, chickpea/besan, lentil, pea, soy, and lupin flours.” Watermelon-seed flour is an additional distinct seed-derived flour gap within that open alternative-flour coverage.
- **Candidate:** A food flour made by milling watermelon seeds. Keep it distinct from watermelon fruit, dried intact watermelon kernels, egusi seeds, and separately defatted seed press-cake/pomace flour.
- **Overlap audit:** R77/R78 exclusions (Tacca/pia and breadfruit starch) are outside this review. The earlier watermelon seed flour mentions found in WP02/WP03 papers are research references, not an earlier identity decision. No dedicated active row, form, ledger candidate, or proposal/batch decision for watermelon-seed flour was found. Existing dried watermelon-kernel and egusi-seed decisions remain in force.
- **Proposal-local IDs:** Prefix WP02-B2-WATERMELON-SEED-FLOUR-R79-20261013. Prefix and proposed reference ID were unused in the catalog, forms, ledger, legacy identities, and dedicated B2/B3 archive at review.

## Recommendation

**Integrate one identity-only base row for watermelon seed flour**, with the provisional descriptive French display **farine de graines de melon d’eau**. Use category FLOUR_AND_STARCH; leave aliases empty, default density null, and unit mappings empty. Add no ingredient form, recipe link, conversion, yield, or substitution.

The product identity is supported across distinct supplier and maker sources: Tradin lists milled watermelon-seed flour as a product separate from whole kernels and defatted protein powder; Beyond the Equator names a single-ingredient flour made by milling watermelon seeds; Simple Mills describes its own seed flour as cold-milled from a seed-grown watermelon varietal. These sources support a useful base identity for flour made by milling watermelon seeds. Cultivar, grind, and brand details remain source-specific; do not add “Wooly Watermelon” as a global alias or species claim. [S02–S04]

Keep defatted watermelon-seed flour or pomace flour outside that base row and unresolved as a distinct ledger-only research candidate. Studies distinguish whole-seed flour from defatted/pomace material, but the reviewed evidence does not establish a standalone Canadian/Québec defatted-flour product or a stable picker-facing scope. [S05, S09] The base row is not a claim that every product containing watermelon seed flour has the same fat content, grind, or process.

I see no material reason to hold the base identity: process-qualified commercial descriptions agree on the core output, milled watermelon seed. Research variation and unknown measures do not erase that identity; they limit forms and numeric data. The Canadian evidence is narrower: a current iHerb Canada finished snack names watermelon seed flour as one component in a seed-and-nut flour blend, and Simple Mills says some products are sold to Canadian customers through retailers that ship to Canada. Neither establishes a standalone Canadian flour package. [S04, S08]

## Québec French and Canadian scope

OQLF’s food GDT entry pairs English watermelon with the preferred Québec French terms melon d’eau and pastèque. It does not name watermelon seeds or a flour product. The proposed display, farine de graines de melon d’eau, is a transparent descriptive compound built from the OQLF-supported Québec base term and the ordinary French word graines; it is **provisional**, not an OQLF ruling or an attested Québec package label. Keep aliasesFr empty, including for the compound with pastèque, until local product wording or independent Québec terminology review supports it. This is a bounded display proposal, not a claim that the exact compound is already established market wording. [S01]

Simple Mills’ official FAQ says some products are available to Canadian customers through online retailers that ship to Canada, and its product-specific answer describes cold-milling its seed flour. The iHerb Canada page for Simple Mills Sweet Thins lists a seed-and-nut flour blend containing watermelon, cashew, sunflower, and flax. This is Canadian-facing finished-product evidence for the ingredient phrase, not evidence of a standalone Canadian flour SKU, a bilingual flour label, or Québec retail availability. [S04, S08]

## Numeric evidence and substitution limits

One research report by Adeleke et al. states a bulk density of 0.146 g/mL for its watermelon-seed flour sample. The abstract says the functional-property measurements were made using standard analytical methods and reports the density; the accessible methods text describes seed removal, washing, sun-drying, and grinding. However, the bulk-density method paragraph says the 10 mL tube was filled with “10g of cashew kernel flour,” despite the paper’s result table/abstract referring to watermelon-seed flour. Treat 0.146 g/mL as a sample-specific, internally inconsistent observation only. Do not activate it as defaultDensity or use it for a grams-per-cup mapping. [S10]

Other numerical results are also not household conversions:

- Akusu and Kiin-Kabari report water absorption of 0.96 g/g and oil absorption of 1.13 g/g for their particular pre-boiled, dried, dehulled, milled sample. These are functional absorption ratios, not bulk density. Their “up to 50%” replacement result is confined to one egusi soup formula. [S07]
- Wójcik et al. test 0%, 5%, 10%, and 15% in one low-carbohydrate, high-protein bread formulation and recommend 5% for that formulation only. This is not a general flour replacement ratio. [S06]
- Mello et al. test whole-seed and pomace/defatted flour in experimental food formulations; these results do not establish general recipe measures or product equivalence. [S05]
- Beyond the Equator advertises replacement percentages for its own flour. Treat that as brand-specific marketing, not a generally supported contextual substitution. [S03]

No source establishes a transferable extraction yield, default density, mass-per-volume conversion, standard measure, or broad substitution against wheat, almond, sunflower, egusi, or another flour. For the proposed base row, leave defaultDensity null, unitMappings empty, and forms/conversions/yields/substitutions absent.
