# WP02 B2 R179 — Tarwi (Andean lupin) flour boundary review

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-16.
- **Status:** Proposal only. This is the sole file changed; no shared catalog, ledger, plan, roadmap, generated, code, or batch file was edited.
- **Plan fit:** A species-qualified candidate within the still-open WP02 Section 04 B2 item, “Almond flour versus almond meal; hazelnut, coconut, chestnut, peanut, chickpea/besan, lentil, pea, soy, and lupin flours.” Existing sweet-lupin references cover *Lupinus albus* and *L. angustifolius*; this review considers flour from Andean lupin, *L. mutabilis*, sold in Ecuador as chocho and called tarwi in Peru/Bolivia.
- **Proposal-local source IDs:** `WP02-B2-TARWI-FLOUR-R179-20261016-S01` through `S04`. The existing whole-lupin CFIA source is cited by its ledger ID and is not reallocated here.

## Recommendation

**Hold a separate *Lupinus mutabilis* flour identity and all aliases pending a current Canadian consumer product and product-matched Québec French name.** The flour is a real, process-sensitive food ingredient: a current producer describes a debittered, lightly defatted whole-seed flour, while research examines other flour preparations and ecotypes. A current Canadian trademark goods list includes “tarwi flour,” and Health Canada confirms that lupin flour occurs in some Canadian products generally. Neither establishes an available Canadian *L. mutabilis* flour product. No Québec retail name or OQLF flour term for tarwi/Andean lupin was found. Do not assign “tarwi flour,” “chocho flour,” “Andean lupin flour,” or constructed French compounds to the existing *L. albus* or *L. angustifolius* rows. [S01–S04; WP03-H-REGIONAL-PULSES-20261011-S27A]

Reopen if a Canadian/Québec food product identifies *L. mutabilis* flour and its debittering/oil-removal scope, with French wording for that same product. The Canadian trademark application is opposed and its goods statement is not evidence of actual sale. The producer is Ecuadorian and invites bulk sample requests; it does not show Canadian availability. Keep the candidate outside active references, forms, aliases, and the ledger in this proposal.

## Product, species, and naming boundary

The producer identifies chocho as *L. mutabilis* (Andean lupin) and distinguishes its CF-52 flour from two concentrates. Its CF-52 is described as debittered, lightly defatted flour from whole seed; the concentrates have more extensive oil/fibre removal. A 2026 primary study separately characterizes flour from three *L. mutabilis* ecotypes. These sources establish a meaningful species and process boundary, not a universal “lupin flour” product class or a standardized set of catalog forms. [S01–S02]

The active catalog has `ingredient-ref-sweet-lupin-flour` for *L. albus*, displayed `sweet lupin flour / farine de lupin blanc`, and `ingredient-ref-sweet-blue-lupin-flour` for *L. angustifolius*, displayed `sweet blue lupin flour / farine de lupin à feuilles étroites`. Their aliases are empty. The whole-seed `ingredient-ref-white-lupin-seeds` is also species-scoped. CFIA’s packaged organic grain-code list records *L. mutabilis* as a distinct seed species (existing source `WP03-H-REGIONAL-PULSES-20261011-S27A`); it does not identify a flour product. Santé Canada’s reviewed flour list names the other two species, not *L. mutabilis*. [S03; existing lupin ledger sources]

The Canadian trademark term `tarwi flour` is applicant-filed product-category wording, not an approved bilingual label. The producer’s “Chocho Flour” name and species description are Ecuadorian supplier evidence, not a Québec name. OQLF’s existing lupin entries concern *L. albus* and *L. angustifolius*; targeted searches found no *L. mutabilis*, tarwi, or chocho flour entry. Do not promote `farine de lupin andin`, `farine de tarwi`, or `farine de chocho` from descriptive constructions to display names or aliases without local naming evidence. [S03–S04]

## Numeric and substitution boundaries

The supplier’s CF-52 specification reports, for its current tested lot, 52% protein, 23% fat, 18% dietary fibre, and 4.2% moisture; its concentrates have different, partly target rather than observed specifications. The 2026 study uses 125 μm samples from three ecotypes and reports compositional, particle, thermal, and structural measurements. These are product- and study-specific facts, not bulk density, a household cup/spoon weight, a universal flour composition, or a conversion. Do not add forms, measures, density, unit mappings, package yields, or kitchen conversions.

Published baking blend percentages and product applications are formulation-specific; they do not establish a transferable replacement ratio for wheat, almond, or other lupin flours. Do not add a global or contextual substitution, recipe link, or yield. The supplier distinguishes flour from concentrates, and no observed formulation licenses treating those categories as interchangeable. [S01–S02]

## Reopen criteria

Reconsider after a current Canadian or Québec product source identifies *L. mutabilis* flour for culinary use, specifies whether it is whole-seed, debittered, and/or defatted, and supplies or supports a matching Québec French name. If accepted, keep it separate from white- and blue-lupin flours, whole lupin seeds, concentrates, and protein isolates. Research measures and substitutions independently against the actual consumer product.
