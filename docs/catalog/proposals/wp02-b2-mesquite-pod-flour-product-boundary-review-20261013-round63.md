## Recommendation

**Keep mesquite pod flour as an unallocated B2 candidate pending a source-matched Québec display and product scope. Do not create an active ingredient row or a ledger candidate from this proposal alone.**

Food sources document flour made from mesquite pods, but the source material varies: Salt River Pima-Maricopa community reporting describes a hammer mill pulverizing pods and seeds; USDA ARS describes flour from pod mesocarp; and a 2026 study specifically names flour from *Prosopis laevigata* pods. These establish a real food product family without establishing one universal pod fraction, species, or milling specification. [S01–S04]

Québec-facing French evidence is relevant but does not resolve the proposed ingredient display. A Montréal-based seller uses `poudre de mesquite biologique` for a single-ingredient food product, and a Canadian celiac/dietitian guide uses `farine de mesquite`. The Québec product page does not specify whether its powder is the same whole-pod or mesocarp flour described in the other sources. TERMIUM records `mesquite` as the tree and its wood-smoking use, and a spice seller uses the exact English title `Mesquite Flour` for maltodextrin plus natural mesquite smoke. The bare English phrase therefore has a documented non-pod product collision. [S05–S09]

Do not use the regional names `cu’i` or `mvar` as global aliases. The community article attributes them to O’odham and Piipaash, respectively; this review does not establish community approval for catalog-wide spelling, scope, or use. Do not map `algarrobo flour` across South American products or species. [S01, S03]

If a future source-matched row is accepted, a conservative working pair would be English `mesquite pod flour` and provisional fr-CA `farine de gousses de mesquite`, with empty aliases. The French compound is a transparent construction, not an attested Québec flour label or OQLF ruling. Keep `mesquite flour`, `mesquite powder`, `farine de mesquite`, and `poudre de mesquite` unassigned until a reviewer establishes which ones consistently identify the same edible pod product rather than smoke flavor or a differently processed powder.

## Root integration outcome

Confirmed the hold. Community, USDA, and species-specific studies establish mesquite pod flour as a real food product family, but the reviewed sources do not settle one species/process scope or a process-matched Québec French display. The Montréal `poudre de mesquite` label does not establish equivalence with whole-pod or mesocarp flour, and the English `Mesquite Flour` smoke-flavor collision makes a broad English alias unsafe. The candidate remains unallocated; no active reference, ledger candidate, alias, form, measure, recipe link, conversion, yield, or substitution was added. The WP02 B2 alternative-flour checklist remains open.
