# WP08: Cultured buttermilk to acidified milk in pancakes and quick breads

**Review date:** 2026-10-10  
**Disposition:** No runtime activation. Preserve as a narrow, source-supported research candidate; do not treat acidified milk as buttermilk identity or as a general dairy substitution.

## Recommendation

Extension guidance supports a volume preparation of **1 tablespoon lemon juice or vinegar, then enough milk to make 1 cup; let stand 5 minutes**. King Arthur Baking also reports direct tests of milk-plus-vinegar in pancake and biscuit recipes. Those tests support only a contextual substitute: the biscuits rose and browned with similar texture but had less depth of flavor; pancake texture and browning were similar, while buttermilk retained a flavor advantage.

The bundled corpus has two candidate dough uses within the requested scope: a chemically leavened pancake batter and a chemically leavened shortcake biscuit dough. Research therefore supports a future ledger finding for this direction and these contexts. **Do not activate a rule in the current runtime.** The replacement is a prepared two-component mixture (milk plus one of two alternative acids), while the current contextual-rule DTO stores one target ingredient and one scalar quantity transformation. Its dish-type filters also cannot isolate pancakes and biscuit/quick-bread dough with the current tags. A single-target suggestion would omit a required component or overstate its scope.

## Identity and active-rule audit

- The active reference is `ingredient-ref-buttermilk` (`buttermilk / babeurre`, French alias `lait de beurre`). Separate active references exist for `milk / lait`, `lemon juice / jus de citron`, and generic and white vinegar. There is no `acidified milk` identity or form. Keep the mixture as preparation guidance, not an alias for cultured buttermilk.
- OQLF describes consumer-market babeurre as milk coagulated by specific lactic bacteria. This supports keeping the cultured product identity distinct from milk acidified directly with lemon juice or vinegar; that distinction is an identity inference, not an equivalence claim. See [OQLF, “babeurre”](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/2073055/babeurre).
- At this repository snapshot, `substitution-rules.v1.json` and `contextual-substitution-rules.v1.json` each contain zero active rules. The plan's original inventory of 16 contextual rules is a historical baseline, not the current file count; neither current file contains a buttermilk rule.
- The ledger entry `ingredient-ref-buttermilk` has no sources and is `not_yet_researched` for substitutions. It contains no acidified-milk finding. The existing `ingredient-ref-baking-soda` finding is the opposite direction (baking soda to baking powder), so it does not support this candidate. `docs/catalog/batches/baking-substitutions.md` discusses USU's baking-powder replacement with soda and soured milk; that is also a different direction and is retained there as a research lead, not evidence for a generalized rule.
- The existing WP08 proposal [`wp08-wheat-flour-contextual-substitution-review-20261010.md`](wp08-wheat-flour-contextual-substitution-review-20261010.md) addresses flour only and has no buttermilk evidence. No prior buttermilk/acidified-milk proposal was found.
- The current `ContextualSubstitutionRule` has one `fromIngredientRefId`, one `toIngredientRefId`, and a scalar ratio/offset. It cannot encode milk plus acid as two replacement components or preserve a source/target volume basis. The resolver only accepts same-unit ratio rules, requires a matching `DISH_TYPE` tag, and rejects role and method filters. The pancake recipe is tagged with breakfast/brunch (`MEAL`) and batter (`USE_CASE`), not a pancake dish type; the biscuit recipe carries dessert/cake tags, not biscuit or quick-bread type. Using `cake` or `dessert` would be too broad, and no existing dish-type tags isolate both uses.

|---|---|---|

| S2 | [University of Illinois Extension, “Recipe Substitutions”](https://extension.illinois.edu/food/recipe-substitutions), gives the same 1-cup final-volume formula and 5-minute rest under milk substitutions for cooking and baking. | General guidance, not a formula-specific test. |

Cooperative Extension, “Baking Substitutions That Work”](https://randolph.ces.ncsu.edu/news/baking-substitutions-that-work/), gives 1 tablespoon acid plus milk to one cup and a 5-minute rest; it says the acid reacts with baking soda and names pancakes and cakes. | General baking advice; it does not provide a quick-bread comparison or results for each formula. |
| S4 | [King Arthur Baking, “Buttermilk substitute: What to use”](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2019/01/28/buttermilk-substitute), describes 1 tablespoon vinegar or lemon juice mixed into 1 cup milk and a 10–15-minute rest. It reports tests in Baking Powder Biscuits and Buttermilk Pancakes: the vinegar mixture produced biscuits with similar texture/rise/browning but less flavor depth; pancake texture/browning were similar, with plain milk rising less and cultured buttermilk retaining a flavor advantage. | A culinary test-kitchen report, not an independent multi-brand or replicated study. Its preparation says “1 cup milk” rather than enough milk to a 1-cup final volume, and its rest time differs from the extension formula. Use S1–S3 for the exact final-volume formula; use S4 for contextual effects and limits. |
| S5 | [OQLF, “babeurre”](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/2073055/babeurre), describes marketed babeurre as milk coagulated by specific lactic bacteria. | Supports product identity only; it provides no substitution ratio or baking evidence. |

These sources are sufficient to retain a **narrow research finding**, but not to promise identical taste, texture, or behavior in arbitrary recipes. The exact amount is a volume formula, not a mass ratio. The sources do not specify vinegar acidity, compare milk fat levels in a controlled way, or establish that the acidified mixture is a cultured dairy product. For recipe `038`, scaling the one-cup formula arithmetically to 1 1/4 cups would call for 1 1/4 tablespoons acid and milk to 1 1/4 cups; that scaled quantity is derived, not separately tested. Do not encode it as a tested ratio. Recipe `049` states 240 ml rather than cups, so retain the source formula's unit basis and do not silently resolve cup conventions in a runtime rule.

## Candidate boundary for future ledger review

- **Direction:** `ingredient-ref-buttermilk` to freshly prepared acidified milk, made from `ingredient-ref-milk` plus either `ingredient-ref-lemon-juice` or `ingredient-ref-vinegar`. This is directional and context-limited; do not infer an inverse or transitive rule.
- **Preparation, source basis:** per 1 cup of requested buttermilk, measure 1 tablespoon lemon juice or vinegar, add milk to a final volume of 1 cup, and let stand 5 minutes (S1–S3). Preserve this as two complete preparation components, with lemon juice and vinegar as alternatives, rather than representing only milk.
- **Context:** chemically leavened pancake batter and biscuit/quick-bread dough, with the tested result qualified as recipe-dependent and flavor-changing. There are two in-scope recipe rows; the direct tested context is pancakes and biscuits as reported by S4, not the exact two bundled formulas.
- **Effects and caution:** source tests report a flavor difference from cultured buttermilk. Do not alter baking-soda or baking-powder quantities based on this finding; the sources describe adding acidified milk as a buttermilk substitute, not a universal leavener reformulation.
- **Explicit exclusions:** sauces, dressings, dips, soups, beverages, cultured dairy production, waffles, cakes, and baking generally. No substitute is proposed for sour cream, crème fraîche, yogurt, milk used on its own, or the optional cream/buttermilk biscuit-top wash.
- **Activation gate:** no runtime rule until the model can express both replacement components and their volume basis, and scope tags can distinguish pancake and quick-bread/biscuit dough without matching broad cake or dessert contexts. Keep the current gap and do not edit shared catalog data from this proposal.
