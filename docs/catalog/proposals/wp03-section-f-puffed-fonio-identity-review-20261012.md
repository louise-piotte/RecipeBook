## Candidate identity and disposition

Current sources establish that puffed fonio exists as a named product class:

- Togo's Ministry of Agriculture lists fonio soufflé among ready-to-consume fonio products, separately from precooked, blanched, dehulled, parboiled fonio and fonio flours.
- US ingredient supplier Grainwell currently offers a page titled “Puffed Fonio”; its 2025 article describes its own process as expanding fonio grains with heat and pressure.
- A 2012 Canadian trademark journal goods list includes “processed fonio puffs” and the French “fonio soufflé transformé,” but labels the use in Canada as proposed. It is terminology evidence, not a current Canadian package or proof of use.
- A 2024 Benin catalogue uses FONIO SOUFFLÉ as the name of a granola containing whole fonio, tamarind/date purée, sesame, and coconut oil. That is a composite food, not evidence that every product called fonio soufflé is plain puffed fonio.

The product scope is therefore not precise enough to activate a new identity. The reviewed pages do not establish whether the named puffed product is made from white fonio, black fonio, a specific cultivar, whole kernels, or a single ingredient. They also do not provide a current Canadian retail package that would settle its French display. Keep the candidate separate from active white/black fonio, their flours, precooked or parboiled fonio, and fonio flakes.

The OQLF entry for fonio blanc lists fonio as a preferred French term for white fonio, alongside fonio blanc, acha, fundi, and findi. Togo's official plan separately identifies white fonio (Digitaria exilis) and black fonio (Digitaria iburua), but its ready-to-consume list does not say which species is puffed. Do not use white fonio, black fonio, acha, fundi, or findi as aliases for this candidate.

## Product boundaries

If later accepted, scope the identity to a food product sold as puffed fonio. Do not infer a universal puffing method, species, cultivar, or ingredient list from the current evidence.

- Keep raw white fonio and black fonio in their existing species-qualified identities.
- Keep fonio flour and black fonio flour in their existing milling-product identities.
- Keep precooked, blanched, dehulled, and parboiled fonio separate; the Togolese plan lists these as distinct ready-to-consume products.
- Keep fonio flakes / flocons de fonio separate. The Canadian goods list names processed fonio flakes independently of processed fonio puffs.
- Exclude granola, cereal blends, bars, seasoned snack mixes, and extruded or flour-based crisps. Poulho's “Fonio soufflé” is one such composite granola, with other ingredients explicitly listed.
- Do not equate a puffed grain with a cooked or precooked fonio preparation.

The distinction between a plain puffed grain and a snack or cereal blend is useful, but current evidence does not prove that the Grainwell product listing is a plain single-ingredient package. A product label or specification is still needed to finalize that boundary.

The domain matcher in IngredientSearch.kt folds case and accents, normalizes punctuation, prefers complete word-phrase matches, and uses substring fallback only when no phrase match exists. Form terms participate in that index; this candidate has no form terms.
