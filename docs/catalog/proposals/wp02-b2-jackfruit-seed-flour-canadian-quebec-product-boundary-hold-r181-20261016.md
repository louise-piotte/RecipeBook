# WP02 B2 R181 — jackfruit seed flour Canadian/Québec product-boundary hold

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-16.
- **Status:** Proposal only. This is the sole file changed; no shared catalog, ledger, plan, roadmap, generated file, code, or batch record was edited.
- **Plan fit:** One seed-derived flour candidate within the still-open WP02 Section 04 B2 alternative-flour checklist. The candidate is flour or fine powder made from *Artocarpus heterophyllus* seeds. The open B2 parent is broad rather than naming jackfruit seed flour verbatim; the WP03 jackfruit review explicitly keeps seed flour separate from jackfruit-bulb flour, while B3 R138 reviewed extracted seed starch only.
- **Proposal-local source-ID prefix:** `WP02-B2-JACKFRUIT-SEED-FLOUR-R181-20261016-S`. The prefix is unused. Existing OQLF, Canadian jackfruit-flour, and seed/starch-boundary evidence is cited by its prior proposal-local IDs where relevant; no source ID or ledger record is reassigned.

## Recommendation

**Hold a separate jackfruit seed-flour identity and all aliases pending a product-matched Québec French name and a clearer Canadian food-product scope.** Current Canadian-facing evidence shows a 100 g jackfruit seed powder listing; its description says the finely ground dried-seed product can be mixed with other flours and used in baked recipes. This establishes a seed-derived culinary powder product, but the sales name is `Jackfruit Seed Powder`, not flour, the listing does not identify seed preparation beyond drying, and it does not supply Québec French wording. Primary studies show that flour preparations can differ by roasting, blanching, soaking/cooking, seed-coat retention, drying, and milling. The Québec-facing evidence supports `jaque` / `jacque` for the fruit and `jaquier` / `jacquier` for the tree; a Canadian retailer’s `farine de jacquier` names unripe jackfruit flour without identifying seeds. It cannot support a seed-flour alias. [S01–S04; R138-S02, R138-S04; WP03-S02–S03, WP03-S10]

Reopen if a current Canadian or Québec seed-flour package or supplier specification distinguishes seed powder/flour from fruit-bulb flour, identifies the process scope, and supports a French display for that same seed product. Keep seed flour separate from jackfruit-bulb flour, whole jackfruit, cooked/roasted whole seeds, and starch isolated from seeds. No active reference, alias, form, shared ledger record, recipe link, measurement, conversion, yield, or substitution is proposed.

## Numeric, process, form, and substitution boundaries

- **Serving/measurement:** Kerala Naturals lists a 20 g serving on its seed-powder page. That is a product-specific mass serving with no accompanying volume; do not turn it into a cup/spoon mapping, generic density, or reusable recipe amount. The Canada-facing reseller lists the 100 g product, but no confirmed Canadian package panel or Québec label was located. [S01–S02]
- **Study-specific processes and properties:** Primary studies prepare flour from raw, roasted, blanched, soaked/cooked, boiled, or seed-coat-retained samples and report treatment-dependent composition and functional properties. Those experimental preparations are not standardized commercial forms, conversion factors, or consumer product specifications. Keep research measurements source-scoped and out of active density, unit mappings, package yields, and kitchen conversions. [S03–S04]
- **Forms:** Add none. Seed flour/powder would be a separate processed ingredient if accepted later. Roasted or boiled whole seeds, seed flour, powder, green-fruit flesh flour, and extracted seed starch are distinct materials/products; do not model one as a form of another without product evidence. [S01–S04; R138; WP03]
- **Substitutions:** Add none. A 2022 study reports high acceptance for 5% jackfruit seed flour / 95% wheat flour in its specific doughnut formulation. A 2026 study examines 0%, 5%, 10%, and 20% in a specific yeast-raised doughnut recipe and reports different process outcomes. Those percentages are formula-specific research conditions, not a transferable wheat replacement ratio for breads, sauces, or other flours. No active global or contextual substitution, cooking yield, or recipe-line link is supported. [S04–S05]

## Reopen criteria

1. Find a current Canadian or Québec food product that identifies *A. heterophyllus* seed as its ingredient and makes clear whether it is sold as seed flour, powder, or another product.
2. Resolve seed preparation and grind scope against the consumer product; do not assume raw, roasted, blanched, boiled, peeled, or coat-retained research samples are equivalent.
3. Obtain a product-matched Québec French name. Do not infer it from `farine de jacquier`, which appears for fruit-bulb flour, or from an OQLF entry for the tree/fruit.
4. Repeat active-name, form, legacy, ledger, archive, picker, and recipe-corpus audits before any catalog promotion. Keep jackfruit seed starch and green/unripe jackfruit flesh flour separate.
5. Reassess forms, measurements, density, cooking yield, and substitutions against the supported market product; leave study ratios and the maker’s 20 g serving source-specific.



## Root integration decision

**Hold confirmed.** Jackfruit seed flour/powder remains outside the active reference catalog and research ledger. The current Canada-facing seed-powder listing does not provide a product-matched Québec French name or enough processing detail to resolve the consumer product boundary. No bundled recipe demand was found. The R181 plan disposition and batch README record this decision; no reference, alias, or ledger record was added.
