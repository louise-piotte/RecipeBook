# WP04 salt-pork identity follow-up

## Scope and coordinator decision

- **Review date:** 2026-10-10
- **Scope:** recheck the held salt-pork candidate against Québec terminology, Canadian labelling sources, and current Québec retail products.
- **Disposition:** keep broad `salt pork` unresolved. The new sources verify real, distinct salt-pork-labelled products and a Québec French term, but do not establish one product scope that covers recipe uses without assuming cut or ready-to-eat state.
- **Coordinator integration:** add the new source observations and hold decision to the research ledger. Do not add a reference, alias, form, measurement, or recipe link.

## Catalog and recipe audit

The audit covered 677 active references, 34 forms, 84 legacy candidates, and all 114 manifest-listed recipes (1,123 ingredient rows). There is no exact active reference, form, or legacy owner for `salt pork`, `petit salé`, `lard salé`, or `porc salé`. Recipe rows contain no salt-pork request. The preceding cured-pork proposal likewise found no recipe match and kept the broad term held.

## Evidence and limits

| Source | Support and limits |
| --- | --- |
| [OQLF GDT, petit salé](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/2298616/petit-sale) | Maps English `salt pork` to preferred French `petit salé` / `petit-salé`, and lists `lard salé` for some contexts. Its note says the most commonly sold product under this name is pork rib or belly salted and cooked in seasoned broth, sold whole, sliced, or in pieces; it also notes that animal species must be included in meat-label wording. This does not make every recipe's salt pork cooked or the same cut. |
| [CFIA, meat-product labelling](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/food-labels/labelling/industry/meat-and-poultry-products) | Names salt pork separately from side bacon and pork jowls for specific Canadian labelling exemptions. The page does not provide a general composition or readiness definition for salt pork. |
| [Super C, Lard salé L. Fortin](https://www.superc.ca/allees/viandes-et-volailles/porc/bacon/lard-sale/p/206232) | A Québec retail item uses `lard salé` and declares pork, water, salt, sodium erythorbate, and sodium nitrite. It supports a current local product name and one brined product, not a universal recipe meaning. |
| [Les Viandes d'Autrefois, lard salé de Mangalitsa](https://www.maturin.ca/index.php/produit/les-viandes-dautrefois/lard-sale-de-mangalitsa/15361/) | A Québec marketplace listing identifies a pork-belly product and declares belly, water, and salt. Its Mangalitsa source and one product formulation cannot define all salt pork. |

The products differ in terminology and declared composition. The sources do not establish whether an unqualified recipe line means uncooked salted pork, cooked *petit salé*, a particular cut, or another regional product. Choosing a generic row now risks making those states appear equivalent.

## Held decision

Keep `salt pork`, `lard salé`, `petit salé`, and `porc salé` out of active reference names and aliases until a recipe or product-specific scope identifies the intended item. Keep salt pork distinct from bacon, pancetta, guanciale, ham, and prosciutto. Do not infer readiness to eat, safe handling, curing chemistry, storage, cut, conversion, or substitution.
