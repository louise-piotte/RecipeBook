- **Review date:** 2026-10-12

- **Status:** The hold was accepted and reconciled on 2026-10-12; see the [integration record](../batches/wp02-pumpkin-seed-flour-scope-hold-integration-20261012/README.md). No catalog or ledger data was changed.

- **Proposal-local source IDs:** `WP02-B2-PUMPKIN-SEED-FLOUR-20261012-S01` through `S08`. The prefix is absent from the shared ledger and archives; these source IDs have not been written to the ledger.

## Recommendation

Treat pumpkin-seed flour as a genuine discovery gap, but **do not allocate an active reference or aliases yet**. Canadian-facing sources use `pumpkin seed flour` for press-cake flour made after oil extraction. A U.S. seed-flour maker uses the same product name for seeds milled while retaining the high oil content of whole seeds. Those processes affect composition and baking behavior; the shared name alone does not show equivalence. [S02–S05]

The English phrase `pumpkin seed flour` is attested in a Canadian storefront and by manufacturers. Canadian French editorial wording `farine de graines de citrouille` is also attested, but the same article describes the source as `graines de courge`; OQLF defines the seed term `graine de citrouille`, not a flour product. A French product listing explicitly uses `farine de graines de citrouille ... déshuilée`, but it describes one Austrian press-cake item sold by a French retailer, not an accepted Québec flour name. [S01–S03, S06]

Do not reserve `ingredient-ref-pumpkin-seed-flour`, attach flour wording to the whole-seed row, or use `pepita flour`, `farine de graines de courge`, `pumpkin flour`, or `farine de citrouille` as broad aliases. Reopen only when a current Canadian product source names the seed preparation/oil-removal scope and a Québec French product label or terminology source supports the same scope. If future demand distinguishes full-fat milled seed from defatted press cake, review them as separate scoped candidates rather than collapsing them under one unqualified identity.
