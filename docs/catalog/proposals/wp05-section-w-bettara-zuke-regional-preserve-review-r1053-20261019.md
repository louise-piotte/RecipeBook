## Recommendation

Propose identity-only **Bettara-zuke**, a Tokyo-preserve identity made from daikon. Japan's Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries (MAFF) describes it as salt-pre-pickled daikon finished with rice koji and sugar; it lists Tokyo as the transmitted region and names daikon, rice koji, kelp, sugar, mirin, and salt among its main ingredients. The NIBN food-classification appendix separately lists `Daikon (Bettara-zuke: pickled with rice koji)`. This supports a named regional culinary identity, distinct from the broader *koji-zuke* method class. [S01–S02]

Keep the Tokyo/name qualifier and preserve the identity as daikon-only. MAFF contrasts this preparation with takuan by describing the daikon as pickled without prior drying. Keep that distinction tied to the official source's account; do not generalize it to every household or commercial preparation. The source's reported recipe timings and optional preparations remain source-specific. [S01]

If accepted for later integration, add one identity-only `SAUCE_AND_CONDIMENT` reference. Add no aliases, forms, density, unit mappings, reusable measures, recipe links, conversions, yields, or active substitutions. Preserve `daikon`, `takuan`, and the generic `koji-zuke` class as separate current identities.

## Identity and bilingual boundary

MAFF identifies **東京都べったら漬け** as a Tokyo pickle with daikon as its main substrate. Its description says daikon is salt-pre-pickled and then pickled with rice koji and sugar; it names kelp, mirin, and salt among the main ingredients. The page also says this daikon is not dried before pickling and describes the resulting preserve as slightly sweet. These support the source-bounded food identity, not a fixed formula or product standard. [S01]

The NIBN food-classification table uses the English spelling `Bettara-zuke` in `Daikon (Bettara-zuke: pickled with rice koji)`. Elsewhere in the same list it separately identifies a small-oval eggplant koji-zuke entry. This is useful boundary evidence: Bettara-zuke is a named daikon pickle within the wider koji-pickle family, not a synonym for all koji-zuke vegetables. The table is a food classification, not a preparation or measure source. [S02]

No reviewed source standardizes a French-Canadian name. Use a borrowed name plus a provisional, descriptive gloss, with no French alias:

| Locale | Proposed display | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|
| English | `Bettara-zuke (Tokyo rice-koji daikon pickle)` | The Romanized food name appears in the NIBN English food-list appendix; Tokyo and daikon scope are supported by MAFF. |
| French (Canada) | `bettara-zuke (radis daïkon de Tokyo mariné au koji de riz)` | Provisional descriptive construction. No Québec-standardized name was found; the gloss is not a search alias. |

Do not add aliases `daikon pickle`, `pickled radish`, `takuan`, `koji-zuke`, `rice-koji pickle`, `Tokyo pickle`, or a transliteration variant not independently assessed. In particular, one named daikon pickle cannot supply a global alias for raw daikon, generic pickled radish, takuan, or every koji-zuke vegetable.

If accepted, add source locators `WP05-SECTIONW-BETTARA-ZUKE-R1053-20261019-S01` and `...-S02`. S01 belongs to the MAFF Tokyo food description; S02 belongs to the NIBN daikon food-list entry.
