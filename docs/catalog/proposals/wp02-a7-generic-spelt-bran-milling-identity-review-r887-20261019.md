## Recommendation and identity boundary

Add `spelt bran / son d’épeautre` as the bran fraction obtained when spelt grain is milled for human food. The Office québécois de la langue française maps `épeautre` to English `spelt` and identifies the crop as *Triticum aestivum* subsp. *spelta*. A Québec food-directory listing directly uses `son d’épeautre`, says it is obtained by milling spelt grain, and describes culinary uses in bakery, pastry, and prepared dishes. Two primary milling studies independently distinguish spelt bran from spelt flour and other cereal fractions. Together these sources support a generic culinary identity and bilingual display, rather than a count of commercial products. [Reused OQLF source; R887-S02–S04]

Include generic edible spelt bran without assigning a universal fine/coarse grade. The studies report study-specific fractions and milling procedures; their particle sizes and compositions do not define a common retail specification. Keep the following separate:

- generic wheat bran and bran from other grains, including buckwheat, barley, corn, oat, rice, rye, sorghum, triticale, and proso millet;
- spelt flour, whole-spelt flour, white spelt flour, malted or sprouted spelt flour, whole spelt berries, and spelt flakes;
- the adherent outer hull/glumes of spelt grain, which are a different structure from the kernel-bran fraction;
- bran-containing whole-grain spelt flour and composite cereals or baked foods.

Spelt is botanically a variety/subspecies of wheat, but the direct Québec ingredient wording and primary comparison studies distinguish its bran as a source-qualified milling identity. Keep it discoverable as `spelt bran`; do not alias it to `wheat bran` or imply that the two are interchangeable. The display `son d’épeautre` is directly attested in Québec food-market language; the OQLF entry supports the base crop term, not this exact bran compound as a separate terminology ruling.

## Limitations and next review boundary

1. The exact French compound is supported by a Québec food-directory listing and not by a separate OQLF bran entry. The proposal describes it as Québec-attested wording, not as an OQLF-approved compound.
2. Spelt bran has varying fine/coarse fractions across milling methods. No universal grade, particle size, ingredient ratio, or composition is modeled.
3. The primary studies are not household recipes and do not supply transferable measures, density, yield, or substitution behavior.
4. No bundled recipe uses spelt bran, so no recipe-specific form or use context is inferred.
5. Further brand-, product-line-, flavour-, SKU-, or retailer-variant-only work remains out of scope because commercial-product counts do not add ingredient diversity. Preserve all existing branded rows and product-specific evidence; require a separate transferability review before applying any product-only measurement or claim to a generic identity.
