## Decision and boundary

Salmon belly is an unfinished seafood-part boundary within the open finfish checklist. The sources show that the phrase can refer to a physical ventral portion and to products prepared from that portion, but the available Québec evidence does not converge on an unqualified, state-neutral catalog meaning:

- The Office québécois de la langue française (OQLF) entry for **ventre de saumon** is in the fishery-product terminology domain. It defines the term as a salmon ventral portion that has been heavily salted, and gives **salmon belly** as its English translation. The entry was last updated in 1983. This is authoritative Québec terminology evidence, but it scopes the paired term to a preserved preparation.
- FAO’s salmon-smoking guidance treats the **belly flap** as a component of each salmon fillet. It says either Atlantic salmon or Pacific *Oncorhynchus* salmon can be used in the process described. The text supports an anatomical cut on salmon sides, not the proposition that OQLF’s consumer term names an unprepared fresh cut.
- A 2022 bilingual Jardins de Métis menu pairs “salmon belly gravlax” with “gravlax de ventre.” This is a named dish and supports a Québec culinary use of belly wording in a cured preparation; it does not define a general ingredient state.
- A current Québec smokehouse listing names “Ventre de saumon aux agrumés fumé.” This supports current local use of the phrase for a smoked, citrus-prepared product only. It does not establish the state, flavour, or terminology of other salmon-belly products.

The evidence places salmon-belly wording in salted, gravlax, and smoked contexts, while FAO describes belly flap as a physical component of a salmon fillet. These do not establish a uniform state-neutral consumer identity. A new row named simply “salmon belly / ventre de saumon” would make each exact phrase resolve to one identity while the strongest Québec terminology source explicitly gives the French term a heavily salted scope. With no matching bundled recipe to select the intended state, keep the phrase out of active references, aliases, and forms for now.

Do not add a form under Atlantic salmon or the generic salmon-fillets identity. The current sources do not show that the OQLF salted product, the gravlax dish, and the smokehouse product are equivalent forms of a single raw-salmon parent. Do not assign the cut to Atlantic salmon, Pacific salmon, sockeye, or another species; the evidence does not provide that assignment. Do not extend salmon-belly wording to a generic fish-belly identity or to belly flaps from other species.

Preserve the existing Atlantic salmon and other salmon-species identities, salmon fillets, canned salmon, smoked salmon, salmon roe, and all existing fish-part references. No existing branded entry or product-specific evidence is changed or generalized.
