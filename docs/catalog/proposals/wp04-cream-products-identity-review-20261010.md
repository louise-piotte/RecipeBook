# WP04 proposal: cream product identities and boundaries

- **Status:** coordinator proposal only; this file does not change the active package, research ledger, recipes, checklist, or roadmap.
- **Scope:** whipping cream, cooking cream, half-and-half, double cream, clotted cream, sour cream, and crème fraîche.
- **Research date:** 2026-10-10.
- **Unique source-ID prefix:** `WP04-Q-CREAM-20261010-S`. A repository-wide collision check found no existing use of this prefix before this proposal was drafted.

## Coordinator summary

Keep the current 15% cooking cream, sour cream, and crème fraîche identities distinct. Retain the current heavy-cream identity for existing recipe links, but narrow its broad “whipping cream” aliases: Québec `crème à fouetter` is defined at 30–35% milk fat, while U.S. `whipping cream` is 30% to under 36% and `heavy cream` is at least 36%. “Heavy whipping cream” is the exact U.S. heavy-cream alternative name, so it remains the only proposed English whipping alias for that row. The active French display `crème 35 %` reflects the Canadian recipe context, not the U.S. federal fat threshold; mark this cross-market equivalence for bilingual review rather than presenting it as a universal composition rule [WP04-Q-CREAM-20261010-S01, S06, S07].

Keep half-and-half separate from cooking cream and table cream. OQLF uses `crème à café` and `crème moitié-moitié` for an approximately 10% Canadian product made from equal parts cream and milk; the U.S. legal class is 10.5% to under 18%. Keep `half-and-half / crème moitié-moitié` as the existing operational identity, but leave the current `coffee cream` alias unresolved until the row's market/fat scope is explicit [WP04-Q-CREAM-20261010-S03, S08, S11].

For double cream and clotted cream, sources establish distinct source-market products, but the bundled library has no exact recipe demand and the Québec French display needs product-label review. Record them as held candidates, not active aliases or conversions. Treat 48% double-cream evidence as a UK-market benchmark; the 1996 UK label rule is no longer a current England-wide legal basis. Cornish clotted cream's 55% and heat-clotted process belong specifically to its protected product specification [WP04-Q-CREAM-20261010-S14, S16, S17].

No global density, unit mapping, edible-portion value, or cream-to-cream substitution is supported for these catalog rows. Health Canada's CNF gives useful food-code-specific observations, including separate whipped and liquid cream weights; do not promote these to a universal density [WP04-Q-CREAM-20261010-S18–S20].

## Measurement, edible portion, and substitutions

- **Do not add a global density or unit mapping.** Active cream rows have no density or unit mappings, and their ledger measurement states are unreviewed or blocked. Canada's 2015 CNF records are exact food-code observations, not a generalized ingredient standard. For example, the 35% whipping-cream record gives 15 mL = 15.1 g, 100 mL = 100.6 g, and 250 mL liquid = 251.5 g; its separately listed 100 mL whipped = 50.7 g and 250 mL whipped = 126.8 g demonstrate that whipped state changes volume-to-mass behavior [WP04-Q-CREAM-20261010-S18]. The 32% record is different (100 mL = 101.0 g) [S19].
- **Half-and-half observation only:** CNF code 150 is explicitly `Cream, cereal (half and half), 10% M.F. / Crème à céréales (moitié-moitié), 10% M.G.` and gives 15 mL = 15.3 g, 100 mL = 102.3 g, and 250 mL = 255.7 g. This supports a 10% food-code observation, not the entire U.S. half-and-half range or a general `coffee cream` mapping [S20].
- **Sour-cream observation only:** CNF code 5299 is light sour cream and gives 15 mL = 15.7 g and 100 mL = 104.8 g. Do not apply it to full-fat sour cream used in the linked recipe lines [S21].
- **Edible portions:** the cited CNF records list 0% refuse. This is record-specific; do not create an edible-portion field for the cream identities from those entries [S18–S21]. The recipe amounts above stay recipe amounts, not reusable unit conversions.
- **Substitutions:** no new global or contextual rule is proposed. Dairy Farmers of Canada's educational card says crème fraîche can be used in place of sour cream, but gives no amount, method, or target recipe; retain this only as a qualitative candidate direction, not an active rule [WP04-Q-CREAM-20261010-S11]. No reviewed source justifies a reusable amount/direction among whipping/heavy cream, cooking cream, half-and-half, double cream, or clotted cream. The manufacturer's crème fraîche cooking claim is product-specific and is not evidence of a substitution ratio. Leave removed rules blocked. Do not reopen the distinct WP08 sour-cream/Greek-style-yogurt evidence [WP04-Q-CREAM-20261010-S12, S22].
