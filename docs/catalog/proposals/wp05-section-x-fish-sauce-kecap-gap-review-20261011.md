### Product variation and source limits

- **Patis and generic fish sauce:** OQLF and the Philippine standards list establish a product-name relationship, but the FDA user manual provides only the title of the 2015 standard; this review did not inspect its full criteria. The Codex standard describes a general fermented fish-and-salt fish sauce scope, not every Patis SKU. Canadian retailer declarations differ: Walmart’s Marca Pina Patis listing reports fish extract (anchovy), water, salt, caramel colour, and sodium benzoate; Metro’s Patis listing reports water, anchovy extract, iodized salt, vinegar, and caramel. These are retailer-reported SKU details, not a universal formula or proof of standard conformity. [S03–S07]
- **Oyster sauce:** Lee Kum Kee Canada’s Panda product names oyster extract and lists water, sugar, salt, starch, MSG, wheat flour, and caramel for that SKU. Bodhï Gourmet’s Québec product is explicitly vegan. These sources support keeping regular and vegan rows separate; they do not establish universal recipes or make all vegetarian mushroom stir-fry sauces vegan oyster sauce. [S09–S10]
- **Kecap manis:** The Indonesian BPOM class description adds sugar and/or sweetener to fermented soybeans or soybean cake. Current Canadian product listings vary: Qualifirst’s Yoshi listing describes palm sugar and fermented soy and gives its own label ingredient list; The Dutch Shop’s Conimex listing has a different sugar/molasses/caramelized-syrup and soy-sauce ingredient list. The Conimex page’s short prose calls it “Salted ketjap soy sauce,” inconsistent with its *Ketjap Manis* title and ingredient list, so that prose is not used for product classification. No one SKU’s ingredients become a universal formula. [S11–12, S16–17]
- **Kecap asin:** BPOM allows additional food ingredients in its salty class. Inproba’s manufacturer product page lists molasses among ingredients; The Dutch Shop’s Canadian Inproba page titles it *Ketjap Asin* and describes it as Indonesian salty soy sauce, while its metadata tags incorrectly include `ketjap manis` and `sweet soy sauce`. Ignore those contradictory tags. This product evidence supports the named class and alternate spelling, not a generic sweet-soy alias or universal salty-soy formula. [S11, S13, S18–19]
- **Product labels and package volumes are not measurement evidence.** No form/state, density, unit mapping, conversion, yield, amount, or substitution rule is proposed. Ricardo’s recipe-specific replacement of kecap manis with homemade soy caramel is not a global substitution recommendation. [S15–19]
