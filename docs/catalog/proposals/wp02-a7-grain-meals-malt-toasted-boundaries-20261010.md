## Candidate boundaries and evidence

### Grain meals

#### Whole wheat meal versus bare wheat meal

The current reference is source-limited to a whole-kernel wheat milling product. Its manufacturer source says the product is milled from whole, cleaned wheat, retains bran/germ/endosperm, and is less finely milled than wholegrain flour; OQLF supplies `farine de blé entier brute` and the English `whole wheat meal` term. These claims support the active row only. They do not make bare `wheat meal`, wheat groats, wheat grits, durum semolina, cracked wheat, or whole-wheat flour synonyms. The earlier follow-up's proposed `coarse whole wheat flour` alias remains inactive because it can overlap the whole-wheat-flour query. Refer to the current ledger sources `WMA7-20261010-S01–S05` and `docs/catalog/proposals/wp02-a7-wheat-meal-product-boundary-followup-20261010.md`.

Canadian food standards list wheat meal separately among possible standardized-bread ingredients but do not give a consumer-product definition. The current Canadian tariff groups wheat meal with groats and semolina; its French wording groups them as `gruaux et semoules de froment (blé)`, not as a standalone Québec display. The catalog therefore keeps the unqualified phrase unresolved. The whole-wheat-meal row may appear for a broad `wheat meal` picker query because its display contains those words; this is a phrase-search result, not a global equivalence rule.

#### Barley meal, oatmeal, and other grain-meal terms

Ottawa Valley Grain's Ontario Made profile lists `Whole Grain Barley Meal`, describes it as crushed and ground from whole barley, and says its texture ranges from fine to coarse [S05]. Statistics Canada's NAPCS examples separately list `barley meal for human consumption` and other meal/grit/groat products [S06]. This establishes that barley meal is a real product/class, but not its Québec French display or an exact relationship to barley flour. OQLF's technical `gruau` entries concern grits and cereal particles, while its Québec `gruau` consumer entry concerns a cooked oatmeal dish [S07]. No single French phrase safely covers the product's fine-to-coarse range. Hold the candidate; do not add an English-only row or a French term inferred from unrelated feed, recipe, or grits sources.

The bundled recipes and the live catalog do not provide a dry flour/meal product named `oat meal` or `oatmeal`. OQLF defines Québec `gruau` as a cooked oatmeal dish and maps English `oatmeal` to that dish; it separately defines `flocon d'avoine` as an oat grain product that has been steamed and flattened [S07]. The active `oat groats / gruau d'avoine entier`, rolled oats, quick oats, instant oats, and oat flour remain distinct. Do not interpret the unqualified English word as a milling-grade ingredient.

#### Existing meal/grit references

Keep `rye meal / gruau de seigle`, `cornmeal / semoule de maïs`, and `hominy grits / gruau de maïs` exactly as currently integrated. The rye source set distinguishes meal from rye flour/grain and supports its current coarse/pumpernickel aliases; OQLF and source-specific rye/bread usage do not justify expanding those aliases. Keep rye grits separate from rye meal; classification lists them separately, and no additional Québec recipe/product term is established in this batch.

### Malted and toasted rye flour

Briess names `Malted Rye Flour L100` and separately lists rye malt flour alongside its wheat/barley malt flours [S08]. A Canadian bilingual product technical sheet for a bun manufactured in Baie-d'Urfé, Québec, uses `malted rye flour` in English and `farine de seigle malté` in its French ingredient declaration [S09]. Together they support the proposed bilingual product name `malted rye flour / farine de seigle malté` as an identity-only catalog candidate. The Canadian label is a finished-product ingredient declaration, not a general terminology standard; the display is exact source wording, not an OQLF-preferred term.

Briess's `L100` product carries a product-specific 100° Lintner descriptor; this packet does not transfer that value to the proposed identity or infer that the Canadian bun ingredient is that Briess SKU. No diastatic/non-diastatic state is added to the new reference. Keep the malted-rye identity separate from `malted barley flour`, `malted wheat flour`, `roasted wheat malt flour`, and ordinary rye flour.

Harinas Polo currently lists `Toasted Rye flour` as a product and groups it with malted and toasted cereal/soy products [S10]. The page provides no matching Canadian French product name or detailed milling/thermal sequence. Hold `toasted rye flour` and do not activate `roasted rye flour`, `farine de seigle torréfiée`, `farine de seigle malté torréfié`, or any other translation as an alias from this evidence. French-language sources from outside Québec were not treated as Québec terminology evidence. Keep the existing `toasted barley flour` alias cereal-specific; its source support does not generalize to rye.

### Gluten flour and vital wheat gluten

OQLF gives `farine de gluten` as the preferred Québec French term and `gluten flour` as the English equivalent. It defines the product as made by removing part of the starch from whole-wheat flour and notes its high gluten content [S02]. The current Canadian CFIA standard defines `Gluten Flour` as food obtained by removing part of the starch from flour, with limits of 10% moisture and 44% starch on a dry basis [S01]. CFIA separately names vital wheat gluten through its OQLF terminology record as industrial wheat gluten that exhibits agglomerating and elastic properties when hydrated [S03].

The terminology and standard alone describe related but not numerically identical product scopes. The current Metro listing for Bob's Red Mill product number `039978002129` has the English product name `Gluten Flour`, a French product route/name `Farine de gluten`, and the English ingredient declaration `VITAL WHEAT GLUTEN` [S04]. That named Canadian retail product directly bridges the two catalog terms. Recommend adding only `gluten flour` and `farine de gluten` as aliases to the existing `ingredient-ref-vital-wheat-gluten` row. Do not create a separate `gluten flour` ID or map the phrase to `high-gluten flour`.

The alias recommendation is for the source-named Canadian retail product and bilingual terminology. It does not claim that every item described under the CFIA `Gluten Flour` standard has the same protein percentage, vitality, functionality, or dry composition as every vital-wheat-gluten product. Do not import the CFIA limits as measurements or catalog values. Preserve the existing display `vital wheat gluten / gluten de froment élastique` and existing French alias `gluten de blé vital`.
