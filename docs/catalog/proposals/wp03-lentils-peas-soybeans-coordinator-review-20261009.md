## Recommendation

**Defer new reference and form rows for this batch, while accepting the evidence-backed identity boundaries below.** The bundled corpus has one explicit dried-yellow-pea line, one mixed frozen-peas-and-carrots line, and no lentil, split-pea, mature-soybean, edamame, or fresh-shell-bean line. The existing dried yellow pea reference can support the one direct pulse use. New color-specific lentil rows and new pea, soy, edamame, or shell-bean rows have no direct recipe demand in this package.

One live naming conflict merits coordinator cleanup: the query `petits pois` currently returns both the fresh-pea reference and the dried-yellow-pea reference because `petits pois jaunes` is an alias on the latter. Remove that ambiguous alias from `ingredient-ref-yellow-peas`; retain the explicit `petits pois` term on the fresh-pea product. Consider changing the fresh reference display to `green peas / petits pois` so its product stage is clear. Preserve `dried yellow peas / pois jaunes séchés` as its own dry product. Do not relink the frozen mixed-vegetable line to peas alone.

Keep `ingredient-ref-split-peas` as an unresolved legacy candidate for now; do not merge it into yellow peas. The identity is well supported as a dry pea split into cotyledons, but the generic term does not specify green or yellow color, and the bundle has no split-pea recipe use. Keep color-specific lentil classes, the protected Puy product, dry-versus-green soybeans, edamame, and fresh shell beans open for a recipe or a coordinator decision that needs those distinctions. Leave measurement and substitution data unset.

## Measures, conversions, and substitutions

- The only direct quantity is `2 cups dried yellow peas`. It gives no mass, cultivar, or explicit whole/split condition. Do not infer density, dry-to-cooked yield, package yield, or a substitution from that line.
- The `1 cup frozen peas and carrots` line is a mixture. CFIA’s compositional standard shows that the named product includes both peas and carrots in a defined weight range; its quantity cannot be reused as a peas-only cup mapping [S09].
- Health Canada’s Canadian Nutrient File lists raw green edamame in English and French and reports `100 ml = 108.2 g`, `125 ml = 135.2 g`, and `250 ml = 270.0 g`, alongside 47% refuse as pods [S12]. This is a food-specific reference observation, not a universal density. The page does not give a recipe-matched pod-on/shelled and cooked/frozen state suitable for this app’s conversion model; retain it as source-scoped evidence only and do not add an active density or mapping.
- Canadian Grain Commission grading classes and Codex dry-pulse presentation definitions establish product identity and commercial classification, not kitchen volumes or weight-to-volume conversions. Agricultural grade moisture or seed-size figures are not culinary mapping values.
- No supported mass/volume mappings or cross-form yields were found for lentils or dry peas. Do not infer that a cup of green, red, French-green, or split lentils/peas weighs the same.
- No source-backed substitution ratio was found between lentil colors, dry and fresh peas, whole and split peas, soybeans and edamame, or any fresh shell bean. Do not add global substitutions or form conversions.
