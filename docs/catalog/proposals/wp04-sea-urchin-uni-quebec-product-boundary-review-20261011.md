This review applies the same recipe-demand gate to uni. It resolves the product boundary for future use, but does not complete the broader Section P checklist.

## Recommendation and contingent naming

Do not add an active ingredient reference or recipe link now. DFO’s current fishery material describes sea-urchin reproductive glands as the edible product and documents “Uni” as a market name for extracted red-sea-urchin roe. Québec product listings independently use gonades d’oursin and uni d’oursin for the edible portion. This is enough to retain a future, distinct product candidate if a recipe names uni or sea-urchin roe.

If future recipe demand appears, review one possible PROTEIN identity with these source-supported wording candidates:

| Locale | Candidate display | Directly attested wording | Naming limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| English | sea urchin roe (uni) | DFO uses “roe” for the reproductive organs and says extracted red-sea-urchin roe is packaged for sale as “Uni”; a Montréal fishmonger sells “Urchin Roe (QC).” [S02, S03, S07] | “Roe” is established culinary and market wording. The edible material is gonads, including male and female gonads, not fish eggs. This display does not identify a species or a whole animal. |
| fr-CA | gonades d’oursin (uni) | A Québec retailer labels the product “Gonades fraîches OU surgelées” and “Partie comestible : Gonades (Uni)”; a Montréal fishmonger labels it “Gonades d’oursins (QC).” [S06, S07] | This is documented Québec retail wording, not an OQLF-normalized compound term. OQLF’s normalized species name oursin vert is narrower and must not be generalized to every uni product. [S01] |

These are contingent candidates, not approved seed names or a proposed row. Re-evaluate actual recipe wording and exact-key behavior at integration. Do not add a generic alias for roe, fish roe, sea urchin, or oursin. If the library later represents both a whole urchin and its edible gonads, keep them as separate identities; a broad query may then find both.

## Product boundary and exact limits

| Boundary | Disposition | Evidence and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Edible gonads vs. whole animal | Distinct requested products; do not link or alias them as equivalent. | DFO says red sea urchins are delivered to processors, where the roe is extracted, treated, and packaged as uni. The same plan says West Coast green sea urchins are sold whole and live. A live, whole urchin is not a package of extracted gonads. These are documented British Columbia market paths, not a universal rule for every fishery. [S02] |
| Gonads vs. biological eggs or fish roe | Treat “sea-urchin roe” as culinary wording for gonads, not as a member of fish-egg products. | DFO identifies the edible reproductive organs/gonads as “roe”; the prior roe packet cites DFO’s description of both male and female gonads. [S03; prior review S11] Do not alias uni to tobiko, masago, salmon roe/ikura, or fish roe. |
| Québec species term | Keep oursin vert attached to the species-qualified animal, not as a generic uni alias. | OQLF’s preferred term oursin vert names Strongylocentrotus droebachiensis and translates it as green sea urchin/sea urchin. OQLF says châtaigne de mer was not retained for Québec commercial fish-product labeling to avoid confusion. This terminology entry does not name extracted gonads or all species sold as uni. [S01] |
| Other commercial species and regional scope | Do not assign a species from uni, sea urchin roe, or gonades d’oursin alone. | DFO’s 2025–26 Pacific plan distinguishes red sea urchin, Mesocentrotus franciscanus, from green sea urchin, Strongylocentrotus droebachiensis; it documents extracted uni for the red fishery and whole/live sales for the West Coast green fishery. These Pacific examples do not establish a single species for the broad culinary product. [S02] |
| Fresh or frozen extracted gonads | Preserve the state when a recipe or product gives it; do not infer one universal form. | A current Québec product listing offers fresh or frozen gonads in that retailer’s 200 g packs; a Montréal retailer lists fresh, opened and prepared 100 g platters. These listings establish those products only, not general package sizes or a universal state relationship. [S06, S07] |
| Salted, frozen, cooked, or preserved gonads | Keep as product-specific processed-state evidence; no form or conversion is proposed. | An older DFO market-survey summary reports Japan-bound fresh gonads and salted/frozen, baked, or boiled gonad preserves for further processing. It summarizes a historical survey and historical markets; it is not a current, complete product standard or cooking instruction. [S05] |
| Corail | Do not promote as a catalog alias in this review. | The reviewed Québec retail labels use gonades d’oursin and uni d’oursin. The sources here do not establish corail as a unique Québec commercial product name for extracted uni; the term needs its own ambiguity review before any global alias is considered. |
| Tobiko and masago | Keep separate from uni. | Tobiko and masago are fish-roe products with different source-fish boundaries; sea-urchin gonads are not fish eggs. The earlier review found no recipe demand for any of these roe identities. [prior review] |
| Salmon roe/ikura and caviar | Keep separate from uni; no cross-alias. | Salmon roe and caviar refer to other roe products. A general culinary or market use of the word roe does not make them the same food. The earlier roe proposal separately checked these product names and kept them distinct. [prior review] |
| Bottarga/poutargue and karasumi | Keep separate from uni. | These are salted/pressed/dried fish-roe products in the earlier Section P reviews. Shared “roe” wording does not establish common identity or substitution. [prior review] |
| Abalone, conch, whelk, sea cucumber | Outside this bounded review. | They remain separate Section P items. This review makes no identity, name, form, or recipe recommendation for them. |

## Measurement and substitution

The historical DFO processing examples do not provide a kitchen conversion. Add no density, count weight, unit mapping, yield, freshness/frozen conversion, or cooking conversion.

The reviewed sources establish product distinction, not a tested replacement rule. Add no substitution between uni and fish roe, caviar, bottarga, fresh whole urchin, or another seafood.
