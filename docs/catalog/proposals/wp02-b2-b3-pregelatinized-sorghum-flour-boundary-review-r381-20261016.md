## Decision and scope

Nu Life Market currently offers UltraLIFT Flour in commercial bulk quantities. Its current product page identifies the branded functional sorghum flour and gives manufacturer-specific bakery usage guidance. A Nu Life brochure says UltraLIFT is labeled as whole-grain sorghum flour. Dempsey Food explicitly calls UltraLIFT a “pre-gelatinized sorghum flour.” Its market page places this description under `Result: CANADA | Cakes, Breads, Doughs, Flatbreads`, then repeats the same Nu Life description under a separate USA bakery result. This is direct evidence of a Canadian-market channel listing for the exact named product and process description; the recommendation below does not depend on proving a Canadian offer. Together, the sources support a real, process-qualified **flour** candidate and distinguish it from an isolated starch. The manufacturer’s functional-flour overview also describes patented pre-cooking and milling, but does not publish a product specification that equates every functional flour with the same process.

The evidence has clear limits: Dempsey’s page is a commercial-market listing, not a Canadian retail stock check, package image, current lot record, or full product specification. Nu Life’s manufacturer product page offers commercial bulk formats but does not itself state a country of sale. Neither reviewed product source supplies product-matched French wording for Québec. The hold therefore concerns the bilingual catalog name for the exact flour form, not whether Dempsey lists the item in Canada.

The Québec French base term `farine de sorgho` is used by the current Aliments du Québec listing for an ordinary sorghum-flour product. OQLF separately defines `amidon prégélatinisé` as starch treated to become cold-water-soluble, and `amidon de sorgho` as starch from the genus *Sorghum*. Neither OQLF entry attests the flour phrase `farine de sorgho prégélatinisée`. That phrase is a plausible descriptive construction, not a product-matched Québec label. A starch-specific French term must not be transferred to a whole-grain flour. Because this reference presents French and English labels, the English Canadian-market listing alone does not clear the bilingual naming threshold.

Keep the existing `sorghum flour / farine de sorgho` reference, its aliases, and the distinct `sorghum starch / amidon de sorgho` reference unchanged. If stronger local evidence later clears the threshold, review UltraLIFT or the exact qualifying Canadian grade as a form under the existing sorghum-flour identity. Do not create a separate identity from this evidence: the manufacturer describes the item as whole-grain sorghum flour, while the active starch row is for extracted starch. Do not add the brand name as a global alias.

Reopen when a current Québec/Canadian product label or authoritative Québec terminology source supplies product-matched French wording for the already Canada-listed pregelatinized sorghum flour, or otherwise attests the flour/process compound in this meaning. A literal translation alone, the base term `farine de sorgho`, OQLF terminology limited to starch, or the English Dempsey Canada listing is not enough to establish the French display. This is a bounded hold on bilingual form integration; it is not a hold on Canadian-market evidence and not a claim that the product is absent from Canada.
