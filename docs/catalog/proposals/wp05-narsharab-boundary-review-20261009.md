# WP05 Section Y: Narsharab boundary review

- **Review date / source access date:** 2026-10-09.
- **Disposition:** **Coordinator accepted unresolved** against the integrated pomegranate molasses identity on 2026-10-09. The evidence confirms a named, standardized Azerbaijani sauce, but does not establish whether the catalog should model it as a separate regional ingredient or as a regional name within the broader pomegranate-molasses family.
- **Proposal-only scope:** No reference, alias, form, conversion, or substitution is proposed for integration. Current catalog data and recipes were read only.
- **Live state checked:** 412 ingredient references, 16 forms, 99 legacy candidates, 577 ledger records, and 114 recipe JSON files.

## Decision

The Azerbaijani spelling **Narşərab** is the subject of active national standard AZS 065:2023. Its public registry abstract describes a product made from boiled/concentrated wild-pomegranate juice with added sugar, used as a condiment for meat, fish, and some national dishes [WP05-NAR-S01]. A state standards catalogue gives the earlier AZS 065-2001 standard the English title **“Pomegranate molasses. Specifications”** [WP05-NAR-S02]. This official historical English crosswalk is material evidence against asserting that Narsharab is necessarily a separate ingredient from pomegranate molasses.

The overlap is also present in Canadian and Québec products. A Québec maker sells *pomegranate molasses / mélasse de grenade* made from concentrated pomegranate juice in a 200 mL package [WP05-NAR-S09, WP05-NAR-S10]. Canadian examples also include pomegranate juice with cane sugar, acid, and salt [WP05-NAR-S11], and a Montréal retailer lists a 380 g KINTO *Narsharab Sweet-Hot Sauce* [WP05-NAR-S12]. These sources confirm the names and some product examples; they do not establish the boundaries across all products. The current Azerbaijani standard is more specific about origin and process, but its public abstract does not provide enough information to show a distinct culinary function or a non-overlapping composition. The complete current standard was not reviewed because the registry offers it for purchase.

Recommendation: keep Narsharab unresolved. Do not create a new identity, merge it into pomegranate molasses, or activate an alias yet. The current pomegranate molasses row remains `pomegranate molasses / mélasse de grenade`, category `SAUCE_AND_CONDIMENT`, with no aliases, density, or unit mappings. This review adds evidence that should be considered at the next boundary decision; it does not amend that accepted row.

## Identity and product boundaries

| Comparison | Disposition |
|---|---|
| Narsharab vs. pomegranate molasses | **Unresolved.** Both have authoritative/market evidence as reduced or concentrated pomegranate products, and the official historical standard catalogue directly translates the Narşərab standard title as “Pomegranate molasses.” Product examples overlap in pomegranate juice/concentrate and sugar, but ingredient lists vary. Current evidence does not support either a clean separate-identity rule or a broad synonym rule. |
| Narsharab vs. pomegranate juice or concentrate | A finished condiment is distinct from plain juice/concentrate: the current standard describes boiling/concentrating juice with added sugar, and regional sources describe serving the result as sauce/condiment [WP05-NAR-S01, WP05-NAR-S03, WP05-NAR-S04]. This is a product boundary, not an accepted catalog identity. |
| Narsharab vs. Ajvar | Separate foods. Narsharab is pomegranate-based; the integrated Ajvar row is a Balkan red-pepper spread/condiment, with `ayvar` as its sole English alias. The accepted Ajvar review keeps pepper-and-eggplant spreads, Ajika, biber salçası, muhammara, Pinđur, and Malidzano separate [WP05-NAR-S14]. No equivalence or substitution is indicated. |
| Narsharab vs. “Narsh” | Do not merge. AZGRANATA calls *Narsh* a pomegranate sauce and lists natural pomegranate juice without sugar or preservatives; this product name and recipe do not establish that `narsh` is a spelling variant or alias of Narsharab [WP05-NAR-S08]. |

Regional sources describe product variation. AZERTAC describes one traditional preparation from pressed fresh pomegranate seeds/juice, infused and evaporated with sugar into a viscous sweet-spicy meat condiment [WP05-NAR-S03]. ShahNar describes Narsharab as a pomegranate-juice sauce used with meat and fish [WP05-NAR-S05]. A Shems manufacturer product lists pomegranate extract and sugar [WP05-NAR-S06, WP05-NAR-S07]. These are source-specific descriptions, not a universal formula. The public AZS abstract covers boiled/concentrated wild-pomegranate juice with sugar, but the full technical requirements are not available in the reviewed abstract [WP05-NAR-S01].

## Recommended acceptance checks for a later decision

1. Decide whether the product model should follow the current Azerbaijani standard’s specific scope or the broader retail/culinary family called pomegranate molasses. Review the complete AZS 065:2023 text if an actual specification boundary is needed.
2. Require direct evidence before choosing either a distinct Narsharab reference or a `narsharab` alias on `ingredient-ref-pomegranate-molasses`. Treat the old standard catalogue’s English title as significant but limited translation evidence.
3. Obtain a Québec French label or authoritative French culinary source before assigning a Narsharab-specific fr-CA descriptor. Retain `narsharab` only as a provisional same-loanword display if a separate row is later accepted.
4. Keep `narsh`, generic `pomegranate sauce`, pomegranate juice/concentrate, pomegranate molasses, and Ajvar separate unless a source supports the specific equivalence. Do not use `narsharob` or “pomegranate wine” as aliases without authoritative product-language evidence.
5. Leave density, conversion, yield, forms, and substitutions unknown unless a source provides product/form-matched numerical evidence. The Harvard honey note remains limited to its one pomegranate-molasses recipe.
6. At any later integration review, repeat exact normalized collision and full 114-recipe scans against the final proposed names and aliases.

## Coordinator decision

Keep Narsharab unresolved. The active Azerbaijani standard describes a specific pomegranate condiment, while the official historical English title for the prior standard is “Pomegranate molasses”; available product listings also show overlapping but variable formulations. This is not enough to add a separate identity or an alias to `ingredient-ref-pomegranate-molasses`. The full current standard was not reviewed, and no Québec French name was established. No canonical reference, form, measurement, conversion, package yield, or substitution was changed. The existing pomegranate-molasses row remains unchanged; Section Y keeps Narsharab open.
