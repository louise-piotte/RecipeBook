- **Open-plan anchor:** The generic A7 parent remains open for other grain meals and milling products, including its unresolved wheat-meal candidate ([plan, Section A7](../../RecipeBook-global-ingredient-catalog-plan.txt#L2289)).
- **Scope:** Reassess generic `wheat meal` / `wheatmeal` as a culinary ingredient identity against the already accepted whole-wheat-meal identity, wheat flour, wheat semolinas, wheat bran, cracked wheat, and the held wheat-grits/groats names. This is ingredient-diversity work, not a product roster.
- **Recommendation:** Keep unqualified `wheat meal` and `wheatmeal` unresolved and inactive. Preserve `ingredient-ref-wheat-meal` only for `whole wheat meal / farine de blé entier brute` and its existing French alternate.

Its earlier R605 record remains historical and is not deleted. This review instead evaluated generic Canadian, Québec, and public standards terminology.

## Recommendation

Generic Canadian sources recognize *wheat meal* as a human-food and trade category: CFIA lists it among possible standardized-bread ingredients, Statistics Canada's human-consumption classification names `wheat meal (except durum semolina)`, and the Canadian tariff groups wheat meal with wheat groats. That evidence supports retaining an unresolved generic candidate, but it does not supply one stable consumer milling boundary or a single Québec French name.

The additional generic Québec terminology review does not resolve the mapping. OQLF's `semoule de blé` is a soft-wheat milling product made of large particles and is translated as `fine wheat granule`; the term is not given as an English equivalent of generic *wheat meal*. The live catalog already has five wheat-semolina identities whose French displays contain `semoule de blé`. OQLF's technical `gruau` entry translates to `grit`, while another Québec entry uses `gruau` for the cooked oatmeal dish. Neither source establishes `gruau de blé` as a stable Québec name for generic dry wheat meal.

Wholemeal sources support the existing qualified identity only. Current UK government guidance describes wholemeal flour as flour from the whole edible cereal grain, including bran, germ, and endosperm; the Australian Food Composition Database tags its generic wholemeal wheat-flour entries with `Wheatmeal flour`. These regional sources show that *wholemeal/wheatmeal* can refer to a whole-grain flour class, but neither establishes that bare Canadian *wheat meal* always has that scope, distinguishes it from the existing whole-wheat-meal row, or provides Québec wording for the broader candidate.

Keep the existing identity as accepted and narrowly scoped. Do not add a generic active row, alias, form, measurement, conversion, yield, recipe link, or substitution. Do not reinterpret the tariff sieve threshold as a kitchen grind rule. The held grits and groats candidates remain separate from meal; the existing flour, semolina, bran, and cracked-wheat owners remain unchanged.

The current Canadian tariff's 1.25-mm criterion is a classification rule for the collective groats-and-meal group. It does not specify bran/germ retention, assign a consumer use, distinguish groats from meal, or determine whether a product fits the whole-wheat-meal row. It must not be stored or presented as a culinary milling specification.

## Decision for integration

1. Keep bare `wheat meal` and `wheatmeal` unresolved and inactive; retain `ingredient-ref-wheat-meal` only for the qualified whole-wheat-meal identity and existing French alternate.
2. Do not add generic French `semoule de blé`, `gruau`, `gruau de blé`, or `gruaux de blé` to the meal row. Keep existing semolina owners and the wheat-grits/groats holds separate.
3. Do not add or transfer measurements, aliases, forms, densities, mappings, yields, recipe links, or substitutions. Preserve the existing blocked recipe-specific finding as scoped evidence.
4. Record further work on the Anson Mills named product as out of scope for this generic-coverage pass; preserve the historical R605 record and all existing references.
5. Keep the A7 parent open for remaining generic grain-meal and milling gaps. Reopen this generic wheat-meal candidate only if Canadian or Québec evidence gives a stable human-food milling boundary and an independently supportable Québec name distinct from whole-wheat meal, flour, semolina, bran, cracked wheat, and wheat groats/grits.
