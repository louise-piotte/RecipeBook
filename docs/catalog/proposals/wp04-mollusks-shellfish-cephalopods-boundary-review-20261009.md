# WP04 Section P — mollusks, shellfish, and cephalopod boundaries

- **Packet type:** proposal-only identity research; no canonical catalog, ledger, recipe, plan, roadmap, code, generated file, or test was changed.
- **Review date:** 2026-10-09.
- **Scope:** generic crab, lobster, crayfish, langoustine, mussel, clam, fresh oyster, scallop, squid, cuttlefish, and octopus search terms. Shrimp/prawn remains covered by the separate fresh-shrimp/prawn boundary review. Existing dried shrimp, dried scallop, dried oyster, and jumbo-lump crab-meat identities were not modified.
- **Disposition:** consider one English alias-only update on the existing calamari reference: add **squid** to ingredient-ref-calamari. Hold the other generic identities in this pass. Section P remains open.

## Recommendation

Add the English search alias **squid** to the existing **calamari / calmar** reference, retaining its current display labels and recipe link. OQLF's already-ledgered WP04-S38 entry treats calmar as a food mollusc, records **squid** as its English equivalent, and lists Loligo and Illex genus names. The reference already serves the bundled calamari recipe, and the live picker currently returns no result for the exact query “squid.” No active reference, form, or legacy candidate owns that normalized key.

Do not add a French **encornet** alias in this pass. Although OQLF lists encornet under the calmar entry, the separate OQLF entry defines encornet in a fishing context as a small calmar used as cod bait, and the plural entry is a FAO term for common squids in the Loligo genus. The current culinary request does not establish that unqualified encornet search should cover the same product scope. Keep the Québec display **calmar**.

Do not add generic fresh rows for crab, lobster, crayfish, langoustine, mussel, clam, oyster, scallop, cuttlefish, or octopus. Some terms have strong Québec and Canadian terminology support, but the only specific recipe requests are jumbo-lump crab meat and calamari, which already have distinct references. A third recipe row says “Cooked meat or seafood, to taste” without choosing a species. Generic queries for crab, oyster, and scallop already overlap more specific live entries; other proposed names have no recipe demand. Keep the active preserved products, sauces, and mushroom identities distinct from fresh shellfish.

No forms, species assumptions, recipe links, measurements, handling or safety guidance, yields, conversions, or substitutions are proposed.

|---|---|---|

| **Lobster / homard** | OQLF maps homard to lobster and says the term homard applies to the genus Homarus; it warns against using it for other crustaceans such as langouste. | Hold; no lobster recipe request or exact live search result. Keep homard separate from langouste. |
| **Crayfish / crawfish / écrevisse** | OQLF defines écrevisse as a freshwater crustacean and gives crayfish and crawfish as English terms. It also notes that crayfish and crawfish are sometimes used colloquially for langouste. | Hold; no recipe request. Do not make crayfish/crawfish global aliases for langouste or lobster. |
| **Langoustine** | OQLF identifies langoustine with Nephrops norvegicus; it is not a small langouste. English names include scampi, Norway lobster, and Dublin Bay prawn. In French, scampi names langoustine only when prepared as a dish. | Hold; no recipe request. Do not alias langoustine to shrimp/prawn, lobster, langouste, or generic scampi. |
| **Mussel / moule** | OQLF normalizes moule for the Québec commercial blue-mussel name Mytilus edulis. Fisheries and Oceans Canada lists one harvested mussel species in Canada. This could support a scoped identity later, but there is no recipe demand. The current picker’s fallback substring search for “moule” also returns cornmeal and semolina rows because their French labels contain “semoule.” | Hold; no recipe request. Avoid adding unrequested aliases such as moule bleue or mouque. |
| **Clam / palourde** | OQLF's Québec fish-product terminology distinguishes several named commercial clams, including palourde américaine, mye, mactre d’Amérique, and species-qualified Pacific or Japanese palourdes. Its entries state that several broad or competing names such as clam, coque, and palourde were not retained for specific commercial species. Fisheries and Oceans Canada lists multiple harvested clam species. | Hold; no recipe request. Reopen with a concrete product or recipe so the French display does not collapse different Québec market names. |
| **Fresh oyster / huître** | The catalog already separates dried oyster from oyster sauce, vegan oyster sauce, and oyster mushrooms. No recipe asks for a fresh oyster. Bare oyster search returns all of those distinct products. | Hold a fresh-oyster row; do not alias it to dried oyster, oyster sauces, or mushrooms. |
| **Scallop / pétoncle** | OQLF maps pétoncle to scallop and identifies the Québec commercial family and species context; it discourages coquille Saint-Jacques for Canadian scallops because that name denotes specified European species. The active dried-scallop identity already owns ordinary scallop and pétoncle searches. | Hold a fresh/generic scallop row until a recipe requires it; preserve the dried product as distinct and do not use coquille Saint-Jacques as an alias. |
| **Squid / calmar** | Existing WP04-S38 records the OQLF preferred Québec term calmar and its English equivalent squid. The existing calamari/calmar reference has a linked ingredient row in recipe 023, and no other reference currently owns squid. | Add only the English alias squid to ingredient-ref-calamari. Preserve the current displays and do not add encornet or a species value. |
| **Cuttlefish / seiche** | OQLF records seiche / cuttlefish as a separate commercial food-mollusc identity. CFIA distinguishes cephalopods such as squid, cuttlefish, and octopus from the bivalves and crustaceans that qualify for French fruits de mer labeling. | Hold; no recipe request or current picker result. Keep distinct from squid and octopus. |
| **Octopus / pieuvre / poulpe** | OQLF gives pieuvre and poulpe as preferred French terms for octopus. CFIA lists octopus among the cephalopod examples outside the French fruits de mer category for seafood mixes. | Hold; no recipe request or current picker result. Do not alias to squid or cuttlefish. |

The English umbrella term **seafood** should not become one ingredient identity or a French synonym for **fruits de mer**. CFIA distinguishes fruits de mer from produits de la mer / produits de la pêche when a mix includes fish or cephalopods, while OQLF's fruit de mer entry describes an edible-marine-invertebrate concept. These are product-labeling categories, not aliases for any one shellfish.

Cockles and other seafood categories on the Section P checklist were not reviewed in this packet.

## Root follow-up disposition — crab meat (2026-10-11)

This later source review supersedes the crab-only hold and the crab search result recorded above. OQLF’s `chair de crabe` entry pairs the preferred French term with English `crab meat`; a current Canadian French recipe uses the term for fresh cooked and pasteurized canned crab meat. The broad edible-product identity is now `ingredient-ref-crab` (`crab meat / chair de crabe`). It has no loose `crab` or `crabe` alias and carries no species, measurement, density, conversion, yield, or substitution behavior. OQLF’s separate `similicrabe` entry supports keeping imitation crab distinct.

The specific grade in recipe 019 is represented by `ingredient-form-crab-jumbo-lump` under the generic crab-meat reference. Phillips describes Jumbo Lump as a distinct grade consisting of two large muscles connected to swimming fins. Recipe 019 row 8 retains its source wording and quantity and links to the generic reference plus that form. The previous grade-specific reference `ingredient-ref-jumbo-lump-crab-meat` is retired. All other holds and decisions in this Section P review remain unchanged. See `docs/catalog/proposals/wp04-crab-meat-grade-and-identity-review-20261011.md` for source-to-claim notes.
