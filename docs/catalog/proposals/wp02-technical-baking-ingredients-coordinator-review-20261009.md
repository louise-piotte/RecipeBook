## Boundary decisions and coordinator dispositions

“Accept” below means the identity distinction and evidence-backed name pair are suitable to carry forward. It does not authorize a new active row without coordinator integration. Suggested categories are internal taxonomy choices, not facts established by the linguistic or food sources.

| Candidate | Evidence-backed EN / fr-CA wording | Identity boundary and recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Lecithin | `lecithin` / `lécithine` | **Accept as a distinct food ingredient/additive when a recipe calls for it; defer activation.** OQLF recognizes the term, its use as a food additive/emulsifier, and plant and animal sources. Source-qualified products such as soy or sunflower lecithin should stay qualified when the recipe or label names that source; do not create a source alias or merge modified lecithin/other phospholipids without product-specific evidence. Candidate internal category: `BAKING_MIXIN_AND_PANTRY`. No recipe usage, source-specific measure, or conversion supports a row now. |
| Glycerol / glycerin | `glycerol` / `glycérol`; name aliases supported by sources: EN `glycerin`, FR `glycérine` | **Accept one chemical identity and these names; defer activation.** OQLF lists `glycérol` and `glycérine` in French and `glycerol` and `glycerin` in English; CFIA lists glycerin as a permitted synonym for the additive Glycerol. Keep glycerol distinct from propylene glycol and glycerol esters. Candidate category: `BAKING_MIXIN_AND_PANTRY`. No recipe use or culinary dose found. |
| Citric acid | `citric acid` / `acide citrique` | **Accept as a distinct identity; defer activation.** OQLF gives the bilingual term pair in food-additive terminology. CFIA notes citric acid can perform multiple functions, so a recipe/product context must establish whether it is an acidulant, sequestrant, or another use. Do not alias lemon juice, vinegar, or citrate salts. Candidate category: `BAKING_AND_SPICE`. |
| Malic acid | `malic acid` / `acide malique` | **Accept as a distinct identity; defer activation.** CFIA’s permitted-synonyms table gives the bilingual additive name and `hydroxysuccinic acid` as a permitted synonym. Keep it distinct from maleic acid and from sodium hydrogen malate. Targeted OQLF searches did not locate a matching GDT entry, so the French pair here rests on the current Canadian regulator table rather than an OQLF record. Candidate category: `BAKING_AND_SPICE`. |
| Ascorbic acid | `ascorbic acid` / `acide ascorbique` | **Accept as a distinct ingredient/additive identity; defer activation.** OQLF gives this bilingual term pair and identifies vitamin C as a related term. CFIA says an ingredient added for a food-additive function such as preservation must be declared as `ascorbic acid`, not `Vitamin C`; the function and naming may differ when it is added for vitamin enrichment. Do not add `vitamin C` as a context-free catalog alias or substitute. Candidate category: `BAKING_AND_SPICE`. |
| Sodium citrate | `sodium citrate` / `citrate de sodium` | **Accept as distinct from citric acid; defer activation.** OQLF defines it as a sodium salt of citric acid and gives this bilingual pair. CFIA lists `trisodium citrate` as a permitted synonym for the additive name; only carry that synonym if the coordinator confirms the recipe/product means that declared additive. It is not an alias for citric acid. Candidate category: `BAKING_AND_SPICE`. |
| Meringue powder | `meringue powder` / `meringue en poudre` | **Accept as a compound product identity distinct from dried egg white; defer activation.** Wilton describes its powder as intended for meringue, frosting, and royal icing, with a brand-scoped egg-white replacement direction. A Canadian retail label for Wilton’s product lists powdered egg white together with sugar, gums, starch, acids, salts, and flavoring; formula varies by product. Do not link it as an alias or form of egg white, or generalize an egg-replacement ratio beyond the specified product and stiff-peak/icing context. Candidate category: `BAKING_MIXIN_AND_PANTRY`. |
| Dried/powdered egg white and yolk | EN `egg white powder` / `powdered egg white`; fr-CA `poudre de blanc d'œuf` / `poudre d'albumen`; EN `egg yolk powder` / `powdered egg yolk`; fr-CA `poudre de jaune d'œuf` | **Accept as dried forms under the existing `egg white` and `egg yolk` references when the product is the corresponding single-ingredient egg fraction; defer form activation.** CFIA’s Canadian identity standards distinguish and name powdered egg white/albumen and powdered yolk. Use an `EGG` form (candidate form code `dried`) if a recipe needs it. Do not create separate ingredient IDs for pure dried forms without a use case; preserve added carriers, sweeteners, salts, or stabilizers as part of the exact product rather than silently treating a mix as pure egg powder. No reconstitution ratio, weight/count equivalence, or substitution is supported by the recipe bundle. |
| Custard powder | `custard powder`; TERMIUM Plus recipe-domain French term `poudre à crème anglaise`; current live FR display `poudre à crème pâtissière` | **Accept the existing identity and its two recipe links; defer any French display/alias edit and category correction for Québec review.** TERMIUM Plus directly maps `custard powder` to `poudre à crème anglaise`, describing a fine-textured compound powder; Bird’s current Canadian label lists corn starch, salt, colours, and flavours, and describes the brand as dairy- and egg-free. OQLF’s `crème prise` entry distinguishes a cooked custard dessert from a powdered dessert preparation. This is a compound mix, not pure cornstarch, egg yolk, prepared custard, or necessarily the same product as a pastry-cream mix. The current category `FLOUR_AND_STARCH` deserves coordinator review; `BAKING_MIXIN_AND_PANTRY` is a plausible internal category for the compound mix, but the source does not prescribe app taxonomy. Keep current recipe quantities in tablespoons and do not apply a brand serving weight or substitution. |

No density, unit mapping, package yield, form-level density, conversion, or substitution is supported for any target. The Bird’s page and Wilton direction contain brand-specific product measurements; they are not catalog-wide conversions. Health Canada’s permitted-additive lists set use conditions by food category and purpose; those regulatory conditions do not supply a home-recipe dose.

## Coordinator recommendation

1. Accept the identity boundaries above as the working Section E decisions; make no new ingredient row, form, alias, or quantitative data change from this review because the only exact recipe demand outside existing references is zero.
2. Retain `ingredient-ref-custard-powder` and both Nanaimo links. Ask the Québec French reviewer whether `poudre à crème anglaise` should replace or supplement the current `poudre à crème pâtissière`; assess `FLOUR_AND_STARCH` versus `BAKING_MIXIN_AND_PANTRY` as an internal classification choice. Do not equate it with prepared crème anglaise or pastry cream.
3. If a future recipe names a pure powdered egg fraction, model it as a dried form under the corresponding existing egg-white/yolk reference; keep meringue powder and egg-replacement mixes distinct compound products.
4. When a recipe actually calls for the remaining technical ingredients, integrate only the source-qualified identity needed, choose the internal category at that time, and keep ingredient function/product source attached to its evidence. Add no general density, spoon-to-weight factor, reconstitution instruction, or substitute from this packet.
5. Keep Section E open. This report covers only the scoped technical-baking candidates and does not complete the wider Section E checklist.
