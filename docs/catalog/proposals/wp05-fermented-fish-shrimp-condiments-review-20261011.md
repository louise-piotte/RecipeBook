## Boundaries for related products

- **Liquid fish sauce / patis:** retain `ingredient-ref-fish-sauce`. `patis` is already an EN/FR alias there. Philippine sources describe patis as liquid extracted from fermented bagoong; do not put patis on either bagoong paste row.
- **Kapi:** retain the active Thai `ingredient-ref-kapi-shrimp-paste`; the Cambodian visitor guide also distinguishes fermented prahok fish paste from fermented kapi prawn paste. Shared general category is not identity equivalence.
- **Padaek:** retain active `ingredient-ref-padaek` as the Lao identity. It is not an alias for fish sauce, prahok, or ngapi.
- **Sambal belacan / sambal terasi:** prepared chili condiments containing their respective paste ingredients. Do not alias these compound names onto belacan or terasi. A separately reviewed sambal compound remains with its WP05 owner.
- **Ngapi subtypes:** `nga-ngapi` (fish) and `hmyin`/`seinsa ngapi` (shrimp) are research leads, not aliases on a future broad ngapi record. A follow-up should choose whether exact subtype rows are useful; do not create a fish-or-shrimp row whose search label hides the ingredient distinction.
- **Other countries and forms:** this is not a blanket identity for Vietnamese shrimp pastes, Thai pla ra, Cambodian kapi, Indonesian fish ferments, Filipino prepared bagoong, or unqualified fermented seafood. Research each exact product before adding or aliasing it.

## Collision, legacy, ledger, and recipe audits

Exact-key normalization followed `normalizeIngredientSearchKey` in `IngredientSearch.kt`: lower-case with `Locale.ROOT`, apply NFKD, fold `œ`/`æ`/`ß` and `&`, remove combining marks, replace non-alphanumeric runs with spaces, then collapse whitespace. The scan covered all 1,090 bilingual active-reference names and aliases, all 79 bilingual form labels and match terms, and all 75 legacy candidate names and aliases.

- No exact normalized active reference, form, or legacy-name collision was found for any proposed display or alias in the table, including `belachan`, `terasi udang`, `bagoong alamang`, `bagoong isda`, `prahok`, `prohok`, and `prohoc`.
- The five proposed IDs (`belacan`, `terasi`, `bagoong-alamang`, `bagoong-isda`, `prahok`) are absent from active references, legacy candidates, and ledger record IDs. There is no proposed ngapi ID while that family remains held.
- The ledger has no identity records for these five candidates. Existing Kapi and padaek records mention these products only as boundaries; the fish-sauce record states the paste/liquid boundary. The existing sambal-oelek and sambal-matah records mention terasi or sambal types as context, not as an accepted paste identity.
- The proposed source-ID prefix `WP05-FERMENTED-CONDIMENTS-20261011-S01` through `S18` was checked against catalog proposal/batch Markdown and ledger content; no source-ID collisions were found.
- A recursive scan of every ingredient-row string in all 114 manifest-listed recipes (1,123 ingredient rows) found no mention of the named candidates or their reviewed spellings. Two rows mention only generic liquid `fish sauce`: 2 tbsp in `093-riz-frit-fried-rice` and 1 tbsp in `095-satay`. Keep both linked to the existing fish-sauce ID; they are not evidence for these paste identities. No recipe-line link is proposed.

## Numeric, form, conversion, and substitution behavior

For the five proposed identities, use `defaultDensity: null`, `unitMappings: []`, no package yield, no conversions, and no substitution rules. Do not infer kitchen values from fermentation salt ratios, nutrient tables, package sizes, or preparation descriptions. The WorldFish Myanmar report's nutrition table normalizes selected sampled fish pastes to a 15 g tablespoon for reporting; it does not establish a general density or a household tablespoon-to-gram mapping for all ngapi varieties.

No `IngredientForm` is proposed in this packet. Cambodia sources describe prahok sach and prahok cha-oeung product types; their differences include bone inclusion and salt level, but the source's occasional product substitution does not provide a recipe-line ratio. Keep those types as documented research context pending recipe demand and form-schema review.

Do not activate a substitution among the country-named pastes, between fish and shrimp products, or from any paste to liquid fish sauce. Shared salinity/umami use, a market category, or regional descriptions are insufficient. This packet did not run a targeted search for every possible contextual replacement, so an integration should leave substitution research unclaimed or blocked pending evidence rather than state that no substitute exists universally.
