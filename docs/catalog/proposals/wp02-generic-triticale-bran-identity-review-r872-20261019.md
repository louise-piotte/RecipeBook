## Generic identity and bilingual boundary

The proposed English identity is the edible bran fraction separated from triticale grain during milling. A 2024 primary milling study distinguishes coarse and fine triticale-bran fractions from whole grain and flour. Independent food research uses triticale bran as an ingredient in yogurt, and another study examines bran from milled triticale grain in bran-enriched bread. These sources establish a distinct food-ingredient identity; they do not establish one universal bran grade, particle size, process, composition, or retail specification.

OQLF supports the Québec French cereal name **triticale** and defines **son** as the peripheral cereal portion separated during milling. The compositional French display **son de triticale** is proposed as a working name, not represented as an OQLF-approved compound or a confirmed Québec retail label. Keep it provisional until independent Québec terminology review. CFIA's general bran naming rule supports stating the source grain in English; it does not decide the French compound.

Keep animal-feed and distilling fractions, bran-containing grain blends, triticale shorts or mill-feed, extracts, and any specific cultivar or milling grade outside this identity. Do not claim that the research fractions or formulas are universal consumer specifications.
