# WP03 Section G: Chinese niangao and prepared rice-cake boundary

## Packet record

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-11.
- **Status:** proposal-only review. No catalog, ledger, plan, roadmap, test, or other proposal file was changed.
- **Scope:** the open prepared-rice-cake item, with a focused boundary between Chinese New Year niangao, Shanghai-style cooking slices, the active Korean tteokbokki-stick identity, rice flour, rice-paper wrappers, and generic cakes.
- **Recommendation:** **hold active Chinese niangao integration.** Do not add a broad `niangao` / `nian gao` identity or alias. Preserve the existing held Shanghai-slice candidate. Any future sweet New Year cake and Shanghai cooking-slice references need separate evidence and separate identities.

## Open item and prior decisions

The plan's open item is “Rice cakes, Korean tteok, Chinese niangao, and comparable regional prepared starches.” It already records the narrow, identity-only Korean rice-cake sticks for tteokbokki and the held Shanghai-style slice review. The current [Korean stick terminology follow-up](wp03-tteokbokki-rice-cake-sticks-quebec-terminology-review-20261011.md) supports the active reference; the [Shanghai-style slice proposal](wp03-section-f-shanghai-rice-cake-slices-review-20261011.md) holds its candidate pending Québec French product naming.

This review expands the boundary audit to Chinese niangao as a family label. It does not treat `年糕` as one product identity. A Shanghai municipal source calls both glutinous-rice festival cakes and Shanghai's thin oval cooking slices niangao, while distinguishing their rice material and use. Current Canadian listings add another caution: Korean tteok can be sold as both sticks and oval slices. Shape or the English words `rice cake` alone do not establish region or ingredient identity.

| Rice flour and glutinous rice flour | Raw powders are cataloged separately. The Walmart Erawan listing's preparation directions use flour as an input for steaming or baking a cake; that does not turn the flour itself into prepared niangao. |
| Generic cakes, rice-paper wrappers, and rice snacks | A generic cake/dessert phrase, rice-paper wrapper, rice cracker, crispy rice cereal, and puffed or flattened rice have different products and uses. OQLF maps unqualified `gâteau de riz` to `rice pudding`; the live Korean display also contains that phrase, while `galettes de riz` is part of the rice-paper wrapper alias. | Do not use `rice cake`, `gâteau de riz`, or `galettes de riz` as a global Chinese-cake alias. |

The same Chinese term `年糕` can label distinct products. The evidence does not support one broad Chinese niangao identity that covers both sweet festival cakes made from glutinous rice and Shanghai slices sold for cooking. The current Canadian listings also show that Korean oval slices are another distinct product class. A recipe or search term containing “rice cake” would therefore need region, product use, or a similarly discriminating qualifier before it could be assigned.

## Disposition

**Hold active Chinese niangao integration.** Keep `candidate-shanghai-rice-cake-slices` in `needs_clarification` and leave `ingredient-ref-shanghai-rice-cake-slices` unallocated. Create no umbrella niangao reference and add no `niangao`, `nian gao`, `年糕`, `Chinese rice cake`, `Chinese New Year cake`, `rice cake`, `gâteau de riz`, or `galettes de riz` alias. The first two spellings cover distinct Chinese product classes in the reviewed regional evidence; the plain English and French terms collide with the active Korean stick identity, rice-paper-wrapper alias, dessert usage, or other rice products.

If work resumes, evaluate two separate narrow candidates: (1) sweet glutinous Chinese New Year cake, using a current Canadian/Québec prepared-product listing or label that distinguishes it from flour and Shanghai cooking slices; and (2) plain Shanghai-style cooking slices, with direct Québec French package or retailer wording for the ingredient itself. The 2023 Montréal `gâteau du Nouvel An` wording can guide that search but is not enough to set a current bilingual catalog display. Do not add forms, measurements, density, conversions, yields, recipe links, or substitutions until direct product-specific evidence and recipe demand support them.
