## Recommendation

Keep the integrated `ingredient-ref-egusi-seeds` row unchanged as a broad West African culinary/trade seed category, and keep the existing dried-watermelon-kernel identity separate. Add one narrowly named Nigerian condiment candidate, `ogiri-egusi`, as an identity-only `SAUCE_AND_CONDIMENT` entry. Two Nigerian research papers use that name for a fermented melon-seed condiment. The National Academies’ 2006 chapter independently describes fermented egusi-seed seasoning and names it `ogiri-isi` in Nigeria and `avrouda` in Benin; its footnote notes variable dried-shrimp inclusion. This establishes a source-specific regional relation among the names, not a source-neutral synonym set. Keep those names in evidence notes rather than global alias arrays: the catalog search has no region-qualified alias scope, the sources do not test name interchangeability across markets or recipes, and this recipe corpus has no matching demand. The narrow `ogiri-egusi` identity remains justified by the exact product name in the Nigerian studies.

## Québec French evidence

OQLF’s 2021 entry gives `melon d’eau` and `pastèque` as terms for the **fruit** watermelon; it does not define a melon-seed product, egusi, or ogiri. A targeted search of OQLF and TERMIUM-style terminology queries found no egusi/ogiri seed or condiment entry. That limited search is not proof that no Québec usage exists. A Canadian retail listing uses `Egussi – Graine de courge – Melon seeds`, but that is a seller’s product label, not an official term or a verified equivalence.

Therefore keep the current `graines d’egusi` label provisional as in the accepted WP03 review, and mark `ogiri-egusi` in French as a borrowed, provisional display pending independent Québec review. Do not turn `graine de courge`, `graines de melon`, `pastèque`, or `melon d’eau` into aliases for egusi or ogiri-egusi. The existing `graines de melon d’eau séchées` display should receive a separate localization/search review because the shorter generic query currently returns it.
