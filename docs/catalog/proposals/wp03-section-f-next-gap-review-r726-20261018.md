## Identity boundaries and bilingual wording

The product is a formulated ready-to-eat cereal mix; it is not an identity for chia, buckwheat, hulled hemp, dried fruit, cinnamon, or any one grain/seed component. It is distinct from Natural, Blueberry Apple, and Mango Coconut. The manufacturer’s FAQ says Natural contains only chia, hemp, and buckwheat, while Apple Cinnamon adds dried fruit and cinnamon. Keep it separate from Nature’s Path Superseeds & Grains instant oatmeal and from Apple Cinnamon Chex cereal.

The manufacturer provides a current English product name but no French product title. Canadian French retailer pages show names for Apple Cinnamon, Blueberry Apple, and Mango Coconut, but the titles vary between translated descriptors and retained English product wording. Metro Québec’s French title is for Natural only. OQLF supports the generic term `céréales`; it does not validate a product-specific translation. A normalized proposal such as `Céréales au mélange de super graines, pomme et cannelle` is therefore only a possible future display, not an R726 catalog name. The family review should settle parallel French names and whether the shared family phrase belongs in active search terms.
