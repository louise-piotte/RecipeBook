## Candidate identity and disposition

The evidence supports a **candidate culinary candlenut kernel/spice identity** for seed kernels of the accepted species *Aleurites moluccanus* (older culinary and food-composition sources often use *A. moluccana*). It does not support treating every plant name, seed state, extracted product, or traditional preparation as the same catalog item. The identity is suitable for coordinator review, but it is not a safety clearance: the sources reviewed do not establish a transferable home preparation method, and the catalog has no safety field to carry that limitation. Do not add a raw form or imply that an unprocessed kernel is safe to eat.

| `ingredient-ref-candlenut` | `candlenut` | `noix de bancoul` | `NUT_SEED_AND_DRIED_FRUIT` | `candle nut` / `candle nuts` as English spelling variants; `kemiri` as an Indonesia-scoped culinary name. `buah keras` and `kembiri` are Malaysia-scoped names; retain their provenance if considered as aliases. `muncang` is reported as a Sundanese species name, but is not proposed as a global alias. No raw, roasted, ground, paste, oil, or cake form is proposed. |

The Québec French label is provisional. PROSEA gives `noix de Bancoul` as a French common name, and Health Canada’s Natural Health Products Ingredients Database records `Noix de Bancoul` as a French plant-name synonym. The OQLF entry found in this review is for **oil** (`huile de bancoul`, `huile de noix de bancoul`, etc.), not the edible kernel; it does not establish an OQLF-preferred food label. Keep `noix de bancoul` pending independent Québec terminology review. [WP03-CANDLENUT-S02, S04, S05]

The identity candidate is a source-named culinary seed product, not an assertion that all states called candlenut share one composition or safe preparation. PROSEA distinguishes the hard endocarp from the kernel and reports that retail seed for spice is sold with the hard endocarp removed. It also gives a 10–14 g weight for an in-shell “nut,” with 65–70% shell and 30–35% kernel. That supports keeping shell-in material distinct from edible kernel; it does not establish a recipe conversion or safety threshold. [WP03-CANDLENUT-S02]

## Handling and safety boundary

- Queensland Poisons Information Centre describes the plant as potentially toxic and warns that eating the raw kernel can cause abdominal cramps, severe vomiting, and diarrhoea; its page says to seek urgent medical attention if a kernel is eaten. This is a poison-centre warning, not a food-processing standard. [WP03-CANDLENUT-S06]
- Hong Kong Hospital Authority’s toxic-plant atlas lists seeds among the poisonous parts, reports poisoning after raw-seed ingestion, and says certain less-toxic varieties may be eaten in small amounts after “appropriate processing.” It does not define the varieties, processing, or a safe amount. It reports that Hawaiian `ʻinamona` is made from roasted seeds, but does not establish roasting as a generally safe home method. [WP03-CANDLENUT-S07]
- PROSEA describes culinary use and mentions raw or briefly roasted seed, but the same passage calls raw seed slightly poisonous. Given that conflict and the poison-centre warnings, the culinary description is not used as preparation advice. [WP03-CANDLENUT-S02, S06, S07]
- Bilang et al. tested **unbroken seeds** in a laboratory study using wet autoclaving at 121 °C for 5–20 minutes followed by oven drying at 70 °C for 7 hours. Their reported result concerns reduced toxalbumin activity in a hemagglutination assay at the 20-minute treatment. This is a specific lab experiment on one assay endpoint, not a validated home-cooking or food-service protocol and not proof that other hazards are removed. Do not reuse these conditions as recipe instructions. [WP03-CANDLENUT-S08]

**Disposition:** do not assert raw safety, a safe dose, or a cooking/preparation protocol. Roasting and `ʻinamona` are evidence of a named prepared-food context only. A general catalog integration should wait for an authoritative food-grade product/processing specification or a safe product representation that does not imply raw kernels are ready to eat. The Section I checklist item remains open.
