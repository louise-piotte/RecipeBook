`Sauce douce au soja` is the direct Canadian French product name for Lee Kum Kee Sweet Bean Sauce. Lee Kum Kee separately sells a different `Sweet Soy Sauce`, titled `Sauce de soja douce` on its French-Canadian site. Keep these two wordings and products separate; do not add `sweet soy sauce` or `sauce de soja douce` as aliases for sweet bean sauce. [S01–S03]

This is a naming recommendation, not a statement that OQLF has standardized either term. The official Yibin technical specification gives `Yibin Bean Sprout` as an informative translation; because that wording is liable to be read as fresh bean sprouts, do not activate it as an alias. [S11, S13–S14]

## Product variation and unsupported numeric behavior

- **Sweet bean/yellow bean sauces:** Lee Kum Kee’s Canadian Sweet Bean Sauce page lists a product containing fermented soybean paste, sugar, sesame paste, and soy sauce. Its industrial page names one product “Sweet Bean Sauce (Tien Mien Jiang).” A separate LKK Yellow Bean Sauce page says that product is also called Soy Bean Sauce and describes whole fermented soybeans. These are maker-specific product facts; do not encode a universal formula or synonym network. Its package-size, serving, and recommended-use statements are not density or unit-conversion evidence. [S01–S04]
- **Tonkatsu/okonomi:** Bull-Dog’s official catalog lists tonkatsu sauce separately from Worcestershire/chuno, okonomiyaki, takoyaki, and yakisoba sauces. Otafuku calls its product “Okonomi or Okonomiyaki sauce.” These manufacturer categories support distinct identities and one bounded alias, not a shared formula. Otafuku lists product variants, including export vegan and other retail sauce products; vegan status or ingredients must not be inferred from the generic name. [S04–S05, S08, S17]
- **Gochugaru:** the Québec product page describes its named product as coarsely ground flakes and lists pepper origin China with Tae Kyung as maker; a Korean food-export listing for Nongshim Taekyung likewise specifies red pepper from China. A current Canadian retailer collection lists Tae Kyung red pepper powder in coarse and fine grinds and separate coarse/fine red pepper powders from other brands. These examples establish product variation, not a universal grind, Scoville value, or country of agricultural origin for all gochugaru. Do not add coarse/fine forms from this evidence alone. [S09–S10, S19]
- **Zha cai:** the current GH/T 1011-2022 entry is an industry standard for the named 榨菜 product. A current supplier page shows whole, sliced, diced, and seasoned zha cai offerings. Those package variants do not justify a universal form or conversion. [S11–S12]
- **Yibin ya cai:** the official GI specification names Yibin ya cai, gives its Latin transcription, and bounds it to a local product made from named local greens. Its informative English translation `Yibin Bean Sprout` is not recommended as a catalog alias. The specification describes one protected product, not every ingredient sold as *ya cai*. [S13–S14]
- **No numeric behavior is supported for the proposed rows.** Do not add densities, mass-per-volume values, package yields, unit mappings, forms, cooking conversions, or numeric substitution ratios. A product’s package size, a label serving, manufacturer use suggestion, or amount in an unrelated recipe is not a global ingredient measure. The gochugaru retailer’s qualitative “substitute for hot pepper” statement lacks a named recipe context and amount; it does not support a directed substitution rule. No source reviewed supports a global substitution among these candidates or with current generic ingredients.
