CFIA distinguishes **sweeteners** from **sweetening agents**: the former are permitted food additives listed by Health Canada, while the latter are subject to Volume 15 of the Canadian Food Compositional Standards. The current public materials reviewed do not classify the candidate's specific allulose product and use into either route [S02]. The product/process context also matters to novelty: Health Canada describes novel foods to include foods made with a process not previously used for that food that causes a major change, and says novel foods receive pre-market assessment [S06].

Accordingly, the public sources provide no product-specific, conventional-food determination that answers the held WP02 question. The NHP records are a useful narrow boundary finding, but they do not close it. No status is inferred from US GRAS information, Canadian retail listings, or absence from a search/list.

## Exact external determination still needed

First identify a single intended ingredient product, not “allulose powder or syrup” as interchangeable possibilities. The evidence package sent for determination needs the actual producer/product and:

1. **Identity and specification:** D-allulose/psicose identity; the selected physical form (crystalline powder or a particular syrup); assay/purity range; composition, including water or carriers for a syrup; and relevant impurities/by-products and specification limits.
2. **Manufacture:** the real production route and source organism/enzyme or chemical process, process conditions relevant to the resulting ingredient, purification steps, processing aids, and any relevant residues. Do not substitute a generic supplier description for the actual process dossier.
3. **Intended conventional-food use:** whether it is sold for direct/tabletop use or added as an ingredient; named food categories, including whether they are standardized or unstandardized; intended function; proposed use levels; and any target consumer restrictions. “Sweetener” alone is too broad to resolve category-specific conditions.

Request a **written Health Canada Food Directorate classification for that exact product and use**, answering:

- whether the specified material/use is a food additive sweetener requiring an authorization on the List of Permitted Sweeteners and, if so, which foods, maximum levels, and conditions are authorized or what pre-market additive submission is required;
- whether it instead qualifies for use as a non-additive food ingredient or a standardized sweetening agent, and which product standard and restrictions apply; and
- whether that exact ingredient specification and manufacturing process is novel under Division B.28 of the Food and Drug Regulations, or whether a product-specific non-novel determination applies. If novel, obtain the applicable pre-market safety assessment/no-objection before treating it as available for that use.

Health Canada's published process directs novelty questions to the Submission Management and Information Unit (SMIU) with the ingredient's scientific or common name and a thorough description; its pre-submission guidance asks for the product/process description, intended use and formulation, and specific questions. A novelty determination only resolves novelty status; additive and other applicable food requirements still need an answer [S04, S07]. This review did not send that request.

If Health Canada classifies the exact product as a non-additive sweetening agent, or if the intended use includes standardized foods, obtain CFIA confirmation of the applicable Volume 15 standard and any product-specific common-name/label requirements. Health Canada's novelty determination by itself would not settle those questions [S02].

Until that product/use-specific response or matching published Canadian determination is available, retain the bilingual display proposal `allulose / allulose` but do not activate a catalog reference or mark the ingredient approved, prohibited, novel, or non-novel. Keep aliases, forms, recipe links, measures, conversions, yields, and substitutions inactive.
