## Coordinator recommendation

When a recipe calls for bottarga, represent it as one distinct `PROTEIN` product identity, separate from loose fish roe and from the fish species whose ovaries it uses. OQLF defines `poutargue` as roe from mullet, tuna, or other species that is lightly salted, strongly pressed, and dried, and gives English `bottarga` [WP04-ROE-S06]. Sardegna Agricoltura likewise treats mullet and tuna bottarga as members of the product family while noting different sensory and cultural profiles [WP04-BOT-S03]. A broad candidate therefore fits the sources, provided recipe text retains a known species qualifier.

| Candidate | EN display | fr-CA display | Supported search wording | Status and limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bottarga / poutargue | `bottarga (salted, pressed, dried fish roe)` | `poutargue (œufs de poisson salés, pressés et séchés)` | EN: `bottarga`; FR candidates: `poutargue`, `boutargue`, `botarque` | OQLF lists `poutargue` and `boutargue` as preferred terms. TERMIUM Plus also lists French `botarque` in its bottarga entry, but does not mark it preferred; keep it a lower-priority search-alias candidate. OQLF explicitly scopes `botargo` to North Africa, so it is research context, not a global alias. No species is implied by the generic identity. Do not alias raw `mullet roe` or `tuna roe` [WP04-ROE-S06; WP04-BOT-CA-20261016-S01]. |

The proposed search names are product names, not claims that every bottarga is made from the same fish or follows one regional process. If a recipe or package specifically says `bottarga di muggine`, `bottarga di tonno`, or another named source, keep that qualifier in the ingredient line. Do not add these source-qualified names as global aliases or infer that they are interchangeable in all dishes. No recipe in the live bundle requires a species-specific bottarga identity.

## Canadian and Québec terminology and product recheck — 2026-10-16

The OQLF record remains the Québec terminology authority: it gives English `bottarga`, preferred French `poutargue` and `boutargue`, and describes the product as roe that is salted, pressed, and dried. The federal TERMIUM Plus seafood record separately lists French `poutargue` and `boutargue` as correct, and `botarque` as a feminine noun for the same product. I therefore retain `botarque` as a sourced, lower-priority French search-alias candidate if the identity is ever integrated. TERMIUM does not mark it correct or preferred, and the local product listings checked here use `poutargue` / `boutargue` instead.

Keep `botargo` out of a global alias list: OQLF says it is used in North Africa, and the current alias schema cannot preserve that region restriction. One Montréal retailer's French product copy says `botarga` is another spelling, but this isolated seller wording is not enough to establish a French alias; record it only as observed market copy. `caviar italien` / `Italian caviar` also remain excluded even though OQLF lists them, because they create a broad `caviar` picker match alongside the separate caviar identity in Section P.

OQLF also lists `caviar italien` / `Italian caviar`, and a Sardinian manufacturer describes its mullet roe as “Sardinian caviar.” Do not make these catalog aliases: they are less precise than `bottarga`/`poutargue` and can collide with the separate caviar boundary in Section P [WP04-ROE-S06; WP04-BOT-S05]. Do not add bare `caviar`, generic `roe`, `fish roe`, `fish eggs`, or generic `dried roe` as aliases. Do not alias `karasumi`, `tobiko`, `masago`, `ikura`/salmon roe, sea-urchin roe/`uni`, or whole fish.

No active identity is needed without recipe demand. The current recipe audit found none. If accepted later, use one identity-only row with no default density, unit mapping, form, form conversion, or general substitution. The regional forms `whole baffe` and grated bottarga are product presentations, not evidence for a general conversion or for separate recipe identities.

## Substitution evidence — separate from identity

NOAA Fisheries provides one narrow, source-explicit substitution statement: in its skipjack ceviche vinaigrette, the 1 oz ingredient line names tuna bottarga and permits another bottarga product [WP04-BOT-S06]. This is evidence for that recipe context only. It is not a ratio, it does not cover other roe or caviar, and it does not support substituting bottarga for tobiko, masago, ikura, or raw fish roe. No catalog substitution rule is proposed. If species-specific bottarga identities are added later, this one recipe may be recorded as a source-limited contextual observation for the vinaigrette, not generalized to all dishes.
