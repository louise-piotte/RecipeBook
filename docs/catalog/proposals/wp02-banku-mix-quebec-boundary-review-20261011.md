## Recommendation

**Hold a global `banku mix` reference.** A LaSalle, Québec marketplace listing supplies a local English product title, `GAF Banku Mix 1kg`, but gives no French product name, ingredient panel, or process. Ghanaian technical and manufacturer sources support a dry maize/cassava banku preparation mix as one product type, and distinguish it from cooked banku; they cannot establish that the local listing has that same composition or stage. A targeted OQLF search found no dedicated `banku`/`banku mix` food entry. Keep the French display and catalog identity unallocated until a Québec package or bilingual source resolves both. [S01–S05]

| Term or product | Boundary and naming decision |
|---|---|
| `banku mix` / `banku flour` | Hold as a candidate dry, source-named preparation mix. English product wording is attested; no exact Québec French display is established. Do not invent `mélange pour banku` or `farine de banku` as a display or alias. [S01–S05] |
| `banku` | Keep the cooked paste/formed serving outside a dry-ingredient row. Nkulenu separately lists banku flour and prepared/precooked banku balls, so the bare word crosses product stages. [S03] |
| `akple` | Do not alias. The Ghana Food Research Institute report says fermented maize flour can be used for banku, kenkey, and akple; this shared use does not make their finished foods or mixes synonymous. [S01] |
| Corn and cassava components | Keep distinct from active corn-flour, edible cassava-flour, and cassava/tapioca-starch references. A banku blend is not a single-root flour or extracted starch. [S01–S03] |

No catalog delta is proposed: no suggested ID, bilingual display, alias, form, recipe link, or numeric field is allocated while the identity and Québec French wording remain unresolved.

## Evidence limits and product stage

- The Ghana Food Research Institute's 2006 company-specific banku-mix process starts with dried maize, includes soaking/fermentation and drying/milling, combines the resulting material with fermented cassava dough, and packages the mix dry. Its preparation directions then reconstitute the mix with water and cook it to paste. This is a technical case for one enterprise, not a market-wide formula or standard. [S01]
- Oxy Industries describes its Sunny Gold mix as corn grains blended with cassava flour. Nkulenu lists `Banku Flour`, `Banku Flour With Extra Cassava`, and separately a precooked `Banku` pack made from corn dough, cassava dough, water, and salt. These brand pages support a maize/cassava product family and stage distinction, but do not establish the composition or processing of the Québec `GAF` listing. [S02–S03]
- The Québec marketplace's indexed listing identifies seller Marche Ehi at LaSalle, Québec and the product as `GAF Banku Mix 1kg` for $4.59. The indexed item has no French display or composition details; direct page retrieval was unavailable during this review. Treat it as local title evidence only. [S04]
- A separate retailer page calls a ground-millet product `Millet Banku Mix`. It does not provide a manufacturer ingredient panel and is not Québec evidence; retain it only as a weak naming-ambiguity signal, not as proof of a standard millet formulation. [S06]
- The targeted OQLF/GDT query returned no dedicated entry for `banku`, `banku mix`, or `akple`. That absence is not a ruling against using a source name in Québec French. The local listing itself remains English-only. [S05]

## Measurement, form, and substitution limits

- The 2006 report gives one product-specific preparation of **200 g banku mix with 6 dL water**. That is a mass-to-water recipe instruction for the report's enterprise product, not flour density, a volume-to-mass mapping, a transferable reconstitution ratio, or a yield. Keep it source-specific and inactive. [S01]
- Retail package sizes (including the Québec 1 kg listing and Nkulenu's 1.2 kg flour package) are declared package quantities, not unit mappings. [S03–S04]
- Add no default density, unit mapping, form, conversion, yield, or global/contextual substitution. The cooking process connects a dry mix to cooked banku; it does not establish a normalized form conversion for all brands or equivalence with corn flour, cassava flour, cassava starch, fufu, akple, or another swallow. [S01–S03]

## Reopen criteria and no-op boundaries

Reopen only when a Québec package or reliable bilingual product record identifies the exact marketed mix's ingredients and dry/prepared stage, and a Québec French display is supported by actual local use or independent terminology review. Then recheck exact picker collisions and recipe demand against the live package before proposing a source-bounded identity.

This packet leaves the WP02 checklist open and changes no canonical reference, alias, form, density, unit mapping, ledger state, recipe line/link, yield, conversion, substitution, roadmap item, package file, or test. Placali and Agbelima decisions remain untouched.
