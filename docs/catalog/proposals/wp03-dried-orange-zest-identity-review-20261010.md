# WP03 dried-orange-zest identity review

## Scope and coordinator decision

- **Review date:** 2026-10-10
- **Scope:** the OQLF-documented dried orange zest product versus generic citrus zest, lemon zest, whole orange peel, and candied peel.
- **Disposition:** accept one species-qualified identity-only product.
- **Coordinator integration:** add `ingredient-ref-dried-orange-zest` as `dried orange zest / zeste d'orange déshydraté` in `SAUCE_AND_CONDIMENT`. Add no aliases, forms, measures, densities, unit mappings, conversions, yields, or substitutions. No recipe line is linked.

## Catalog and recipe audit

The audit covered 677 active references, 34 forms, 84 legacy candidates, and all 114 manifest-listed recipes (1,123 ingredient rows). The catalog already has generic `citrus zest / zeste d’agrumes` and `lemon zest / zeste de citron`; it has no orange-zest-specific identity. Exact normalized checks found no reference, form, or legacy owner for `dried orange zest` or `zeste d'orange déshydraté`.

Recipe rows include lemon zest in Linzer Cookies, Lemon Crinkle Cookies, Blueberry Cake Filling, and Blueberry Pie, plus one grouped `citrus zest` note in Sous Vide Tuna. None requests dried orange zest or whole dried orange peel. No line is changed or linked.

## Evidence

The [OQLF GDT entry for dried orange zest](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/2299059/zeste-dorange-deshydrate) gives `zeste d'orange déshydraté` and the English term `dried orange zest`. Its note distinguishes *zeste*, the colored aromatic surface, from the full *écorce*; it says labels sometimes conflate *peel* and *zest*, but the French distinction should be maintained. The same note describes the full peel as chiefly edible when candied. This is direct product terminology and a boundary warning; it does not define all dried citrus peel or all candied citrus products.
