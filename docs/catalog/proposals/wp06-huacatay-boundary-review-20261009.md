- **Status:** Integrated as `ingredient-ref-huacatay`; see the coordinator's [identity batch](../batches/wp06-huacatay-identity-20261009/README.md). The scan below records the pre-integration 478-reference snapshot.
- **Recommendation:** Accept one source-named culinary identity, scoped to documented Peruvian food use. The strongest direct food sources identify huacatay as *Tagetes minuta*. INIA’s germplasm database also applies the name to *T. terniflora*, but those records are in a medicinal-plant collection and do not document culinary use. Do not claim that every plant or accession called huacatay is *T. minuta*.
- **Proposed catalog row:** `ingredient-ref-huacatay`; EN `huacatay`; FR `huacatay` (provisional); category `VEGETABLE_AND_AROMATIC`; no aliases, forms, density, unit mappings, measurements, conversions, substitutions, or recipe links.

## Evidence and decision

Peru’s official tourism agency describes huacatay as the aromatic herb in Arequipa’s ocopa. INIA’s aromatic-herb cultivation booklet lists huacatay/quincho under *Tagetes* spp. and names ocopa, pachamanca, ají dishes, picante, and soups as culinary uses, but leaves the species at genus level. A Peruvian government-hosted cultural guide explicitly identifies huacatay as *Tagetes minuta* and says it is used in food. A 2018 Peruvian university thesis likewise identifies huacatay as *T. minuta* in a sauce study and distinguishes it from chincho, which it identifies as *T. eliptica* [sic in the repository title]. Together, these sources support a safe, source-named culinary ingredient identity; they do not establish a universal botanical meaning for the common name.

The INIA germplasm query is a real boundary signal: in its “Plantas Medicinales de Costa” collection, it lists an accession of *T. terniflora* and two accessions of *T. minuta* under “huacatay.” The page does not attach food-use information to those accessions. Kew’s Plants of the World Online treats *T. minuta* and *T. terniflora* as separate accepted species; it does not connect the *T. terniflora* record to Peruvian culinary huacatay. Do not merge the botanical species, and do not infer that the medicinal collection records represent two culinary ingredients.

### Names and boundary

- **English display:** `huacatay`. The reviewed Peruvian food sources use the source name; no authoritative English standard name was found. A borrowed culinary name is safer than translating the name into “Peruvian black mint.” The Peruvian guide’s Spanish variant *menta negra peruana* is not evidence that “Peruvian black mint” is an established English ingredient name, and “mint” can mislead about its plant family.
- **Québec French display:** `huacatay` (provisional). A targeted search of the OQLF GDT and TERMIUM did not surface a preferred Québec French term. Retain the source name rather than presenting an unverified descriptive translation as a Québec term.
- **Aliases:** none recommended. Do not add *Peruvian black mint*, *black mint*, *menta negra peruana*, *Tagetes minuta*, *Tagetes terniflora*, *chincho*, or *quincho*. These are, respectively, an unverified English rendering, a broad/misleading phrase, a Spanish source variant, botanical names, a distinct plant name in the cited food-science study, and a co-listed name in an INIA genus-level heading. The INIA accession spellings and other regional variants remain evidence, not aliases.
- **Scope note:** The row names the culinary product “huacatay.” The research evidence associates documented Peruvian culinary use especially with *T. minuta*; it does not assert that all regional uses of the vernacular name have one taxon.
