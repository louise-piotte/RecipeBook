## Recommendation

**Keep this exact source-named product unallocated pending a product-matched Québec French name and a usable milling description.** Do not add an active identity, alias, legacy candidate, or research-ledger candidate. Preserve the existing whole-wheat-meal, roasted-wheat-flour, cracked-wheat, and wheat-semolina owners unchanged.

Anson Mills currently lists `Colonial-Style Toasted Stone Cut Wheat Meal` as a wholesale product [S01–S02]. That establishes that the name is used for a commercial item. The listing gives no ingredient declaration, wheat variety, kernel fraction, toasting sequence, particle-size range, culinary use, or French name. `Stone Cut` and `Toasted` appear in the title but are not defined by the product page. The same wholesale collection separately lists a `16th Century English-Style Toasted Wheat Chisel`; its presence does not establish that either product is a synonym, form, or substitute for the other [S02].

The Québec terminology that is closest to the phrase is already qualified or belongs to a different product. OQLF pairs `farine de blé entier brute` and `farine de blé complet brute` with English `whole wheat meal` and `coarse whole wheat flour`; it does not approve those terms for this toasted, stone-cut product [reused `WMA7-20261010-S02`]. A Québec mill's `farine de blé torréfiée` is an active roasted-wheat-flour identity, not evidence that `farine` is the French display for Anson's meal [reused `WP02-S127`]. Do not invent `gruau de blé torréfié`, `semoule de blé torréfiée`, or another French translation from the English title.

- The study evaluates a bounded durum-meal/semolina pasta formula. It does not support a general replacement for flour, whole-wheat meal, cracked wheat, semolina, or other grains; do not create a global or contextual substitution rule from it.
