# WP06 — Andean pepper candidate resolution

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-09
- **Status:** Integrated with the boundaries below; coordinator review and verification are recorded at the end of this file and in the [batch README](../batches/wp06-andean-peppers-identity-20261009/README.md).
- **Scope:** Resolve the separate identity candidates ají amarillo and ají panca, their fresh and dried states, source-name aliases, ground/powder products, and pepper pastes. Recommend safe EN and fr-CA display wording.
- **New source-ID prefix:** WP06-ANDEAN-PEPPERS-RESOLUTION-20261009-S. The prefix was checked across the repository and had no prior matches. Existing pepper-review and South American sweep source IDs are reused by reference.

## Resolution

Accept **ají amarillo** and **ají panca** as separate Peruvian culinary ingredient identities. PROMPERÚ gives them separate product profiles, and an INIA-hosted field study describes different pepper types and uses for both. The evidence is sufficient to keep them separate from each other and from generic peppers, chili powder, cayenne, and flakes. It does not justify attaching one botanical species as a universal catalog identity field: the 2019 PROMPERÚ export profile conflicts with the other reviewed species assignments.

Accept **fresh and dried whole-fruit states** for each identity. Use explicit state wording where the source or recipe specifies it. Ají panca is commonly sold dry in PROMPERÚ’s market description, but the same profile and INIA research also document fresh panca; do not infer that every unqualified ají panca line is dried.

Treat pastes, ground products, and powders as distinct prepared presentations, never aliases of the whole pepper. The evidence supports separate ají amarillo and ají panca paste product concepts, but not universal formulations. Defer adding paste identities, conversions, or substitutions until the catalog’s prepared-product scope and formulation boundaries are reviewed.

## Product boundary and evidence

PROMPERÚ’s *Pimientos, rocotos y ajíes: Capsicum del Perú* gives ají panca and ají amarillo separate entries. Its panca description says the pepper is commonly sold dry in Peruvian markets, while its export-presentation list also includes fresh fruit, whole dehydrated fruit, powder, preserves, and paste. The yellow entry identifies the fresh pepper as ají amarillo and the dried product as mirasol; it separately names escabeche and ají cristal. These are product-profile descriptions for Peru, not universal naming rules. [New source S01]

An INIA-hosted 2021 field study separately identifies ají panca and ají amarillo, describes different mature-fruit colors, and reports fresh and dry use for both. Its table identifies the studied panca as Capsicum chinense and amarillo as C. baccatum. A 2019 PROMPERÚ export profile listed both under C. baccatum, while the 2016 PROMPERÚ profile and the 2021 study place panca under C. chinense. Keep the culinary product identities separate, but do not add a species claim to their catalog labels. [Existing review sources S02 and S04; new source S01]

PROMPERÚ’s 2016 profile lists several panca common names, including ají seco del Perú, especial, rojo, and negro. Its panca section describes fresh fruit as purple and dried fruit as dark brown, nearly black. Those descriptors explain why the color-only names are unsafe as global aliases. The profile also describes panca as sold mainly dry in markets, while documenting fresh export presentations. INIA research reports both fresh and dry use. The evidence supports both forms, not a universal assumption about unqualified ají panca. [New source S01; existing review source S02]

The SENASA inspection roster is a material naming limit: it groups ají amarillo with ají escabeche and ají mirasol and groups ají panca with ají limo, ají dulce, and ají habanero in export entries. The roster is a phytosanitary export schedule, not a variety or culinary identity standard. It supports treating these as real trade labels, but does not override the separate pepper profiles or justify catalog aliases. In particular, its unqualified use of mirasol makes an unqualified form match unsafe for now. [New source S03]

The Instituto Nacional de Salud food tables distinguish fresh ají amarillo, fresh ground ají amarillo without salt, dried ají amarillo, and an ají panca entry. The government recipe booklet “Come Sano, Come Pescado” lists a whole ají amarillo alongside separate pasta de ají amarillo and pasta de ají panca ingredients. Compañía Peruana de Salsas lists product-specific paste ingredients, including salt in both pastes and onion and oil in its amarillo paste. Together these sources support the boundary between whole peppers, ground products, and prepared pastes; they do not establish universal paste formulations or conversion data. [Existing review sources S07–S09]

Perú Travel’s papa a la huancaína and carapulcra recipes use ají amarillo and ají panca separately. Their source IDs remain registered in the South American gap sweep as WP06-SOUTHAM-20261009-S02 and S03; they are reused, not re-registered here. The recipes demonstrate use, not a universal form assumption. [Reused SOUTHAM S02–S03]

### Evidence limits

- The strongest product evidence is Peruvian. It does not settle every Andean regional use or every trade label.
- PROMPERÚ and INIA distinguish ají amarillo and ají panca, while a SENASA export roster groups other pepper names with them. Do not treat the roster’s grouped declarations as universal culinary synonyms.
- The sources disagree on panca’s botanical species. No species field, cultivar claim, or species-based alias is proposed.
- Québec French evidence is limited to SAQ’s Peruvian-context piment jaune (ají amarillo) and CIPO goods wording for piment panca. Targeted OQLF searches for ají amarillo, ají panca, and piment panca found no terminology entry; this does not prove that none exists.
- Producer paste ingredients are specific to that producer. No universal paste composition, density, unit mapping, yield, conversion, or substitution is inferred.
- All online sources were accessed 2026-10-09. The proposal was initially research-only; the coordinator integrated the accepted identities and forms and recorded final validation below.
