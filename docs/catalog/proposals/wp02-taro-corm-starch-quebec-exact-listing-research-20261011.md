# WP02 extracted taro-corm starch: Québec exact-listing research

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-11
- **Status:** Research-only proposal. The French display remains unresolved; no catalog or ledger changes are proposed.
- **Scope:** A standalone food-grade starch fraction extracted from edible *Colocasia esculenta* corms. This review checks current Québec/Canadian French product-name evidence and keeps whole-corm flour, fresh taro, taro-flavoured products, and starch from other aroids outside the candidate.
- **Proposal-local source IDs:** `WP02-TARO-QC-EXACT-LISTING-RESEARCH-20261011-S01`–`S06`. This prefix was checked against `docs/` and `app/`; these IDs are not ledger records.

## Recommendation

**Keep `candidate-taro-corm-starch` on hold.** This pass found no Québec/Canadian French retail or manufacturer listing that names standalone extracted *C. esculenta* corm starch. OQLF supports the general plant term `taro` and the distinction between `amidon` and context-specific `fécule`; it does not attest either `fécule de taro` or `amidon de taro` as the product name. Local retail pages found during this pass use taro/root wording for a tuber item or `farine de taro` for a separate flour product.

The missing evidence is a current Québec/Canadian product listing or label whose French name is explicitly paired with a plain extracted food starch from *C. esculenta* corms. A manufacturer specification or package ingredient/source statement is needed where the retail wording does not establish the botanical source. A general French construction, foreign classification, English trademark goods statement, taro-flour listing, or taro-flavoured product does not close that gap.

Do not choose `fécule de taro` or `amidon de taro` as the fr-CA display or activate either as an alias by inference. If naming is resolved later, evaluate a separate `FLOUR_AND_STARCH` identity. Do not reserve or activate a future ID in this proposal.

## Terminology evidence and non-local wording

- OQLF’s [taro entry](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/19466379/taro) [S01], last updated in 1995, lists `taro`, `colocase`, and `colocasie` for the plant/food *Colocasia esculenta*. It does not name a flour or isolated starch.
- OQLF’s [amidon entry](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/8869736/amidon) [S02], last updated in 2026, prefers `amidon` generally and describes `fécule` as a term used in some contexts for starch in tubers or other underground organs. This makes `fécule de taro` linguistically plausible, but the entry does not attest that product name.
- OAPI’s 2025 [BOPI 13MQ/2025](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/BOPI_13MQ2025.pdf), p. 118 [S06], includes `amidon de taro à usage alimentaire` in a French-language commercial classification. It is not a Québec/Canadian product listing and does not identify the botanical source.

Those reviews likewise find no local exact product name. ## Alias and form boundary

- Keep extracted *C. esculenta* starch separate from active `taro / taro` (fresh corm) and `taro flour / farine de taro` (whole-corm flour). The 2024 primary study already cited in the candidate ledger treats flour and starch as separate *C. esculenta* products.
- Do not infer a starch identity or alias from `colocase`, `colocasie`, `dasheen`, or `cocoyam`; those terms may refer to a plant/root and do not establish extracted starch. Keep *Xanthosoma sagittifolium* / malanga starch outside this *Colocasia* candidate.
- Do not conflate the candidate with cassava/tapioca starch, arrow-root starch, taro powder, or taro-flavoured products. The manufacturer comparator above expressly names a different aroid source.
- Add no form, density, unit mapping, kitchen measure, conversion, yield, recipe link, or substitution. The naming review supplies no product-specific numeric or culinary-behavior evidence.

## Disposition

Keep `candidate-taro-corm-starch` at `needs_clarification`. The exact display remains unresolved because no current Québec/Canadian French retail or manufacturer product name has been found for extracted *C. esculenta* corm starch. Reopen only when a local product listing or label gives the French name for the standalone starch and product evidence ties it to the intended corm source. Until then, retain `farine de taro` as a separate identity and add no display, alias, form, numeric behavior, recipe link, conversion, yield, or substitution.
