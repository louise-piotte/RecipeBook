# WP02 A4 whole rye flour identity and grade-boundary review

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-11
- **Status:** Proposal only. Recommend one narrowly scoped, identity-only whole-rye-flour reference. This file changes no catalog, ledger, recipe, test, plan, roadmap, or other artifact.
- **Checklist item:** A4, `Resolve rye by useful extraction/grind`. The general rye-flour row and the separate coarse rye-meal row are already integrated; whole-grain wording remains explicitly open.
- **Scope:** Whole-grain rye flour as a named milling product, its boundary with generic rye flour and rye meal, and whether broader white/light, medium, and dark grades have stable enough definitions and Québec French names for separate identities.
- **Proposal-local source IDs:** `WP02-RYE-GRADES-20261011-S01` through `S07`. These IDs are local to this proposal and are not ledger entries.

## Recommendation

**Accept one source-bounded identity-only candidate:** `ingredient-ref-whole-rye-flour`, displayed as **whole rye flour / farine entière de seigle**, in `FLOUR_AND_STARCH`. A Québec rye mill sells a product under the exact French name and its English page calls the same item `Complete Rye Flour`; the manufacturer says it is made from whole grains. A current certification directory lists the maker's paired name as `Farine de Seigle entière du Québec / Whole rye flour`. These sources support a useful whole-grain flour identity and a Québec-attested French display. They do not establish a standardized extraction percentage, ash threshold, or grind size. [S02–S04]

Keep `ingredient-ref-rye-flour` (`rye flour / farine de seigle`) as the general identity. Keep `ingredient-ref-rye-meal` (`rye meal / gruau de seigle`) as the source-defined coarse product. Do not move `pumpernickel flour` or `pumpernickel meal` from the rye-meal row: some suppliers use those terms for whole-grain rye flour, while other sources distinguish whole rye flour from coarse rye meal. The market evidence does not support global synonymy. [S04, S06–S07]

**Hold separate white/light, medium, and dark rye-flour identities and aliases.** One current mill distinguishes these classes by relative bran content, but another producer warns that dark rye definitions vary and lack a standard classification. This review found no Québec product source establishing consistent fr-CA names and matching class boundaries for those tiers. Keep those exact product grades source-specific until such evidence exists. [S01, S06–S07]

## Identity and naming boundary

The existing OQLF entry prefers `farine de seigle` and pairs it with English `rye flour`. Its note recognizes light- and dark-colored rye flour but does not define separate classes or name a whole-grain rye-flour product. The active row therefore remains a sound general identity, while the Québec mill's separate whole-grain product can be represented without converting that qualifier into a generic alias. [S01]

The Québec manufacturer's French product page is titled `Farine entière de seigle` and says the flour is made from whole grains; the paired English page names `Complete Rye Flour` and repeats the whole-grain description. It identifies the producer in Coteau-du-Lac, Québec. The wording is direct Québec product evidence, not an OQLF ruling or a national definition. The manufacturer's page does not specify a sieve size, extraction percentage, ash content, or a whole-grain threshold. [S02–S03]

The Natural Food Certifiers directory separately lists the same mill's `Farine de Seigle entière du Québec / Whole rye flour` and `Farine Pumpernickel / Pumpernickel flour`. This corroborates that the mill has separately named product listings, but the directory does not describe either product's grind or prove that all products sold as whole rye flour are distinct from pumpernickel meal. Do not discard the alias overlap in other markets: Ardent Mills presents a `Rye Meal Pumpernickel` product as whole rye flour, and King Arthur says pumpernickel flour may also be called rye meal or whole rye flour. [S04, S06–S07]

For the proposed row, keep the scope to flour marketed as whole-grain rye flour. Do not broaden it to rye meal, coarse rye flour, pumpernickel, cracked rye, rye chops, rye flakes, or rye berries. The source-specific French display `farine entière de seigle` is preferable here because it is the exact Québec mill wording. Keep the retailer's `farine de seigle intégrale` out of the alias array until a Québec terminology reviewer or matching product-level source establishes that it names the same whole-grain class rather than a T130 or other source-specific grade. [S02–S05]

## Light, medium, and dark rye flour grades

Ardent Mills currently presents white, medium, and dark rye flours in one product family. It describes white rye flour as refined to remove bran and germ, and says medium and dark flours contain more bran than white rye flour. This is a useful manufacturer taxonomy, but it gives no common extraction or ash thresholds for the medium and dark classes. [S06]

King Arthur's rye-flour guide cautions that `dark rye flour` varies between producers: some products are whole grain while others sift out portions of bran or contain little endosperm. Its page also uses `pumpernickel flour`, `rye meal`, and `whole rye flour` as overlapping labels for a whole-kernel product. The guide supports keeping broad grade and synonym claims bounded; it is not a Québec terminology source or a cross-industry standard. [S07]

OQLF's general rye-flour entry mentions light and dark flour but does not establish their processing thresholds or provide matching Québec display names. The sources reviewed do not justify adding universal rows for `white rye flour`, `light rye flour`, `medium rye flour`, or `dark rye flour`, or converting `white/light` and `medium/dark` into forms of the generic rye-flour row. A future review can add source-qualified identities if a Québec product or a reliable standard gives the class definition and bilingual product label. [S01, S06–S07]

## Measurements, forms, conversions, yields, and substitutions

- **Density and unit mappings:** leave `defaultDensity` null and `unitMappings` empty. The mill's 20 kg commercial sack and the Québec retailer's 1 kg, 5 kg, and 20 kg pack options are package sizes, not household measures or density observations. [S02–S05]
- **Forms:** add no `IngredientForm`. Whole-grain milling scope is a named product identity. Do not create `whole_grain`, light, medium, dark, or pumpernickel forms under generic rye flour.
- **Conversions and yields:** add none. No source provides a recipe-ready cup weight, mass-volume conversion, milling recovery yield, or conversion between generic rye flour, whole rye flour, and rye meal. A statement about water absorption or a 20 kg bag is not such evidence. [S02–S06]
- **Substitutions:** add no global or contextual substitution. Supplier baking guidance and the qualitative flavor/texture descriptions do not establish a transferable rye-grade ratio or equivalence. Existing data does not justify converting whole rye flour to rye meal or to refined rye flour.
- **Recipe links:** add none. The bundled corpus has no matching rye flour or whole-rye-flour ingredient line.

## Final disposition

**Accept** a source-bounded, identity-only `whole rye flour / farine entière de seigle` candidate, with only the exact manufacturer English title `complete rye flour` as an English alias. **Hold** general white/light, medium, and dark rye-flour grade rows and their proposed Québec French grade names. No measurements, density, forms, conversions, yields, substitutions, or recipe links are supported by this evidence.
