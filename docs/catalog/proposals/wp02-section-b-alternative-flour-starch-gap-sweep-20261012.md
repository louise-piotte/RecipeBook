# WP02 Section B — cassava-flour French alias review

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-12.
- **Status:** Proposal only. No shared catalog, ledger, checklist, roadmap, workflow, recipe, or generated file was changed.
- **Scope:** One naming gap on the existing `ingredient-ref-cassava-flour` identity: whether generic Québec French `farine de manioc` is a safe alias for `edible cassava flour / farine comestible de manioc`. This packet does not add a new identity and does not reopen the cassava-flour/tapioca-starch product boundary.
- **Source IDs:** `WP02-SECTION-B-MANIOC-FLOUR-ALIAS-20261012-S01` through `S08`. The prefix was absent from the live ledger and proposal corpus before this packet was drafted; these IDs are proposal-local, not current ledger entries.

## Recommendation

**Accept `farine de manioc` as a French alias for the existing `ingredient-ref-cassava-flour` row.** OQLF’s GDT lists this exact Québec French term for powder obtained by dry-grinding cassava roots and pairs it with English `manioc flour` and `cassava flour`. Codex defines edible cassava flour as dried cassava chips or paste milled and sifted to separate fibre, and gives `farine comestible de manioc` as the standard’s French product name. Current Québec and Grand Montréal retail listings use `farine de manioc` for manioc-flour products; the Grand Montréal listing pairs it with the English `Cassava Flour` title. Together these sources support a generic search alias, while keeping the canonical Codex display unchanged. [S01, S03, S06–S07]

This is a generic root-flour alias only. Do not add `farine de manioc fermentée`, `farine de manioc fumée`, or other process-qualified names to this row or to a regional candidate. Preserve the separate source-named lafun and pupuru rows and the existing held boundaries for kokonte, agbelima, fufu/foufou, and other regional cassava preparations. [S06; see the overlapping reviews below]

Keep wet-extracted cassava starch separate. OQLF defines `fécule de manioc` as an industrial product extracted from manioc roots by a wet process, lists `cassava starch` and `tapioca starch` in English, and marks `tapioca flour` as an obsolete associated term. OQLF’s 2025 `tapioca` entry describes the starch-derived food as powder, flakes, small balls, or pearls. Do not add `fécule de manioc`, `tapioca`, `tapioca flour`, or `farine de tapioca` as aliases for cassava flour. [S04–S05]

## Identity and Québec French evidence

OQLF’s GDT entry for `farine de manioc` defines the term as powder obtained by dry-grinding cassava roots and gives English `manioc flour` and `cassava flour`. That is the same product family as the active edible cassava-flour reference. The entry attributes its term to AFNOR (1979), so it supports Québec terminology and a broad product class, not a current process standard. [S01]

Codex CXS 176-1989 supplies the narrower food-product boundary: its French version defines edible cassava flour as dried cassava flakes or paste that are pounded, milled, or ground and then sifted to separate fibre; the standard’s stated label name is `farine comestible de manioc`. It treats this product as flour from processed edible cassava, not wet-extracted starch. This supports retaining the current canonical display while accepting the shorter OQLF headword as a search alias. [S03]

The GDT also has an older `farine de manioc` entry that describes variation in fineness and water-absorption/cohesion among manioc flours. Treat that as a warning against assuming one generic physical behavior, not as a numeric measurement. A Grand Montréal seller currently titles a cassava-flour product `SAC – Cassava Flour – Farine De Manioc`; a Québec City retailer lists `Farine de manioc (Cameroun)`. These local labels support the exact French wording but do not disclose enough package composition or processing to define every sold product. [S02, S06–S07]

## Measurement, form, and substitution findings

The current cassava-flour row has `defaultDensity: null`, `unitMappings: []`, and no linked `IngredientForm`. Keep all three facts. The reviewed sources provide no general g/mL density or mass-per-cup figure for this flour. The old OQLF note explicitly describes variable grind fineness and water absorption/cohesion; it is not a kitchen measure, hydration ratio, or basis for a unit mapping. [S02]

Otto’s Naturals currently describes its own organic, single-ingredient cassava flour as usable in most recipes at 1:1 by weight, or with 25% less flour volume where a recipe calls for cups. This is manufacturer advice for that branded product and does not establish a general cassava-flour substitution rule, a density, or an app unit conversion. Keep it source-specific; do not add a global or contextual substitution. The current ledger `measurementState` and `substitutionResearchState` remain `not_yet_researched` because this alias packet is not a full quantitative or recipe-context review. [S08]

No form or recipe link is justified. Flour’s dry state is already inherent in the ingredient identity; the review does not establish a cassava-flour preparation form, a flour-to-starch conversion, or bundled recipe demand.
