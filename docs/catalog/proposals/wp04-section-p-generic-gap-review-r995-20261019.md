# WP04 Section P — generic fish-cheeks identity gap review (R995)

- **Review and source-access date:** 2026-10-19.
- **Status:** Proposal-only. This file recommends one generic identity; no catalog, form, ledger, plan, roadmap, recipe, or generated file was changed.
- **Open checklist anchor:** The Section P finfish line remains open: “Salmon, cod, pollock, halibut, sole, flounder, turbot, sea bass, sea bream, snapper, grouper, tilapia, catfish, carp, mackerel, sardine, anchovy, tuna, bonito, eel, and regional fish” ([global catalog plan, line 7333](../../RecipeBook-global-ingredient-catalog-plan.txt#L7333)). The broader Section P parent remains open as well.
- **Candidate:** Generic edible fish-cheek flesh used as food, across species where the cut is marketable or harvested.
- **Recommendation:** Add one identity-only PROTEIN reference, fish cheeks / joues de poisson, with only the singular inflection aliases fish cheek / joue de poisson. Add no species-specific forms, state, measurement, conversion, yield, recipe link, or substitution.
- **Scope:** One generic culinary seafood identity gap. No brand, product-line, flavour-family, SKU, or retailer-variant roster was researched.

## Decision and boundary

The current catalog has no generic fish-cheek identity. A bounded generic reference is supported by bilingual terminology, a fish-marketing regulation that defines cheeks generically as a fish form, and culinary evidence applying fish cheeks to multiple saltwater and freshwater species. This fills a distinct fish-part gap without asserting that every fish species has a harvestable cheek cut.

This recommendation revisits the generic boundary left open by the [Atlantic cod cheeks hold (R65)](wp04-section-p-atlantic-cod-cheeks-cut-form-boundary-review-20261013-round65.md) and the [Atlantic halibut cheeks hold (R551)](wp04-section-p-next-gap-review-r551-20261017.md). Those reviews correctly held a single species-specific form because it would have made broad cheek/joue searches look species-specific. R65 also explicitly declined to promote a generic fish-cheek identity on its evidence then reviewed. R995 addresses that separate generic question using the Manitoba regulation’s species-neutral definition and a culinary article documenting cheeks from several fish species. It does not overturn either species-specific hold or transfer their product evidence.

Keep Atlantic cod cheeks and Atlantic halibut cheeks unallocated as forms under their species parents. Do not add cod cheeks, joues de morue, or halibut cheeks as aliases of the generic reference: the corpus and reviewed sources do not support using those qualified terms as synonyms for a species-neutral identity. The generic display identifies the part across species; it does not establish a shared size, texture, cooking time, yield, state, or interchangeability.

Keep fish cheeks distinct from fish heads, fish bones, fish frames, fish trimmings, fish stock, fish fillets, and every named fish species. Existing references and evidence remain intact. The generic row would be another culinary part identity, not a parent species, form conversion, or substitute for those entries.

## Measurement assessment

Neither the OQLF terminology entry nor the Manitoba fish-form definition supplies a kitchen measure. The external recipe amounts are scoped:

- Taste of Nova Scotia uses 3–4 pieces of halibut cheek for two servings in that recipe.
- Wisconsin Sea Grant uses ½ lb of frozen lake-trout cheeks in the author’s recipe.

Neither establishes a generic per-cheek mass, common piece size, standard serving, edible-weight basis, density, volume measure, package yield, cooking or thawing yield, or count-to-mass conversion. The article’s scallop-size comparison is not a unit mapping. Keep measurements absent: no observation, household measure, conversion, default density, or unit mapping. If the identity is later added to the ledger, record that no supported generic measure was found without promoting either recipe amount to a reusable value.

## Context-scoped substitution assessment

The Wisconsin author says the lake-trout-cheek dish was prepared based on a halibut-cheek recipe. That is a bounded example of adapting one pan-cooked cheek recipe to a different cheek species. It does not state a reusable exchange ratio, demonstrate every preparation method, or establish that fillets, steaks, scallops, heads, bones, or other seafood can replace cheeks. The Taste of Nova Scotia halibut recipe supplies no substitution instruction.

Do not encode a global or contextual substitution rule. In this proposal both species’ cheek portions belong to the same generic identity, and the single author adaptation does not define a general rule, scope, amount, or warning behavior between catalog identities or forms. If the system later needs species-specific cheek substitutions, review source evidence for that rule and its exact role, method, and amount separately. No recipe-line substitution is supported here.

## Remaining gaps and bounded recommendation

- The bundled recipes contain no fish-cheek demand.
- Atlantic cod and Atlantic halibut cheek forms remain held; this proposal does not allocate their species-qualified terms.
- Sources identify several fish species with harvestable cheeks, not every fish or one universal size, state, composition, or preparation.
- Joues de poisson is a descriptive French display. No exact OQLF-normalized multiword term or current Québec retail label was established.
- No generic measurement, form, conversion, yield, or substitution is supported.
- Section P remains open.

Recommend adding only the identity-only ingredient-ref-fish-cheeks row described above. Preserve all current entries and source ownership. Keep source-specific amounts and recipe language scoped to their cited recipes. Do not create any form, measure, conversion, yield, recipe link, or substitution from this review.
