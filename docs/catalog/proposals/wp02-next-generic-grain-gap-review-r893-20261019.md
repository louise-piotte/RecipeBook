## Recommendation and identity boundary

Add generic `finger millet bran` as the edible seed-coat fraction separated from finger millet (*Eleusine coracana*) during milling. A primary biscuit study describes finger-millet seed-coat matter as edible and as a milling by-product, then evaluates it in composite-flour biscuits. A separate primary study uses the exact regional ingredient name `ragi bran`, identifying its source as *E. coracana*. These sources support a distinct, source-grain-qualified milling ingredient, although the food-use evidence is specialized and does not establish common retail availability or broad everyday use. [R893-S01–S02]

Treat `finger millet seed coat` and `finger millet seed coat matter` as source-attested English names for this same ingredient. Do not add the narrower study treatments `native`, `malted`, or `hydrothermally treated` as aliases or separate active forms. The study formulations and outcomes remain source-specific. [R893-S01, R893-S03]

Keep this ingredient separate from:

- finger millet grain and finger millet flour, including the existing flour identity displayed in French as `farine de ragi`;
- the bran or seed-coat fractions of foxtail millet, proso millet, rice, wheat, oats, rye, barley, corn, sorghum, triticale, buckwheat, and spelt;
- whole finger-millet flour, refined finger-millet flour, malted grain, and isolated dietary fibre or polysaccharides;
- African finger millet, *Eleusine coracana* subsp. *africana*, and other *Eleusine* species unless a source explicitly establishes their food identity and milling fraction for this catalog.

The 2011 biscuit study uses source- and process-specific seed-coat fractions and formula percentages. Its variety, extraction yield, composition, texture, sensory observations, and biscuit inclusion amounts do not establish a universal bran grade, recipe ratio, household measure, density, yield, substitution, or nutritional claim. The 2012 ragi-bran paper extracts polysaccharides for laboratory analysis; those extraction procedures and results are not culinary behavior. A 2022 review describes the ingredient as underused and notes limited food-formulation research, so this proposal records a source-backed generic identity, not a claim that the ingredient is widely sold or commonly used. [R893-S01–S03]

The proposed French display `son d’éleusine cultivée` uses the current French crop name for *E. coracana*; no Québec terminology source reviewed here attests this exact bran compound. Keep it provisional pending Québec terminology review. Do not add bare `éleusine`, `millet`, `son`, or `bran` as aliases. [WP03-S193]

## Limitations and next review boundary

1. The direct culinary evidence is a single-cultivar biscuit study. It supports a real food ingredient but does not establish common current retail availability or broad everyday use.
2. The reviewed literature sometimes says “seed coat” and sometimes “bran.” The alias boundary here follows the direct finger-millet sources and must not be transferred to unrelated cereal brans.
3. The French compound is descriptive and provisional; the cited French source supports the crop name only.
4. Native, malted, and hydrothermally treated sample data, yields, compositions, recipe proportions, sensory outcomes, and laboratory extraction results remain source-specific. No measures, density, package relationship, cooking yield, substitution, or health claim is proposed.
5. Further brand-, product-line-, flavour-family-, SKU-, or retailer-variant-only work remains out of scope because commercial-product counts do not add ingredient diversity. Preserve existing branded rows and product-scoped evidence; require an explicit transferability review before applying any product-specific claim to a generic identity.
