# WP04 proposal — banded cricket powder identity and boundary review

**Scope:** the human-food powder candidate made from whole banded crickets, *Gryllodes sigillatus*. This review addresses the edible-insect protein subfamily assigned under WP04 Section Q. Pet food, livestock feed, supplements, whole insects, extracts, and other insect powders are boundary cases, not part of the candidate.

## Refreshed repository and recipe check

- The current plan still marks the broader edible-insect and insect-flour checklist open. It separately marks an identity-only *Acheta domesticus* house-cricket powder reference integrated.
- The bundled manifest currently lists **114 recipes**. A scan of every manifest-listed recipe file counted **1,123 ingredient rows**. A case-insensitive scan of the full recipe JSON text and ingredient rows for cricket, *Gryllodes*, *Acheta*, banded cricket, insect, and the English/French powder and flour terms found **zero** matches. There is no recipe demand or recipe link to resolve this candidate.
- The active catalog has one exact cross-species entry: **ingredient-ref-house-cricket-powder**, displayed as **house cricket powder / poudre de grillon domestique**, with empty alias arrays. No *Gryllodes sigillatus* or banded-cricket reference exists. The generic queries “cricket powder” and “poudre de grillon” are not exact current aliases, but they occur within the English and French house-cricket display names and would be ambiguous in search.
- No cricket-related form or legacy-import candidate was found. The 76 legacy candidates contain no cricket, insect, *Gryllodes*, or *Acheta* name/key.
- The prior *Acheta domesticus* proposal and its live ledger record already document the separate *Gryllodes sigillatus* Health Canada determinations as a reason to exclude generic cricket aliases. No other banded-cricket proposal was found in the proposal or batch folders.

## Evidence and disposition

Health Canada’s current non-novel determinations list includes four separate *Gryllodes sigillatus* entries dated 2017-04-18: banded-cricket flour; cricket powder described as a powder made from whole crickets; ground banded crickets described as a powder from whole ground crickets; and roasted banded crickets described as a whole food. Each entry gives history of safe use as its rationale. The French list names the powder “Poudre de grillon (*Gryllodes sigillatus*)” and also uses “grillon domestique des tropiques” in its flour, ground-cricket, and roasted-cricket entries.

That is a species- and product-state-specific non-novel determination for food. Health Canada says listed items did not undergo a food-use safety assessment, remain subject to other applicable food laws, and are covered only as described. The same page says extraction is not a minimal process and is excluded from the minimally processed category. These points do not determine whether a different extract or concentrate is novel; they do rule out transferring this whole-cricket powder determination to an extracted protein.

A current Ontario manufacturer, Entomo Farms, documents a human-food cricket powder made from 100% *G. sigillatus*: whole crickets are inspected, rinsed, roasted, cooled, ground, and packed at its Norwood facility. It lists the product for Canadian retail sale and provides English and French consumer product pages. Its current French wording is “poudre de protéine de grillon” / “poudre de grillon,” with the species stated in the product description. This supports a real Canadian food product and process; it does not make the manufacturer’s generic label unambiguous in this catalog, establish a species-specific CFIA label approval, or prove compliance of every lot.

CFIA says edible insects, including whole, dried, powdered, and flour products, are sold as foods in Canada and must meet food requirements; imports, exports, and interprovincial sales are subject to the Safe Food for Canadians Regulations. Its common-name guidance requires a principal-display-panel common name for prepackaged food and generally requires consumer-prepackaged food names in English and French. Where no prescribed name applies, the name should be generally known or specifically and accurately describe the food. I found no species-specific CFIA prescribed common name or CFIA approval of the proposed catalog display in the sources reviewed.

**Disposition: an active identity-only PROTEIN reference is justified** for human-food powder made from whole *G. sigillatus* crickets. Use the species-qualified catalog displays **banded cricket powder / poudre de grillon domestique des tropiques**. These are editorial catalog names, not verbatim package labels: the English display combines Health Canada’s “banded cricket” species name with its powder determination; the French display combines the official French species name used in the same list with the product state. I found no OQLF entry for this species, so the French display remains open to independent Québec terminology review. That terminology caveat does not require holding the identity or its Canadian food novelty status.

Do not add generic “cricket powder,” “cricket flour,” “insect powder,” “protein powder,” “poudre de grillon,” or “farine de grillon” aliases. The current house-cricket reference already causes a generic-search collision, and Health Canada separately names multiple species and product states. Do not alias “banded cricket flour” to the powder: Health Canada lists flour separately, and the current *G. sigillatus* manufacturer product reviewed here is sold as powder, with no current product source equating the two labels. Keep the scientific name in identity evidence rather than treating it as a common-name alias.

## Product and regulatory boundaries

- **Human food:** The candidate is the manufacturer-documented food-grade powder for food use. Health Canada’s determination concerns food or food ingredients only as described; CFIA’s edible-insect page describes general food obligations, not blanket clearance for every insect product.
- **Pet food, treats, feeder insects, and livestock feed:** Do not merge those intended uses into the human-food identity. Entomo Farms separately markets cricket powder and whole crickets to pet-food and feed formulators, including products from the same species. The supplier’s shared species/process does not make a feed product a human-food item. CFIA’s livestock-feed requirements are administered under a separate feed framework; the feed source reviewed here is about livestock feed and is not applied to pet food.
- **Whole roasted insects:** Health Canada lists roasted whole banded crickets separately, and Entomo Farms distinguishes whole roasted crickets from the ground powder. Do not alias or link whole insects to the powder reference.
- **Flour versus powder:** Health Canada lists *G. sigillatus* flour separately from powder made from whole crickets. Only powder is proposed here; do not infer that every “cricket flour” product has the target composition or process.
- **Other insect powders:** Keep *Acheta domesticus* house-cricket powder separate; the current catalog has that active identity already. Keep mealworm, locust, and other insect species separate as well. “Cricket” or “insect” alone does not identify this species.
- **Extracts and concentrates:** Health Canada’s list limits its entries to their described scope and says extraction is not minimal processing. As a product-category example, C-fu Foods distinguishes whole-cricket flour/powder from textured insect protein and a more water-soluble insect protein concentrate. That manufacturer describes its flour as *Acheta domesticus*, so it is a boundary example only, not evidence about the status or composition of a *Gryllodes* extract.
- **Supplements and generic protein powders:** No *G. sigillatus* supplement product or supplement authorization was identified in this review. Health Canada treats food/Natural Health Product classification as a product-specific question that considers representation, intended use, composition, and format. Do not use a food non-novel entry as NHP approval, create a generic supplement identity, or equate this ingredient with whey, soy, pea, or other protein powders.

## Measures, forms, composition, yields, and substitutions

- The proposed identity is already for powder; add no separate “powdered” form. Add no whole-cricket or roasted-whole form to this row.
- Entomo Farms reports **about 67 g protein per 100 g** for its current product specification and a **level tablespoon of about 10 g**. These are source-specific manufacturer observations. If the latter is recorded in a future ledger update, qualify it to this product and retain “about”; do not activate a general tablespoon-to-gram mapping, density, or cross-brand conversion. No recipe row supplies an amount to apply.
- The manufacturer lists retail packs of 113 g and 454 g. These are package sizes, not recipe yields or general conversion data. Its reported composition, including protein and fat, must not be generalized to other species, defatted products, or extracts.
- The manufacturer advises that its powder is not a one-to-one flour substitute and suggests beginning with roughly one tenth of a recipe’s flour or dry mix. Treat that as product-specific recipe guidance, not a safe or general substitution rule. Add no substitution to flour, meat, dairy, egg, whey, or another insect ingredient.
- Add no recipe links, forms, measures, density, unit mappings, conversions, yields, or substitution rules in the proposed catalog change.
