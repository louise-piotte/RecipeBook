## Matsumae-zuke evidence and limits

MAFF's English regional-cuisine page describes Matsumae-zuke as a Hokkaido preserve built around shredded dried squid and kelp, and says recipes vary by region and household. Its published recipe is labelled four servings and lists one dried squid, 60 cm kelp, two or three herring roe, a little yuzu and salt, one cup soy sauce, and three to five tablespoons each of mirin and sake. The page describes marinating for about one week. These are quantities and method for that recipe only; the page does not report finished mass, portion mass, yield, cup convention, or a transferable recipe formula. [Existing ledger source WP05-SECTIONW-MATSUMAE-ZUKE-R1062-20261019-S01.]

The Japanese MAFF source describes the identity, ingredient and preparation variation, and an arrangement recipe that uses 100 g Matsumae-zuke on silken tofu. That recipe does not specify a serving count or a finished Matsumae-zuke yield. The 100 g amount is a source-specific topping quantity, not a standalone portion standard. Do not turn it into 100 g per person. [Existing ledger source WP05-SECTIONW-MATSUMAE-ZUKE-R1062-20261019-S02.]

MEXT Food Composition Database food code 18023 is listed as prepared/circulating foods / Japanese cuisine / other / Matsumae-zuke / soy-sauce pickled. Its standard table gives nutrient composition on an edible-portion mass basis. That basis describes nutrient values for the food code; it does not provide a household portion, volume measure, density, source recipe yield, or count-weight relationship. It also does not establish one formulation for every Matsumae-zuke preparation. Keep this source confined to the exact food-code row.

No Matsumae-zuke measurement observation is supported by these pages. Do not infer ingredient conversions from the source recipe's household units or use the tofu topping amount as a generic serving.
