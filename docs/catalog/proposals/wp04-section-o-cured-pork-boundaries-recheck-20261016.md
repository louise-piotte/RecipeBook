# WP04 Section O cured-pork identity boundaries — recheck 2026-10-16

- **Packet type:** proposal-only evidence and catalog audit. This file is the sole proposed change from this review.
- **Scope:** bacon, pancetta, guanciale, salt pork, ham, prosciutto, jamón, and closely related regional pork-product names used to test boundaries.
- **Disposition:** retain the five integrated identities and their current aliases; keep broad salt pork and unqualified jamón held. Keep bacon de dos/back bacon and named regional hams outside broader identities' aliases. No recipe or catalog edit is proposed.
- **Audit snapshot:** 1,595 active ingredient references; 122 forms; 61 legacy candidates; 1,940 research-ledger records; 114 manifest-listed recipes and 1,123 ingredient rows.
- **Source IDs:** proposal-local IDs WP04-CURED-PORK-RECHECK-20261016-S01 through S13 were checked for collision in docs/catalog before writing.

## Regional naming and evidence limits

- Belly bacon, loin back bacon, rolled-and-dried pancetta, and jowl guanciale have different source-attested boundaries. Shared species or curing language does not make them aliases or interchangeable recipe forms.
- Québec terminology supports both bacon américain and bacon canadien, but they name different cuts in the cited OQLF entries. Only bacon américain remains the current alias of ingredient-ref-bacon.
- OQLF’s petit salé entry gives a Québec French naming path for salt pork and a cooked-broth product note. CFIA’s separate use of “salt pork” is naming evidence only. Neither source resolves a broad raw pantry ingredient identity. The earlier salt-pork follow-up records two Québec retail listings, but refresh attempts on 2026-10-16 returned 403 and cache-miss responses; those listings are not treated as newly verified current products here.
- Ham is a broad hind-leg identity, while prosciutto is an Italian dry-salted raw ham and the Spanish sources define qualified products. The 2026 OQLF Black Forest ham entry also cautions that Québec/Canadian and European naming and typical products differ. None of these regional names should be collapsed into ham or into each other.
- The reviewed sources establish names and bounded product descriptions. They do not establish recipe equivalence, safe handling, cooking state beyond the named source’s product description, storage, cure chemistry, or substitutions.
- No source gap changes an accepted identity. The remaining naming gap is OQLF terminology for guanciale and unqualified jamón; the catalog keeps guanciale’s source name and holds unqualified jamón pending a relevant local request.

## Proposal outcome

Retain the active identities and aliases for bacon, pancetta, guanciale, ham, and prosciutto as currently stored. Keep broad salt pork and unqualified jamón unresolved. Keep back bacon and Black Forest ham as qualified product boundaries, with no new identity or alias in this review because the recipe audit found no demand. Do not relink recipes or propose forms, numeric conversions, yields, or substitutions.
