# WP03 Section G — Naan, roti, and paratha identity review

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-11

**Scope:** Naan, roti, and paratha as prepared breads used as recipe ingredients. Chapati is included only as a boundary comparator.
**Disposition:** Promote three separate identity-only bread references; do not merge them with each other, chapati, atta, pita, lavash, or generic flatbread. The current recipe corpus has no matching ingredient lines, so no recipe links change.

OQLF lists `nan` and `naan` as preferred French terms and as English terms [S01]. | EN `nan`; fr-CA `nan`. The names follow Québec use of the borrowed product words: OQLF directly recognizes *nan/naan*, and a Québec recipe title directly supports *pain naan* as the fr-CA display [S01, S04]. Montréal menu labels use *roti* and *paratha* in their French bread sections, and a Montréal menu also lists *Roti* as a bread item [S05, S10]; Québec recipe writing uses *pain paratha* [S06]. The OQLF's broad *pain plat* entry defines a family and gives tortillas, chapatis, and some pitas as examples; it supports the umbrella boundary only, not an alias or display name for any one of these three products [S09]. The OQLF's atta entry names chapati, nan, and paratha as distinct products made with atta; it does not make the flour itself an alias for any prepared bread [S02].

## Identity boundaries and unsupported behavior

- **Naan is a named bread.** OQLF describes it as an Indian flatbread made from leavened dough and baked in an oven [S01]. This supports the identity; it does not justify a catalog form for leavened, tandoori, garlic, cheese, or stuffed naan.
- **Roti is a separate bread name in the requested prepared-bread context.** Montréal restaurant menus place roti under breads and list it separately from naan and paratha [S05, S10]. The name also occurs in regional usage for a filled meal/wrap, including on Trinidad and Tobago's official tourism page [S08]. Keep that dish use outside the bread identity and do not let a recipe's curry filling become a roti alias.
- **Paratha is separately named in Québec use.** Québec recipe writing calls it a *pain paratha* and serves it as a bread accompaniment; Montréal bilingual restaurant menus list paratha separately from roti and naan [S05–S06, S10]. Potato, onion, paneer, and layered varieties appear as separate product/menu names, not equivalent names for every paratha [S05, S07, S10].
- **Chapati is only the comparator.** OQLF defines chapati as a very thin, round, crisp flatbread made with atta and generally cooked on a griddle, and accepts the borrowed French term *chapati* [S03]. Do not alias it to roti or create a chapati identity in this tranche. The active atta record's `chapati flour` and `farine à chapati` aliases name flour, not bread [S02–S03].
- **No generic flatbread alias.** `flatbread`, `flat bread`, and `pain plat` remain outside all three identities. The 2026 OQLF entry uses *pain plat* for the broad family of flour-and-water doughs with varied thickness and shows that the family includes both unleavened examples (tortillas, chapatis) and leavened examples (some pitas) [S09]. It does not establish *pain plat* as a synonym for naan, roti, or paratha. Pita, lavash, and injera remain their own prepared-bread references.
- **No forms or numbers.** Do not add fresh/frozen, plain/stuffed, leavened/unleavened, tandoori/griddle, or other forms from isolated menus or product variants. Recipe quantities, yields, package counts, and retail sizes are source-specific; none establishes a standard mass, volume, piece count, density, unit mapping, or conversion.
- **No substitutions.** Similar service with curries does not establish naan↔roti, roti↔chapati, paratha↔roti, or any substitution to pita/lavash/tortilla/flatbread. Add no global or contextual substitution rule.
