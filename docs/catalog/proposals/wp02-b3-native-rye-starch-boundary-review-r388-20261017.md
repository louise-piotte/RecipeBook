## Decision and scope

Rye starch is a distinct technical material when starch is separated from rye flour. Primary studies isolate it from rye flour with alkaline extraction or proteinase-based methods and measure properties that differ from wheat starch. This supports a research-level identity boundary between extracted rye starch and whole-grain rye, rye flour, rye meal, rye bran, flakes, and other rye products. It does not establish a current consumer ingredient, a consistent commercial grade, or a recipe-ready product specification. The research literature also reports that comparatively little had been published on rye-starch isolation and that industrial use was limited. [S01–S03]

The bounded Canadian/Québec search found no current standalone human-food offer that identifies an extracted rye-starch grade. The current Québec government consumer guide names rye as flour, bran, flakes, and whole rye; a current indexed Super C rye-bread listing names `farine de seigle` as a bread ingredient. These are adjacent local rye-product examples, not proof that no supplier can offer rye starch. The Super C page could not be fetched directly during this review. [S07–S08]

For a future Québec display, **`amidon de seigle`** is a reasonable descriptive construction: OQLF prefers `amidon` for starch and recognizes `seigle` as the cereal name; CFIA's current common-name table requires a starch source plus “starch.” OQLF's `amidon` entry reserves `fécule` as the more specific term for starch in tubers or other underground plant organs, so `fécule de seigle` is not recommended. Neither the exact compound `amidon de seigle` nor the qualified phrase `amidon de seigle natif` is attested by the reviewed Québec product or terminology sources. Keep both as reviewer constructions, not active labels. [S04–S06]

Do not create a reference, alias, form, measurement, conversion, recipe link, yield, or substitution now. Reopen if a current Canadian/Québec human-food supplier or manufacturer identifies an extracted, unmodified rye-starch product and provides enough product context to distinguish it from modified or pregelatinized grades. At that point, independently review the Québec display against a product-matched label or authoritative terminology. This is a scoped evidence hold, not a market-wide availability claim.

## Numeric and substitution limits

The studies' extraction yields and laboratory composition/property measurements belong to research procedures and samples; none is a household measure, product-specific mass-per-volume value, reusable unit mapping, or recipe yield. The 2009 technical chapter notes past trials of rye starch as a gelling/thickening agent and a possible potato-starch substitute, while describing industrial use as limited. It gives no universal kitchen replacement amount or ratio. No active density, form, conversion, yield, recipe link, or directional/global/contextual substitution is supported. [S01–S03]
