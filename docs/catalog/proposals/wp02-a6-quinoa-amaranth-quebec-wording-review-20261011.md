## Terminology and agreement

OQLF’s GDT entry gives `quinoa` as a masculine noun and defines it as a plant and food whose seeds are consumed. The adjective in `quinoa blanc` and `quinoa noir` therefore takes masculine agreement. This is also directly reflected in the Québec product name `farine de quinoa blanc` and the French certificate wording `farine de quinoa noir`. Do not change these to `blanche` or `noire`: in these phrases, the color names the quinoa, not the feminine noun `farine`.

The OQLF entry for `amarante` marks the food name feminine and says its seeds can be milled into flour. Its `graine germée` entry defines a sprouted seed. The Canadian French product name `amarante germée` corroborates the feminine participle in contemporary retail usage. Thus `farine d'amarante germée` reads as flour made from germinated amaranth. The English product listing from Everspring uses `Sprouted Amaranth Flour` and describes the supplier as sprouting and milling grains; it supports the intended process sense, not a claim about a standardized germination protocol.

Targeted OQLF and TERMIUM Plus searches did not surface official entries for `farine de quinoa blanc/rouge/noir` or `farine d'amarante germée` as complete product terms. TERMIUM’s relevant genus-level entry gives English `amaranth` / French `amarante`; OQLF is the direct source for grammatical gender and Québec food terminology. The complete compounds should therefore be described as source-attested product wording, not as official standardized terms.

## Integration guidance

- The French displays are linguistically acceptable as proposed; the parent proposal may clear its terminology hold for `farine d'amarante germée`.
- Preserve the distinction between generic quinoa flour and color-qualified named products. Do not infer that generic flour is white.
- Keep the black-quinoa wording decision within the certification annex’s scope; it does not verify a retail label or current SKU.
- Keep sprouted amaranth separate from generic amaranth flour and from the whole `amarante germée` retail product. The display says the amaranth is sprouted before/for milling, but does not encode a drying, roasting, or other process step.
- No French aliases, forms, measures, density, conversions, substitutions, or recipe links are supported by this wording review.
