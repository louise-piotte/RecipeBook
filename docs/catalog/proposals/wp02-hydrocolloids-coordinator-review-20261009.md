# WP02 Section E — Hydrocolloids coordinator review

- **Review date:** 2026-10-09.
- **Packet type:** proposal-only identity and recipe-demand review.
- **Scope:** xanthan, guar, locust-bean gum, psyllium husk/powder, methylcellulose, sodium alginate, relevant calcium salts, and other established food-grade hydrocolloid candidates in WP02 Section E.
- **Write scope:** this report only. No seed, ledger, checklist, roadmap, recipe, code, or test was changed.

## Coordinator recommendation

**Defer new catalog integration for the reviewed hydrocolloids and calcium salts.** The current 114-recipe bundle has no ingredient line for xanthan, guar, locust-bean gum, psyllium, methylcellulose, sodium alginate, or the reviewed calcium salts. The only exact hydrocolloid demand is three gelatin lines in one mousse-cake recipe; those lines already reference the active `ingredient-ref-gelatin` row.

The checklist remains open. Its preceding item separately covers gelatin powder/sheets, agar, pectins, and carrageenan; this report does not complete or combine those decisions. Treat “hydrocolloid” as a functional family, not a catalog identity. Each named substance or food product needs a distinct review if a recipe calls for it. Do not infer a general recipe substitution, quantity, or food-use permission from a hydrocolloid’s shared thickening or gelling function.

## Product, functional, and form boundaries

| Candidate or neighboring item | Source-supported future name | Disposition and limits |
|---|---|---|
| Xanthan gum | EN `xanthan gum`; fr-CA `gomme de xanthane` | Distinct food-additive identity, not a synonym for guar, carob gum, or generic `gum`. OQLF describes it as a fermentation-derived polysaccharide gum; Health Canada lists xanthan gum as a separate additive with food-specific conditions. Do not add generic `xanthan` or `gum` aliases. |
| Guar gum | EN `guar gum`; fr-CA `gomme de guar` | Distinct seed-derived gum. OQLF also lists `farine de guar` as a preferred French term; retain it only as a candidate label/alias when the recipe or product label identifies the food gum. Do not alias bare `guar`, guar seed/beans, or unrelated legume flour. The bare search `guar` currently falls back to the unrelated `guarana seed` result. |
| Locust-bean / carob gum | EN `locust bean gum` or `carob bean gum`; fr-CA `gomme de caroube` | One candidate gum identity: OQLF and Health Canada use `gomme de caroube` / `carob bean gum`, and OQLF lists `carob gum` as an English term. The gum is from carob seeds. Keep it separate from carob pods/pulp, carob powder, carob flour, and carob syrup; these names do not establish the seed-gum product. |
| Psyllium husk | EN `psyllium husk`; fr-CA `tégument de psyllium` or `enveloppe de psyllium` | Treat the husk as a distinct food product from whole psyllium seeds. Health Canada’s dietary-fibre page defines psyllium fibre as dried *Plantago ovata* or *P. arenaria* husk mechanically separated from the seed. OQLF’s older `psyllium` entry names a seed, so it does not by itself establish that generic `psyllium` means husk in every recipe. A powdered-husk form could be reviewed only when a recipe or product label distinguishes it; do not equate husk powder with whole seeds or assume a scoop conversion. |
| Methylcellulose | EN `methylcellulose`; fr-CA `méthylcellulose` | A distinct food additive name on Health Canada’s permitted-additive list. Keep it separate from hydroxypropyl methylcellulose (HPMC), hydroxypropyl cellulose, methyl-ethyl cellulose, and unspecified cellulose. A technical or pharmaceutical grade is not a food-grade identity. |
| Sodium alginate | EN `sodium alginate`; fr-CA `alginate de sodium` | Distinct from alginic acid, potassium/calcium/ammonium alginates, and propylene-glycol alginate. OQLF and Health Canada use `alginate de sodium` / `sodium alginate`. Do not collapse the different salts under a generic `alginate` row. |
| Calcium chloride, calcium lactate, calcium gluconate | EN and fr-CA: `calcium chloride / chlorure de calcium`; `calcium lactate / lactate de calcium`; `calcium gluconate / gluconate de calcium` | Separate salts, not hydrocolloids or aliases of each other. Health Canada lists them as firming agents under specified food/use conditions. In alginate gel research, calcium source affects gelation kinetics; the tested salts therefore do not support a generic calcium-salt substitution. Do not treat a permitted firming-agent use as a blanket food-use authorization or a recipe conversion. |
| Calcium lactate gluconate and other calcium products | Exact source/product label required | Keep out of the active identity list pending a primary food-use or standard source and a recipe/product demand. Do not alias it to calcium lactate or calcium gluconate. It was not found by name in the Health Canada firming-agent list reviewed here; that observation is not a claim that every possible use is prohibited. |
| Agar, pectins, carrageenans, gelatin, and other listed hydrocolloids | Separate established products | Section E already lists agar, pectin types, carrageenan types, and gelatin products on the preceding line. Health Canada and Codex also list other named additive products, including acacia gum, gellan gum, and sodium carboxymethyl cellulose (cellulose gum). Keep each product separate; do not create one generic hydrocolloid identity. OQLF’s stabilizer entry groups materials by source and function, which supports a family boundary but not ingredient equivalence. |
| Cornstarch and other starches | Existing neighboring ingredient identities | Keep starches distinct from gums and alginates. A shared thickening effect does not make them aliases or general substitutes. |

**Search wording limits.** Do not add bare `gum`, `guar`, `alginate`, or a generic “hydrocolloid” alias. The picker first matches normalized whole-word phrases and falls back to substring matching; the current short query `guar` can therefore show `guarana seed`, and `gum` can show `vegetable stock` through the French word `légumes`. Use complete, product-qualified names only if a future recipe warrants an identity.

## Measurements and substitutions

- Leave `defaultDensity` null, `unitMappings` empty, and numeric/form conversion data unset for any future hydrocolloid or calcium-salt identity unless a matching recipe and source establish the exact product, method, and unit basis.
- Do not generalize Health Canada’s regulatory ppm limits into kitchen recipe amounts; the permitted list is food-category and purpose specific.
- Do not add generic xanthan↔guar, gum↔starch, gelatin↔agar, pectin-type, alginate↔carrageenan, or calcium-salt substitutions. Section E already rejects generic gelatin-to-agar and pectin-to-pectin equivalences. The calcium-source study shows formulation and process effects, not a universal ratio or interchangeable product rule.
- The three gelatin rows remain correctly linked to the existing gelatin identity. No recipe relink or new form is warranted by the current corpus.
