- **Review and source-access date:** 2026-10-16.

- **Disposition:** Hold the product outside active references, forms, legacy mappings, and the shared research ledger. Do not attach `pagne` to the generic clam or cockle candidate, and do not assign the GuineaSea product to *Senilia senilis* without product-specific taxonomic evidence.

## One bounded question

Should the Canada-listed **Wild-Caught Dried Clams from Guinea (pagne)** product be represented by a new catalog identity or by assigning `pagne` to a broader dried-clam or cockle identity?

The product page establishes that one Canadian seller markets a 200 g sun-dried Guinean shellfish as “dried clams” and calls it “pagne.” An independent Senegalese shellfish assessment and an IRD research account use the local name *pagne* for *Senilia senilis*, a bivalve also described as an ark / bloody cockle. Neither source identifies the GuineaSea SKU or proves that this Guinean product is a different species. The evidence does, however, make a species-free mapping of `pagne` to generic “dried clams” or to a generic cockle candidate unsafe without a product-specific link.

This proposal covers only the GuineaSea product boundary and the search ownership of its `pagne` name. It does not resolve generic clams or cockles, add a general dried-clam identity, review another region’s dried shellfish, or reopen R207’s dried-mussel or R359’s dried-cuttlefish holds.

## Product, regional identity, and Québec terminology evidence

GuineaSea’s English product page names **Wild-Caught Dried Clams from Guinea (pagne)** and describes `Pagnes` as a Guinean cooking ingredient that is sun-dried and used in sauces, stews, and rice. Its French-language product page calls the SKU **Pagnes Séchés Sauvages de la Guinée** and describes it as `palourdes séchées sauvages (Pagnes)`. The two pages show a 200 g retail package and were marked sold out when indexed. GuineaSea says its purpose is to introduce marine products from Guinea to Canada. This is direct evidence for one Canadian seller’s bilingual SKU naming and culinary positioning; it is not a species declaration, complete ingredient label, or independent Québec terminology ruling. [S01–S02]

Regional research shows why the seller’s broad English gloss should not be turned into a species-free catalog alias:

- The University of Rhode Island / University of Cape Coast participatory assessment prepared for USAID lists the local name **Pagne**, the English common name **Arches/Bloody cockle**, and *Senilia senilis* for multiple Senegalese estuaries. Its processing table lists boiling, sun-drying, and pickling for *S. senilis*. This is species-and-region evidence for Senegal; it does not identify the GuineaSea SKU or establish a universal food-label translation. [S03]
- An IRD Éditions research account on Senegalese shellfish describes the bivalve *Senilia senilis* as locally called `pagne` in the Sine-Saloum. The account corroborates that `pagne` can be a species-linked local name, but covers Senegalese fisheries rather than GuineaSea’s product. [S04]
- The European Commission’s commercial-designation page classifies *S. senilis* in Bivalvia / Arcidae and lists `arche` as a French commercial designation in France. This helps distinguish the taxon and ark-shell terminology; it is not a Québec label or a synonym ruling for `clam`, `cockle`, or `palourde`. [S05]
- A Guinea coastal mollusc inventory identifies *S. senilis* as an important edible bivalve in the Tristao Islands and discusses cooking and sun-drying, but does not call it `pagne` or identify the GuineaSea package. Its authors also describe local limits on commercial sale, making it unsuitable as evidence that this specific retail SKU is that species. [S06]

The Guinea and Senegal evidence is not a demonstrated conflict: *S. senilis* may be present in both regional products. It is an unresolved identity link. GuineaSea does not disclose a binomial, product ingredient declaration, or a source document connecting its SKU to *S. senilis*; the Senegal sources cannot fill that missing SKU-level link. Do not convert the regional relationship into a cross-market species assertion.

Québec terminology sources establish useful boundaries but do not close this gap. OQLF’s existing food-mollusc entries are species-scoped: `mye` identifies *Mya arenaria*, and `palourde américaine` identifies *Mercenaria mercenaria*. For Québec fish-product marketing, the `mye` entry says `clam`, `coque`, `palourde`, and `quahog` were not retained as the standardized species name; the `palourde américaine` entry similarly notes that `clam`, `coque`, and `mye` were not retained for that species. These notes warn against using the GuineaSea seller’s broad French `palourdes` descriptor as a generic Québec identity; they do not identify the SKU’s species. [Reused `WP04-MOLLUSKS-20261009-S07` and `S08`]

MAPAQ’s current Québec marine-processing overview describes the Stimpson’s mactre as a variety of palourde and describes the mye, also called `coque` or `clam`, as locally sold chilled or frozen. It does not document dried `pagne`, dried clams, or a Québec French package term for this Guinean product. [Reused `WP04-SECTIONP-SEA-SCALLOP-R89-20261013-S05`]

CFIA’s fish-product labelling guidance treats condition and presentation terms such as dried, salted, smoked, filleted, and packed in a liquid as descriptors that may be needed to communicate a product’s true nature. That supports keeping product state explicit; it does not define `pagne`, identify this SKU, or authorize an ingredient form/conversion. [Reused `WP04-DOYS-S04`]

Do not assign a current row solely to make the present `pagne` substring false positives disappear.

Sun-drying and the phrase “briny” do not establish added salt, moisture content, dry yield, rehydration behavior, shell-removal state, or a generally ready-to-cook form. No density, count-to-mass value, household measure, unit mapping, package-to-edible-weight conversion, dry-to-rehydrated ratio, or cooking yield is supported.

Do not infer substitution between this item and fresh clam, canned baby clams, clam juice, dried shrimp, dried mussel, dried cuttlefish, generic cockle, or *Senilia senilis*. The product page’s broad culinary suggestions are not a recipe-specific amount or equivalence study. Add no global form-conversion rule, contextual substitution, warning, or recipe-line substitution link.

## Final disposition

**Hold** this Guinean dried-pagne product boundary outside the active catalog, forms, legacy mappings, and shared research ledger. Preserve the unresolved generic clam and cockle boundaries. The Canadian seller supports one bilingual SKU and a useful regional food term; available independent evidence links `pagne` to *Senilia senilis* in Senegal but does not establish that this specific Guinean package has that taxon. Do not attach `pagne` to generic clam/cockle, do not map the product to *Senilia senilis*, and do not create a general `dried clams / palourdes séchées` identity from the single listing. No recipe link, measure, conversion, yield, or substitution is justified. No tests or emulator checks were needed for this documentation-only integration.
