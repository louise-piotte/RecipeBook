## Evidence and limits

ICAR–Indian Institute of Millets Research's current common-name table maps Hindi `Chena; Barri` to English Proso Millet and *Panicum miliaceum*. Its page footer says it was updated 2026-07-07. This primary agricultural-research crosswalk confirms the source-grain name; it does not itself name flour. Reuse its existing ledger source ID `WP02-A6-PROSO-MILLET-ATTA-ALIAS-20261013-R2-S03`, already recorded on both proso grain and flour records. [WP02-A6-PROSO-MILLET-ATTA-ALIAS-20261013-R2-S03]

The new positive product wording is from two India-based commercial sources. A-1 Overseas currently lists `Proso Millet Flour (Chena / Barri)` in its millet-flour catalogue. Pure Tree Foods identifies its product as `Organic Proso Flour (Chena / Barri)` and `Barri Millet Flour`, and lists its manufacturer in Mumbai, India. These pages independently pair the exact spelling with proso flour. They are supplier/manufacturer product evidence, not a Canadian standard or a guarantee of export availability. Pure Tree shows 850 g and 1800 g package variants and an `Add to cart` control alongside a `Notify Me When Available` prompt; I make no stock claim. The package sizes are net weights, not recipe measures. [S01–S02]

Nutriarrow's accessible product page explicitly calls proso flour `Barri Atta / Barri Flour / Proso Millet Atta` and says its product is made from 100% proso millet. At review, all listed weight variants showed sold out, and the page was indexed as crawled last year. It supports the phrase as source-observed product wording only; it does not establish current availability or Canadian usage. [S03]

For current Canadian context, A1 Indian Grocery's accessible Ontario product page lists `Proso Millet 2.2LB (Chena / Cheno / Barri)`; its listing uses a Canadian-dollar price and an add-to-cart control. This is retailer copy for grain, not a flour label, botanical declaration, or durable stock record. Punjab Flour Mill Ltd's current Surrey, BC catalogue includes `PROSO MILLET FLOUR`; it supplies Canadian product-category context for the existing flour reference, not the Barri spelling. Spice Divine's current Canadian French-facing page names `Farine de millet S&V Proso` and its description uses `farine de millet commun`; it supports retaining the existing Québec French display, not `farine de Barri`. These sources do not establish a current Canadian or Québec packaged product explicitly labelled `Barri flour` or `Barri atta`. [WP03-COMMON-CHENA-ALIAS-20261013-R15-S01; WP02-A6-MILLET-FLOUR-IDENTITIES-20261012-S05–S06]

OQLF's GDT entry lists preferred French `millet commun` and English `common millet` / `proso millet` for *Panicum miliaceum*; the entry dates to 2010. It is authoritative terminology evidence for the existing French grain name, not a flour-specific ruling. The current Canadian French retail page provides the separate flour wording. Keep the established display and do not construct a French Barri term. [WP02-A6-MILLET-FLOUR-IDENTITIES-20261012-S04, S06]

The exact `barri atta` hold is intentionally narrow. In a 2018 Ethiopian ethnobotanical paper about the Shinasha community, the phrase labels an unidentified medicinal plant in a ritual account. The source is not a food-name authority and does not connect that plant to proso millet. It therefore does not undermine the `barri flour` product evidence, but it means that adding the unqualified exact key `barri atta` would erase a documented, non-food homonym in the global picker. No alias is proposed for that exact phrase. [S04]
