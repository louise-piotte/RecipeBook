# Independent Québec French display review: pot barley, Mochiko, and Shiratamako

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-11
- **Status:** proposal-only; French display review
- **Scope:** current displays `orge en pot`, `farine de riz gluant Mochiko`, and `farine de riz gluant Shiratamako`.
- **Source limits:** this is an independent source-based recommendation, not an OQLF ruling, manufacturer-package verification, or human Québec bilingual sign-off.

## Decisions

| Current display | Disposition | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `farine de riz gluant Mochiko` | **Accept for the internal fr-CA display.** | A Montréal-area Japanese grocer’s French product title directly pairs `Mochiko` with `farine de riz gluant`. Télé-Québec also uses the descriptor for Japanese glutinous-rice flour. OQLF supports the component terms for the rice and generic flour, but does not rule on this full phrase or on Mochiko. |
| `farine de riz gluant Shiratamako` | **Provisional.** | Télé-Québec uses `shiratamako (farine de riz gluant)` and `farine de type shiratamako`, giving strong local culinary support for the wording. OQLF supports the component terms, and a Canadian French retailer lists `Farine Tamasan Shiratamako`, but no reviewed Québec product listing or official entry uses the full current display as a product name. |
| `orge en pot` | **Provisional.** | Canadian French product listings attest the exact wording, including a listing that distinguishes it from `orge perlé`. However, OQLF defines `orge mondé` as hulled barley, while TERMIUM and current French-language retail results also apply `orge mondé` to pot-barley searches/products. That conflict prevents treating `orge en pot` as a settled preferred Québec term. |

These decisions concern display wording only. They do not establish product composition or process specifications. The two rice-flour names remain product-qualified displays; neither is recommended as an alias for generic glutinous rice flour or for the other named product. Keep the existing barley wording and its alias boundary unchanged. This review proposes no new aliases, forms, measurements, conversions, yields, or substitution behavior.

## Review and plan disposition

The Mochiko string is suitable for the current internal French display because a Québec retailer directly uses its key descriptor with the product name, and the component terms are understandable in Québec French. The acceptance is limited to that display decision: the OQLF has not approved the complete phrase, and the retailer’s differing title/description is not composition evidence.

The Shiratamako string is well supported as descriptive Québec French, but I leave it provisional because the exact wording comes from culinary explanation rather than a Shiratamako product title. The Canadian product title supports keeping the Japanese name visible, not the entire compound display.

The pot-barley string has real Canadian French product-title support, but the Québec and federal sources do not establish a stable French crosswalk. OQLF’s `orge mondé` entry points to hulled barley, while TERMIUM’s grouped record and retail search results expose the competing use. Keep `orge en pot` provisional and retain the distinction from hulled barley; do not treat the conflict as evidence of synonymy.

The 2026-10-11 checkpoint in the [global ingredient-catalog plan](../../RecipeBook-global-ingredient-catalog-plan.txt) records all three active displays, recommends Mochiko, provisionally accepts Shiratamako, and leaves independent Québec bilingual reviewer approval open. This source-based review supports that split and does not close the separate sign-off. No shared seed, catalog, ledger, plan, roadmap, code, or test change is proposed.
