## Evidence and naming boundary

Japan's Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries presents kamaboko and chikuwa as distinct members of a diverse fish-paste tradition. It describes kamaboko and chikuwa production as varying in ingredient preparation, grinding, shape, and heat treatment; examples across the country include sasa-kamaboko, hanpen, steamed board-shaped kamaboko, regional chikuwa, jakoten, satsuma-age, and others. The source establishes a Japanese product-family context, not a single Canadian/Québec recipe ingredient or an identity that absorbs every regional fish cake. [S01]

The Québec-facing terminology is narrower and does not settle the family name. OQLF's `simili-fruits de mer` entry defines imitation shellfish products made from textured surimi paste and cooked by steaming, states that this type is called kamaboko in Japan, and prefers the French term `simili-fruits de mer`. Its English translations include `kamaboko`. The entry was last updated in 1998 and describes the imitation-shellfish type; it does not establish `kamaboko` as the French name for every steamed, broiled, or fried Japanese fish-paste product. [S02]

The Québec government's current allergen page lists `Kamaboko (simili-crabe et simili-homard)` as another name under fish and seafood. This is useful Québec-facing evidence of local use, but it is an allergen-identification guide rather than a culinary terminology ruling or a definition of all kamaboko. It does not prove that every kamaboko product imitates crab or lobster. [S03]

CFIA's current French fish-labeling guidance says surimi-based products without a prescribed common name must use the name by which the food is generally known or a descriptive non-generic name, and imitation products should identify the imitation. It gives `kamaboko à saveur de homard` as an example of a generic common name when different species are used. This is labeling guidance for a finished imitation product, not a definition or endorsement of bare `kamaboko` as one universal product identity. [S04]

Current Canadian manufacturer and distributor pages show why a family-level recipe row would be underspecified. Toronto manufacturer Ocean Food separates fresh, ready-to-eat steamed kamaboko (Naruto Maki, Rainbow, Red), broiled kamaboko, fried kamaboko with different flavours/fillings, fish balls, and surimi crab. Those are manufacturer-specific product lines; the page's fresh/never-frozen states do not transfer to frozen retailer SKUs. [S05] Québec-based distributor Ozawa's current frozen-seafood category lists Ocean Food Kamaboko (Fish Cake), Ocean Food Kamaboko (Red Fish Cake), and Naruto Maki as distinct items; its Red product page lists item FS-3008 and 180 g packaging but gives no ingredient declaration or French product title. The distributor also lists a Montréal office. This establishes Canadian retailer/distributor wording and separate SKU presentations, not the composition or cooking state of all kamaboko. [S06–S07]

Taken together, the same word can refer to a broad Japanese formed-fish family, a Québec-facing imitation-crustacean category, or a manufacturer's named color/shape product. The reviewed sources do not establish a single Québec French consumer name for the broad family. That is a limit in this source set, not a claim that no such term exists.

## Recommendation

**Hold the unqualified kamaboko family outside the active picker and shared ledger.** Preserve the current chikuwa row, generic fish-ball row, separate fish-tofu/cuttlefish-ball/shrimp-ball/crab-flavoured-pollock-flake rows, the generic fish-cake hold, and the unresolved surimi candidate. Keep naruto maki held as its own named product. Do not create `ingredient-ref-kamaboko`, a legacy mapping, aliases, a form, a recipe link, or a ledger record from this review.

Revisit the boundary when a bundled recipe or exact ingredient request names a specific kamaboko style/product and its Québec-facing wording can be tied to that intended product. If broader family search is later required, preserve the distinct named styles in the picker instead of letting the single phrase `kamaboko` or the seller's `fish cake` descriptor hide them.
