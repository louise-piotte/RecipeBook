# WP05 Section T — bouillon-powder product boundary review

- **Packet type:** Proposal-only bilingual product identity and recipe-demand review.
- **Prepared and source-access date:** 2026-10-11.
- **Plan:** Section T, pantry powders and seasonings; related WP05 stock/broth coverage.
- **Scope:** Canadian and Québec product wording and boundaries among bouillon powders, broth/stock powders and mixes, cubes/granules, liquid concentrates, dry broth bases, and prepared stock/broth.
- **Recommendation:** Add one narrowly scoped, identity-only `chicken bouillon powder / bouillon de poulet en poudre` reference in `STOCK_AND_BROTH`, with no aliases. Keep the broader unqualified powder/mix terms and other flavors unresolved; this review does not complete the full seasoning checklist.
- **Proposal source IDs:** `WP05-BOUILLON-POWDER-20261011-S01` through `S10`. Integration added S11 for the French manufacturer page so its wording and differing ingredient declaration remain independently traceable; the source ID was checked against the live ledger.

## Recommendation

Lee Kum Kee Professional Canada names a dry chicken-flavoured product **Premium Bouillon Powder Flavoured with Chicken** in English and **Bouillon de poulet en poudre** in French [S01, S11]. The pages describe seasoning and soup-base use. Their ingredient declarations differ, including the stated presence of monosodium glutamate, so they support the bilingual wording only; they do not establish a same-formula or exact-SKU crosswalk. A separate current Québec-market product listing corroborates use of the English chicken-bouillon-powder wording and shows product variation [S02].

If integrated, use `chicken bouillon powder / bouillon de poulet en poudre`, category `STOCK_AND_BROTH`, and no aliases. This is the flavor-qualified dry product class; it does not encode Lee Kum Kee’s recipe, sodium level, package size, or chicken percentage. Do not add a powder form to the prepared chicken-broth reference. No recipe line in the bundled corpus calls for chicken bouillon powder or any other dry bouillon product, so add no recipe link. Add no density, unit mapping, volume-to-mass conversion, yield, or substitution.

Do not add an unqualified umbrella reference or aliases for `bouillon powder`, `broth powder`, `stock powder`, `stock mix`, `granules`, or `bouillon cubes`. These terms occur across different flavors, dry blends, package shapes, and product uses. Some Canadian sellers use “broth powder” and “stock powder” on the same brand page, but that single product description does not establish global equivalence. Satau’s current English and French GoBio product pages also disagree on UPC associations for chicken and vegetable powder listings; do not use those page pairs as a same-SKU translation crosswalk [S03]. Keep exact product terms and flavors when a future recipe or product source identifies them.

## Live catalog, proposal, and corpus audit

The audit used the live bundled files in the shared worktree on 2026-10-11. Counts can change as parallel catalog work is integrated.

- At audit time the active catalog had **1,032 references and 72 forms**, the legacy-import file had **75 candidates**, and the ledger had **1,324 records**. No active reference or form names a bouillon/broth/stock powder, granule, or cube. The one active `ingredient-ref-broth-concentrate` has no ledger sources and remains unreviewed; its aliases include `better than bouillon`. No powder form exists.
- The only adjacent legacy candidate is `ingredient-ref-chicken-stock` (`chicken stock / bouillon de poulet`), state `unreviewed`. The active `ingredient-ref-low-salt-chicken-broth` also displays `bouillon de poulet / chicken broth`, without the low-salt qualifier in its display. Thus the French query `bouillon de poulet` is already ambiguous between a prepared broth and a legacy stock candidate. Keep the full powder qualifier in both proposed displays and add no shorter alias.
- Exact normalized-key checks against active names/aliases, form labels/match terms, and legacy names/aliases found no full-key collision for `chicken bouillon powder` or `bouillon de poulet en poudre`. Exact unqualified keys such as `bouillon powder`, `bouillon en poudre`, `broth powder`, `stock powder`, `bouillon cubes`, `bouillon granules`, and `base pour bouillon` also have no current exact catalog match. This does not eliminate token-level overlap: a query for `bouillon de poulet` can return both the prepared broth and the longer powder display.
- The ledger and proposal audit found no focused bouillon-powder identity record. Related work is limited to: the existing WP04 beef-stock/broth identity decision; the WP04 fish-powder review, which keeps stock/broth powder and `base pour bouillon` separate from single-ingredient fish powder; the WP06 Caribbean gap sweep, which records an unresolved bouillon-cube mention in a djon djon context; and the protein/dairy batch’s caution against global stock/broth/concentrate synonyms. None establishes a generic powder identity or a recipe link.
- The manifest-listed corpus contains **114 recipes and 1,123 ingredient rows**. A full structured-row scan found **zero** bouillon powder, broth/stock powder, cube, or granule ingredient lines. Seven rows mention prepared broth/stock or concentrate: 015#4, 034#11, 037#9, 044#5, 078#13, 099#3, and 105#2. They are liquid broth/stock or broth-concentrate demand, not powder demand. Recipe 099#3 names `Better Than Bouillon`; it does not establish a powder identity or powder-to-liquid conversion.
