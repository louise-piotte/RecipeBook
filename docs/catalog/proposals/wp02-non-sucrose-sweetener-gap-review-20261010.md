### Boundary decisions

- **Pure sweetener vs. blend:** represent named pure ingredients separately from table-top products that include erythritol, sugar, starch, maltodextrin, flavours, or other carriers. CFIA warns that a non-nutritive sweetener product can contribute calories through accompanying carriers. Ingredient names on a blend do not make that blend an alias for any one constituent. [S14, S22–S24]
- **Sweetener vs. sugar-function replacement:** relative sweetness does not establish equivalent bulk, water activity, browning, crystallization, structure, or fermentation behavior. Do not infer baking substitution from an identity or regulatory class.
- **Sugar-related glucose terms:** preserve the live glucose-syrup/corn-syrup boundary from the previous proposal. `Dextrose`, `glucose syrup`, `glucose solids`, and `corn syrup solids` do not become aliases of one another because their names share `glucose`.
- **Products with `fondant` in their name:** keep dry fondant sugar, cooked/confectionery fondant, poured fondant, and rolled fondant out of each other's aliases. The available manufacturer describes multiple physical forms and custom formulations; the recipe must name the actual product.
- **Honey and invert sugar:** a chemical description of honey as naturally invert does not replace the ordinary catalog distinction between honey and an invert-sugar preparation.
- **Stevia and monk fruit:** plant/fruit material, crude extract, purified high-intensity extract, and carrier-containing retail blends are separate identity scopes.

## Measurement, density, and substitution evidence

No source reviewed establishes an app-ready mass/volume pair, density, unit mapping, or yield relation for a pure candidate in this packet. Canadian composition standards give solids or identity criteria, not kitchen measures. Health Canada's polyol values compare sweetness to sucrose; they are not replacement ratios. Keep `defaultDensity` null, `unitMappings` empty, and substitution rules absent for these candidates.

## Open questions and recommended next step

1. **No exact bundled recipe line names a target in this review.** The integrated rows are a global-catalog identity layer, not recipe relinks or inferred user demand; keep all quantities and runtime conversions inactive.
2. **Keep the narrow standard classes:** dextrose with anhydrous/monohydrate form; fructose separate from fructose syrup; fondant sugar distinct from icing sugar and fondant icing; invert sugar distinct from liquid invert sugar; glucose solids/dried glucose syrup distinct from the existing glucose-syrup reference; individual polyols; and specified stevia/monk-fruit product types.
3. **Hold allulose pending Canadian food-status and fr-CA review.** Canadian B2B availability or a marketplace listing is not regulatory approval or a verified French display term.
4. **Do not create blend rows until a recipe names the product.** If that happens, retain the exact brand/product/formulation, declared carrier ingredients, package statement, and any recipe-specific amount separately from pure-ingredient references.
5. **Do not add measurements or substitutions from sweetness comparisons.** Keep source-specific product statements in research context only unless later evidence establishes a recipe-compatible conversion and scope.
