- **Plan anchor:** Section K remains open for broader leafy-green coverage ([line 6555](../../RecipeBook-global-ingredient-catalog-plan.txt#L6555)) and other plant-part findings from independent sweeps ([line 6699](../../RecipeBook-global-ingredient-catalog-plan.txt#L6699)). This review evaluates that remaining generic plant-part gap.

## Recommendation

Add one identity-only `VEGETABLE_AND_AROMATIC` reference for **celery leaves / feuilles de céleri**, limited to leaves of culinary stalk celery (*Apium graveolens* L.). Iowa State University Extension describes celery leaves as culinary greens: the outer leaves can flavour soups and stews, the inner leaves can be used as garnish, and dried leaves can season food. Two Québec recipes directly use the French phrase `feuilles de céleri`. The OQLF and Kew support the base celery name and accepted species; they do not establish leaf use, which is supported by the extension and recipe sources. [S01–S05]

Keep this leaf-part identity separate from the current celery stalk, celeriac, and celery-seed identities. The sources do not justify merging, renaming, or transferring evidence from any of those rows. Do not add a generic `celery leaves` form under the stalk identity.

The scan found **zero ingredient-row matches and zero full-recipe-text matches**. Do not create a bundled recipe link or relink an existing celery ingredient line.

External Québec recipe sources demonstrate use of celery leaves but do not constitute bundled demand. Télé-Québec’s *Soupe tofu et nouilles* uses 30 mL (2 tbsp) of chopped celery leaves in one four-portion soup and garnishes that soup with the leaves. That amount is specific to the recipe. Its *Pesto de fanes* lists two cups of carrot tops and/or radish and/or celery leaves as one combined optional line; the quantity cannot be assigned to celery leaves alone, and the alternatives do not establish a global substitution.
