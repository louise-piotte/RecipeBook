# WP06 resolution: Andean potato variety and product names

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-09
- **Status:** Source-grounded proposal only; this is the only file changed by this review.
- **Recommendation:** Accept five narrowly source-scoped identity candidates: Peruvian `papa amarilla`, `papa blanca`, and `papa huayro`; Colombian `papa criolla`; and Colombian market-name `papa sabanera`. Defer a separate identity for bare `papa pastusa`; the Colombian recipe name does not identify one cultivar. Keep all six names distinct from aliases of generic potato. Where a source-qualified product is not modeled, an editor may retain its original phrase while linking only its underlying potato ingredient to `ingredient-ref-potato`; that parent link does not imply product equivalence or substitution.
- **Scope:** Resolve the six candidate names in the South American cuisine gap sweep. This proposal compares regional food use, agricultural classification, English and fr-CA wording, and the live potato references/forms/legacy/recipe corpus. It does not change shared catalog, ledger, recipe, test, plan, roadmap, or generated files.
- **New source-ID prefix:** `WP06-ANDEAN-POTATO-VARIETIES-RESOLUTION-20261009-S`. A repository search found no prior use of this prefix.

The three Peruvian names are not a universal cultivar list. A 2014 national survey manual groups papa amarilla and papa blanca, but treats papa Huayro as a single-variety exception. A 2020 Lima wholesale-market report instead places Huayro among native `papa color`; it separately groups papa blanca and papa amarilla with multiple cultivars. The recipe source names all three as separate cooked ingredients but does not say which exact cultivar or market grade is intended. Keep the classes and source names, but do not attach a botanical species or cultivar to the candidate rows. [S01–S04]

In Colombia, the ajiaco recipe lists criolla, pastusa, and sabanera separately. AGROSAVIA’s agricultural evidence supports a product class for `criolla`, identifies multiple cultivars, and shows that `sabanera` is a Bogotá market name for the named Tuquerreña cultivar. By contrast, its Parda Pastusa model shows that the bare word `pastusa` cannot reliably identify that cultivar: several named cultivars coexist, and use of “pastusa” has spread to materials that are not Parda Pastusa. Preserve the distinctions and do not infer interchangeability from the shared recipe. [S05–S08]

## Locale, aliases, and product boundaries

- **English:** Perú Travel’s bilingual recipe provides direct source-scoped English wording for yellow, Huayro, and white potatoes. For the Colombian names, the reviewed government recipe and agricultural sources are Spanish. “Colombian criolla potato,” “pastusa potato,” and “Sabanera potato” are transparent descriptive constructions, not verified standard English product names. Retain the Spanish source terms beside any gloss.
- **fr-CA:** OQLF establishes `pomme de terre` as the preferred generic term and `potato` in English. A targeted OQLF site search for the six qualified names returned no entries. The proposed French descriptions above use the generic head term and retain source terminology; they are provisional, not OQLF-approved product names.
- **No generic aliases:** Do not add any of these qualified names as an alias of `ingredient-ref-potato`. The current active potato row is generic and already has the separate alias `russet potato`; that alias is not evidence for flattening the six names here. Likewise, keep active potato flour and potato starch separate.
- **Within-family boundaries:** Do not alias papa amarilla to papa blanca or Huayro; do not equate Peruvian papa amarilla with Colombian papa criolla; do not equate criolla with every yellow potato or with any one named Phureja cultivar; do not resolve bare pastusa to Parda Pastusa, Pastusa Suprema, or Pastusa Superior; and do not make sabanera, tocarreña, and Tuquerreña unscoped catalog aliases. Huayro Amazonense and the other named Huayro examples are not aliases for all Huayro use.
- **Forms and numeric data:** The Peruvian source recipe says the potatoes are cooked and then sliced; the Colombian recipe describes peeling and cutting. These are recipe operations, not evidence for reusable catalog forms. Add no cooked, peeled, sliced, or variety-specific form; no density, measure mapping, cooking yield, conversion, substitution, or recipe link is supported here.

## Remaining evidence limits

The agricultural sources provide different operational scopes: a Peruvian wholesale-market classification, a national survey's crop-group coding, Colombian cultivar research, and local Bogotá/Boyacá market naming. None is a universal Andean naming standard. English evidence is direct only for the three Peruvian names on Perú Travel; Colombian glosses remain descriptive. No specialized fr-CA product wording was verified. Accept only the five source-scoped identity candidates above, defer unqualified `papa pastusa`, and leave aliases, forms, cultivar fields, measurements, conversions, substitutions, and recipe relinking unimplemented pending stronger scoped evidence.
