# WP06 jackfruit and green/unripe jackfruit boundary review

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-09
- **Status:** Integrated on 2026-10-09 as an identity-only fruit reference. No maturity forms or recipe links were added. The catalog and recipe audit below is the pre-integration snapshot. See the [integration batch record](../batches/wp06-jackfruit-identity-20261009/README.md).
- **Recommendation:** Accept one `jackfruit` fruit identity candidate for *Artocarpus heterophyllus*. Do not create a second ingredient identity for green/unripe jackfruit: maturity belongs to the same fruit identity. Maturity can be represented as a form only when a recipe or product source names the stage. Do not collapse `green`, `young`/`tender`, and `unripe` into one global alias or one universal form; authoritative sources distinguish more than one immature stage and do not establish a common threshold for those labels.
- **Source-ID prefix:** `WP06-JACKFRUIT-RIPENESS-BOUNDARY-20261009-S`. A repository search found no prior use before this review; the accepted identity evidence is now registered in the shared research ledger.

## Identity and maturity decisions

The recommended identity is `ingredient-ref-jackfruit`, category `FRUIT`, with English display `jackfruit` and fr-CA display `jaque`; it has been integrated with spelling aliases `jakfruit` and `jacque`. No forms were added. The active taxonomy source accepts *Artocarpus heterophyllus* and records jackfruit as a food plant. OQLF's fruit entry is `jaque`, with `jacque` also preferred; its separate `jaquier/jacquier` entry names the tree, not the fruit. [S01–S03]

The sources describe a progression rather than one binary ripe/unripe division. The Ministry of Food Processing Industries lists tender, slightly grown, mature unripe, ripe sweet fruit, and seed as separate raw-material categories. A Government of Bihar processing guide likewise distinguishes very immature fruit used as a vegetable or pickle from fruit with developed fruitlets and seeds used in curries, then describes ripe fruit. These descriptions support maturity-sensitive handling but do not establish standardized age, colour, firmness, or other thresholds that can be encoded as a universal form definition. [S05–S06]

## Product and part boundaries

- **Jackfruit seed:** Treat the seed as a separate edible part/product if it is reviewed for catalog inclusion. Sources describe seeds boiled or roasted and also processed into seed powder; do not make `jackfruit seed(s)` an alias or form of the fruit flesh. No seed identity is proposed here. [S07–S08]
- **Tender fruit, fresh vs. retorted/canned:** The Kerala processing account describes tender jackfruit cleaned, cut, blanched, and sealed in cans or retort pouches with preservatives. This is a processed product with additional packaging/process details, not simply a ripeness state. Keep the product distinct from fresh tender jackfruit in future review; do not add a generic canned/drained form without product-label evidence for contents, medium, and drain state. [S07]
- **Ripe bulbs in syrup:** The Government of India MSME canning profile describes ripe edible bulbs canned in syrup and with seeds removed. Treat that as a preserved product concept, not as the ripe-fresh form and not as an alias for jackfruit purée. [S08]
- **Other processing:** Chips, pickles, dried or powdered fruit, pulp, flour, jam, and syrup are prepared products. Do not add them as aliases or raw-fruit forms on the basis of the processing summaries alone. A future identity or form review should use product-specific composition and label evidence. [S07–S08]
- **Breadfruit:** Keep breadfruit separate. Kew names breadfruit as *Artocarpus altilis* and jackfruit as *A. heterophyllus*. Similar culinary uses or related genus do not make them aliases or substitutes. [S09]

No density, household measure, edible-yield factor, drained weight, conversion, substitution, or recipe relink is supported by this review. Government processing profiles include product-specific processing observations and quantities, but those are not recipe-ready universal factors.

## English and Québec French limits

OQLF supplies a verified generic fruit term: `jaque`; it also accepts `jacque`. English `jackfruit` and `jakfruit` are listed translations. The related tree name is `jaquier` or `jacquier`, which should not be used for the edible fruit. [S02–S03]

No OQLF or TERMIUM entry was found in targeted searches for `green jackfruit`, `unripe jackfruit`, `jaque vert`, or `jaque non mûr`. The suggested fr-CA stage labels (`jaque mûr`, `jaque jeune/tendre`, `jaque non mûr`) are descriptive constructions from the verified noun, not established specialized terminology. Keep the English or source-language maturity label visible until Québec terminology review establishes a preferred stage term. Do not translate `green` as a settled synonym for `non mûr`.

## Evidence limits

The evidence supports one botanical jackfruit identity and source-specific culinary maturity stages, not a universal binary `green = unripe` rule. The reviewed maturity labels have no common measurable threshold, and English `green jackfruit` is an APEDA export-list label rather than a globally standardized product definition. OQLF establishes the generic fruit name but not an unripe or green variant. Processed product sources describe particular processes and packaging, not uniform ingredient composition. Keep stage labels source-qualified, avoid broad aliases, and leave products, forms, amounts, yields, conversions, and substitutions unimplemented until recipe-specific or label-specific evidence warrants them.
