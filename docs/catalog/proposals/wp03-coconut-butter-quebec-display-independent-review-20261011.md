# Independent review: Québec French display for coconut butter

## Scope and decision

- **Review date:** 2026-10-11
- **Status:** Proposal only; this review changes no shared catalog, ledger, roadmap, recipe, code, or test files.
- **Question:** Is `beurre de noix de coco` a defensible Québec French display for the separate, whole-meat coconut-butter identity?

**Recommendation: accept provisionally `beurre de noix de coco` as the French display** for an ingredient defined narrowly as edible coconut meat ground into a paste or spread. It is used in Health Canada’s French food-category wording and by current Québec makers and retailers. I did not find a matching coconut-butter entry in the OQLF’s Grand dictionnaire terminologique (GDT) during a focused search, so this is a source-backed catalog choice, not an OQLF preferred-term ruling.

Keep the English display `coconut butter`. Do not create a second row for coconut butter under a French variant. The identity scope comes from specific whole-meat products below; the wording does not guarantee that every commercial product sold as “coconut butter” has the same recipe. Current Québec maker and retailer webpages were directly inspected, but I did not verify a current package panel; that leaves package-front wording unconfirmed.

**Hold `beurre de coco` out of automatic aliases for now.** It is directly attested on a Québec whole-coconut-purée listing, so it is a valid product-local name. However, the unqualified string is also used across sources with food, toiletries, and coconut-oil terminology. The catalog’s alias field does not encode that context. Keep it as a manual match candidate where surrounding text confirms a culinary whole-meat paste.

## Term-by-term boundary

| Wording | Recommendation | Evidence and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| `beurre de noix de coco` | **Accept provisionally as the FR display.** | Health Canada uses this exact term in its category for nut butters and substitutes. Québec retailer La Moisson and Québec maker Cru Nature use the phrase in product names for coconut meat ground into a spread. No OQLF GDT ruling for coconut butter was found. |
| `beurre de coco` | **Hold as an automatic alias; retain as a contextual candidate.** | Marché Tau uses it for an Artisana item whose listing title also says `purée de noix de coco entière`. This is direct local food-product evidence. A Government of Canada TERMIUM record also lists the shorter term, but its 2000 record spans food fats and toiletries and does not state the whole-meat process. A Canadian aromatherapy listing uses the same short wording with `huile de coco` as a synonym. These sources do not make the string reliably unambiguous without food-product context. |
| `tartinade de noix de coco` / `tartinade de coco` | **Do not add as an alias.** | TERMIUM has an older `coconut spread` record with `tartinade de noix de coco`, but a Québec retailer currently uses `Tartinade de Coco` for a product containing coconut milk, coconut meat, and coconut sugar. The phrase can name broader, sweetened, or composite spreads. |
| `creamed coconut` / `crème de noix de coco pure` | **Do not add as an alias.** | Grace Foods uses `Crème de noix de coco pure` for its 141 g `Creamed Coconut` product and describes a concentrated product that can replace coconut milk. The page does not establish that this product is identical in preparation, texture, or culinary role to a whole-meat spread. |
| `coconut cream` / `crème de coco` | **Keep separate.** | OQLF defines `crème de coco` as fat collected at the surface of coconut milk. A Québec retailer also uses `crème de noix de coco` for a 20% coconut-and-water product. Neither source describes a whole-meat paste. |
| `cream of coconut` / `crème de noix de coco` | **Keep the active identity separate.** | The live catalog already has a separate `ingredient-ref-cream-of-coconut` displayed as `crème de noix de coco`; the corpus has a distinct cream-of-coconut recipe line. Québec product wording also uses `crème de noix de coco` for unsweetened coconut cream, so that French phrase is not safe as a coconut-butter alias. |
| `coconut oil` / `huile de coco` / `huile de noix de coco` | **Keep separate.** | OQLF defines coconut oil as an extracted fat and lists `huile de coco` and `huile de noix de coco` as terms. It is not an alias for ground whole coconut meat. |

The direct product evidence supports distinguishing the ingredients by composition and use. The interpretation that the longer display will reduce confusion is an editorial inference from those sources; no source explicitly rules on catalog display design.

## Measurement and substitution boundary

This review makes no density, package-yield, unit-mapping, conversion, or substitution recommendation. Health Canada’s 15 g reference amount is for nutrition-labelling rules in the nut-butter category. It is not a recipe serving recommendation, a tablespoon-to-gram conversion, or evidence for substituting coconut butter for coconut oil, coconut cream, creamed coconut, or cream of coconut.

## Integration note

If the coconut-butter identity is integrated, use one row with English display `coconut butter` and French display `beurre de noix de coco`, defined as ground edible coconut meat. Keep the existing coconut-oil and cream-of-coconut rows separate. Hold `beurre de coco` and other ambiguous short or cream/spread terms from automatic aliases until the catalog can represent their context safely. Keep all measures, conversions, and substitutions unknown.
