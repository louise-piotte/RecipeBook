# WP03 Section F Québec French Display Review — Roasted Buckwheat Groats

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-11
- **Scope:** independent Québec French review of the existing French display for `ingredient-ref-roasted-buckwheat-groats-dry` only.
- **Recommendation:** **Revise the French display** to `grains de sarrasin rôtis et secs`. Keep the English display and the active identity unchanged. This is a display-only recommendation; it adds no alias, form, measurement, conversion, yield, recipe link, or substitution.

## Current display and plan context

The live row is `buckwheat groats, roasted, dry / gruau de sarrasin, rôti, sec`, with empty alias lists, null density, and no unit mappings or forms. Section F lines 1613–1616 of the [global catalog plan](../../RecipeBook-global-ingredient-catalog-plan.txt#L1706) asks for an independent review of this exact qualified display and keeps the dry groats distinct from buckwheat flour and the prepared *kacha / kasha* gruel.

This terminology review supplements the [roasted-groat identity packet](../batches/roasted-buckwheat-groats-identity.md), the [Section F residual-cereal review](wp03-section-f-residual-cereal-review-20261010.md), and the [buckwheat-flakes review](wp03-section-f-buckwheat-flakes-identity-review-20261010.md). Those records accept the narrow dry-groat identity, keep the prepared-dish boundary, and leave this display open for independent Québec review.

## Québec French assessment

`Gruau de sarrasin` is attested in Canadian French for buckwheat kernels, so the current display is understandable and not a factual mistranslation. Health Canada's Canadian Nutrient File (CNF) uses the exact bilingual label. Bob's Red Mill Canada also markets `gruaux de sarrasin biologique` and describes the product as dehulled seeds. These sources support the term's real food-market use.

The term is less clear as a picker label for dry kernels. The OQLF's food entry for *gruau* defines the usual Québec sense as cooked oatmeal and says the sense can extend to a dish made from other cereals. Its separate technical entry describes *gruau* as grits or cereal grain fragments associated with incomplete milling. Neither OQLF entry specifically names intact buckwheat groats. OQLF's *kacha / kasha* entry defines a gruel made from roasted buckwheat, so the headword *gruau* can evoke the prepared dish even when followed by `de sarrasin`.

Québec product evidence uses a more immediately descriptive label for the roasted kernels. Comptoir Saint-Vrac, a Québec retailer listing a product sourced from Saint-François-de-la-Rivière-du-Sud, sells it as `Graine de sarrasin rôtie Kasha Bio` / `Sarrasin rôti Kasha Bio` and describes it as whole roasted buckwheat grains. This supports `grains de sarrasin rôtis`; the CNF supports retaining the dry-state qualifier as `secs`. The proposed wording is a plain descriptive display, not an OQLF-prescribed term.

## Current bundled demand

A fresh scan of the manifest-listed corpus found **0 of 114 recipe files** and **0 of 1,123 ingredient rows** mentioning buckwheat, *sarrasin*, *kasha*, or *kacha*. No bundled recipe currently requires this French label or a preparation state for this identity.

## Disposition

Revise only `nameFr` to `grains de sarrasin rôtis et secs` if this review is integrated. Retain `nameEn: buckwheat groats, roasted, dry`, the existing identity and category, and the separation from flour and prepared *kacha / kasha*. Keep aliases, forms, measurements, conversions, yields, and substitutions empty. The recommendation favors Québec product wording that names the grain directly while preserving the source-supported roasted and dry qualifiers; it does not claim that the current CNF wording is erroneous.

This review is proposal-only. No catalog, ledger, plan, roadmap, code, test, or recipe file was changed.
