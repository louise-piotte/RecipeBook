## Why this is a distinct open Section P gap

The Section P preserved-fish boundary review keeps product-specific smoked, salted, dried, canned, packed, and fermented products open for exact follow-up, while rejecting a generic preserved-fish row or universal state form. Salmon jerky is one such product, not a generic `dried fish` identity or a form that belongs on every salmon species.

R169 added only the species-neutral market identity `smoked salmon / saumon fumé`. It expressly excluded salmon jerky, spreads, pizzas, and other composites. R169 therefore established a boundary but did not determine whether the jerky preparation should be separately allocated. The current Québec product sources make this a concrete market-named product review rather than a hypothetical combination of salmon and a generic dried form. [R169 proposal](wp04-section-p-smoked-salmon-product-boundary-review-r169-20261016.md); [R169 integration](../batches/wp02-wp03-wp04-r169-gap-review-integration-20261016/README.md); [S01–S05]

This review is separate from the completed crab-cake, crab-claw, crab-leg, lobster-claw, and shrimp-preparation reviews. It does not reopen those identities or the smoked-salmon row. A salmon jerky product may start with smoked salmon, but current sources do not show that smoking is required for every product marketed as salmon jerky. Keep any smoke method, species, seasoning, storage, and package details product-specific.

The accepted Section O R22 review is directly relevant to picker scope. It keeps `candidate-beef-jerky` in the ledger only, because an active beef-only row would make bare `jerky` select beef even though the reviewed Québec and CFIA sources cover other species. That hold leaves the broad `jerky` query unresolved in the live picker. R463 finds that a salmon-only row would create the same singleton problem in the opposite direction; it does not change or supersede R22. [R22 proposal](wp04-section-o-jerky-gap-picker-boundary-review-20261013-round22.md); [R22 integration](../batches/wp02-wp03-wp04-round22-gap-review-integration-20261013/README.md); reused `WP04-SECTION-O-JERKY-GAP-20261013-S03`.

## Product evidence and naming boundary

Aliments du Québec currently lists Fumoirs Gosselin’s **Jerky de saumon** as Québec-prepared. It describes differently sized strips that are lightly sweet, salted and smoked, and lists a 65 g format at IGA. A second Aliments du Québec listing names **Jerky de saumon fumé à chaud**, describes it as dehydrated hot-smoked salmon, and lists 100 g and 1.5 kg formats. These are two product listings, not a definition that all salmon jerky is smoked, sweetened, salted, or sold in those weights. [S01–S02]

La Boucanerie d’Henri’s own product page also uses **Jerky de saumon fumé** and says its product is dehydrated from artisanal hot-smoked salmon. Its page specifies Atlantic salmon, sugar, salt, whole spices, dehydrated garlic, gluten-free soy sauce, a vacuum pouch, refrigeration, and a reported 50% weight loss. The page currently marks the 60 g variant sold out; its process, ingredients, storage and weight loss are facts for that product page only. Aliments du Québec’s separate listing for the maker gives 100 g and 1.5 kg formats, so those page-specific size differences should not be reconciled into a reusable value. [S02–S03]

A current British Columbia seller uses **Salmon Jerky**, lists Original, Hot, Maple and Teriyaki flavours, says its product uses wild Pacific salmon, and lists 70 g packs. The seller page contains conflicting storage copy: it describes frozen shipping and then calls the product shelf-stable. R463 uses the page for its English product name and Canadian product-market evidence only, not for a general storage or process rule. A Québec wholesale retailer also lists **Jerky Saumon Fumé** as a product available in branch, in a 10 × 100 g case; this is a channel-specific item and case format. [S04–S05]

Together, the Québec product listings and the Canadian seller support the candidate phrase pair `salmon jerky / jerky de saumon` as market-search wording. They do not establish a single species, smoke treatment, cure, seasoning, moisture level, readiness, or storage condition. The Québec French wording is directly attested in product titles. OQLF’s 2016 `charqui` entry instead defines charqui as dried meat, usually beef, and marks the English borrowing `jerky` as discouraged in French; it does not give a fish-specific French name. Do not present `jerky de saumon` as an OQLF-preferred term, and do not invent `charqui de saumon` from the meat definition. [Reused `WP06-SOUTHAM-20261009-S51`; R22]

- **Identity and forms:** If a future exact request establishes the need and picker scope is resolved, review `salmon jerky / jerky de saumon` as a market preparation, not as a salmon species or universal `dried` form. - **Storage and handling:** The Henri page says to keep its product refrigerated; West Coast Select’s page conflicts about frozen delivery versus shelf-stable storage. No shared storage or food-safety state follows from these product pages. This identity review provides no processing or preservation instructions.
- **Substitution:** No bundled recipe uses salmon jerky, and the reviewed sources do not provide a tested replacement amount or method compatibility. Do not create a global or contextual substitution with fresh/raw salmon, smoked salmon, dried bonito, stockfish, charqui, or another jerky product.

No active forms, measures, density, unit mappings, recipe links, package yields, conversions, substitutions, or legacy mapping are recommended. The existing ledger-only beef-jerky candidate and its findings remain unchanged; this proposal does not create a salmon-jerky ledger record.

## Final disposition

However, the current phrase-first picker would make `jerky` return only the salmon row, repeating the scope problem behind R22’s unresolved beef-jerky candidate. Keep salmon jerky unallocated in the active references, forms, legacy candidates and shared ledger; leave `jerky` unresolved; preserve the current smoked-salmon, salmon-species, charqui and beef-jerky decisions. Revisit when the catalog or picker can expose jerky without routing the broad query to one species. Section P remains open.
