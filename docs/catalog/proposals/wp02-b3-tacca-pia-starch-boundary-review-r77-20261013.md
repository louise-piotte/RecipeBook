- **Recommendation:** **Hold a new active identity and all aliases pending independent Québec French product-name review and a picker-boundary decision.** The extracted-starch product is well documented and is distinct from whole-tuber material, but the current Québec term `arrow-root` is deliberately broader than one botanical source. ## Identity and product boundary

The reviewed candidate is the **washed, settled, dried starch fraction recovered from *Tacca leontopetaloides* tubers**. The FAO food table names its item `Arrowroot, Polynesian, flour` and identifies the taxon as *Tacca leontopetaloides*. A tropical botanical database describes the traditional sequence as grating the tuber, soaking it, settling and rinsing the starch, then drying it. A 2023 study separately reports wet extraction of Tacca tuber starch. An Indonesian ethnobotany study also documents local tuber use for starch or flour and snacks, with names differing by place. In these sources, `flour` names the dried edible product of starch extraction; it is not evidence for a second whole-tuber flour identity. [S02, S04, S05, S07]

Keep these boundaries:

| Product or term | Decision |
|---|---|
| Extracted starch from *Tacca leontopetaloides* | Source-supported candidate. Hold catalog activation until a Québec display name and picker boundary are reviewed. |
| Whole *Tacca* tuber | A separate raw plant ingredient, not the extracted starch. No whole-tuber reference or flour form is proposed here. |
| Generic `arrowroot / arrow-root` | Keep the current broad identity unchanged. OQLF defines it as a food starch that can come from different tropical species and explicitly distinguishes it from species-specific `fécule de maranta`; it does not identify Tacca as the Québec meaning. Do not assume that every source-qualified arrowroot product is interchangeable with the broad row. [S01] |
| `pia` | Do not add as a global alias. POLLEX records *pia* for Tacca/arrowroot in some Pacific language entries, but also records local uses for cassava/tapioca, sago, or starch without that source specificity. [S06] |
| Kudzu, canna, lotus-root, taro, yam, potato, tapioca/cassava, and palm-sago starches | Keep their existing identities and holds separate. Shared use as a starch or thickener does not establish a synonym, botanical equivalence, or substitution. |
| Modified or resistant Tacca starch | A processed research product, not a form or alias for the unmodified food-starch candidate. The 2025 study's autoclaving/cooling and acid-hydrolysis treatment is a specific laboratory modification. [S04] |

The French-language IRD report uses the botanical name and the general words `amidon` and `fécule` when describing Tacca. TERMIUM Plus lists French `tacca`, `pia`, and `arrowroot de Taïti` in a food-industries record whose definition is the plant with tubers that provide edible starch; the record names the plant, not a package of extracted starch. OQLF says `fécule` is more specific for starch contained in tubers or other underground organs. These support `fécule de tacca` as a transparent fr-CA construction for review, but do not establish that exact phrase as a Québec ingredient name. No OQLF product entry or Québec/Canadian retail listing for the isolated Tacca starch surfaced in this bounded pass. Keep `fécule de tacca`, `fécule de pia`, and `amidon de Tacca` unassigned until independent Québec review selects a product display. [S01, S03, S08–S09]
