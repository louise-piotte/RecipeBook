# WP06 Andean chuño and tunta product-boundary review

- **Date:** 2026-10-09
- **Status:** integrated as `ingredient-ref-chuno-negro` and `ingredient-ref-tunta`; see the coordinator batch at `../batches/wp06-chuno-tunta-identity-20261009/README.md`. The 481-reference / 17-form audit snapshot below predates later integrations.
- **Scope:** Determine whether chuño negro and tunta are separate potato products; distinguish them from the active potato row, papa seca, generic dried potato, potato flour, and potato starch; document regional naming limits and safe EN/fr-CA labels.

## Conclusion

The evidence supports two separate whole-tuber product identities: **chuño negro** and **tunta / chuño blanco**. Their processing differs in a product-defining way: black chuño is freeze-dried through freezing, treading, and drying, while white chuño/tunta includes water immersion between freezing and drying. Peruvian INIA and INACAL sources explicitly pair `chuño blanco` with `tunta`; Bolivia's Ministry describes chuño negro and chuño tunta as distinct foods. Regional details vary, so these source-specific steps are product-boundary evidence, not recipe instructions or a universal manufacturing specification. [S01–S04, S09]

Recommend separate identity-only catalog candidates, pending coordinator review:

- **Chuño negro:** EN display `black chuño`; fr-CA display `chuño negro` (provisional, retaining the source name).
- **Tunta / chuño blanco:** EN display `tunta (white chuño)`; fr-CA display `tunta (chuño blanco)` (provisional, retaining source-language product terms). FAO's French technical text uses `chuño blanco` and `chuño negro`; no OQLF or TERMIUM entry for `tunta` or `chuño` was surfaced in the targeted lookup. [S09, S11]

Suggested category for both is `VEGETABLE_AND_AROMATIC`. Do not add density, unit mappings, forms, processing yields, recipe links, conversions, or substitutions. The present bundled recipes have no matching ingredient lines. The process descriptions establish product boundaries, not ingredient equivalence or safe preparation directions.

## Product evidence and limits

| Product | Evidence-supported distinction | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| Chuño negro | An INIA-affiliated study describes black chuño processing as freezing, treading, and drying, distinct from the more complex white chuño process involving water. Bolivia's Ministry calls it `chuño negro` / “normal,” says it is soaked before cooking, and describes a culinary use with ground peanut or other flavorings. Peru's food table has a separate `papa chuño negro` entry. [S01, S02, S08] | The study reports regional variants shaped by tradition, environment, and market. The ministry overview is not a complete manufacturing standard. A public nutrition-table product label is not a preparation protocol. |
| Tunta / chuño blanco | Peruvian INIA names `chuño blanco (tunta)` and documents one Puno trial: repeated freezing, a 20-day river soak/wash, a second freeze, peeling, and 8–10 days of drying. INACAL's NTP 011.401:2020 summary describes successive freezes, river-water immersion for 21–30 days depending on variety, peeling, and sun drying. FAO's Peruvian cookbook names `white chuño (tunta)` in a quinoa-and-lamb broth. [S03, S04, S10] | INIA's process is one field trial in Chijichaya; INACAL summarizes a voluntary artisanal-processing standard. These sources support a product boundary and culinary use, not a household recipe or universal protocol. |
| Papa seca | Peru's INACAL summary of NTP 011.802:2017 describes selected, washed, peeled, cooked, cut, and dried potato. The public procurement list treats papa seca as a separate line from its grouped tunta/moraya/chuño line; FAO's French technical text likewise distinguishes `papa seca` from `chuño blanco` and `chuño negro`. [S05, S07, S09] | Papa seca has regional and industrial variation; the cited standard summary is Peru-specific. Perú Travel's carapulcra method is a recipe-level preparation, not manufacturing evidence. |
| Generic dried potato | The phrase describes moisture removal, not one recipe or market product. FAO's technical treatment describes ordinary dehydrated potato pieces separately from named Andean chuño products and papa seca. [S09] | No reviewed source establishes generic dried potato as a standardized identity, synonym, or interchangeable ingredient. The current `papa seca` display name makes this generic query resolve to a product-specific row. |
| Fresh potato, flour, starch, and chuño powder | The active fresh-potato row means generic potato; the active flour and starch rows are separate. Peru's food table lists black chuño flour and moraya flour separately from packaged potato starch/fécule. [S08, S11] | The local food table establishes distinct terms, but not enough formulation detail to map flour/powder to a form of either whole-tuber identity. Defer all powder/flour rows and mappings. |

The Peru INIA study identifies `chuño blanco (tunta)` but does not settle whether every use of `moraya` names the same product. The Peruvian food table's separate moraya and moraya-flour entries, and Cuna Más' grouped procurement phrase, make a global alias especially unsafe. Keep `moraya` unmodeled pending a more local and product-specific boundary review.

## Safe display-name recommendation

No Québec French term was established in the sources consulted. Targeted OQLF GDT and TERMIUM searches for `chuño`, `chuno`, and `tunta` did not surface a corresponding term entry; this negative search result is not proof that no term exists. The OQLF does establish `pomme de terre` for potato and distinguishes `fécule` as starch from tubers. FAO's French technical chapter itself uses the names `chuño blanco`, `chuño negro`, and `papa seca`. Retain the Andean product terms in fr-CA and mark the proposed labels provisional rather than inventing a translation. [S09, S11]

| Candidate | EN display | fr-CA display | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chuño negro | `black chuño` | `chuño negro` | Provisional; source-language product name supported in French FAO usage. |
| Tunta / chuño blanco | `tunta (white chuño)` | `tunta (chuño blanco)` | Provisional; both names are paired by Peruvian sources, and `chuño blanco` appears in French FAO usage. |

`White chuño` is an explanatory English gloss supported by the source pairing with `chuño blanco`; it is not a claim that every use of plain `chuño` means tunta. Keep `chuño blanco` and `tunta` as scoped name variants only for this source-supported product pair. Do not add plain `chuño`, `moraya`, `muraya`, `chuño flour`, `chuño powder`, or `chuño de papa` as aliases to either whole-tuber identity.
