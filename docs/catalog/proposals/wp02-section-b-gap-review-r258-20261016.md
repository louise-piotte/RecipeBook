# WP02 Section B R258 — mango-kernel flour boundary review

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-16.
- **Status:** Proposal only; no shared catalog, form, ledger, recipe, plan, or roadmap data changed.
- **Plan fit:** A focused review of mango-kernel flour, kept distinct from the mango-kernel starch review R182. The open WP02 Section 04 B3 parent remains “Water-chestnut flour and other documented regional starches.” R182 explicitly deferred kernel flour without proposing an identity or alias.
- **Proposal-local source-ID prefix:** `WP02-SECTION-B-MANGO-KERNEL-FLOUR-R258-20261016-S`. The prefix and the source URLs listed below were not found in the research ledger at review time.

## Recommendation

**Hold an active mango-kernel-flour identity, display, aliases, forms, and ledger candidate pending a current Canadian culinary flour product and product-matched Québec French wording.** A Philippine manufacturer currently describes and sells a distinct culinary product named GEMS Mango Flour, made from 100% mango kernel, and research studies explicitly process mango kernels into flour. This establishes that the ingredient concept exists; it does not establish a current Canadian food-market name or a Québec-facing label. The Canadian-facing mango-seed listing located in this review is a sold-out dried botanical powder listing with no stated culinary use or food-grade specification, while a current Canadian “mango powder” product is made from ripe mango fruit. These bounded findings do not establish that no Canadian culinary supplier exists. [S01–S03, S07–S08]

French-language tropical-agriculture material distinguishes whole mango stones/seeds from the inner kernel (“amande”) and uses different flour phrases for each. It supports `farine d’amandes de mangue` as a possible descriptive rendering for ground inner kernels, but it is not Québec retail terminology. OQLF’s entry for *mangue* covers the fruit and does not establish a kernel-flour compound. Do not activate a guessed French display or aliases until a Canadian/Québec culinary product label or authoritative Québec terminology supports the exact product scope. [S05–S06]

If reopened, assess a separate identity for the dehulled inner kernel milled as flour, provisionally scoped as `mango kernel flour`. Do not treat whole mango-stone flour, isolated mango-kernel starch, fruit/peel powders, oil-extraction cake, fermented flour, or mango–cassava composite flour as aliases or forms of that identity.

## Forms, measures, and substitution boundaries

- **Category if later accepted:** `FLOUR_AND_STARCH`.
- **Possible future display, not approved now:** EN `mango kernel flour`; FR `farine d’amandes de mangue` is a source-attested descriptive phrase outside Québec and needs local/product-matched confirmation before use.
- **Aliases:** none proposed for activation. Keep `mango flour`, `mango seed flour`, `mango powder`, `mango seed powder`, `farine de mangue`, and `mangue en poudre` inactive. Do not use `farine de noyaux de mangue` for the dehulled inner-kernel product.
- **Forms:** none. Do not model dehulled, roasted, soaked, blanched, defatted, or fermented kernels as consumer forms without a current product and a clear shared preparation state. Keep isolated starch and composite flour outside this identity.
- **Density and unit mappings:** none. The reviewed sources do not establish a reusable mass/volume measure or density for a standardized retail product.
- **Yield and processing:** Yatnatti et al. report 403 g recovered flour from 500 g of kernels under one Totapuri-kernel process involving drying, separation, soaking, blanching, drying, grinding, and sieving. This is a laboratory/process-specific recovery figure, not a kitchen yield or package conversion. Other primary work varies mango varieties, soaking times, and drying temperatures; these differences prevent treating one process or composition as a universal flour standard. [S03–S04]
- **Substitution:** GEMS recommends replacing 10%–50% of a recipe’s flour requirement with its own product and notes its slightly bitter taste. A separate defatted-flour cookie study reports formulations at several source-specific inclusion levels. Keep both as bounded research evidence only: neither supplies a reusable wheat-flour substitution ratio or a contextual rule for arbitrary recipes. Product-specific package sizes likewise are not active measures or unit mappings. [S01, S11]
- **Limitations:** This review does not establish Canadian import status, regulatory acceptance, a standardized commercial grade, a Québec culinary label, food-safety suitability for other suppliers, or a general culinary substitution. Negative supplier and terminology searches are bounded to the sources listed and are not an exhaustive market census.
