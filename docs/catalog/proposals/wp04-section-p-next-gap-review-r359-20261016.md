# WP04 Section P — dried cuttlefish / seiche séchée product-boundary review (R359)

- **Review and source-access date:** 2026-10-16.
- **Packet type:** review and documentation-only integration of one unallocated human-food product boundary.
- **Disposition:** **Hold** dried cuttlefish outside active references and forms until a bundled recipe or a better specified product request establishes what preparation it means.
- **Write scope:** documentation only. The plan, roadmap, proposal, and integration README record the hold. No shared catalog data, legacy mappings, research ledger, recipe, generated file, or app code changed.

## Gap and recommendation

The active catalog already has identity-only **cuttlefish / seiche**. The product state **dried cuttlefish** remains a distinct, uncovered question: no active row, attached form, exact legacy candidate, or dedicated research-ledger record owns it. This is separate from the integrated frozen cuttlefish-ball product and from the earlier cuttlefish-ink, dried-squid, and dried-octopus reviews.

Canadian retailer pages reviewed on 2026-10-16 show that the label can cover materially different product contexts; page snapshots do not establish current stock. T&T's page lists a 100 g product as **Pacific Intl Dried Cuttlefish**, without a species or useful preparation details in the reviewed information. Another Canadian retailer describes its 110 g **CARADA Cuttlefish** as a dried seafood product, gives a seasoned ingredient list, and describes eating it directly or using it in soups and other dishes. Neither reviewed page identifies a cuttlefish species or gives a Québec French product title. These records establish a retail product boundary, but not a single culinary form or recipe-ready identity.

The evidence-backed English product phrase is **dried cuttlefish**. The proposed Québec French descriptor **seiche séchée** combines OQLF's preferred food name *seiche* with the dried condition; a 2012 Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada French report uses **seiche séchée ou salée** for 2011 imports to Portugal. That historical trade category is not a current Québec package label and groups dried and salted products together. Therefore the proposed French descriptor is useful for a picker projection, but it is not established as current Québec retail wording.

Keep this candidate unallocated. Do not assign a binomial from a generic seller label. Do not infer that one prepared snack listing defines all dried cuttlefish, or that any dried product should be attached as a universal form on plain cuttlefish. Revisit if a recipe names the dried product and its source or package resolves whether it calls for a plain dried cooking ingredient, seasoned ready-to-eat snack, freeze-dried product, or another presentation.

## Species and bilingual naming evidence

The generic Canadian species-name evidence does not identify the products' species. CFIA's Fish List pairs **Cuttlefish / Seiche** with both *Sepia* spp. and *Sepia pharaonis*, and lists *Sepia aculeata* as **Needle cuttlefish / Seiche aiguille**, *Sepia esculenta* as **Golden Cuttlefish / Seiche dorée**, and *Sepiella japonica* as **Japanese Spineless Cuttlefish / Sépia inerme Japonaise**. It separately pairs *Sepiolidae* spp. with **Bobtail squid / Seiches**. The sellers provide no binomial, so do not assign any of these species or names to a package. In particular, do not add *Sepia* or plural *seiches* as aliases.

OQLF's GDT entry gives **seiche** as the preferred Québec term in the edible-mollusc domain, English **cuttlefish**, and Latin *Sepia* sp. It was last updated in 1998 and does not cover dried retail products. CFIA's current fish-labelling guidance says condition descriptors such as **dried** are used where needed to communicate a fish product's true nature; this supports keeping the product state visible, but does not establish a recipe form or a conversion.
