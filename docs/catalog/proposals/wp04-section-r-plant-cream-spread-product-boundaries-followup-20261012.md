# WP04 Section R — Plant Cream and Spread Product-Boundary Follow-up

- **Review date:** 2026-10-12
- **Status:** Proposal only; no canonical or shared integration files changed.
- **Scope:** Plant-based whipping cream, plant cooking cream, plant butter, and plant spreads. Plant beverages/coconut products, plant yogurt/cheese, and egg replacers are excluded because they have separate reviews.

This follow-up supplements the [plant dairy replacement review](wp04-section-r-plant-dairy-replacements-and-egg-replacers-boundary-review-20261012.md). Current Canadian product sources support two narrower identity candidates beyond the earlier broad product-specific holds: a plant-based whipping-cream class and a plant-based butter-substitute class. They do not establish a generic plant cream, plant cooking cream, or plant spread. These are proposal-level candidates only; the recipe corpus has no matching demand.

## Recommendation summary

- **Candidate, identity only:** `plant-based whipping cream / crème à fouetter à base de plantes`, category `DAIRY_AND_ALTERNATIVE`. Current Becel product usage and Violife's current Canadian whipping-cream product support this bounded functional class. Keep the candidate distinct from dairy heavy/whipping cream and from coconut cream. Do not generalize SKU performance or a one-to-one replacement claim.
- **Hold:** generic `plant cream`, plant cooking cream, `crème végétale`, and `crème à cuisson à base de plantes`. The reviewed products are whipping-cream products that their manufacturers also use in cooking; this does not establish a separate cooking-cream class. Keep coffee creamers, whipped toppings, coconut cream, and cream of coconut separate.
- **Candidate, identity only:** `plant-based butter substitute / substitut de beurre végane`, category `FAT_AND_OIL`. Current Becel and Québec-made Le Grand products support the English terms `plant butter` and `vegan butter` and the French term `beurre végane`. If aliases are later accepted, do not include unqualified `beurre végétal`: the OQLF entry assigns that term to tropical-seed fats such as cocoa butter and shea butter.
- **Hold:** generic plant spread, vegan spread, and other broad spread identities. A spreadable tub is one SKU presentation of a marketed plant-butter line; it does not make all plant spreads the same ingredient. Keep nut and seed butters, sweetened spreads, margarine, and dairy butter separate.
- Add no recipe links, forms, measurements, density, unit mappings, conversions, yields, or substitution rules. Keep the six existing margarine recipe links intact.

## Findings and recommendations

| Product boundary | Current Québec/Canadian evidence | Recommendation and alias limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Plant-based whipping cream** | Becel's current Canadian recipe page names `Plant-based Whipping Cream` and explicitly whips it to stiff peaks. Current Becel Québec recipes use `crème à fouetter à base de plantes` in a sauce, cake, soup, and whipped applications. Violife independently markets `Dairy Free Whipping Cream / Crème à fouetter sans produits laitiers`; its Canadian product page says that SKU is plant-based and vegan and describes its own cooking and whipping uses. [S04–S06] | **Candidate identity:** `plant-based whipping cream / crème à fouetter à base de plantes`, `DAIRY_AND_ALTERNATIVE`. The two Becel phrases are bounded display/alias candidates. Keep `Dairy Free Whipping Cream / Crème à fouetter sans produits laitiers` as a Violife product label rather than a generic alias: the claim and formulation are SKU-specific. Do not add aliases `plant cream`, `vegan cream`, `non-dairy cream`, `crème végétale`, or `crème fouettée à base de plantes`; the last can describe an already-whipped state. Do not alias the candidate to dairy heavy/whipping cream or the two coconut products. |
| **Plant cooking cream** | The reviewed Canadian products are named as whipping cream. Becel and Violife both publish manufacturer-specific uses in soups or sauces, while Becel also shows whipping and baking use. These are uses of those whipping products, not evidence of a separately named cooking-cream product class. [S04–S06] | **Hold** generic plant cooking cream and `crème à cuisson à base de plantes`. Do not create a form or alias on `15% cooking cream`; retain the exact 15% dairy identity for its one recipe line. Review a distinct cooking SKU only if an exact recipe or product request makes it relevant. |
| **Plant-based butter substitute** | Becel's current product family uses `Plant Butter` in English and `beurre végétal sans produits laitiers` in French, with salted, unsalted, olive-oil, garlic, spreadable, and stick products. Québec maker Le Grand currently uses the paired terms `Unsalted Vegan Butter / Beurre Végane Non Salé`; a Québec government food-directory record, last updated 2025-03-24, classifies the Le Grand product as a butter/butter substitute. Current maker product pages show different ingredient systems: Becel lists a palm/canola-oil blend with water and pea protein; Le Grand lists coconut and sunflower oils with potato, carrot, and cultures. [S07–S10] | **Candidate identity:** `plant-based butter substitute / substitut de beurre végane`, `FAT_AND_OIL`. If aliases are accepted, use the current product-class terms `plant butter`, `vegan butter`, and French `beurre végane`. Keep `beurre végétal sans produits laitiers` as the exact Becel product-family wording, not a generic alias; keep `beurre de plantes non salé` as the exact product-directory wording, not an unqualified synonym. Do not add unqualified `beurre végétal` or `vegetable butter`: OQLF defines `beurre végétal / vegetable butter` as fats from seeds of certain tropical trees and names cocoa and shea butter as examples. [S01] |
| **Plant spreads and flavored/spreadable SKUs** | Becel sells a specifically named spreadable plant-butter tub alongside stick, salted/unsalted, olive-oil, and garlic variants. Le Grand's current line has salted and unsalted vegan-butter products and describes product-specific uses. These sources establish product variants, not a common formula or a generic spread class. [S07–S10] | **Hold** generic `plant spread`, `vegan spread`, and `tartinade végétale` identities or aliases. Do not create tub/stick, salt, flavor, or spreadability forms from brand variants. Keep natural nut/seed butters and sweetened spreads separate. A later product-specific record should preserve the label and variant rather than claim a family-wide recipe behavior. |
| **Margarine and dairy butter** | CFIA defines standardized butter from milk or milk products. The margarine standard describes a separate water-in-fat/oil emulsion and permits optional milk-derived powders, buttermilk, and whey solids. CFIA's common-name guidance requires names to identify the food clearly and not mislead. [S02–S03] | Keep margarine, dairy butter, and the proposed plant-butter substitute as separate identities. Do not add `plant butter`, `vegan butter`, `beurre végane`, or dairy-free aliases to `ingredient-ref-margarine` or either dairy-butter reference. This review adds no allergy, vegan, dairy-free, composition, or substitution property to a broad identity. |

The evidence justifies only the narrow product classes above. It does not establish that every product sold as plant-based or dairy-free is vegan, that every plant-based whipping cream behaves alike, or that every butter substitute is interchangeable with butter for cooking or baking. Violife's and Becel's product-specific replacement claims are not global conversion rules.

## Unresolved cases and limits

1. The recipe corpus has no plant-whipping-cream or plant-butter line. The two candidates are identity-coverage proposals only; no recipe link is proposed.
2. The targeted OQLF search did not locate a general plant-cream or plant-whipping-cream terminology entry. Market product labels support the narrower candidate, but they do not establish an official Québec common-name standard.
3. Becel's English/French whipping-cream wording is available in current manufacturer recipes, but a current paired product package/formula page for that SKU was not located in this review. No Becel whipping formula is inferred.
4. The English and French plant-butter terms are not a universal standardized bilingual pair. Product-family wording and formulation differ by maker; alias review should preserve source and product scope.
5. OQLF's `beurre végétal` entry was last updated in 1987. It is still a direct official Québec term record and creates a clear homonym risk with modern plant-butter marketing. The qualified Becel wording does not justify adding the unqualified phrase.
6. The product-specific one-to-one claims do not support a general butter/cream substitution, density, volume-to-weight conversion, or performance rule.
7. No forms, measures, conversions, yields, substitutions, recipe links, or shared-ledger records are proposed. No tests or emulator checks were run for this proposal-only documentation review.
