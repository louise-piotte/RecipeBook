## Recommendation

The Montréal retailer Marché Mamou currently lists a 100 g food product titled **Bonga Dried Fish Powder** in English and **Poudre de poisson Bonga** in French. Its listed ingredient is dried, smoked Bonga fish, ground; the page gives a sauce-use instruction and offers Montréal pickup/delivery and Canada-wide shipping [S01]. A second listing shows the 200 g variant and identifies its origin as Guinea [S02]. FAO identifies Bonga as *Ethmalosa fimbriata* [S03]. Together these sources define one specific human-food product candidate without making generic fish powder a single identity.

If integrated, represent only that scoped product, preferably as `ingredient-ref-bonga-dried-fish-powder` in `PROTEIN`, with the source-attested displays `Bonga dried fish powder` and `poudre de poisson Bonga`, and no aliases. Do not put `Guinea` in the canonical display: the 100 g listing does not state its origin, while the 200 g listing does. Do not add `fish powder`, `poudre de poisson`, `dried fish`, `smoked fish`, `Bonga shad`, or the scientific name as aliases. The retailer's local French product title is direct Québec-market wording; targeted OQLF searches did not locate a Bonga-specific or fish-powder product entry, so the display is source-attested rather than an OQLF-standardized species/product term.

The two displays contain the broad phrases `fish powder` and `poudre de poisson`. Under the app's current ingredient search, a complete phrase found inside a display is searchable even when it is not a stored alias. Treat this as a broad-query overlap, not as a separate generic identity: searching either phrase can surface the Bonga-specific result, but an unqualified recipe phrase must not be automatically linked to it. A source line naming Bonga is sufficient to select this candidate; an unqualified powder or bouillon line remains unresolved until its product is known.

The vendor directions (one tablespoon in the sauce for the 100 g listing; one to two tablespoons for the 200 g listing) are product-specific use suggestions with no recipe serving basis. Retain them only as source context. Add no density, unit mapping, recipe measurement, package yield, form/conversion, or substitution. The listings' net package weights are not cooking measures or interchangeable package relationships.
