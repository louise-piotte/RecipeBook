# WP02 Section E R245 — Ube powder identity review

**Review date:** 2026-10-16; **Disposition:** Integrated as an identity-only ingredient reference.

**Scope:** The open Section E item covering ube preparations and flavoring. This review addresses pure powdered ube only; it does not close the broader Section E item.

## Decision

Add `ube powder / poudre d’ubé` as a `VEGETABLE_AND_AROMATIC` ingredient identity for pure powdered *Dioscorea alata*. Kubo Trading Company’s current Canadian food-trade listing explicitly identifies *D. alata*, states 100% ube, describes the powder-making process, and lists 1 kg and 5 kg supplier formats [S01](https://kubotradingco.ca/products/pure-ube-powder?variant=49608982921458). Kubo identifies itself as a Canadian-based importer and distributor [S02](https://kubotradingco.ca/pages/about-us).

A second Canadian retailer lists `Ube Powder` as a single-ingredient product made of Organic Ube Powder and gives the Philippines as its origin, but it does not identify the botanical species [S03](https://www.lemonlily.ca/collections/brilliant-blends/products/ube-powder). A Québec City breakfast menu uses `poudre d’ubé` for its ube latte [S04](https://www.lepicurien-maguire.com/_files/ugd/a91e46_9eed2707face42589b9545ddf1c45b9b.pdf). This supports the French display wording but is not product-label evidence. The existing OQLF `igname` entry defines true yam within *Dioscorea* and distinguishes it from North American sweet-potato use of “yam” [existing WP03-J-ROOTS-AROMATICS-20261009-S10](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/fiche-gdt/fiche/2082772/igname); the Philippine DOST composition table already cited by the ube-halaya record distinguishes *D. alata* from purple sweet potato and taro [existing WP05-UBE-S03](https://i.fnri.dost.gov.ph/fct/library/food_content/B).

The active reference has no aliases. In particular, do not alias broad `purple yam powder`, bare `ube`, or purple-sweet-potato powder to this species-specific powder. Keep it distinct from generic yam flour, ube halaya, McCormick Ube Flavor, purple sweet potato, taro, and blended ube latte products.

## Data boundary

- The corpus contains no ube-powder ingredient line, so no recipe links were added.
- Kubo’s 1 kg and 5 kg supplier formats are recorded only as package-specific research-ledger observations. They do not establish generic package sizes, kitchen measures, recipe amounts, or yields.
- No form, default density, unit mapping, dose, conversion, active package yield, or substitution was added.
- The broader Section E item remains open for other ube preparations, generic flavor/extract boundaries, food coloring, and edible luster products.
