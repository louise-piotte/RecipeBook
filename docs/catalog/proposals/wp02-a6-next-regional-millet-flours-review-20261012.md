## Identity and Québec French wording

### Kodo millet flour

The current English and French Spice Divine product pages name S&V Kodo Millet Flour / `Farine de millet Kodo`; a Canadian mill in Surrey separately lists `KODO MILLET FLOUR` and describes flour made from ground kodo millet. Puremill Canada also sells a `Kodo Millet Flour` made from kodo millet grains [S02–S03]. The product identity is therefore directly established in the Canadian market and is separate from the already-active kodo-grain row.

The Translation Bureau's TERMIUM Plus record maps *Paspalum scrobiculatum* to standardized English `kodo millet` and French `kodo` in grain-growing / agricultural food-product terminology [S01]. The Canadian French retailer directly uses `farine de millet Kodo`; recommend the lowercase display `farine de millet kodo`. This is current Canadian French product wording supported by the federal grain term; it is not an OQLF ruling. Do not add `farine de kodo`, `kodo flour`, `kodo atta`, other regional spellings, or the botanical name as aliases in this proposal.

### Proso millet flour

The English Spice Divine product page names `S&V Proso Millet Flour`, and its French page names `Farine de millet S&V Proso`. The French description identifies the product as finely ground proso millet and later uses `farine de millet commun` and `millet commun` [S06]. A Canadian flour mill independently sells `PROSO MILLET FLOUR` and says it is made from ground proso millet [S05].

OQLF's entry for *millet commun* identifies *Panicum miliaceum* and gives `common millet` and `proso millet` as English terms [S04]. Use `farine de millet commun` as the fr-CA display: it follows the OQLF-preferred Québec grain term and is also attested in the current Canadian French product description. Do not add `farine de millet proso` or bare `proso` as aliases. Keep the product distinct from both the generic `millet flour / farine de millet` row and the common-millet grain row.

### Foxtail millet flour

Canadian sellers identify a separate `Foxtail Millet Flour` product. Punjab Flour Mill in Surrey describes its product as flour made from ground foxtail millet; Spice Divine's English and French pages describe S&V Foxtail Millet Flour, with the French listing headed `Farine de millet S&V Foxtail` [S08–S09]. These pages support a source-grain-qualified flour identity, not an additional form on the grain row.

OQLF maps *Setaria italica* to English `foxtail millet` and lists `millet d'Italie` among the preferred French names [S07]. Its same entry notes the bird-feed association of `millet des oiseaux`; `millet d'Italie` is the clearer Québec display for the food-flour row. Recommend the transparent construction `farine de millet d'Italie`, while recording that the full compound is not copied verbatim from a Québec flour label. Keep `farine de millet des oiseaux`, `farine de foxtail`, and other common names out of aliases. Do not imply that `d'Italie` states the grain's country of origin.

### Little millet flour hold

Spice Divine's Canadian French page currently names `Farine de petit millet S&V` and describes the product as little millet (`kutki`); a Canadian mill also lists `LITTLE MILLET FLOUR` [S10]. These sources establish a marketed product phrase, but the retailer page does not provide its Latin species. More importantly for the bilingual catalog, the existing WP03 little-millet review records the English grain identity as held pending a distinct Québec French display. OQLF uses `petit mil` as a generic term and lists it for other millet species as well; it does not provide a dedicated Québec name for little millet [S11].

Keep this flour candidate held. Do not infer that the retailer's `farine de petit millet` resolves the earlier ambiguity, and do not create an active flour row before both its source-grain scope and fr-CA display are independently established. This packet does not change the existing `candidate-little-millet` record or reopen that grain decision.
