# WP05 Section W R974 generic kimchi-type identity review: gat kimchi

- **Packet type:** Proposal-only identity, naming, measurement, and substitution review. Only this proposal file is part of this packet; shared catalog data, ledger, plan, roadmap, recipes, and tests were not changed by this task.
- **Plan anchor:** Section W leaves named kimchi types open in the [global ingredient catalog plan](../../RecipeBook-global-ingredient-catalog-plan.txt#L10338). A prior Section W review held `gat kimchi` with other named types because it lacked a reviewed Québec French display; this packet adds a government French-language label source for this one identity only.
- **Recommendation:** Add one identity-only `gat kimchi` reference in `SAUCE_AND_CONDIMENT`. Use English `gat kimchi` with the source-attested alias `leaf mustard kimchi`. Use the provisional fr-CA display `gat kimchi (kimchi de feuilles de moutarde)`, matching the French-language Korean government source. Do not add Dolsan cultivar, product, brand, or regional specialty variants.
- **Scope:** One named Korean mustard-leaf kimchi identity. This does not create a kimchi taxonomy or a commercial-product roster. Commercial-product counts do not add ingredient diversity; preserve existing branded entries, and do not transfer product-specific measurements or claims without a separate transferability review.

## Identity and bilingual naming

The [Korea Tourism Organization's guide to translating Korean food](https://datalab.visitkorea.or.kr/common/board/Download.do?bcIdx=306156&cbIdx=1129&streFileNm=4e05d4e7-3afe-4e3d-9125-a1a6de297a52.pdf) maps Korean `갓김치` to **Leaf Mustard Kimchi**. The Republic of Korea's French-language [Facts About Korea](https://www.korea.net/koreanet/fileDownload?fileUrl=pdfdata%2F2016%2F12%2FFactsAboutKorea%281611logomod%29_fr_1221.pdf) names `gat kimchi` and gives the French gloss `kimchi de feuilles de moutarde` in its account of Jeolla regional foods. These sources support one named mustard-leaf kimchi identity and the proposed bilingual displays; the French publication is not Québec terminology authority.

MAFRA separately lists Gat (Leaf Mustard) Kimchi among named Korean kimchi types alongside baechu and chonggak kimchi. This supports a distinct type boundary, not a complete type taxonomy or a universal formula. The earlier MAFRA source ID remains with `ingredient-ref-kkakdugi`; if integrated, use the new proposal-local source ID and locator below rather than moving or editing that record.

OQLF's existing source `WP05-EAST-S06` defines the parent French term `kimchi` and its English equivalents but does not standardize this subtype. Its existing source `WP03-LEAFY-GREENS-20261009-S01` supports Québec French `feuilles de moutarde` for the raw leaves only. Keep those source records and claims intact. The combined fr-CA display is therefore a source-backed descriptive construction and remains provisional, not an OQLF-approved gat-kimchi term. Do not add bare `gat`, `mustard greens`, or the cultivar-specific `Dolsan` as aliases.

## Measurement and substitution review

The official naming sources identify the product but supply no reusable serving mass, household measure, density, unit mapping, or yield. The Korean Traditional Knowledge Portal records one historical *Joseonyori* gat-kimchi preparation with 5 kg mustard greens, 300 g salt, and 100 g `bunji`, plus its own drying, salting, and serving directions. These are ingredients and quantities for that cited historical preparation; they do not establish a generic formula or a measure for finished gat kimchi. Keep all of them source-scoped.

No matching bundled recipe supplies a dish role or method for a finished gat-kimchi ingredient, and no reviewed source establishes a safe contextual replacement for it. Similar condiment use does not justify replacing it with generic kimchi, kkakdugi, dongchimi, baek-kimchi, pickled mustard greens, or takana-zuke. Recipe-level swaps between ingredients used to make a particular gat-kimchi batch do not establish a substitution for the finished condiment. Propose no active global or contextual substitution rule.
