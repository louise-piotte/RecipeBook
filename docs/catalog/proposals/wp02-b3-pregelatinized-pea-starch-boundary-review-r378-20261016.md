## Decision and scope

Pregelatinized pea starch is a distinct, source-qualified food-ingredient candidate. Roquette currently markets PREGEFLO® L 100 G as pregelatinized pea starch. COSUCRA's current French-language NASTAR™ page explicitly distinguishes native NASTAR™ from NASTAR™ Instant, describes the latter as pea starch treated by heat and drying, and calls its pregelatinization physical. ADM's North American pea-ingredient sheet also lists a product code for “Pre-Gel Pea Starch.” A Canadian patent uses the exact French phrase `amidon de pois prégélatinisé` in its title and describes a pea-starch food application. Together these sources establish that the candidate is a real processed product class, not a synonym for pea flour or merely a household cooking instruction.

The Canadian/Québec sourcing review did not establish a current Canadian product offer that joins the pea-source and pregelatinized-process qualifiers. Allzone's current Canadian-facing pages list native pea starch and generic pregelatinized starch as separate quote-based products. The generic pregelatinized page does not disclose its botanical source; its quotation will determine source, grade, and origin. The separate pea-starch page identifies native pea starch and does not say it is pregelatinized. The pages are useful evidence of the current local boundary, but they do not prove that no Canadian supplier can source a pregelatinized pea grade. ADM's North American document is dated 2022 and is not a Canada-specific offer. Roquette and COSUCRA identify commercial food ingredients but do not establish a Canadian or Québec sale of the reviewed pea-starch grade.

Québec terminology supports the general construction `amidon de pois prégélatinisé`: OQLF prefers `amidon` as the general term for starch, the existing pea-starch identity is displayed as `amidon de pois`, and OQLF lists `amidon prégélatinisé` for the process. The OQLF entry is general to starch and does not attest the pea-specific compound. The Canadian patent and COSUCRA's French product page attest related French wording, but neither is a Québec product label or a Québec terminology decision for this catalog candidate. A phrase built from these parts remains a reviewer construction, not verified Québec product naming.

Apply the local threshold narrowly: reopen when a current Canadian or Québec source identifies a standalone human-food pea-derived pregelatinized-starch product or grade, and a matching French product label or authoritative Québec terminology source supports the picker wording. A generic Canadian pregelatinized-starch offer with undisclosed botanical source, an older North American portfolio sheet, a foreign manufacturer's French page, or a Canadian patent title alone does not clear both conditions. This is a candidate-specific hold, not a market-wide claim that the ingredient is unavailable.

Do not add a reference, `candidate-` ledger record, alias, form, measurement, conversion, recipe link, yield, or substitution rule now. Keep the existing `pea starch / amidon de pois` and `modified pea starch / amidon de pois modifié` identities unchanged. If reopened, review the exact product grade and its Québec wording first; do not infer a general product specification from one brand or grade.

## Reopen threshold and verification scope

Reopen this bounded hold only after documenting both:

1. A current Canadian or Québec human-food product/grade source that explicitly names pea-derived pregelatinized starch, not an unqualified pregelatinized starch, a native pea starch, a patent, or a source from an unrelated market.
2. Product-matched Québec French labeling, or authoritative Québec terminology that supports the exact botanical-plus-process phrase. OQLF's general `amidon prégélatinisé` entry and a Canadian patent title are helpful context but do not independently meet this product-name threshold.

At that review, verify whether the exact grade is physically pregelatinized from native pea starch, chemically modified, or both. Keep `native pea starch` and `modified pea starch` on their current rows; do not equate `precooked`, `instant`, and `pregelatinized` unless the same product source does so. Re-scan picker collisions and the manifest recipes before any integration.
