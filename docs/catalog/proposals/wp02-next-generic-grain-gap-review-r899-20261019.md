## Recommendation and ingredient boundary

Add `amaranth bran` as a distinct named milling fraction from amaranth grain. A primary milling study describes stone-milling amaranth seed and separating a fraction called “bran” from starchy perisperm; a separate primary food study uses amaranth bran as an ingredient in experimental 3D-printed snacks. Together these sources support a generic culinary ingredient identity and documented human-food research use. They do not establish a standard bran grade, common retail availability, or routine household use. [R899-S01–S02]

Keep this identity separate from:

- whole amaranth grain and amaranth flour;
- sprouted amaranth flour, amaranth flakes, puffed amaranth, and amaranth greens;
- bran from wheat, barley, corn, oat, rice, rye, sorghum, triticale, millet, buckwheat, spelt, and other grain crops;
- extracted amaranth oil, oilseed meal, protein or fibre concentrates, germ grits, perisperm, and other processing fractions.

The proposed name follows the source grain and the research literature’s use of `amaranth bran`. It does not specify a species, cultivar, milling route, particle size, purity, or processing state. Do not expand it to bran from every *Amaranthus* species or treat related fractions as synonyms without separate evidence.

### No-transfer boundary

Keep the measurements and claims from the cited studies attached to those study materials and methods:

- Sun et al. examined *Amaranthus hypochondriacus* cv. K432 processed for oil. Their stone mill, separation of oil-rich embryonic tissue from starchy perisperm, fraction yields, oil distribution, stability observations, and subsequent extrusion/extraction steps describe that cultivar and process. The study does not establish a universal anatomical definition, composition, milling yield, or culinary property for amaranth bran. [R899-S02]
- Habuš et al. bioprocessed one amaranth-bran study material and tested it in 3D-printed snacks. Its 20% flour-based formulation, 24-hour treatments, measurements, sensory findings, and conclusions apply to that research setup; they are not a household portion, general recipe ratio, preparation instruction, substitution, or guaranteed nutritional outcome. [R899-S01]
- No claim or measurement is transferred from an amaranth flour, branded product, or other grain’s bran. Existing catalog rows and their evidence remain unchanged. Any future transfer of product-specific information requires its own review of whether the evidence applies beyond that product.

For Québec French, propose `son d’amarante` as a descriptive display built from OQLF’s preferred crop name `amarante` and its cereal-milling base term `son`. Neither entry attests the complete phrase or establishes a Québec-preferred human-food name for this fraction. Keep the compound provisional and add no French alias. Reuse `WP03-S23` for the crop wording and `WP03-BARLEY-BRAN-QC-R89-20261013-S01` for the base milling term; reuse `WP03-BARLEY-BRAN-20261013-R56-S03` only for the general English source-grain naming guidance. [WP03-S23; WP03-BARLEY-BRAN-QC-R89-20261013-S01; WP03-BARLEY-BRAN-20261013-R56-S03]

## Limitations and next review boundary

1. The sources establish a named milling fraction and human-food research use, but not broad retail availability or routine home cooking demand.
2. The milling study’s definition of bran is tied to a particular species/cultivar and an oil-processing method; it is not a universal anatomical or purity specification.
3. The snack study's material, 20% formulation, bioprocessing, analytical values, and outcomes are specific to its experiments and must not be generalized to the entire ingredient category.
4. The proposed French display is descriptive and provisional. OQLF supports the source-grain and base milling terms, not the full phrase `son d’amarante`.
5. No household measure, density, particle grade, milling yield, cooking behavior, substitution, or nutrition/health claim is proposed.
