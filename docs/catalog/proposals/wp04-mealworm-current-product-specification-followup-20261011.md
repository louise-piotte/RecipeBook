# WP04 follow-up — current mealworm powder specification and Canadian scope

**Review date:** 2026-10-11  
**Scope:** Find a current human-food mealworm powder specification that names species and process; compare that product to Health Canada’s named non-novel determination.

**Status:** Evidence follow-up integrated into the held candidate record on 2026-10-11. No active catalog row was added; see `docs/catalog/batches/wp04-mealworm-current-product-specification-followup-20261011/README.md`.

## Disposition

**Keep the generic active mealworm identity on hold.** A current human-food product page now supplies the species and powder process: Jimini’s lists mealworm powder made from whole dehydrated *Tenebrio molitor* larvae. Its storefront is European, and the reviewed pages do not establish Canadian availability or a Canadian package label.

The product does not establish a match to Health Canada’s 2023 determination for the specifically named ϔnMeal™ protein-rich flour. Health Canada describes that determination as product-specific. Its public row does not publish a manufacturing process. A European Commission application summary for ŸnMeal™ describes a product made after mechanical separation of larval parts, followed by drying; Jimini’s instead specifies powder made from whole dehydrated larvae. The European summary is not the Canadian submission, so it cannot prove the exact confidential Canadian product specification. It does show that species plus the word “powder” do not establish an exact product match.

The current Québec human-food maker TriCycle lists both dried mealworms and mealworm powder, but its food page does not name the species or describe the powder process. Its separate live-larva and animal-nutrition pages are not used to assign species or human-food status to those food SKUs. The historic Entomo Farms sheet and Landish help-center entry also do not provide a current matching Canadian product specification.

This is an evidence hold. It is not a finding that other mealworm products are novel, prohibited, or unsafe. Do not infer status from an absent list row or from feeder, pet, or feed products. Add no active reference, alias, form, measure, density, conversion, yield, recipe link, or substitution rule.

## Current product and naming findings

- **Jimini’s, current human-food listing:** The French product page lists “La poudre de molitor 250g” and an ingredient of whole dehydrated mealworms. Its English product page lists “Mealworm powder 500g” and says the powder is made from whole dehydrated *Tenebrio molitor*. The current English category page shows 250 g, 500 g, and 1 kg pack options. The product page reports 55 g protein per 100 g, 30 g fat, 4 g carbohydrate, 4.7 g fibre, and 0.25 g salt. These are manufacturer product-page statements, not a verified image of the physical package. The pages use euro pricing and promote delivery in Europe or metropolitan France; they do not establish Canadian retail availability.
- **TriCycle, Québec human-food page:** The current French page lists “Ténébrions séchés nature” and “Poudre de ténébrions,” says edible insects are sold whole dried or powdered, and describes culinary use in muffins, smoothies, vinaigrettes, and snacks. It says the food is made in Québec and offers retailer and bulk channels. It does not state the Latin species, ingredient declaration, or powder process. Its live-larva product and animal-nutrition pages are separate categories and are not evidence for the human-food powder.
- **Historic Canadian product specification:** Entomo Farms’ 2014 sheet describes a Canadian “Organic Mealworm Flour” made from pure *T. molitor*, with cleaning, freezing, roasting, cooling, grinding, and packing. It reports 55.43 g protein and 18.9 g fat per 100 g and a 25 kg pail. This is useful historical process/composition evidence, not a current product listing or recipe package amount.
- **Landish product-specific help content:** The accessible FAQ describes a *Tenebrio molitor* insect-protein powder and reports a 2 tbsp (12.5 g) serving with 7 g protein; a sourcing answer says the mealworms were raised and processed in Montréal. The current French storefront collection reviewed here does not list that product. The FAQ does not disclose whole-ground versus separated or extracted process, and the collection-page observation does not prove the item is unavailable through every channel.
- **Current Québec French terminology:** OQLF prefers “ténébrion meunier” for *Tenebrio molitor* and says “ver de farine” designates this species in its larval form; plural “vers de farine.” The current Jimini’s French product page uses “poudre de vers de farine” and “poudre de molitor.” CFIA guidance requires a common name in English and French for consumer prepackaged foods and permits a generally known or accurate descriptive name when no prescribed name applies. No mealworm-specific prescribed common name was established in this review. Species-qualified displays such as “yellow mealworm powder” and “poudre de ténébrion meunier” are editorial candidates only, not approved label names.
- **Alias boundary:** The repository has no exact or accent-insensitive match for mealworm, mealworm powder/flour, yellow mealworm, *Tenebrio molitor*, ténébrion meunier, ver de farine, poudre de molitor, or the current product-page phrases. Keep generic “insect powder,” “insect flour,” “poudre d’insectes,” “cricket powder,” and “poudre de grillon” out of mealworm aliases; they are shared or ambiguous across insect species. The two active insect powder references remain the house-cricket and banded-cricket products, both with empty alias arrays.
