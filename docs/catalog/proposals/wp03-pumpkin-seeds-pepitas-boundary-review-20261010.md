## Recommendation

Review one identity-only seed-family reference for edible seeds from culinary pumpkins and squash. Do not create separate botanical identities for pumpkin versus squash seeds, and do not create a separate ingredient identity for pepitas. This is a family-level inference from food sources that group pumpkins with other squash and CFIA’s common-crop table mapping both `citrouille` and `courge` to *Cucurbita* spp. The CFIA table concerns propagation seed, and none of these sources establishes one species for a commercial edible-seed item.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ingredient-ref-pumpkin-squash-seeds` | EN `pumpkin seeds`; fr-CA `graines de citrouille`; `NUT_SEED_AND_DRIED_FRUIT` | Consider EN `squash seeds` as a family-level alias. Add singular FR `graine de citrouille` because OQLF records that singular term and the app’s exact phrase matching does not equate it to the plural display. Do not add `pepitas` or `graines de courge` as global aliases in this pass. | Integrate one identity-only family row if the coordinator accepts the bounded *Cucurbita* scope. | Pepitas | No separate ID or display proposed | Hold `pepita(s)` out of the global alias arrays. The sources disagree on whether the term is broad or specifically a hull-removed product, and some sources add roast/salt qualifiers. | Treat it as product-state wording for a pumpkin/squash seed kernel in source context, not as an exact synonym for every whole seed and not as a distinct botanical identity. Keep the term in evidence only until recipe demand and search semantics can preserve its qualifiers. |

The fr-CA display follows OQLF’s preferred **graine de citrouille** and its stated English equivalent **pumpkin seed**. A Canadian Armed Forces food specification uses “graines de citrouille” for seeds of pumpkins and other squash, so this Québec term can label the proposed wider seed-family row without assigning a species. The direct Québec government study title **citrouilles à graines sans écale** is useful evidence for a specific hull-less cultivar product; it does not make *sans écale* a synonym for all pepitas.

The French phrase **graines de courge** is not recommended as a global alias. The existing egusi boundary review documents it as a trade name used for multiple Cucurbitaceae products and notes that marketplace use may not establish either crop or whether a product is whole seed or flour. This recommendation keeps that regional trade ambiguity visible rather than assigning the phrase to one global reference. See the [WP03 egusi and melon-seed review](wp03-egusi-melon-seeds-identity-review-20261009.md) and the later [West/Central African egusi review](wp06-west-central-african-egusi-product-boundary-review-20261010.md).

## Product and identity boundaries

| Term or product | Evidence and boundary | Catalog disposition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pumpkin and squash seeds | OQLF defines *graine de citrouille* as the edible seed from a pumpkin. The Canadian Armed Forces specification states that its “graines de citrouille” item includes seeds of pumpkin and other squashes. USDA’s historical Food Group 12 has a combined “pumpkin and squash” seed-kernel food entry. These sources support one useful culinary family, not one fixed cultivar or species. | One family-level seed identity is supportable. Use the common pumpkin label, retain the wider squash scope in the evidence record, and do not attach the existing pumpkin-fruit species claim to it. |
| Source species and cultivars | The existing `ingredient-ref-pumpkin` is a produce reference for pumpkin / citrouille with a ledger claim tied to *Cucurbita pepo*. The Québec MAPAQ brief documents hull-less pumpkin cultivars but does not name their species; a separate USDA-published study identifies Styrian oil pumpkin as *C. pepo*. The broader pumpkin/squash food entries do not fix a taxon. | Do not set the new seed-family row to *C. pepo* or reuse `ingredient-ref-pumpkin`. Keep a species-specific seed candidate for later only if recipe or product evidence requires it. |
| Whole seed with coat versus edible kernel | Government food-composition records distinguish “pumpkin and squash seed kernels” from the broader recipe-language “pumpkin seeds.” The sources support a real hull/edible-kernel distinction; they do not make that distinction a different plant identity. | Preserve explicit hull wording in source/recipe text. Do not create a separate seed identity for whole versus kernel product in this review. |
| “Pepitas” | A U.S. CDC/NHANES food glossary defines pepitas as hulled, unsalted pumpkin or squash seeds. The Canadian Armed Forces specification says “pepitas” may refer to either shelled or unshelled seeds and most often describes a roasted final product. This is a material difference in product-state scope, not evidence of a new taxon. | No global alias and no separate identity now. The evidence does not justify assigning unqualified pepita wording to one state across markets. |
| Hull-less cultivars versus mechanically hulled seeds | A USDA-published study describes a *Cucurbita pepo* Styrian oil-pumpkin mutant whose seed-coat layers lack the usual lignification. MAPAQ separately reports Quebec production trials of pumpkin cultivars grown for seeds “sans tégument (écale).” A naturally hull-less cultivar and a seed mechanically hulled after harvest are related foods but are not the same production description. | Keep *sans écale* / hull-less as source-specific cultivar wording. Do not equate it automatically with “hulled” or infer that all green kernels came from such cultivars. |
| Roasted, salted, and unsalted products | The Canadian specification describes roasted seeds as the most common retail product and notes both salted and unsalted products. USDA SR28 maintains separate historical entries for dried kernels, roasted kernels without salt, and roasted kernels with salt. Roast and salt are independent processing/product qualifiers, not botanical identities. | No product row or form is proposed for roast/salt states. Do not discard qualifiers when a source or recipe names them. |
| Ground, paste, flour, meal, oil, and dishes | The seed evidence does not make a ground seed, seed paste, oil, or prepared dish identical to a whole seed. The related egusi review already documents why broad seed-product and trade names need source and product context. | No alias, identity, or substitution to these products is proposed. |
| Other seed identities | Existing catalog/ledger work keeps egusi, dried watermelon seed kernels, sacred lotus seeds, and *Euryale ferox* makhana separate from one another. Shared “seed” wording is not a basis for merging them with Cucurbita seeds. | Keep all existing rows, boundaries, and source-specific wording separate. |

No forms, active mappings, densities, yields, substitutions, or recipe links are recommended. Food-composition sources contain portion tables, but this review deliberately does not lift numeric observations or turn them into mappings. The state evidence above is classification context only; it does not supply conversion data.
