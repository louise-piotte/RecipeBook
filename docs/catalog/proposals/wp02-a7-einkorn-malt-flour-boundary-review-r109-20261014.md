## Recommendation

**Hold einkorn malt flour outside active references, aliases, forms, and the research ledger.** A current European wholesale page lists “Einkorn malt flour” among its grain-specific malt-flour varieties, and a primary study establishes laboratory malted einkorn grain. The reviewed sources do not provide a product-specific composition or process, a current Canadian or Québec product declaration, or a supported Québec French name. They therefore do not establish a stable bilingual ingredient-reference identity for this app. [S01–S06]

This is a bounded evidence hold, not a claim that no such flour is made or sold. Reopen when a current Canadian or Québec-facing source identifies the standalone product, describes enough of its ingredients or process to settle its relation to plain einkorn flour and malted wheat flour, and supports its French display.

## Identity and naming boundary

ATCO's wholesale product-range page names **Einkorn malt flour**. It also lists einkorn separately from wheat and other cereal sources, but provides no einkorn-specific ingredient declaration, technical sheet, malt activity, roast state, or process description. Treat the phrase as evidence of a marketed product-range category, not a complete product specification. [S01]

Sachambula, Hartman, and Psota malted einkorn grain in a laboratory study of malt quality. Their work evaluates malted einkorn, not flour milled from that malt, and does not establish a current retail product or a reusable culinary grade. The study's malting times and steeping conditions are laboratory process settings, not kitchen measurements or flour conversions. [S02]

The product boundary remains unresolved against the existing broad `malted wheat flour` identity because einkorn is a wheat species, while ATCO's product range lists einkorn and wheat malt flours separately. That listing suggests a source-specific distinction, but without the einkorn product specification it does not define a market-wide class. Keep the candidate distinct during review; do not infer that it is an alias of general malted wheat flour or that every source uses the same boundary. [S01, S03–S04]

- **Malted wheat flour** (`ingredient-ref-malted-wheat-flour`). It remains a separate current identity; the available product-range listing is insufficient to settle whether every einkorn malt flour belongs within that broad wheat-malt class.
- **Malted spelt flour** (`ingredient-ref-malted-spelt-flour`), whose Canadian product wording and French display were reviewed separately in [R24](wp02-a7-malted-spelt-flour-quebec-name-boundary-review-20261013-round24.md).
- **Malt flour** (`ingredient-ref-malt-flour`), whose English alias `malted cereal flour` and French display `farine maltée` remain unchanged. TERMIUM's entry defines this generic term for ground malted wheat or barley; it does not name an einkorn product. [S04]
- Whole einkorn malt, malt extract or syrup, roasted malt flours, and composite bakery or brewing products. None is interchangeable with this candidate on the evidence reviewed.
