# WP06 Section 05 Australia, Aotearoa/New Zealand, and Pacific pantry and condiment gap sweep

**Date:** 2026-10-12  
**Status:** Proposal-only, bounded source-led sample.  
**Local source IDs:** `WP06-OCEANIA-PANTRY-20261012-S01`–`S21`.

## Scope and result

This review samples source-named herbs, seasonings, salt and honey products, and coconut-based condiment terms from Australia, Aotearoa/New Zealand, Hawaiʻi, Fiji, and Samoa. It does not complete the WP06 regional checklist. The plan's Australia/Aotearoa/Pacific line remains open.

Three source-supported leaf seasonings are the clearest new identity candidates: Australian lemon myrtle (*Backhousia citriodora*) leaves, Māori horopito (*Pseudowintera colorata*) leaves, and Māori kawakawa (*Macropiper excelsum*) leaves. A species-bounded Australian bush-tomato identity is plausible, but the sources do not support activating the Indigenous names as global aliases or settle a Québec French display. Pepperberry, wattleseed, and saltbush remain unresolved at the scope reviewed. Other candidates route to existing owners: mānuka honey to honey, Fijian lolo/miti to coconut products, and karengo/parengo to the seaweed category. Hawaiian paʻakai is a source-supported alias candidate for generic salt; paʻakai ʻulaʻula is a distinct red-salt variant candidate, not a synonym for plain salt.

No canonical JSON, ledger, plan, checklist, roadmap, generated catalog, or other proposal file was changed. This packet does not establish measures, densities, unit mappings, forms, yields, conversions, or substitutions.

## Audit method and ownership

I reviewed the open Section 05 plan entry, the active references and forms, all 66 legacy import candidates, the ledger, and relevant existing proposals and integration READMEs. Prior category owners were treated as boundaries: the active seaweed work, the WP02 honey review, the WP03 candlenut/ʻinamona boundary, the WP03 citrus/finger-lime identity, and existing breadfruit, taro-leaf, sago, macadamia, salt, and coconut-product references. No prior Australia/Aotearoa/Pacific pantry sweep was found. The WP03 `ʻinamona` decision is left untouched; it is a prepared candlenut condiment, not a kukui or salt alias.

Exact-key comparison used case folding, Unicode compatibility decomposition, accent removal, punctuation folding, and whitespace normalization over reference names/aliases, form labels/match terms, and legacy names/aliases. Candidate spellings included botanical names, source-language forms, spelling variants, and French wording where found. No exact owner exists for the regional keys reviewed. The sole exact overlap among the broad boundary search terms is the existing English alias `luau leaves` on `ingredient-ref-taro-leaves`; it is not a new WP06 candidate. The ledger contains no exact record text for the regional candidates and no source IDs under the proposed prefix.

I scanned the `originalText` and `ingredientName` values in all 114 manifest-listed recipe files for whole-phrase matches to the regional names and botanical terms below. There are **zero exact ingredient-line hits**. Generic occurrences of `salt`, `honey`, and `coconut` do not establish regional product demand. The corpus shows no recipe-specific quantity, form, measurement, conversion, or substitution demand for the proposed candidates.

### Regional terms kept out of pantry identities

Fiji’s `nama` is described by Tourism Fiji as fresh raw sea grapes (*Caulerpa racemosa*), and `lumi` as a cooked seaweed-and-coconut-milk preparation. These are WP03 seaweed/compound boundaries, not new WP06 pantry seasonings in this sample. `Kokoda`, `ika vakalolo`, and Samoa’s `palusami`/`oka` are prepared dishes, not ingredient aliases. Lūʻau/luau leaves are already covered by the active taro-leaf alias. [S15–S16, S20]

## Québec French and source-language policy

The strongest Québec evidence in this sample is the local product wording **myrte citronné** for lemon myrtle. It is a market term, supported by a Canadian government trademark listing that names the same species, but it is not presented here as an OQLF recommendation. For horopito, kawakawa, karengo/parengo, bush tomato, pepperberry, wattleseed, saltbush, mānuka honey, Fijian lolo/miti, and Hawaiian paʻakai, no Québec terminology record or local product label was established in this bounded search. Keep their source-language forms or mark the French display unresolved; do not invent literal translations.

Retain diacritics and language-specific punctuation in the evidence: Māori names as published, Māori macrons where they occur in surrounding context, Hawaiian ʻokina in `paʻakai`/`ʻalaea`, and the source spelling variants separately. Do not use one Pacific-wide label to collapse words whose meanings differ by island or language.

## Remaining misses and next-owner boundaries

This bounded sample does not close Australia, Aotearoa/New Zealand, or Pacific cuisine coverage. Specific remaining work includes:

- Australian First Nations ingredients and pantry products by Nation, language, species, plant part, and producer; regional sauces, herb salts, spice blends, and preserved foods were not systematically surveyed.
- Aotearoa/New Zealand regional preserves, relishes, pickles, dried seafood and seaweeds beyond the karengo handoff, and other Māori pantry products were not reviewed. Mānuka honey was routed to the WP02 sugar/sweetener owner for reconciliation with WP04's finding.
- Fiji’s Indo-Fijian pantry and condiment vocabulary, regional preserves, and other island-specific Fijian products remain open. Samoa was sampled only for the coconut `lolo` meaning boundary.
- Tonga, Cook Islands, Tokelau, Niue, Tuvalu, Vanuatu, Solomon Islands, Papua New Guinea, Kiribati, French Polynesia, New Caledonia, and other Melanesian, Micronesian, and Polynesian food traditions were not surveyed here.
- Exact recipe demand was absent for the terms searched in this corpus; future recipe additions may establish demand and change the priority of these gaps.
