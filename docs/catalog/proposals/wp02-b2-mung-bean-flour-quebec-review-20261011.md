## Independent Québec French review of the lupin rows

### White lupin

Santé Canada’s bilingual non-novel ingredient list pairs sweet lupin flour with farine de lupin blanc and gives *Lupinus albus*. OQLF’s entry for *L. albus* lists lupin blanc as a preferred French term. The Québec retailer La Moisson also uses farine de lupin in its French product presentation while the ingredient statement specifies organic white lupin. Together, these sources support the current display farine de lupin blanc as a species-qualified French name. They do not support assigning unqualified farine de lupin to the white-lupin row. [S01, S03, S05]

**Decision:** keep farine de lupin blanc. Add no generic aliases and no form, measure, conversion, yield, substitution, or recipe link.

### Narrow-leaf / blue lupin

Santé Canada’s flour entry gives the French wording farine de lupin à feuilles étroites ou lupin bleu and identifies *L. angustifolius*. OQLF’s species entry records lupin à feuilles étroites for *L. angustifolius*; its terminology source is older and concerns lupin seed, not flour. CFIA’s current seed-inspection material also uses lupin bleu for *L. angustifolius*. The current display keeps the OQLF species descriptor and matches the French flour label used by Santé Canada. [S01, S02, S04]

**Decision:** keep farine de lupin à feuilles étroites. Do not shorten the display to farine de lupin, which would remove the species distinction. Do not activate lupin bleu as a standalone alias: it names the crop and may be used for grain, and the reviewed product sources do not establish a French flour picker alias separate from the full flour label.

### Scope and limits

These are language and identity findings, not proof that the two flours share composition or culinary function. Santé Canada’s list concerns novelty determinations; it is not a food-safety assessment or an OQLF ruling. OQLF’s entries name lupin species, not a flour product. The Québec retailer page uses generic farine de lupin for a white-lupin product and does not establish a global alias. Retain the current species-qualified displays, the empty alias arrays, null densities, empty unit mappings, and absence of forms, conversions, yields, substitutions, and recipe links.

## Separate B2/B3-adjacent gap: generic mung-bean flour

The B2/B3 checklist remains open broadly. This review selects one unallocated pulse-milling product that is separate from the integrated mung-bean-starch identity. A focused search found no previous proposal deciding the unqualified flour identity. Earlier mentions have narrower scopes: the B3 Québec review distinguishes OQLF’s finished bean-thread noodle term from the starch row; the roasted-flour review holds Niru’s specific “Roasted Mong Flour” because that page describes it as a very fine grain starch without resolving species, flour versus extracted starch, or process sequence. Those records do not decide whether ordinary, source-named mung-bean flour merits an identity.

### Product and French-name evidence

- Pride of India’s current manufacturer page names a product “Mung Bean Flour” and describes it as milled from mung beans. It does not give a botanical name, seed-coat status, or roasting sequence. This supports a real flour candidate, not a complete manufacturing specification. [S09]
- A Canadian bilingual snack manufacturer labels one puff ingredient Mung bean flour in English and Farine de haricot mungo in French. The source calls the product Canadian-made, but the flour is an ingredient in a multi-ingredient snack rather than a standalone flour package; it gives no species or milling details. [S11]
- A Walmart Canada listing for PTI Moong Flour describes a different, explicitly split, decorticated, roasted green-gram product that is then ground. This is product-specific evidence that the regional flour market includes a processed grade; it does not make that process universal or establish synonymy with every unqualified flour listing. [S10]
- OQLF’s preferred term haricot mungo appears in its species entry for *V. radiata*, but OQLF also warns that the same French term is used for *V. mungo*, whose English names include urd and black gram. CFIA’s product-code table maps English mung bean / green gram to *V. radiata* and distinguishes *V. mungo* as urd. The English candidate can therefore be species-scoped, while the proposed French compound farine de haricot mungo remains ambiguous in Québec terminology. [S06–S08]
- OQLF’s nouilles à base de farine de haricot mungo entry names a finished noodle product. It supports the French phrase in that noodle context, not a standalone flour identity or a flour-to-starch equivalence. [S12]

A candidate definition, if reopened, should be limited to food flour sold as mung-bean flour and sourced to *Vigna radiata*. Do not let that candidate absorb *V. mungo* / urd flour, the unresolved roasted-mong SKU, extracted mung-bean starch, or finished bean-thread noodles. The evidence does not establish one universal seed-coat or heat-treatment specification. Keep moong flour, green gram flour, moong dal atta, mung flour, mung powder, and roasted-mong phrases out of aliases until each is checked against a source-qualified product and species.

### Why the catalog row is held

The Canadian French product wording is attested, but the same phrase is explicitly species-ambiguous in OQLF. No reviewed source supplies a current Québec standalone flour label that resolves that ambiguity with a species-qualified French display. Do not invent farine de haricot mungo vert or add a Latin name to the display as a workaround. Ask for independent Québec terminology review or a current bilingual package naming the flour and its species before adding the row.

## Measurement, forms, conversions, yields, substitutions, and links

- **Measurements and density:** None of the reviewed plain-flour pages reports a reusable mass-to-volume measure. The PTI item’s package amount and the Pride listing’s package size are package-specific quantities, not flour density or cup weight.
- **Forms:** Do not create a raw, roasted, split, decorticated, or seed-coat form. Sources show differing processing descriptions but do not establish a common form taxonomy, shared behavior, or cross-product conversion.
- **Conversions and yields:** No source supports seed-to-flour yield, flour density, household-unit mapping, package-yield normalization, or a conversion between the candidate flour and mung-bean starch.
- **Substitutions:** Product-use claims and snack ingredient lists do not test recipe replacements. Add no global or contextual rule to or from mung flour, mung starch, urid flour, chickpea flour, or other pulse flour.
- **Recipe links:** The bundled corpus has no relevant line; create no link or relink.

If Québec wording is later resolved and a flour row is accepted, keep it identity-only until separate sources support any alias, form, measurement, conversion, yield, substitution, or recipe link.

## Proposal reconciliation and exact decision boundary

- The integrated WP02 pulse-flour proposal explicitly left broad, color-free pulse flour labels and unsupported aliases inactive; this review does not reopen its accepted lentil, pea, peanut, or lupin rows.
- The roasted-flour proposal holds only the Niru item titled Roasted Mong Flour. Its product page calls it a very fine grain starch from mong beans without botanical identity or process sequence. Keep that hold unchanged; do not use the page as evidence for generic mung-bean flour.
- The WP02 B3 Québec wording review covers extracted mung-bean starch and keeps the OQLF bean-thread noodle identity separate. This review adds no claim to either row.
- This proposal does not close any broad B2/B3 checklist item. Its mung-bean-flour recommendation remains unintegrated; the lupin wording decision is separately recorded in the shared ledger and root recheck proposal.
