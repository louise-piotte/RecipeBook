## Candidate boundary

Corn bran flour would mean a flour/powder milling product made from the bran fraction of food-grade corn. A primary paper studies “corn bran flour” in bread, but that supports use of its experimental sample, not a universal grade, grind, composition, or household product definition [S01]. The active `ingredient-ref-corn-bran` owns generic corn bran / son de maïs. Keep the bran fraction and a possible flour milling identity conceptually distinct; do not treat every bran product as flour.

Exclude cornmeal, corn flour milled from endosperm or whole kernels, corn germ, corn fiber/extract, feed-grade mill feed, composite or bran-enriched flour products, and processed or branded variants. Do not create separate identities for study-only particle sizes, heat treatment, colours, or formulation percentages. Preserve all existing branded entries and their evidence; no merge is proposed absent a transferability review.

The suggested French compound **farine de son de maïs** is descriptive only. OQLF supports the base term *maïs* and the cereal-processing term *son*, but this review found no authoritative Québec attestation for the compound. Keep French aliases and display unresolved pending a dedicated language review.

## Numeric and substitution limits

The primary study examines corn bran flour in bread formulations. Any reported experimental inclusion levels, sample preparation, particle properties, or results are specific to that study and must not become a household measure, bulk density, general flour-replacement ratio, baking recommendation, or unit mapping [S01]. No general yield or transferable volume-to-mass value is established here. If the identity is reconsidered, begin measurement research with the exact food-grade material and record unsupported values as researched without a supported value rather than extrapolating.

The study's formulation does not establish a dish-independent substitution rule, method adjustment, or safe recipe-line replacement. If later integrated, leave substitution blocked pending evidence; add no global same-ingredient form conversion or contextual ingredient substitution. Recipe-specific study percentages remain evidence about that formulation only.

## Root review and final disposition

Add identity-only generic **corn bran flour / farine de son de maïs** as a milling identity. The 2016 steamed-bread study names corn bran flour directly; the USDA ARS cake study independently identifies food-grade corn bran milled to a fine fraction and used as a flour substitute. Together they support the generic culinary identity without requiring a standardized consumer grade. The French display remains descriptive and provisional; no authoritative Québec attestation of the full compound was found. Reuse the existing French CFIA corn-bran source ID `WP03-CORN-BRAN-20261013-S02` for the source-grain naming rule; retain the English CFIA and OQLF component sources as bounded terminology evidence.

Keep the new row separate from corn bran, cornmeal, corn flour, corn germ, isolated corn fibre, bran-enriched composite flours, and prepared cereals. Add no aliases, forms, measures, recipe links, conversions, yields, or active substitutions. Experimental grind and formulation details remain scoped to each study. The existing corn-bran record, branded entries, and product-specific evidence remain unchanged; no roster-only research was pursued.

The final data state and static validation are recorded in the [R1022–R1024 integration README](../batches/wp02-wp03-wp04-r1022-r1024-generic-gap-integration-20261019/README.md). No software tests were run because the active application changes only add database JSON under `AGENTS.md`.
