## Recommendation

**Hold a new active identity for sprouted pearl millet flour until its Québec French product name is supported.** The product itself is well enough attested to remain a distinct A6 candidate: the manufacturer calls it Sprouted Bajra Flour, identifies the ingredient as sprouted pearl millet (*Pennisetum glaucum*), and describes germinating, drying, and grinding the grain [S01]. A current Canada-facing retailer lists the same named product and pairs Bajra with Pearl Millet [S02].

The language evidence does not yet settle the bilingual catalog name. OQLF provides the Québec terms for pearl millet, but no flour or sprouted-flour compound [S04]. A French-facing Canadian marketplace listing uses farine de millet perlé for ordinary pearl millet flour, and a Canadian mill sells a different multigrain product as farine à haute protéine germée; neither names single-grain sprouted pearl millet flour [S05–S07]. Do not make farine de millet perlé germé an active display or alias without a product-specific Québec/Canadian French source or independent Québec wording review.

Keep sprouted bajra flour, sprouted pearl millet flour, and their qualified variants off the existing plain flour row. The current ingredient-ref-pearl-millet-flour remains pearl millet flour / farine de mil chandelle with its accepted bajra flour alias. If the bilingual name is later established, re-review a separate process-qualified flour identity and its search behavior. Do not represent the maker-specific germination, drying, or grinding sequence as a universal product specification.

No recipe link, form, measurement, density, unit mapping, conversion, yield, substitution, or other numeric behavior is supported or proposed. The product pages' nutritional, health, dietary, price, package-size, and recipe-use claims are not catalog behavior.

## Product and Québec/Canadian naming evidence

The 2025 FSSAI cereal standard lists Pearl Millet (Bajra) with *Pennisetum glaucum* and defines Bajra Flour (Pearl Millet Flour) as pearl millet grains milled into flour [S03]. This supports the underlying grain/product boundary; the standard does not define a sprouted-flour class.

Dhatu Organics names a product Sprouted Bajra Flour and declares 100% sprouted Bajra, identifying it as Pearl Millet / *Pennisetum glaucum*. Its product description says whole pearl millet is germinated, gently dried, and stone-ground [S01]. That is direct manufacturer evidence for this product's process and scope only. The page displayed a currently unavailable variant when reviewed; its nutritional, health, and serving claims are not adopted.

NavaFresh, a Canada-facing seller displaying Canadian-dollar pricing, currently lists Dhatu Organics Sprouted Bajra Flour / Pearl Millet Flour and describes germinated pearl millet flour [S02]. This provides Canadian-market evidence that the product is offered under those English names. It is a reseller listing, not a Canadian manufacturer or a French-language package; its vendor-specific cold-milling wording is not generalized.

For French terminology, OQLF gives millet perle, millet à chandelle, and related preferred Québec terms, mapping pearl millet to those terms and identifying the relevant *Pennisetum* species [S04]. It does not define a flour or a sprouted-flour name. Walmart Canada's French-facing listing for a plain Yogi pearl millet flour product uses farine de millet perlé in its product description [S05]; the listing is a third-party marketplace translation and does not concern sprouted flour. Anita's / Nature's Path has a current Canadian French product page titled farine à haute protéine germée, whose ingredient statement includes sprouted millet among a blend of grains and pulses [S06]. It confirms a Canadian French sprouted-flour wording pattern, but neither a pearl-millet-only product nor the proposed full compound. OQLF's graine germée entry supplies Québec food terminology for sprouts and notes that seeds may come from Poaceae, but it defines the whole sprout, not flour made from germinated grain [S07].

These sources justify keeping a species- and process-qualified product candidate visible for future review. They do not establish that the constructed French phrase is a Québec product name. Do not infer that every pearl millet flour is sprouted, or that every product called sprouted Bajra flour follows Dhatu's process.
