# WP02 Section 04 B3 R391 — African yam bean seed-starch boundary review

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-17.
- **Status:** Proposal-only hold. No catalog, ledger, plan, roadmap, generated, recipe, or application file was changed.
- **Checklist:** The open [WP02 Section 04 B3 item](../../RecipeBook-global-ingredient-catalog-plan.txt#L2793), “Water-chestnut flour and other documented regional starches.”
- **Candidate:** Starch isolated from seeds of *Sphenostylis stenocarpa* (African yam bean), distinct from seed flour and the species’ tuber products.
- **Relation to prior work:** R261 held African yam bean seed flour and explicitly left isolated starch as a separate future review. This review assesses the starch fraction only; it does not reopen the seed-flour hold or the separate jicama-starch boundary in R119.
- **Proposal-local source-ID prefix:** `WP02-B3-AFRICAN-YAM-BEAN-SEED-STARCH-R391-20261017`.

## Recommendation

**Keep African yam bean seed starch outside active references and the research ledger.** Primary studies isolate and characterize a starch fraction from *S. stenocarpa* seeds. That supports a technical ingredient boundary from whole seeds, seed flour, tuber material, and protein fractions. It does not establish a current Canadian or Québec standalone human-food starch product, a standardized consumer grade, or a product-matched Québec French name. [S01–S03]

The Canadian-facing human-food evidence is adjacent rather than product-matched: Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada’s GRIN-Global-CA record lists the species’ economic uses as pulse and vegetable, while current Canadian retailer pages show generic beans flour and true-yam flour rather than *S. stenocarpa* starch. The latter are bounded catalog checks, not proof that the starch is unavailable anywhere in Canada. [S04; R261-S09–S10]

OQLF prefers **amidon** as the general starch term and reserves **fécule** more specifically for starch in tubers or other underground organs. That supports the general term, but neither the reviewed OQLF entries nor a Québec product label attests a name for seed starch from this species. Feedipedia’s French plant names are not Québec product terminology. Leave the French display and all aliases unallocated. [R70-S01; R261-S03–S04]

Do not add `ingredient-ref-african-yam-bean-seed-starch`, a candidate ledger record, aliases, forms, density, unit mappings, recipe links, conversions, yields, or active substitutions. Keep Section 04 B3 open.

## Scientific identity and product boundary

Kew accepts *Sphenostylis stenocarpa* as a species and lists African Yam Bean and Yam Pea as common names; AAFC’s taxon record also lists human-food pulse and vegetable uses. The seed-starch study specifically separates starch from African yam bean seeds and reports varietal starch properties. Those extraction yields and laboratory properties establish a research fraction, not a retail ingredient specification or a kitchen yield. [S01, S04, R261-S01]

The starch fraction is not whole African yam bean seed, African yam bean seed flour, or the species’ tuber starch. Seed and tuber sources must remain separate because the plant has edible seeds and tuberous roots. The R261 seed-flour proposal notes these as separate products and future reviews; no cross-product alias or form is proposed here. [R261-S02, R261-S04]

Heat-moisture treatment and annealing research, along with the 2025 African-yam-bean/cassava starch-blend study, concerns experimental processing and blend behavior. It does not establish a general consumer grade or a form conversion for an unqualified seed-starch product. Do not infer `native`, `modified`, `resistant`, or `pregelatinized` as interchangeable market labels from these research samples. [S02–S03]

## Canadian and Québec product and naming evidence

The current Canadian/Québec product check searched `African yam bean starch`, `African yam bean seed starch`, `Sphenostylis stenocarpa starch`, `Sphenostylis stenocarpa seed starch`, `yam bean starch`, `haricot igname amidon`, and `amidon de haricot igname africain`, with Canada and Québec retailer/product qualifiers. It found no current standalone Canadian human-food package or supplier page for the species’ seed starch. This is a bounded non-discovery, not a market-wide absence claim. [S05]

The rechecked Canadian retail context does not close that gap. Toronto-area iLeOja currently lists generic `Beans Flour`; the Starfish Market flour shelf lists a generic `Motherland's Beans Flour` product (shown sold out when checked) and multiple `Yam Flour` products. None identifies the bean species or an extracted starch. Those pages support only the distinction between generic beans/yam-flour retail labels and the candidate seed-starch product. [R261-S09–S10]

For Québec French, OQLF’s 2026 `amidon` entry prefers **amidon** and explains the narrower use of **fécule**. Its separate `dolique bulbeux` entry lists English `yam bean` and `yam-bean` for jicama (*Pachyrhizus erosus*) while noting that jicama differs from yam-bean. Feedipedia lists French plant names including `haricot igname` and `pois tubéreux africain`, but it does not provide a product-matched Québec starch label. Do not use those terms as French aliases, and do not construct a consumer display from a generic starch term plus an unverified plant name. [R70-S01; R261-S03–S04]
