# WP06 tropical leafy greens identity and naming review

- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-09
- **Status:** integrated on 2026-10-09; see the coordinator integration record below
- **Scope:** water spinach (*Ipomoea aquatica*), Malabar spinach (*Basella* spp.; common-name species boundary), and jute leaves / molokhia (*Corchorus* spp. / *C. olitorius*).
- **Recommendation:** accept four distinct ingredient identities: water spinach; Malabar spinach with no species assignment; genus-level jute leaves; and the narrower molokhia leaf ingredient associated with *C. olitorius*. Do not alias the broad jute-leaves name to molokhia. Keep the source-named greens intact without part-ratio records, and add no preparation forms.

### Identity boundaries

Water spinach is not ordinary spinach. The University of Georgia Extension identifies it as *Ipomoea aquatica*, describes its use as a cooked vegetable, and says its common names include kang kong and water convolvulus. A University of Hawaiʻi postharvest chapter lists leaves and young stems as food and separately names ung choi, kong xin cai, and other market names. Keep these source-named edible greens as one identity; do not split stems from leaves or invent a part ratio. Health Canada distinguishes ordinary spinach (*Spinacia oleracea*) from Malabar spinach (*Basella alba*) in its leafy-greens commodity listing. Keep water spinach separate from the active ordinary-spinach reference and from sweet-potato leaves: shared family or genus relationships do not establish ingredient equivalence. [WP06-TROPICAL-LEAFY-GREENS-20261009-S01–S05]

Malabar spinach is a distinct leafy vegetable from ordinary spinach. North Carolina State Extension describes *Basella alba* as Malabar spinach and documents its leaves as a vegetable; Québec’s French terminology and municipal horticulture sources use `baselle` and `épinard de Malabar`. However, the University of Florida IFAS guide uses `Malabar spinach` for *Basella* spp. and describes several species under that market name, while the University of Guam extension document treats green and red forms under a *B. alba* heading and also mentions *B. rubra*. For identity matching, accept the culinary label at the *Basella* genus level and defer a species-level mapping or red-form conversion. IFAS describes *B. rubra* as typically ornamental, so do not treat that taxon as a food alias without direct food-product evidence. Do not map Malabar spinach to true spinach (*Spinacia oleracea*). [WP06-TROPICAL-LEAFY-GREENS-20261009-S05–S10]

`Jute leaves` and `molokhia` have overlapping but non-equivalent scopes. Health Canada’s crop list calls the commodity `Jute leaves (Corchorus spp.)`; historical USDA plant-introduction records state that young leaves of both *C. capsularis* and *C. olitorius* are eaten as vegetables. Kew currently accepts both species. An Egyptian agricultural study identifies molokhia as *C. olitorius* and associates the name with the plant’s leaves used as a vegetable. Therefore keep generic jute leaves at the genus level and molokhia leaves at the narrower *C. olitorius* culinary scope. Their shared genus does not justify treating the names as interchangeable aliases. [WP06-TROPICAL-LEAFY-GREENS-20261009-S05, S11–S13]

For fr-CA, `épinard d'eau` is supported by the Canadian federal terminology service; `baselle` is the Québec preferred term, and Québec government and municipal sources connect it with *Basella alba* / Malabar spinach. For molokhia, the evidence supports retaining `molokhia` in the French label and a Montréal-area retailer provides `corète potagère` as local product wording. That listing names a dried product, so it supports wording only; it does not establish a general preparation state. `Feuilles de jute` has not been verified in Québec culinary terminology. Keep both jute-leaf French and molokhia French labels marked provisional pending bilingual review. [WP06-TROPICAL-LEAFY-GREENS-20261009-S04, S08–S10, S14]
