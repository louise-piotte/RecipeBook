# WP05 Japanese Condiments and Preserves Boundary Review

**Review date:** 2026-10-10  
**Status:** proposal only; no canonical catalog, ledger, plan, roadmap, recipe, or test files changed.  
**Scope:** the open Japanese pantry groups in Section 05 of `docs/RecipeBook-global-ingredient-catalog-plan.txt`: miso styles, amazake, ponzu, mentsuyu, shiro dashi, teriyaki/tare, tonkatsu sauce, okonomiyaki sauce, Japanese mayonnaise, yuzu kosho, umeboshi, pickled ginger, and other preserved condiments.

## Review method and live-catalog findings

I inspected the canonical ingredient-reference package, legacy identity catalog, research ledger, and all 114 bundled recipe files (1,123 ingredient rows). Exact ingredient-text and reference-ID searches found:

| Existing identity or recipe line | Live state | Recipe coverage |
|---|---|---|

## Proposed identity and alias dispositions

These are recommendations for root review. All proposals are identity-only: no forms, measurements, densities, unit mappings, yields, conversions, recipe links, or substitutions are supported here.

| Group | Disposition | Proposed scope and naming | Boundary / hold |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Generic miso and styles** | **Accept generic identity; preserve white miso separately.** Reconcile legacy candidate `ingredient-ref-miso` into a canonical generic miso row. | English `miso`; French `miso`; French alias `pâte de miso` (OQLF preferred term). Keep existing `ingredient-ref-white-miso-paste` as its own rice-miso style identity because a bundled recipe explicitly calls for white miso. | Do not map white miso to generic miso or assign `white miso` as a generic alias. Hold red/white, rice/barley/soybean, sweet/mild/salty, and mixed miso labels as a bulk set: MAFF treats these as overlapping classification dimensions, not as spelling variants of generic miso. Keep the current white-miso French display provisional pending independent fr-CA review. [S01–S03] |
| **Amazake** | **Accept a separate identity.** | Shared English/French display `amazake`; no aliases. | MAFF describes two product types (rice-koji amazake and sake-lees amazake) and classifies amazake as a drink. Keep it distinct from shio koji, rice koji, rice malt, sake lees, and amazake-based seasoning mixtures. Hold the two specific type labels as separate catalog identities unless recipe demand makes the distinction operationally useful. No French descriptive alias is source-attested here. [S04] |
| **Ponzu** | **Accept one bounded soy-and-citrus sauce identity.** | English `ponzu sauce`; French `sauce ponzu`; no aliases. Scope this row to ready-to-use soy-and-citrus ponzu seasoning. | Kikkoman documents both a narrower citrus-only use and the common soy/citrus bottled use, and notes that modern usage does not consistently preserve the distinction. Do not let this proposed row silently cover citrus-only ponzu; hold a bare `ponzu` alias and the French generic descriptor `sauce soja citronnée`. Kikkoman France’s product supports the proposed French name for its soy/citrus product, not every ponzu formulation. [S05–S06] |
| **Mentsuyu** | **Accept distinct identity.** | English and French display `mentsuyu`; no aliases. | Yamaki identifies `mentsuyu` as its own noodle-seasoning product line; Ninben separately describes its `tsuyu no moto` as a concentrated seasoning used for noodle soup and other dishes. Keep it apart from Hondashi granules, shiro dashi, plain dashi, and dish-specific tsuyu products. No French descriptive alias is supported; retain the Japanese romanization pending Québec terminology review. [S07–S08, S10] |
| **Shiro dashi** | **Accept distinct identity.** | English and French display `shiro dashi`; no aliases. | Ninben lists white dashi separately from its tsuyu products. Keep it separate from Hondashi, mentsuyu, generic dashi, and white soy sauce (`shiro shoyu`). No French descriptive alias is source-attested here. [S07, S09–S10] |
| **Teriyaki sauce** | **Accept distinct sauce identity.** | English `teriyaki sauce`; French `sauce teriyaki`; no aliases. | Both Kikkoman and OQLF distinguish the sauce from teriyaki as a dish/preparation method. Do not alias bare `teriyaki` to the sauce or to soy sauce. [S11–S13] |
| **Tare** | **Hold generic identity and generic alias.** | No general-purpose `tare` row proposed. | Manufacturer product catalogs show many dish-specific sauces grouped as tare (for example, yakitori, teriyaki, sukiyaki, and other regional sauces); Kikkoman’s yakitori recipe uses tare specifically for a soy basting sauce. Add a named sauce only when its full product/use identity is known. Never map bare `tare` to teriyaki sauce. [S14–S15] |
| **Tonkatsu sauce** | **Hold French activation pending Québec term review; English product identity is supported.** | Candidate English name `tonkatsu sauce`; no aliases. | Bull-Dog lists `TONKATSU SAUCE` as a separate manufacturer product. Keep it distinct from Worcestershire sauce, generic brown sauce, okonomiyaki sauce, and yakisoba sauce. The reviewed primary sources did not settle a fr-CA display term; do not commit a French alias in this batch. [S17] |
| **Okonomiyaki sauce** | **Hold French activation pending Québec term review; English product identity is supported.** | Candidate English name `okonomiyaki sauce`, with manufacturer wording `Okonomi Sauce` available as an English alias; no French aliases. | Otafuku describes Okonomi Sauce as a Japanese topping condiment for okonomiyaki. Keep it distinct from tonkatsu sauce and generic Worcestershire/brown sauce. Do not apply the alias `okonomi sauce` as a French label without a bilingual authority or independent Québec review. [S16] |
| **Japanese mayonnaise** | **Keep the existing generic mayonnaise; do not create a Japanese or brand alias.** | Keep `ingredient-ref-mayo` unchanged. | OQLF defines the generic identity; Kewpie’s own material identifies its flagship mayonnaise as an egg-yolk product while also listing a whole-egg mayonnaise. This is product/style detail within the existing mayonnaise identity, not support for treating all mayonnaise as Kewpie-style or linking the generic recipe lines to a Japanese style. No recipe requests it. [WP05-MAYO-20261010-S01, S18] |
| **Yuzu kosho** | **Accept distinct condiment identity.** | English `yuzu kosho`; French `piment au yuzu` (JETRO’s French rendering); English alias `yuzu chili paste`; no `yuzu pepper` alias. | MAFF describes the condiment as yuzu with chili peppers and salt and explains that regional `kosho` here means chili, not ordinary black pepper. Keep it separate from the existing yuzu fruit, chili pastes, yuzu juice/peel, and yuzu-flavored sauces. The JETRO French phrase is exporter terminology, not an OQLF preference. [S19–S21] |
| **Umeboshi** | **Accept distinct preserve identity.** | English and French display `umeboshi`; English alias `Japanese pickled plum`; no French descriptive alias in this batch. | MAFF identifies umeboshi as salted and dried Japanese ume. Keep it separate from fresh plum/ume, ume sauce, ume vinegar, and ordinary plum pickles. Hold `pickled plum` without the Japanese qualifier and any French descriptive equivalent until terminology review. [S22] |
| **Pickled ginger: gari** | **Hold separate-row/alias activation because of an existing exact-name collision.** | No new `gari` ID or alias proposed. | Endo Foods defines gari as sweetened pickled ginger and separately lists beni shoga. The canonical `ingredient-ref-gari` already means West African cassava gari/garri. Do not merge into that row, rename it, or add Japanese `gari` as its alias. OQLF supports the generic French wording `gingembre mariné` in sushi/sashimi context, but does not resolve the exact French name for the sweetened Japanese product. [S23–S24] |
| **Pickled ginger: beni shoga** | **Accept a named identity-only candidate, with French descriptive aliases held.** | Shared display `beni shoga`; English alias `red pickled ginger`; no French aliases. | MAFF and the manufacturer support red pickled ginger as a distinct preserve; Endo separately lists Beni Shoga and gari. Keep it distinct from generic fresh ginger, gari, and the existing African `gari` row. The shared romanized display is source-attested; a Québec French descriptive label remains open. [S22–S24] |
| **Other regional Japanese preserves** | **Hold broad catch-all identity.** | None proposed. | The plan’s “other regional” wording is a discovery area, not one ingredient identity. Review named products individually against recipe demand and authoritative product/terminology evidence. [S22] |

## Limits and follow-up

- This packet verifies product boundaries and a small set of names only. It does not attach recipe lines to new or existing references, nor does it establish any measurement or interchange rule.
- French terms are accepted only where a Québec authority or a direct French-language manufacturer source supports them. The remaining French display names/descriptive aliases for mentsuyu, shiro dashi, tonkatsu sauce, okonomiyaki sauce, amazake, umeboshi, and preserved-ginger variants need independent Québec terminology review before they become aliases.
- Source-specific formulations (including brand recipes, concentrations, and regional variants) remain attached to their sources. No recipe ratios or product ingredient lists are generalized.
- The West African `gari` alias collision must be resolved in the search/canonical-name policy before Japanese `gari` is stored as a searchable name.
