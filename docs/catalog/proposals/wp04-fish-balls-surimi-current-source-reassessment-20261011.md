## Product boundary evidence

A current Québec manufacturer, Atkins & Frères, lists **galettes de poisson** and **boulettes de poisson** in separate sections and offers species-qualified redfish and halibut examples under each. Its descriptions say the balls contain fish flesh, but it does not publish a full formulation or establish a universal recipe for fish balls. This supports the formed-product identity and a patties-versus-balls distinction; it does not establish that all fish balls are made without surimi. [S02]

Metro Québec lists **Boulette de poisson surgelée / Frozen Fish Ball** under matching English and French product pages with the same product number. The page reported the product out of stock when checked. This is useful bilingual product-name evidence, not an ingredient declaration or current availability guarantee. [S03]

A current Walmart Canada listing for **Gold Label Mélange de boulettes de poisson / Fish Ball Mix** says its fish balls are formed from fish paste and lists surimi in the product ingredients. The listing identifies this one product as a mix, and its fish-paste/surimi wording confirms that some fish-ball products use surimi as an ingredient. Walmart notes that product information can be supplied by manufacturers, suppliers, or third parties and is not independently verified; the page showed the item out of stock when accessed. Treat its formulation as a seller-reported, product-specific example only. [S04]

This evidence supports a relationship between product states without collapsing them:

- **Fish ball** is a formed finished product. Product examples include species-specific fish-flesh balls and a surimi-based mix.
- **Surimi** is a paste ingredient according to OQLF; **surimi brut** is a narrower paste without additives. OQLF also has a separate `pâte de surimi` entry for an added-ingredient paste used to manufacture imitation seafood. A finished retail product is listed as `râpé de surimi`; that shows the same word in a retail item name, though the page does not specify its exact surimi state. [S05–S08]
- **Fish paste** is not a safe alias for every surimi product or fish ball. A fish ball can be made from a fish-paste input, but the paste and formed product occupy different stages and have no supported global conversion or substitution.
- **Imitation crab** is a finished food made from surimi that imitates crab. CFIA says surimi-based products resembling more valuable seafood should be clearly identified as imitations or by a suitable descriptive common name. OQLF lists `similicrabe` and `simili-chair de crabe`; neither term is an alias for actual crab meat. [S08–S09]
- **Fish powder** is a dried/ground state. The catalog has the narrowly sourced Bonga product only, with no generic `fish powder` or `poudre de poisson` alias. Its separate active identity and source limits are documented in the [Bonga fish-powder review](wp04-section-p-fish-powder-identity-boundary-review-20261011.md). [S10]
- **Fish cakes/croquettes** are not aliases for balls. The Québec manufacturer’s product line separates patties and balls. OQLF defines `croquette de poisson` as a fish-based mixture with breadcrumbs and egg, shaped and fried. No broad fish-cake row is proposed here. [S02, S11]

## Limits

This reassessment confirms the existing narrow fish-ball identity and the existing surimi hold. It does not add or revise an active identity. Retail listings show more than one product construction, and the Québec manufacturer does not publish a complete formulation. An exact recipe or a specifically requested retail product could justify a narrower future row for raw surimi paste, fish-cake product, imitation crab, or another variant. Until then, preserve the existing product-state boundaries and keep numeric and recipe behavior unset.
