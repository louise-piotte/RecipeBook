# WP02 A5/A7 — Hattai-ko, Mugikogashi, and Regional Roasted-Grain / Gram Products

- **Date:** 2026-10-11
- **Status:** Proposal only. This review changed no shared catalog, ledger, plan, roadmap, code, recipe, or test file.
- **Scope:** Recheck Japanese Hattai-ko / mugikogashi against current Japanese authorities and manufacturers; separate grain-roasted products from Québec heat-treated functional flours; revisit roasted Bengal gram, urid, mung/mong, and related regional flour wording; audit the current seed and all bundled recipes.

## Recommendation

Keep the unqualified search names **Hattai-ko**, **mugikogashi**, and **roasted grain flour** unassigned. Do not add them as aliases to the existing Québec roasted-barley-flour identity. Japanese official sources support a standard barley product called mugikogashi, also called Hattai-ko, made by roasting whole barley and milling it. A Japanese manufacturer also documents regional use of the Hattai-ko family for roasted and ground corn or millet. An exact current Maehara product sold as Hattai-ko / mugikogashi is 100% domestic barley and is therefore a barley product, but that SKU-specific fact does not make the unqualified regional name safe as a global alias. The current Québec mill’s roasted barley flour is a separately described functional, heat-treated product; its published page does not state the treatment order or establish process equivalence with Japanese grain-roasted flour. The Québec display **farine d’orge torréfiée** remains defensible for barley-qualified flour; no Québec source located in this review names Hattai-ko or mugikogashi.

Keep the current product-specific pulse records and holds:

- Retain **chana sattu / farine de sattu** for roasted *Cicer arietinum* flour. Its official definition, Ontario product page, and Canadian French retail descriptions align. Keep the current aliases; do not add bare **roasted gram flour**, **gram flour**, **besan**, **sattu**, or **pottukadalai maavu** globally.
- Retain the existing Niru **roasted urid flour / farine de haricot urd torréfiée** identity as product-specific. Niru describes a different pulse and a different order of operations: finely milled urid flour is then slow-roasted. Canada’s CFIA French species wording supports **haricot urd**; the exact compound flour name remains provisional because no Québec French product label was found.
- Keep Niru **Roasted Mong Flour** as the existing clarification candidate. Its current product description says “mong beans” and “very fine grain starch” but does not identify the species or explain whether the flour is roasted before or after milling, or whether it is an extracted starch product. Do not map it to mung-bean starch, whole mung, chana sattu, or roasted urid flour.
- Treat whole roasted Bengal gram / pottu kadalai as a pulse product, not a flour. A historical Canadian manufacturer list distinguishes a whole roasted chana-dhal item from flour products. If a recipe later calls for pottukadalai maavu, resolve that exact flour context against the recipe’s source before adding an alias or row.

No new identity, alias, form, measurement, conversion, yield, recipe link, or substitution is justified by the current corpus. If a future recipe names a specific Japanese or South Asian product, use its stated grain/species and product state; do not infer them from an unqualified regional name.

The product-word overlap **roasted / toasted flour** is not enough to establish that all items share one product identity. Product-specific sources differ on the cereal or pulse and on whether the raw grain is roasted before milling or already-milled flour is roasted. The A5 roasted-corn and A7 roasted-wheat/barley products already in the catalog are not universal matches for every regional “roasted flour.”

## Québec French and search-name decision

1. **For a recipe that explicitly identifies roasted barley flour**, retain the current Québec-backed display **roasted barley flour / farine d’orge torréfiée**. It is the existing row for this ingredient class. A recipe naming only Hattai-ko or mugikogashi still needs product or region context before it is linked. Do not make a second row solely to encode Japanese origin.
2. **Do not add Hattai-ko, mugikogashi, 麦こがし, or はったい粉 as global aliases.** MEXT and MAFF support a barley-specific standardized/recipe context, while a manufacturer documents regional corn and millet products under the broader Hattai-ko family. A global alias would erase that context difference. No Québec French product label or terminology entry for these native names was located.
3. **Do not translate the unqualified Japanese terms as farine d’orge torréfiée.** That French phrase is defensible only once the recipe or product context establishes barley. A generic phrase such as *farine de céréale torréfiée* would leave the grain unresolved and was not found as a Québec product name.
4. **Keep the current chana-sattu bilingual naming.** The existing French alias **farine de pois chiches grillés** is supported by Canadian French product pages and aligns with OQLF’s species name **pois chiche** for *Cicer arietinum*. Retailer wording **farine de gramme rôtie** is recorded as a listing title only; it is not preferred over the current, clearer French phrase [S12–S13, S21].
5. **Keep haricot urd separate from haricot mungo.** CFIA’s Canadian French code table supplies **haricot urd** for *Vigna mungo*, while OQLF documents the two-species ambiguity of **haricot mungo**. The active compound display **farine de haricot urd torréfiée** is a defensible provisional construction from a Canada-attested species name and the manufacturer’s roast descriptor, but no direct Québec flour label confirms the full phrase. Do not add **farine de haricot mungo torréfiée** as an alias [S14–S16].
6. **Do not allocate a French name for roasted mong flour yet.** Species and product type remain unsettled, so a phrase such as *farine de haricot mungo torréfiée* would be premature and could collapse *Vigna radiata* into *Vigna mungo* [S15–S17].

## Precise evidence gaps

- The Japanese official table is a national food-composition classification, not a product standard specifying all regional uses, milling grades, roast temperatures, or heat-transfer sequence.
- Maehara’s broad page describes corn and millet as regional Hattai-ko inputs, but does not provide named SKUs, exact formulations, or Québec/Canadian export labels for those variants.
- The exact Maehara barley SKU proves that one Hattai-ko / mugikogashi product is 100% barley; it cannot resolve a recipe that gives only the regional name.
- Les Moulins de Soulanges describes its heat-treated functional flour but does not publish enough process detail to establish equivalence to grain-roasted Japanese flour.
- No Québec OQLF entry, Québec manufacturer label, Canadian bilingual package, or Québec retailer page for Hattai-ko, mugikogashi, or their corn/millet variants was located. The base French phrase **farine d’orge torréfiée** is available only for the barley-qualified product.
- Niru does not publish the species and process specification for Roasted Mong Flour. The current phrase “very fine grain starch” is insufficient to establish whether its product belongs to a flour, extracted-starch, mung-grain, or urid-grain identity.
- No current Canadian French label for roasted urid flour or Tamil pottukadalai maavu was located. The CFIA/OQLF species sources and Canadian English product labels support species scoping, not a preferred French name for every flour product.

## Integration boundary

No integration change is proposed. Keep the existing roasted barley, chana-sattu, roasted-urid, and kinako references as they are; retain **candidate-roasted-mong-flour** as unresolved; leave Hattai-ko / mugikogashi and pottukadalai maavu off active aliases. The exact product-specific context that would change this recommendation is a recipe or current bilingual package that identifies the grain/species and process, plus a Québec French name where one is proposed.
