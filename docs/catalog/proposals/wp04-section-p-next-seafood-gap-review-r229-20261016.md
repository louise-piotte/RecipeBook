# WP04 Section P — frozen cooked shrimp product-boundary review (R229)

- **Review and source-access date:** 2026-10-16.
- **Packet type:** proposal-only identity and picker-boundary research.
- **Recommendation:** hold the generic frozen-cooked-shrimp product group outside active references, forms, legacy identities, and the research ledger until the picker can preserve the frozen qualifier or present the other cooked-shrimp product boundary alongside it.
- **Candidate wording reviewed:** English `frozen cooked shrimp`; Québec French `crevette cuite surgelée`, with the directly listed plural `crevettes cuites surgelées`.
- **Shared-data effect:** none. No catalog, form, ledger, recipe, generated-index, plan, roadmap, or checklist change is proposed.
- **Tests:** none. This is a proposal-only review; no tests or emulator checks were run.

## Bounded decision

Canadian and Québec product listings establish a real frozen, cooked shrimp product class. A Costco Canada bilingual item and several current Mayrand seafood listings use that scope; Codex separately treats raw, partly or fully cooked, peeled or unpeeled, and quick-frozen states as product descriptors. The product is not a species identity, and retailer package counts or weights do not support reusable conversions.

The current picker creates a blocking search result collision. A row named `frozen cooked shrimp` would also be the sole result for the shorter query `cooked shrimp`. The bilingual Québec retailer entry for Ocean Jewel has the exact product title `Cooked Shrimp / Crevettes cuites`, appears under prepared fish and seafood, and does not state whether it is frozen. A frozen-only row therefore cannot safely own the unqualified cooked-shrimp query. The same problem appears in French if the display follows the current plural market wording.

**Hold the frozen-only candidate.** Reopen this specific product boundary when search can preserve the full state qualifier or when a reviewed cooked-shrimp parent/state representation can show both state-specific choices and maintain the accepted canned-cocktail boundary. This is not a reconsideration of the held generic raw/fresh shrimp question or any species-specific shrimp hold.

## Product evidence and limits

Costco Business Centre currently lists bilingual views of item **175548** as `Kirkland Signature Frozen 90-130 Tail-off Cooked Shrimp` and `Kirkland Signature – Crevettes cuites surgelées sans queue 90-130`. The same item is described as cooked, individually quick-frozen, peeled, deveined, tail-off, 90–130 per 454 g, and packed in a 907 g bag. Those English and French pages are two language views of one SKU, not two independent product formulations. They prove the local market wording and state for that SKU only. [S01–S02]

Québec wholesaler Mayrand has a current `Crevette cuite surgelée` category with multiple products and brands. Its Sea Horse listing for item **02222** is titled `Crevettes cuites 70/90 surgelées 1135 g`, and the current page reports stock at four Québec locations. Other entries show different tail/presentation descriptions and package/count-grade labels. These listings support a recurring product class and show that presentation/count labels vary. They do not identify the shrimp species, define a count denominator for every SKU, or establish an interchangeable quantity. [S03–S04]

Metro has a current 280 g Ocean Jewel product titled `Cooked Shrimp / Crevettes cuites`, product **059371809866**, in its prepared fish-and-seafood listing. Its bilingual pages do not state frozen status, species, or ingredients. That missing state detail cannot be read as proof the product is chilled; it does establish that the shorter bilingual names also appear in a distinct current sale listing without a frozen qualifier. This is the material picker collision, not evidence that the Metro item and the frozen SKUs are the same product. [S05–S06]

## Québec naming and species boundary

The OQLF GDT entry makes **crevette** the preferred Québec French term and gives English **shrimp**; it labels generic English **prawn** as United Kingdom usage. The entry was last updated in 1998 and does not define frozen or cooked products. Mayrand's category heading directly supplies the singular descriptive phrase `crevette cuite surgelée`, while the Costco and Metro Québec-facing product titles use the plural `crevettes cuites surgelées` and `crevettes cuites`. These are market wording observations, not a claim that OQLF has approved the full compound term. Do not add `prawn` as an alias. [Existing source `WP04-FRESH-SHRIMP-20261009-S01`; S02–S06]

The current CFIA Fish List search for `shrimp` returns **93 name/taxon rows** across multiple taxa and families. It includes generic `Shrimp / Crevette` rows for multiple shrimp taxa, including members of Penaeidae, Pandalidae, Palaemonidae, Sergestidae, and other families in the results. The CFIA guidance says its Fish List is a Canadian labelling reference and that only names listed for the actual fish species should be used for that species. This supports keeping this proposal at a market-product level, without assigning a Latin species or making generic product wording a species alias. The result page reports a 2023-09-18 modification date, so it is not a live census of market products. [Existing sources `WP04-SECTIONP-SPOT-SHRIMP-R95-20261014-S03` and `WP04-SECTIONP-WHITELEG-SHRIMP-R94-20261014-S02`]

Codex CXS 92-1981 applies to quick-frozen raw, partly cooked, or fully cooked shrimp/prawns, peeled or unpeeled. It specifies families including Penaeidae, Pandalidae, Crangonidae, and Palaemonidae; a package must not mix genera, though similar species of one genus may be combined. It allows count-based package descriptions and requires the product's presentation to be adequately described. This is a primary international food standard, not Canadian law and not an app taxonomy. It supports the state distinctions and the instruction not to infer mixed taxa inside one package; it does not establish species, local prevalence, a universal product formula, or recipe equivalence. [S07]

Do not attach a cooked/frozen form to Northern shrimp or another species-specific row. These source-specific grades/package labels are not exact counts, common package sizes, household measures, mass-per-shrimp mappings, or cooking yields. Store none as active unit mappings or reusable amounts.
- **Density and conversions:** No density, volume mapping, package/drained yield, frozen-to-thawed mass conversion, or raw-to-cooked conversion is supported. No product-weight amount appears in the bundled recipes.
- **Substitutions:** No method- and quantity-scoped evidence supports replacing frozen cooked shrimp with raw shrimp, Northern shrimp, canned cocktail shrimp, dried shrimp, shrimp balls, shrimp paste, or another shellfish. Add no global/contextual substitution, warning, conversion, yield, or recipe link.

## Final recommendation

**Hold** the generic frozen cooked shrimp / `crevette cuite surgelée` candidate outside active references, forms, legacy identities, and the research ledger. The product group and its bilingual market wording are real and source-verifiable. The current phrase-first picker would nevertheless assign the broad `cooked shrimp` / `crevettes cuites` query to a frozen-only item even though Metro currently sells a cooked shrimp product with no frozen state disclosed. No bundled recipe demand resolves which product state is intended.

Keep the hold narrowly scoped to this frozen-only search boundary. Do not add an alias to canned cocktail shrimp, dried shrimp, Northern shrimp, shrimp balls, or a shrimp paste; do not promote generic `shrimp`, `prawn`, or `raw shrimp`; and do not reopen the R221 fish-stick or R224 frozen-tempura decisions. Reconsider only after state-aware picker handling or a reviewed parent/state model can preserve the frozen qualifier and keep the Metro cooked-shrimp product and accepted canned product boundary visible.
